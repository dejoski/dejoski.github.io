Boaz, a wealthy Bethlehemite, married Ruth and became an ancestor of King David and Jesus Christ.

🗺 Places mentioned alongside Boaz on the map →

Also known as Booz

Boaz was a wealthy man from Bethlehem who played a significant role in the story of Ruth (Ruth 2-4). He was a relative of Naomi's deceased husband, Elimelech, and acted as a kinsman-redeemer for Ruth, a Moabite widow. When Ruth gleaned grain in Boaz's field, he showed her kindness and protection, recognizing her loyalty to Naomi (Ruth 2). Later, Naomi instructed Ruth to approach Boaz at night and request that he fulfill his role as a kinsman-redeemer (Ruth 3). Boaz agreed to marry Ruth, after ensuring that a closer relative did not wish to claim this right (Rut.4.1-12). Their marriage led to the birth of Obed, who became the grandfather of King David (Rut.4.13-17). Boaz is also mentioned in the genealogy of Jesus Christ (Matt. Rut.1.5; Luk.3.32), highlighting his importance in God's plan of redemption. Additionally, Boaz is associated with one of the two bronze pillars in Solomon's temple, which was named after him (1Ki.7.21; 1 Chr. 1Ki.2.11-12). His story demonstrates the importance of integrity, kindness, and faithfulness in fulfilling one's duties and God's purposes.

Judah to David: Ruth 4 and Matthew 1

FamilyStated in the text

Ruth 4:18-22 lists ten generations from Perez to David, matching Matthew 1:3-6 and Luke 3:31-33. Scholars note that ten names cover about seven centuries, so the list is likely selective.

Ruth and Boaz: the kinsman-redeemer

FamilyStated in the text

Boaz, a relative of Elimelech, allowed Ruth to glean, acted as redeemer after a closer relative declined, bought the family land and married Ruth. The account joins the law of redemption (Lev 25) and levirate practice with a story of kindness (hesed).

Boaz, Obed, Jesse and David

FamilyStated in the text

Ruth and Boaz's son Obed became father of Jesse, father of David. The book of Ruth ends with this genealogy, presenting the Moabite Ruth as David's great-grandmother; Matthew repeats the line.

Salmon and Rahab: the Jericho woman in David's ancestry

FamilyChurch tradition

Matthew names Rahab as the wife of Salmon and mother of Boaz. The identification with the Rahab of Jericho is the standard reading, but the Old Testament never says Salmon married her, and chronology is debated.

Bible dictionary

Boaz Easton Easton’s Bible Dictionary (1897)

Alacrity. (1.) The husband of Ruth, a wealthy Bethlehemite. By the “levirate law” the duty devolved on him of marrying Ruth the Moabitess (Ruth 4:1-13). He was a kinsman of Mahlon, her first husband. (2.) The name given (for what reason is unknown) to one of the two (the other was called Jachin) brazen pillars which Solomon erected in the court of the temple (1 Kings 7:21; 2 Chr. 3:17). These pillars were broken up and carried to Babylon by Nebuchadnezzar.

Boaz Hastings Hastings’ Dictionary of the Bible (1898)

BO'AZ, or BO'OZ (lovely), was a descendant of Judah, Ruth 2:1, and through him is traced the regular succession of Jewish kings. Matt 1:5. Boaz was a man of wealth and of great respectability. He married Ruth and begat Obed, the father of Jesse, the father of David. See Ruth.

BO'AZ (lively), one of the brazen pillars erected by Solomon before the portico of the temple. Its companion was Jachin. They were named for their givers or makers, or else had a symbolical meaning. 1 Kgs 7:21; 2 Chr. 3:17; Jer 52:21.
