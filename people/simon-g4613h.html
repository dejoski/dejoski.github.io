Simon was one of the brothers of Jesus, a son of Mary and Joseph.

🗺 Places mentioned alongside Simon on the map →

Simon is mentioned in Mat.13.55 and Mrk.6.3 as one of the brothers of Jesus, along with James, Joseph (or Joses), and Judas (or Jude). He was a son of Mary and Joseph and grew up in Nazareth with Jesus and his other siblings.

The gospels do not provide any further details about Simon's life or his relationship with Jesus during his earthly ministry. However, it is noted that Jesus' brothers did not believe in him initially (Jhn.7.5).

After Jesus' resurrection and ascension, his brothers, including Simon, are mentioned as being part of the early Christian community in Jerusalem, joining the apostles in prayer (Act.1.14). This suggests that Simon, along with his brothers, came to faith in Jesus as the Messiah and became involved in the early church.

Who are the brothers of Jesus?

FamilyDebated

Mark names James, Joses, Simon, and Jude as brothers of Jesus. Protestants usually hold they were younger half-siblings, Catholic and Orthodox traditions (Hieronymian and Epiphanian views) hold they were cousins or Joseph's sons from an earlier marriage.

Jesus' "brothers" James, Joses, Simon and Judas

FamilyStated in the text

Mark and Matthew say the people of Nazareth called Jesus the son of Mary and named four "brothers" (adelphoi). The word can mean full or half brothers, or another relative, which is the core of the traditional debate.

The Hieronymian view: the brothers as cousins

FamilyChurch tradition

Jerome argued that the four "brothers" were Jesus' cousins, sons of Mary of Clopas, whom he took to be Mary's sister or relative. He held that Mary remained a virgin. This became the standard view in the Latin church.

Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — Mary of Clopas at the cross.Mark 15:40 — Mary mother of James the less and Joses.Galatians 1:19 — James the Lord's brother.
Ways people read this (4)
  • Hieronymian: cousins via Mary of Clopas.
  • Helvidian: younger children of Mary and Joseph.
  • Epiphanian: children of Joseph from an earlier marriage.
  • Key counter-point: Mark 15:40 names Mary as mother of James and Joses separately from Mary the mother of Jesus.

Sources: Jerome, Against Helvidius (c. AD 383) · Jerome, Commentary on Galatians (c. AD 386)

The Epiphanian view: sons of Joseph by an earlier marriage

FamilyChurch tradition

Epiphanius and much of the eastern church held that the brothers were Joseph's children from a prior marriage, which preserves Mary's perpetual virginity. Its early source is the second-century Protoevangelium of James.

The Helvidian view: younger children of Mary and Joseph

FamilyDebated

This reads the brothers as sons of Mary and Joseph after Jesus. Helvidius argued from the plain sense of "brothers" and Jesus as "firstborn"; Jerome wrote against him. Many Protestant scholars hold it today.

Where the text shows it: Luke 2:7 — Mary's firstborn son.Matthew 1:25 — Joseph did not know her until she bore a son.Mark 6:3 — Brothers and sisters named.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Plain reading of adelphos and the "firstborn" phrase.
  • Against: perpetual virginity held by the early church, and Jesus entrusting Mary to John at the cross.
  • "Until" in Matthew 1:25 does not settle what followed.

Sources: Jerome, Against Helvidius (c. AD 383) · Tertullian, On the Flesh of Christ (early 3rd c.)

The Lord's brothers are distinguished from the Twelve

RoleWidely accepted

John says his brothers did not believe during the ministry, and Acts lists the brothers with Mary separately from the apostles. Both point against simply counting the brothers among the Twelve.
