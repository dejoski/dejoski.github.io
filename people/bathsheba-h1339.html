Also known as Bath-shua · Bath-sheba

Bathsheba was the wife of Uriah the Hittite, one of David's mighty men. David committed adultery with her, resulting in her pregnancy (2Sa.11.3). After Uriah's death in battle, arranged by David, Bathsheba became David's wife and gave birth to Solomon (2Sa.12.24). Later, when David was old, Bathsheba and the prophet Nathan worked together to ensure Solomon's ascension to the throne (1Ki.1.11-31). As queen mother, Bathsheba held a position of influence in the royal court (1Ki.2.13-19). She is also mentioned in the superscription of Psalm 51, which is associated with David's repentance after his sin with Bathsheba. In 1Ch.3.5, Bathsheba is referred to as "Bath-shua," and her four sons with David are listed. Bathsheba's story illustrates the consequences of sin, God's mercy and forgiveness, and the importance of divine sovereignty in the fulfillment of God's plans.

Ahithophel as Bathsheba's grandfather

FamilyHypothesis

Bathsheba is daughter of Eliam, and a mighty man Eliam is called son of Ahithophel the Gilonite; some therefore see Ahithophel's betrayal as tied to David's treatment of his granddaughter. Scripture does not make this link.

Solomon versus Adonijah: the succession struggle

RoleStated in the text

Adonijah, backed by Joab and Abiathar, declared himself king. Nathan and Bathsheba got David to confirm Solomon, and Zadok, Nathan and Benaiah anointed him at Gihon. Solomon then had Adonijah and Joab killed and sent Abiathar to Anathoth.

Four women in Matthew's genealogy

FamilyDebated

Matthew names Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and "the wife of Uriah" in a list otherwise of men. Tamar, Rahab and Ruth are likely non-Israelites or of irregular circumstances, and Bathsheba is named by Uriah. Commentators propose that Matthew highlights Gentile inclusion, scandal, or God's use of irregular paths.

Bible dictionary

Bathsheba, Or Bathsheba Smith Smith’s Bible Dictionary (1863)

(daughter of the oath), (2 Samuel 11:3) etc., also called Bath-shua in (1 Chronicles 3:5) the daughter of Eliam, (2 Samuel 11:3) or Ammiel, (1 Chronicles 3:5) the son of Ahithophel, (2 Samuel 23:34) and wife of Uriah the Hittite. (B.C. 1035.) The child which was the fruit of her adulterous intercourse with David died; but after marriage she became the mother of four sons, Solomon, (Matthew 1:6) Shimea, Shobab and Nathan. When Adonijah attempted to set aside the succession promised to Solomon, Bath-sheba informed the king of the conspiracy. (1 Kings 1:11,15,23) After the accession of Solomon, she, as queen-mother, requested permission of her son for Adonijah to take in marriage Abishag the Shunammite. (1 Kings 2:21-25)
