Simon, also known as the Zealot, was one of the twelve apostles chosen by Jesus.

🗺 Places mentioned alongside Simon on the map →

Also known as Zealot · Zelotes

Simon, also called the Zealot, was one of the twelve apostles chosen by Jesus Christ (Mat.10.4; Mrk.3.18; Luk.6.15). The title "Zealot" likely indicates that Simon belonged to a Jewish political faction known for its fierce nationalism and opposition to Roman rule before following Jesus.

After Jesus' ascension, Simon the Zealot is mentioned as being among the apostles who gathered in the upper room in Jerusalem to pray and await the coming of the Holy Spirit (Act.1.13).

Beyond these brief mentions, no further details about Simon the Zealot's life or ministry are provided in the New Testament. Church tradition holds that he preached the gospel in various regions, possibly including Egypt, North Africa, and Persia, before being martyred for his faith, though the accounts vary.

Jesus chooses the Twelve

RoleStated in the text

Jesus selected twelve from his wider group of disciples and named them apostles. The four lists (Matthew, Mark, Luke, Acts) agree on twelve names with one variation in the tenth to twelfth positions (Thaddaeus or Judas of James).

Simon the Zealot as a brother in the Alphaeus family

FamilyHypothesis

The claim that Simon the Zealot was a brother of James, Matthew and Judas is not supported by any text. It combines the Hieronymian list of four "brothers" (James, Joses, Simon, Jude) with the apostle list by shared names.

Ways people read this (3)
  • For: James, Simon and Judas appear in both lists; Jerome made James and Judas apostles.
  • Against: no text gives Simon the Zealot a father; "Simon" was extremely common; the name Joses is not in the apostle list.
  • Some later traditions identify Simon the Zealot with the Symeon of Jerusalem; the ancient testimony is weak.

Sources: Luke 6:15 · Matthew 13:55

Four apostles as sons of Alphaeus

FamilyHypothesis

The proposal that James, Matthew, Judas and Simon the Zealot were all brothers and all cousins of Jesus combines several separate inferences. Each step is uncertain and only the first has support in the text (a shared father for James and Levi).

Ways people read this (3)
  • Attested: James and Levi share a father named Alphaeus; Mary mother of James is at the cross.
  • Inferred: Levi = Matthew; Alphaeus = Clopas; Judas is brother of James; Simon is also brother; Mary of Clopas is Mary's sister.
  • Each inference has defenders, but the combined case depends on all of them at once.

Sources: Mark 2:14 · Luke 6:15-16

Pairings in the apostle lists

RoleDebated

Matthew's list arranges the Twelve in six apparent pairs, and Mark says Jesus sent them out two by two. Some see these as mission pairs; the texts do not say the list order gives partners.

Is the Twelve one extended family network?

FriendsHypothesis

One proposal is that most of the Twelve were kin, neighbours or business contacts around the Sea of Galilee. The Gospels attest two brother pairs, a partnership, a shared home town for three and an invitation between two; the wider network is a pattern suggested by those links, not a stated fact.
