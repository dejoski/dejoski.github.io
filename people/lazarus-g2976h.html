Lazarus, the brother of Mary and Martha, was raised from the dead by Jesus (Jhn.11.1-44; 12.1-2,9-11).

🗺 Places mentioned alongside Lazarus on the map →

Lazarus was the brother of Mary and Martha, who lived in the village of Bethany (Jhn.11.1). Jesus loved Lazarus and his sisters (Jhn.11.5). When Lazarus fell ill, his sisters sent word to Jesus, but by the time Jesus arrived, Lazarus had been dead for four days (Jhn.11.6,17). Jesus went to the tomb and called for Lazarus to come out, and Lazarus emerged, still wrapped in burial cloths (Jhn.11.43-44). This miraculous sign led many to believe in Jesus (Jhn.11.45). Later, Jesus visited Lazarus' home, where Martha served a meal and Mary anointed Jesus' feet with perfume (Jhn.12.1-3). The chief priests plotted to kill Lazarus because his resurrection caused many to believe in Jesus (Jhn.12.9-11). Lazarus' story demonstrates Jesus' power over death and foreshadows His own resurrection.

Simon the leper's house and the anointing

FriendsHypothesis

Mark and Matthew place the anointing at Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, and John places it at a supper with Martha serving and Lazarus reclining. Simon may have been related to the family, but the texts do not say.

Ways people read this (3)
  • Simon was a healed leper who hosted a supper for which Martha served (a neighbor).
  • Simon was a relative, possibly the father or husband of Martha.
  • The two suppers are different events (less common).

Lazarus as the Beloved Disciple?

FriendsHypothesis

John 11 says Jesus loved Lazarus and calls him "he whom you love". Later the Beloved Disciple appears only after John 13. Some see Lazarus there. Early tradition (Irenaeus) names John the apostle.

Ways people read this (4)
  • John son of Zebedee (Irenaeus and most traditions).
  • Lazarus (Filson and others), since he is the only man said to be loved.
  • John Mark, or an otherwise unnamed Judean disciple.
  • A literary ideal figure.

Sources: Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.1.1 · Floyd Filson, Journal of Biblical Literature (1949)

Bible dictionary

Lazarus Easton Easton’s Bible Dictionary (1897)

An abbreviation of Eleazar, whom God helps. (1.) The brother of Mary and Martha of Bethany. He was raised from the dead after he had lain four days in the tomb (John 11:1-44). This miracle so excited the wrath of the Jews that they sought to put both Jesus and Lazarus to death. (2.) A beggar named in the parable recorded Luke 16:19-31.
