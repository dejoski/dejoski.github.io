Mary, the mother of Jesus and wife of Joseph, was a virgin when she conceived by the Holy Spirit.

🗺 Places mentioned alongside Mary on the map →

Mary, the mother of Jesus, was a young Jewish woman from Nazareth betrothed to Joseph, a descendant of David. She was likely a sister of Salome (see Jhn.19.25 and Gosple of Thomas but contra Diatessaron). Before their marriage, an angel appeared to Mary, announcing that she would conceive a child by the Holy Spirit and name Him Jesus. Mary humbly accepted this divine plan, and the virgin birth of Jesus fulfilled the prophecy of the Messiah (Mat.1.16, 18, 20; Luk.1.26-38). After Jesus' birth in Bethlehem, Mary and Joseph presented Him at the temple, where Simeon prophesied that a sword would pierce her soul (Luk.2.5, 16, 19, 34). Mary treasured these events in her heart. She and Joseph raised Jesus in Nazareth, and He was known as the son of Mary (Mat.13.55; Mrk.6.3). During Jesus' crucifixion, Mary stood near the cross with other women, including her sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene (Mat.27.56, 61; Mrk.15.40, 47). After Jesus' resurrection, Mary was among the women who went to the tomb and later informed the disciples (Mat.28.1; Mrk.16.1; Luk.24.10). In the early church, Mary joined the disciples in prayer as they awaited the coming of the Holy Spirit (Act.1.14).

Who is "his mother's sister" in John 19:25?

FamilyDebated

John names either three or four women at the cross: "his mother's sister, Mary of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene" may be 3 or 4. If four, the sister is likely Salome, mother of Zebedee's sons, making James and John Jesus' first cousins.

Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — Four women or threeMark 15:40 — SalomeMatthew 27:56 — Mother of Zebedee's sons
Ways people read this (3)
  • Four women: sister = Salome, so James and John are cousins of Jesus.
  • Three women: Mary of Clopas is the sister, or sister means sister-in-law.
  • Two women sharing the name Mary as sisters is unlikely, which favors the four-woman reading.

Sources: Scripture

Joseph's father: Jacob or Heli

FamilyDebated

Matthew says Joseph's father was Jacob; Luke says Joseph was the son of Heli. Africanus reconciled this with a levirate marriage; others read Luke's line as Mary's and TIPNR lists Heli as Mary's father.

Where the text shows it: Matthew 1:16 — Jacob begat Joseph.Luke 3:23 — Joseph the son of Heli.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Levirate: Jacob and Heli were half brothers (Africanus).
  • Mary's genealogy: Luke lists Heli as Mary's father through Joseph.
  • Two different lines of royal and natural descent.

Sources: Matthew 1:16 · Luke 3:23 · Julius Africanus, Letter to Aristides (cited in Eusebius, Church History)

The Helvidian view: younger children of Mary and Joseph

FamilyDebated

This reads the brothers as sons of Mary and Joseph after Jesus. Helvidius argued from the plain sense of "brothers" and Jesus as "firstborn"; Jerome wrote against him. Many Protestant scholars hold it today.

Where the text shows it: Luke 2:7 — Mary's firstborn son.Matthew 1:25 — Joseph did not know her until she bore a son.Mark 6:3 — Brothers and sisters named.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Plain reading of adelphos and the "firstborn" phrase.
  • Against: perpetual virginity held by the early church, and Jesus entrusting Mary to John at the cross.
  • "Until" in Matthew 1:25 does not settle what followed.

Sources: Jerome, Against Helvidius (c. AD 383) · Tertullian, On the Flesh of Christ (early 3rd c.)

Mary and Salome as sisters

FamilyDebated

John 19:25 lists the women at the cross; if "his mother's sister" is a separate person from Mary of Clopas, she could be Salome of Mark 15:40 or the mother of Zebedee's sons of Matthew 27:56. That would make James and John Jesus' first cousins.

MaryMosther of JesusSalomeSister of Mary.JesusSon of JosephJamesAn apostleJohnAn apostle
Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — His mother, his mother's sister, Mary of Clopas, Mary Magdalene.Mark 15:40 — Mary Magdalene, Mary mother of James and Joses, Salome.Matthew 27:56 — The mother of the sons of Zebedee.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Four women in John (the sister is Salome) versus three (the sister is Mary of Clopas).
  • Two sisters named Mary would be unusual in one family, which supports the four-women reading.
  • No ancient source states that James and John were Jesus' cousins and TIPNR adopts the four-women reading.

Sources: John 19:25 · Mark 15:40 · Matthew 27:56

Mary of Clopas as sister of Mary

FamilyChurch tradition

Reading John 19:25 as three women, "his mother's sister, Mary of Clopas" are one person. Jerome held it, with "sister" meaning a relative or sister-in-law, since two sisters named Mary is hard to credit.

MaryMosther of JesusMaryAlphaeus or Cleopas' wife
Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — The list can be read as three women, with no "and" between the sister and Mary of Clopas.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Jerome: sister in a broad sense.
  • Objection: two sisters with the same name in one family.
  • Alternative: the four-women reading of John 19:25 avoids the problem.

Sources: Jerome, Against Helvidius (c. AD 383)
