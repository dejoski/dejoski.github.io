Clopas, Alphaeus, and the family behind them

FamilyHypothesis

Hegesippus (via Eusebius) says Clopas was Joseph's brother and father of Symeon, second bishop of Jerusalem. Some equate Clopas with Alphaeus (father of James the younger), but the Greek names differ and the match is a hypothesis.

MaryAlphaeus or Cleopas' wifeAlphaeusSon of JacobJamesAn apostleJosephMary's husband
Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — Mary of ClopasMark 3:18 — James son of AlphaeusMark 2:14 — Levi son of Alphaeus
Ways people read this (3)
  • Clopas and Alphaeus are the same man (Aramaic Halphai behind both names).
  • They are different men.
  • If Alphaeus is Joseph's brother, James the younger would be a cousin of Jesus.

Sources: Eusebius, Church History 3.11 and 4.22 (citing Hegesippus)

RoleStated in the text

Matthew and James as brothers (sons of Alphaeus)

FamilyDebated

Mark gives the same father, Alphaeus, to Levi and to the apostle James. Some take the two as brothers, as TIPNR does. The text never says so, though it does name other sets of brothers explicitly.

MatthewAn apostleJamesAn apostleAlphaeusSon of Jacob
Where the text shows it: Mark 2:14 — Levi son of Alphaeus.Mark 3:18 — James son of Alphaeus.Matthew 4:18-22 — Contrast: other brother pairs are named as brothers.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Brothers: one father name; Mark lists Matthew and James close together.
  • Not brothers: Alphaeus was a common name; if brothers, the Gospels would probably say so as with Peter/Andrew and James/John; Matthew is paired with Thomas, not James, in Matthew's list.
  • Jerome did not list Matthew among the Lord's brothers.

Sources: Mark 2:14 · Mark 3:18

Are Clopas and Alphaeus the same man?

FamilyDebated

Some identify Clopas (John 19:25) with Alphaeus (Mark 3:18), as variant Greek renderings of an Aramaic name. The Gospels never say so, and the Greek forms differ.

MaryAlphaeus or Cleopas' wifeAlphaeusSon of JacobJamesAn apostle
Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — Mary of Clopas.Mark 3:18 — James son of Alphaeus.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Yes: both could be from Aramaic Halphai; Jerome held this.
  • No: Klopas is a different Greek name from Alphaios; the two are commonly distinguished. A separate Cleopas in Luke 24:18 is yet another figure.
  • TIPNR ties the names together in its notes, which is a scholarly judgment.

Sources: John 19:25 · Mark 3:18 · Jerome, Against Helvidius (c. AD 383)

Judas "of James" (Thaddaeus)

FamilyWidely accepted

Luke and Acts call an apostle "Judas of James" (Greek: Judas Iakobou), leaving open "son of" or "brother of". Matthew and Mark list Thaddaeus in that position, and the two are commonly taken as the same man.

Judas as brother of James (Hieronymian reading)

FamilyDebated

Jerome read "Judas of James" as "brother of James" and linked Judas to the author of Jude, who calls himself brother of James. Others read "son of James", and some distinguish the apostle from the letter writer.

JudasAn apostleJamesAn apostleJudeSon of Joseph
Where the text shows it: Luke 6:16 — Judas of James.Jude 1 — Jude, brother of James.Jude 1:17 — The writer speaks of "the apostles" as a group he seems to stand apart from.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Brother: fits Jude 1 and Jerome's identification of James of Alphaeus with the Lord's brother.
  • Son: the normal Greek genitive reading; the brother reading takes it as an ellipsis.
  • Jude 17 has been taken to show the writer was not one of the Twelve.

Sources: Luke 6:16 · Jude 1 · Jerome, Against Helvidius (c. AD 383)

Simon the Zealot as a brother in the Alphaeus family

FamilyHypothesis

The claim that Simon the Zealot was a brother of James, Matthew and Judas is not supported by any text. It combines the Hieronymian list of four "brothers" (James, Joses, Simon, Jude) with the apostle list by shared names.

SimonAn apostleJamesAn apostleMatthewAn apostleJudasAn apostle
Where the text shows it: Luke 6:15 — Simon who was called the Zealot.Matthew 10:4 — Simon the Cananaean.Matthew 13:55 — The four brothers named.
Ways people read this (3)
  • For: James, Simon and Judas appear in both lists; Jerome made James and Judas apostles.
  • Against: no text gives Simon the Zealot a father; "Simon" was extremely common; the name Joses is not in the apostle list.
  • Some later traditions identify Simon the Zealot with the Symeon of Jerusalem; the ancient testimony is weak.

Sources: Luke 6:15 · Matthew 13:55

Four apostles as sons of Alphaeus

FamilyHypothesis

The proposal that James, Matthew, Judas and Simon the Zealot were all brothers and all cousins of Jesus combines several separate inferences. Each step is uncertain and only the first has support in the text (a shared father for James and Levi).

JamesAn apostleMatthewAn apostleJudasAn apostleSimonAn apostleAlphaeusSon of Jacob
Where the text shows it: Mark 2:14 — Levi son of Alphaeus.Mark 3:18 — James son of Alphaeus.Luke 6:16 — Judas of James.Luke 6:15 — Simon the Zealot.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Attested: James and Levi share a father named Alphaeus; Mary mother of James is at the cross.
  • Inferred: Levi = Matthew; Alphaeus = Clopas; Judas is brother of James; Simon is also brother; Mary of Clopas is Mary's sister.
  • Each inference has defenders, but the combined case depends on all of them at once.

Sources: Mark 2:14 · Luke 6:15-16

Thomas within the Alphaeus/Clopas family

FamilyHypothesis

No text links Thomas to the Alphaeus or Clopas branch. The idea rests on Thomas being called Didymus ("twin"), combined with lists where Thomas is placed near Matthew or James; it is an inference, not a report.

Where the text shows it: John 11:16 — Thomas called Didymus.John 20:24 — Thomas, one of the Twelve, called Didymus.Matthew 10:3 — Thomas and Matthew paired.
Ways people read this (3)
  • Attested: Thomas is named Didymus; no sibling is named.
  • Not attested: his father, his family, or his place of origin.
  • The pairing in Matthew's list is of Thomas with Matthew, which could equally support friendship or a shared mission.

Sources: John 11:16 · John 20:24

Is the Twelve one extended family network?

FriendsHypothesis

One proposal is that most of the Twelve were kin, neighbours or business contacts around the Sea of Galilee. The Gospels attest two brother pairs, a partnership, a shared home town for three and an invitation between two; the wider network is a pattern suggested by those links, not a stated fact.

Ways people read this (3)
  • Attested: two sets of brothers, partners, a shared home town, one invitation.
  • Inferred: kinship among Alphaeus's sons, Thomas, Simon, Judas.
  • The Galilean setting makes local links likely but not proven for each apostle.

Sources: Luke 5:10 · John 1:44

Is James the Less the Lord's brother?

FamilyChurch tradition

Jerome identified James son of Alphaeus with James the Lord's brother, so that the "brother" was a cousin and an apostle. Paul calls James an apostle in Galatians 1:19. Others keep them distinct.

JamesAn apostleJamesSon of Joseph
Where the text shows it: Galatians 1:19 — I saw none of the other apostles except James the Lord's brother.Mark 15:40 — James the less, son of Mary.
Ways people read this (3)
  • For: Paul counts James among apostles; Mark has a second Mary as mother of a James.
  • Against: John 7:5 says the brothers did not believe, while the son of Alphaeus was among the Twelve; "apostle" in Galatians 1:19 may be used more broadly.
  • TIPNR keeps two separate entries.
