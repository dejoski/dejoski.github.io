Shealtiel, the grandfather of Zerubbabel, was a descendant of David and played a significant role in the rebuilding of the temple after the Babylonian exile.

🗺 Places mentioned alongside Shealtiel on the map →

Also known as Salathiel

Shealtiel, mentioned in several passages in the Old Testament (1Ch.3.17; Ezr.3.2,8; 5.2; Neh.12.1; Hag.1.1,12,14; 2.2,23; Mat.1.12), was a descendant of King David and the father of Peaish who was father of Zerubbabel, a prominent figure in the rebuilding of the temple after the Babylonian exile. Shealtiel is identified as the son of Jeconiah (also known as Jehoiachin), the king of Judah who was taken into captivity by the Babylonians. In the genealogies of 1 Chronicles and Matthew, Shealtiel is listed as a direct ancestor of Jesus Christ, emphasizing the connection between the Davidic line and the Messiah. During the early stages of the return from exile, Shealtiel's son Zerubbabel was appointed as the governor of Judah and played a key role in rebuilding the altar and laying the foundation of the temple. The prophets Haggai and Zechariah also mention Shealtiel and Zerubbabel as important figures in the restoration of Jerusalem and the temple. The frequent mention of Shealtiel in connection with Zerubbabel highlights his significance in the lineage of David and the continuity of God's plan for His people.

Zerubbabel: son of Shealtiel or of Pedaiah?

FamilyDebated

Ezra, Haggai and Matthew call Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, while 1 Chronicles 3:19 calls him son of Shealtiel's brother Pedaiah. The common solutions are a levirate arrangement or a legal versus biological sonship.

Where the text shows it: Ezra 3:2 — Zerubbabel son of ShealtielHag 1:1 — Same attribution1 Chr 3:19 — Sons of Pedaiah: Zerubbabel and ShimeiMatt 1:12 — Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel
Ways people read this (3)
  • Levirate view: Shealtiel died childless and Pedaiah fathered Zerubbabel for him.
  • Legal adoption or transcription variation; some manuscripts of 1 Chr 3:19 differ.
  • Some scholars read Pedaiah as the other father on a separate line of descent.

Sources: Ezra 3:2 · Hag 1:1 · 1 Chr 3:19 · Matt 1:12

Jehoiachin, Shealtiel and Zerubbabel: the royal line through exile

RoleStated in the text

Shealtiel is called Jehoiachin's son in 1 Chronicles 3:17 and Matthew 1:12. Zerubbabel, governor under Persia, became the focus of hope for the Davidic line, and Haggai calls him the LORD's "signet ring", reversing the Jeremiah 22:24 sentence on Jehoiachin's signet.

David to Jesus through Solomon (Matthew)

FamilyStated in the text

Matthew traces the royal line from David through Solomon and the kings of Judah to Jehoiachin, then through Shealtiel and Zerubbabel to Joseph, husband of Mary. It lists fourteen generations in each of three groups, selectively.

David to Jesus through Nathan (Luke)

FamilyDebated

Luke's genealogy runs from David through his son Nathan, not Solomon, and also includes Shealtiel and Zerubbabel. Views differ whether Luke gives Mary's line or Joseph's legal line, with the two crossing at Shealtiel.

Where the text shows it: Luke 3:23-31 — Jesus back to David through Nathan2 Sam 5:14 — Nathan is a son of David born in JerusalemZech 12:12 — The family of the house of Nathan mentioned
Ways people read this (3)
  • Julius Africanus (third century) proposed levirate marriages explaining the differences between Matthew and Luke.
  • Many later interpreters take Luke as Mary's line (Heli as her father), though the text says "son of Heli" of Joseph.
  • Others think Matthew gives the royal succession and Luke the physical descent, both through Joseph.

Sources: Luke 3:23-31 · 2 Sam 5:14 · Zech 12:12
