Cyrus's decree and the first return

RoleStated in the text

Cyrus issued a decree in his first year permitting exiles to return and rebuild the temple, and returned the temple vessels. Zerubbabel, a Davidic descendant, and the priest Joshua led the first group.

Zerubbabel and Joshua: governor and high priest

WorkStated in the text

Zerubbabel, governor, and Joshua (Jeshua) son of Jehozadak, high priest, rebuilt the altar and started the temple foundation, then led the work after opposition halted it. Zechariah 3-4 pairs them as the two "anointed ones".

Haggai and Zechariah: prophets of the second temple

WorkStated in the text

In 520 BC Haggai and Zechariah urged Zerubbabel and Joshua to resume building the temple, which had stalled for sixteen years. Haggai speaks first (month 6), Zechariah two months later. Ezra 6:14 says the elders prospered by their prophesying.

Zerubbabel: son of Shealtiel or of Pedaiah?

FamilyDebated

Ezra, Haggai and Matthew call Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, while 1 Chronicles 3:19 calls him son of Shealtiel's brother Pedaiah. The common solutions are a levirate arrangement or a legal versus biological sonship.

ZerubbabelGovernorShealtielGrandfather of Zerubbabel
Where the text shows it: Ezra 3:2 — Zerubbabel son of ShealtielHag 1:1 — Same attribution1 Chr 3:19 — Sons of Pedaiah: Zerubbabel and ShimeiMatt 1:12 — Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel
Ways people read this (3)
  • Levirate view: Shealtiel died childless and Pedaiah fathered Zerubbabel for him.
  • Legal adoption or transcription variation; some manuscripts of 1 Chr 3:19 differ.
  • Some scholars read Pedaiah as the other father on a separate line of descent.

Sources: Ezra 3:2 · Hag 1:1 · 1 Chr 3:19 · Matt 1:12

Zechariah: son of Berechiah, or of Iddo?

FamilyDebated

Zechariah 1:1 calls the prophet son of Berechiah, son of Iddo; Ezra 5:1 and 6:14 say "Zechariah the son of Iddo". The usual explanation is that Iddo was the more prominent grandfather and "son" means descendant; another view is that Berechiah is a scribal insertion.

ZechariahA prophetIddoReturned priest
Where the text shows it: Zech 1:1 — Son of Berechiah, son of IddoEzra 5:1 — "Zechariah the son of Iddo"Neh 12:16 — Zechariah of the house of Iddo, priest
Ways people read this (3)
  • Grandson view: Berechiah may have died young, so Iddo is named in Ezra as family head.
  • Corruption view: Berechiah entered Zech 1:1 under the influence of Isa 8:2.
  • Matthew 23:35 also mentions a Zechariah son of Berechiah, feeding a separate discussion about whom Jesus means.

Sources: Zech 1:1 · Ezra 5:1 · Neh 12:16

Jehoiachin, Shealtiel and Zerubbabel: the royal line through exile

Ahasuerus is Xerxes I

RoleWidely accepted

The Hebrew Ahasuerus (Ahashverosh) corresponds to Persian Khshayarsha, Greek Xerxes, who ruled 486-465 BC. Esther's reign, in the king's seventh year, falls about 479 BC. The Septuagint renders the name as Artaxerxes.

Mordecai and Haman: the Agagite

RoleStated in the text

Mordecai refused to bow to Haman, who plotted to kill all Jews; Esther exposed the plot at a banquet and Haman was hanged on the gallows he prepared. The text calls him "Haman the Agagite", and Mordecai is "son of Kish, a Benjamite", echoing Saul's failure to destroy Agag.

Ezra before or after Nehemiah?

RoleDebated

Ezra arrives in Artaxerxes' seventh year and Nehemiah in his twentieth, which gives the traditional order Ezra then Nehemiah. Some scholars, observing that Ezra finds the city lightly populated and Nehemiah alone reorganises it, propose a different king or reverse order.

EzraReturned priestNehemiahReturned leader
Where the text shows it: Ezra 7:7 — Seventh year of ArtaxerxesNeh 2:1 — Twentieth year of ArtaxerxesNeh 8:1-2 — Ezra reads the law
Ways people read this (3)
  • Traditional: Ezra 458 BC, Nehemiah 445 BC, both under Artaxerxes I.
  • Reversal view: the "Artaxerxes" of Ezra 7 is Artaxerxes II, so Ezra arrives around 398 BC.
  • Emendation view: Ezra 7:7 should read "thirty-seventh" year (428 BC).

Sources: Ezra 7:7 · Neh 2:1 · Neh 8:1-2
