A priestly couple

FamilyStated in the text

Zechariah served in the priestly division of Abijah and his wife Elizabeth was herself a daughter of Aaron. Both were righteous but childless until old age. John was thus a priest by birth.

Mary and Elizabeth are relatives

FamilyStated in the text

The angel tells Mary that Elizabeth is her relative (syngenis). Mary visited her for about three months, and the child in Elizabeth's womb leapt. The exact relationship is not stated.

John and Jesus as cousins

FamilyChurch tradition

Because Mary and Elizabeth are relatives, John and Jesus are commonly called cousins. Luke says only that they were kin, and Luke 1:36 uses a general term that could cover several degrees of relationship.

JohnA prophetJesusSon of Joseph
Where the text shows it: Luke 1:36 — RelativeJohn 1:31 — John says "I did not know him"
Ways people read this (3)
  • Cousins (traditional).
  • Some propose a looser kin tie, e.g. through the priestly tribe (Mary might be of a Levitical family, which is not stated).
  • John 1:31 "I did not know him" is often reconciled with their family tie as meaning he did not know him as Messiah.

RoleStated in the text

The unnamed second disciple in John 1

FriendsChurch tradition

Andrew is named but his companion in John 1:35-40 is not. Tradition identifies the unnamed disciple as John son of Zebedee, consistent with the Gospel's habit of not naming its likely author.

John's baptism still alive in Ephesus

RoleStated in the text

Acts says Apollos knew only the baptism of John when he taught at Ephesus, and Paul soon met twelve disciples who had only John's baptism. So John's movement persisted outside Judea a generation later.
