Herod the Great and three sons

FamilyWidely accepted

The NT Herods belong to one family. After Herod the Great died, Archelaus ruled Judea, Antipas became tetrarch of Galilee and Perea, and Philip tetrarch of the northeast. Matthew and Luke name three of the four; Josephus supplies the genealogy.

Sources: Scripture · Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews

Two births in the days of Herod

RoleStated in the text

Luke opens with Zechariah serving in the days of Herod, king of Judea. Matthew places Jesus' birth in the same reign. So John the Baptist and Jesus are born under the same king whose family would later kill John and try Jesus.

Herodias, married to two half-brothers

FamilyStated in the text

Mark says Herod Antipas took Herodias, his brother Philip's wife. John said it was unlawful. Josephus names her first husband as another half-brother (also called Herod). The Philip in the Gospels is usually distinguished from the tetrarch Philip.

Did Joanna and Manaen supply Luke's Herod stories?

RoleHypothesis

Luke alone reports the news of Herod hearing about Jesus, the warning about "that fox", and Jesus before Herod. Because Joanna and Manaen had Herodian ties, some suggest they were Luke's sources.

LukePhysician; companion of Paul; author of Luke and ActsJoannaA follower of Jesus who supported His ministryManaenChristian prophetHerodA Tetrarch
Where the text shows it: Luke 8:3 — Joanna in Herod's householdActs 13:1 — Manaen raised with HerodLuke 23:8-12 — Herod material unique to Luke
Ways people read this (3)
  • Luke drew on Joanna and Manaen for court material.
  • Luke used general informants or traditions, and the pairing is coincidental.
  • Bauckham argues the Junia of Romans 16:7 may be this Joanna, a separate and more speculative claim.

Sources: Richard Bauckham, Gospel Women
