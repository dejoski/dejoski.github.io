Mark as Peter's interpreter

Teacher & studentChurch tradition

A tradition reported by Eusebius from Papias (citing "the elder") says Mark, as Peter's interpreter, wrote down accurately what Peter taught, though not in order. Modern scholars weigh how far the Gospel of Mark reflects Peter's preaching.

MarkPaul's companianPeterAn apostle
Where the text shows it: 1 Peter 5:13 — Earliest NT link between Peter and MarkActs 12:12 — Peter's early connection to Mark's household
Ways people read this (3)
  • Church tradition: Mark composed his Gospel from Peter's preaching in Rome.
  • Some critical scholars see the Papias note as apologetic and Mark as drawing on several traditions.
  • Others accept a real Petrine link while allowing wider sources.

Sources: Eusebius, Church History 3.39 (citing Papias)

Is Mark the young man who fled naked?

FriendsHypothesis

Only Mark records a young man wearing a linen cloth who fled naked at Jesus' arrest. Since Mark alone tells it and the incident has no other narrative role, some have suggested it is the author's signature. Nothing in the text says so.

Was Luke a Gentile?

FriendsDebated

In Colossians 4 Paul names Aristarchus, Mark, and Justus as "of the circumcision" and then lists Epaphras, Luke, and Demas. Many read the second group as Gentiles, making Luke the only non-Jewish NT writer, but the text does not say so directly.

LukePhysician; companion of Paul; author of Luke and ActsEpaphrasA faithful minister and fellow servant of Paul.DemasCompanion of Paul who later deserted him
Where the text shows it: Colossians 4:10-11 — Three named as of the circumcisionColossians 4:12-14 — Epaphras, Luke, Demas listed afterwards
Ways people read this (3)
  • Common reading: Luke was a Gentile, as the grouping in Colossians 4 implies.
  • Some scholars argue the list does not exclude Luke being a Hellenistic Jew, given his knowledge of the LXX and synagogue life.
  • Tradition (Eusebius) calls him a native of Antioch.

Did Apollos write Hebrews?

WorkHypothesis

Hebrews is anonymous. Luther suggested Apollos, noting the Alexandrian style and scriptural skill. Others propose Barnabas (early North African tradition, Tertullian), Priscilla, or Paul. Origen concluded only God knows who wrote it.

ApollosEloquent Jewish Christian from Alexandria, co-worker with PaulPaulAn apostlePriscillaChristian teacherBarnabasBrother of Mary
Where the text shows it: Acts 18:24-28 — Apollos is eloquent and mighty in the ScripturesHebrews 13:23 — Timothy is mentioned as freed, and greetings come from Italy
Ways people read this (4)
  • Apollos: Luther, and many modern scholars, on style and Alexandrian background.
  • Barnabas: Tertullian and a North African tradition, since he was a Levite.
  • Paul: traditional Eastern view, though styles differ and Hebrews is anonymous.
  • Priscilla: a modern proposal (Harnack).

Sources: Tertullian, On Modesty 20 · Origen, in Eusebius, Church History 6.25 · Martin Luther
