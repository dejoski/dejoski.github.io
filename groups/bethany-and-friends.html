Simon the leper's house and the anointing

FriendsHypothesis

Mark and Matthew place the anointing at Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, and John places it at a supper with Martha serving and Lazarus reclining. Simon may have been related to the family, but the texts do not say.

  • Simon was a healed leper who hosted a supper for which Martha served (a neighbor).
  • Simon was a relative, possibly the father or husband of Martha.
  • The two suppers are different events (less common).

Are Mary Magdalene, Mary of Bethany, and the sinful woman one person?

RoleDebated

In 591 Pope Gregory the Great preached that Mary Magdalene, Mary of Bethany, and the anointing woman of Luke 7 were one. The NT names three separate people and never links them. Eastern churches keep them separate.

Mary MagdaleneFollower of Jesus, first witness of His resurrection.MarySister of Martha
Where the text shows it: Luke 7:36-50 — Unnamed sinful woman anoints Jesus' feetLuke 8:2 — Mary called Magdalene, seven demonsJohn 12:3 — Mary of Bethany anoints his feet
Ways people read this (3)
  • Western medieval tradition (Gregory): one woman.
  • Eastern tradition and most modern scholars: three distinct women.
  • Some see John 11:2 as the Gospel itself merging Mary of Bethany with the anointing.

Sources: Gregory the Great, Homilies on the Gospels 33

Clopas, Alphaeus, and the family behind them

FamilyHypothesis

Hegesippus (via Eusebius) says Clopas was Joseph's brother and father of Symeon, second bishop of Jerusalem. Some equate Clopas with Alphaeus (father of James the younger), but the Greek names differ and the match is a hypothesis.

MaryAlphaeus or Cleopas' wifeAlphaeusSon of JacobJamesAn apostleJosephMary's husband
Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — Mary of ClopasMark 3:18 — James son of AlphaeusMark 2:14 — Levi son of Alphaeus
Ways people read this (3)
  • Clopas and Alphaeus are the same man (Aramaic Halphai behind both names).
  • They are different men.
  • If Alphaeus is Joseph's brother, James the younger would be a cousin of Jesus.

Sources: Eusebius, Church History 3.11 and 4.22 (citing Hegesippus)

Who is "his mother's sister" in John 19:25?

FamilyDebated

John names either three or four women at the cross: "his mother's sister, Mary of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene" may be 3 or 4. If four, the sister is likely Salome, mother of Zebedee's sons, making James and John Jesus' first cousins.

SalomeSister of Mary.MaryMosther of JesusMaryAlphaeus or Cleopas' wifeJamesAn apostleJohnAn apostle
Where the text shows it: John 19:25 — Four women or threeMark 15:40 — SalomeMatthew 27:56 — Mother of Zebedee's sons
Ways people read this (3)
  • Four women: sister = Salome, so James and John are cousins of Jesus.
  • Three women: Mary of Clopas is the sister, or sister means sister-in-law.
  • Two women sharing the name Mary as sisters is unlikely, which favors the four-woman reading.

Sources: Scripture

Lazarus as the Beloved Disciple?

FriendsHypothesis

John 11 says Jesus loved Lazarus and calls him "he whom you love". Later the Beloved Disciple appears only after John 13. Some see Lazarus there. Early tradition (Irenaeus) names John the apostle.

LazarusMary's brotherJohnAn apostleMarkPaul's companian
Where the text shows it: John 11:3 — He whom you love is illJohn 13:23 — Disciple whom Jesus lovedJohn 21:20-24 — Disciple who wrote these things
Ways people read this (4)
  • John son of Zebedee (Irenaeus and most traditions).
  • Lazarus (Filson and others), since he is the only man said to be loved.
  • John Mark, or an otherwise unnamed Judean disciple.
  • A literary ideal figure.

Sources: Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.1.1 · Floyd Filson, Journal of Biblical Literature (1949)
