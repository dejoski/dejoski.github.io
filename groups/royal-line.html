Judah to David: Ruth 4 and Matthew 1

FamilyStated in the text

Ruth 4:18-22 lists ten generations from Perez to David, matching Matthew 1:3-6 and Luke 3:31-33. Scholars note that ten names cover about seven centuries, so the list is likely selective.

David to Jesus through Solomon (Matthew)

FamilyStated in the text

Matthew traces the royal line from David through Solomon and the kings of Judah to Jehoiachin, then through Shealtiel and Zerubbabel to Joseph, husband of Mary. It lists fourteen generations in each of three groups, selectively.

David to Jesus through Nathan (Luke)

FamilyDebated

Luke's genealogy runs from David through his son Nathan, not Solomon, and also includes Shealtiel and Zerubbabel. Views differ whether Luke gives Mary's line or Joseph's legal line, with the two crossing at Shealtiel.

Where the text shows it: Luke 3:23-31 — Jesus back to David through Nathan2 Sam 5:14 — Nathan is a son of David born in JerusalemZech 12:12 — The family of the house of Nathan mentioned
Ways people read this (3)
  • Julius Africanus (third century) proposed levirate marriages explaining the differences between Matthew and Luke.
  • Many later interpreters take Luke as Mary's line (Heli as her father), though the text says "son of Heli" of Joseph.
  • Others think Matthew gives the royal succession and Luke the physical descent, both through Joseph.

Sources: Luke 3:23-31 · 2 Sam 5:14 · Zech 12:12

Jehoiachin's curse and the Davidic hope

RoleDebated

Jeremiah 22:30 says none of Jehoiachin's descendants would sit on David's throne, yet Matthew 1 places him in Jesus' legal line. Luke's different route through Nathan is sometimes used to explain the tension; others note that the oracle concerns Jehoiachin's immediate sons.

Where the text shows it: Jer 22:24-30 — Judgement on ConiahMatt 1:11-12 — Jeconiah in the genealogyHag 2:23 — Zerubbabel as signet
Ways people read this (2)
  • Some hold that Matthew gives Jesus' legal right through Joseph and Luke the physical line avoids the curse.
  • Others argue the curse was specific to Jehoiachin's lifetime and was reversed by his release and Zerubbabel's appointment.

Sources: Jer 22:24-30 · Matt 1:11-12 · Hag 2:23

The Davidic covenant: from 2 Samuel 7 to the New Testament

RoleWidely accepted

God promised David a house and a throne "forever" through his descendant. Psalm 89 and Isaiah 9 and 11 develop this, and Luke 1:32-33 and Acts 2:30 apply it to Jesus; Jewish readers await a future Davidic king.

  • Jewish reading: awaits a future Davidic king; fulfilment in the Davidic dynasty's continuation.

Sources: 2 Sam 7:12-16 · Ps 89:3-4 · Luke 1:32-33 · Acts 2:30

Four women in Matthew's genealogy

FamilyDebated

Matthew names Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and "the wife of Uriah" in a list otherwise of men. Tamar, Rahab and Ruth are likely non-Israelites or of irregular circumstances, and Bathsheba is named by Uriah. Commentators propose that Matthew highlights Gentile inclusion, scandal, or God's use of irregular paths.

Ways people read this (3)
  • Gentile-inclusion view: the women are foreigners or associated with foreigners (Bathsheba as the wife of a Hittite).
  • Irregularity view: each woman stands in an ambiguous moral or marital situation.
  • Messianic-prophecy view: each story shows God working outside human expectation.

Sources: Matt 1:3 · Matt 1:5 · Matt 1:6
