Naomi and Ruth: mother-in-law and daughter-in-law

FamilyStated in the text

After Elimelech and their sons died in Moab, Naomi returned to Bethlehem; her Moabite daughter-in-law Ruth refused to leave ("Your people shall be my people"). Their bond drives the plot, and the women of Bethlehem call the child born to Ruth "Naomi's son".

Ruth and Boaz: the kinsman-redeemer

FamilyStated in the text

Boaz, a relative of Elimelech, allowed Ruth to glean, acted as redeemer after a closer relative declined, bought the family land and married Ruth. The account joins the law of redemption (Lev 25) and levirate practice with a story of kindness (hesed).

Boaz, Obed, Jesse and David

FamilyStated in the text

Ruth and Boaz's son Obed became father of Jesse, father of David. The book of Ruth ends with this genealogy, presenting the Moabite Ruth as David's great-grandmother; Matthew repeats the line.

Salmon and Rahab: the Jericho woman in David's ancestry

FamilyChurch tradition

Matthew names Rahab as the wife of Salmon and mother of Boaz. The identification with the Rahab of Jericho is the standard reading, but the Old Testament never says Salmon married her, and chronology is debated.

Nahshon: Judah's leader and ancestor of David

FamilyStated in the text

Nahshon son of Amminadab led Judah's tribe in the wilderness. Aaron's wife Elisheba was Amminadab's daughter, making Nahshon Aaron's brother-in-law: the line of David and the line of the priests are thus linked by marriage.

Eli, Hophni and Phinehas: a priestly house judged

RoleStated in the text

Eli's sons abused their priestly office. Eli failed to restrain them; the ark was captured and both sons died in one day, and Eli died on hearing it. The story also marks the transfer of priestly honour from the line of Eli (of Ithamar) toward Zadok (of Eleazar) under Solomon.

RoleStated in the text

Samson and Delilah

RoleStated in the text

Samson loved Delilah, who was paid by the Philistine rulers to learn the secret of his strength. After three false answers he revealed that his hair, sign of his Nazirite vow, was the source. He was blinded and imprisoned, then died toppling Dagon's temple.
