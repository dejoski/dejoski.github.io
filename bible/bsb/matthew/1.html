The Genealogy of Jesus

Ruth 4:18–22; Luke 3:23–38

1This is the record of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham:

2Abraham was the father of Isaac,
Isaac the father of Jacob,
and Jacob the father of Judah and his brothers.
3Judah was the father of Perez and Zerah by Tamar,
Perez the father of Hezron,
and Hezron the father of Ram.
4Ram was the father of Amminadab,
Amminadab the father of Nahshon,
and Nahshon the father of Salmon.
5Salmon was the father of Boaz by Rahab,
Boaz the father of Obed by Ruth,
Obed the father of Jesse,
6and Jesse the father of David the king.

Next:

David was the father of Solomon by Uriahʼs wife,
7Solomon the father of Rehoboam,
Rehoboam the father of Abijah,
and Abijah the father of Asa.
8Asa was the father of Jehoshaphat,
Jehoshaphat the father of Joram,
and Joram the father of Uzziah.
9Uzziah was the father of Jotham,
Jotham the father of Ahaz,
and Ahaz the father of Hezekiah.
10Hezekiah was the father of Manasseh,
Manasseh the father of Amon,
Amon the father of Josiah,
11and Josiah the father of Jeconiah and his brothers
at the time of the exile to Babylon.

12After the exile to Babylon:

Jeconiah was the father of Shealtiel,
Shealtiel the father of Zerubbabel,
13Zerubbabel the father of Abiud,
Abiud the father of Eliakim,
and Eliakim the father of Azor.
14Azor was the father of Zadok,
Zadok the father of Achim,
and Achim the father of Eliud.
15Eliud was the father of Eleazar,
Eleazar the father of Matthan,
Matthan the father of Jacob,
16and Jacob the father of Joseph, the husband of Mary,
of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.

17In all, then, there were fourteen generations from Abraham to David, fourteen from David to the exile to Babylon, and fourteen from the exile to the Christ.

The Birth of Jesus

Isaiah 7:10–16; Luke 2:1–7

18This is how the birth of Jesus Christ came about: His mother Mary was pledged in marriage to Joseph, but before they came together, she was found to be with child through the Holy Spirit. 19Because Joseph her husband was a righteous man and was unwilling to disgrace her publicly, he resolved to divorce her quietly.

20But after he had pondered these things, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and said, “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to embrace Mary as your wife, for the One conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit. 21She will give birth to a Son, and you are to give Him the name Jesus, because He will save His people from their sins.”

22All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had said through the prophet:

23“Behold, the virgin will be with child
and will give birth to a son,
and they will call Him Immanuel”
(which means, “God with us”).

24When Joseph woke up, he did as the angel of the Lord had commanded him, and embraced Mary as his wife. 25But he had no union with her until she gave birth to a Son. And he gave Him the name Jesus.

Study notes on this chapter · 4

Type / patternTraditional reading

Rahab's scarlet cord

Rahab hung a scarlet cord from her window so that her household would be spared when Jericho fell. Some early Christian writers saw the cord as a sign of Christ's blood, while the New Testament itself presents Rahab as an example of faith and in Matthew's genealogy of Jesus.

When: Joshua, c. 1406 BC (traditional date).

Original passageJoshua 2:17–21Joshua 6:22–25
Echoed inHebrews 11:31Matthew 1:5
Ways people read this (3)
  • Patristic allegory: the red cord stands for the blood of Christ that saves those inside the house (Clement of Rome, 1 Clement 12).
  • The New Testament does not make this connection, and it cites Rahab as a model of faith and works (Hebrews 11:31; James 2:25).
  • The scarlet cord most plainly recalls the Passover blood on the doorposts.

Type / patternTraditional reading

Boaz the kinsman-redeemer

In Ruth, Boaz acts as a relative who redeems the family land and marries Ruth, preserving the name of her husband, and becomes the great-grandfather of David. The law provided for a close relative to redeem land and kin. Many Christians see Boaz as a pattern of Christ as redeemer, although the New Testament does not draw the comparison.

When: Ruth (era of the judges, c. 1100 BC).

Original passageRuth 4:1–10Leviticus 25:25
Echoed inMatthew 1:5–6Galatians 4:4–5

Prophecy & fulfillmentDebated

A virgin will conceive and bear a son

Isaiah gives King Ahaz a sign: a young woman will conceive, bear a son, and call him Immanuel ('God with us'). Matthew says Jesus' virginal conception fulfilled this. Readers differ about how the sign worked in Isaiah's own day and how it points to Jesus.

When: Spoken c. 735–732 BC during the Syro-Ephraimite crisis; applied by Matthew, c. AD 60–85.

Original passageIsaiah 7:14
Where the New Testament applies itMatthew 1:22–23
Ways people read this (4)
  • Direct: Isaiah was speaking about the Messiah's birth.
  • Dual fulfillment: a child born in Ahaz's time was a sign, and Jesus is the fullest fulfillment.
  • Typological: Matthew sees a pattern in Isaiah's sign that Jesus completes.
  • Hebrew 'almah' means a young woman; the Greek translation (Septuagint) used 'parthenos', which Matthew follows.

Prophecy & fulfillmentDebated

Jehoiachin written down as childless

Jeremiah says that none of Jehoiachin's offspring will sit on David's throne, and that he will be recorded as childless. Jehoiachin had children, and his line continued through Zerubbabel, who governed Judah but was not crowned. Matthew's genealogy includes Jeconiah in the line to Jesus, and Luke traces another line through Nathan.

When: Jeremiah, c. 598 BC; Jehoiachin was exiled in 597 BC and released c. 561 BC.

Original passageJeremiah 22:24–302 Kings 24:8–15
Echoed inMatthew 1:11–12Luke 3:27Haggai 2:23
Ways people read this (4)
  • The word 'childless' means without heirs to the throne, and the verse was fulfilled when no descendant reigned after him.
  • Matthew's line is Joseph's royal (legal) lineage, and Luke's is Mary's or another branch, so Jesus avoids the curse through his virgin birth (a common explanation).
  • Jesus inherits the throne through legal adoption by Joseph, and the curse on Jehoiachin's line was later reversed (Haggai 2:23).
  • The two genealogies are an unsolved puzzle and were written for different purposes.
