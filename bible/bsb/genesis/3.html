The Serpentʼs Deception

Romans 5:12–21

1Now the serpent was more crafty than any beast of the field that the LordGod had made. And he said to the woman, “Did God really say, ‘You must not eat from any tree in the garden?’”

2The woman answered the serpent, “We may eat the fruit of the trees of the garden, 3but about the fruit of the tree in the middle of the garden, God has said, ‘You must not eat of it or touch it, or you will die.’”

4“You will not surely die,” the serpent told the woman. 5“For God knows that in the day you eat of it, your eyes will be opened and you will be like God, knowing good and evil.”

6When the woman saw that the tree was good for food and pleasing to the eyes, and that it was desirable for obtaining wisdom, she took the fruit and ate it. She also gave some to her husband who was with her, and he ate it.

7And the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked; so they sewed together fig leaves and made coverings for themselves.

God Arraigns Adam and Eve

8Then the man and his wife heard the voice of the LordGod walking in the garden in the breeze of the day, and they hid themselves from the presence of the LordGod among the trees of the garden.

9But the LordGod called out to the man, “Where are you?”

10“I heard Your voice in the garden,” he replied, “and I was afraid because I was naked; so I hid myself.”

11“Who told you that you were naked?” asked the LordGod. “Have you eaten from the tree of which I commanded you not to eat?”

12And the man answered, “The woman whom You gave me, she gave me fruit from the tree, and I ate it.”

13Then the LordGod said to the woman, “What is this you have done?”

“The serpent deceived me,” she replied, “and I ate.”

The Fate of the Serpent

14So the LordGod said to the serpent:

“Because you have done this,
cursed are you above all livestock
and every beast of the field!
On your belly will you go,
and dust you will eat,
all the days of your life.
15And I will put enmity between you and the woman,
and between your seed and her seed.
He will crush your head,
and you will strike his heel.”

The Punishment of Mankind

16To the woman He said:

“I will sharply increase your pain in childbirth;
in pain you will bring forth children.
Your desire will be for your husband,
and he will rule over you.”

17And to Adam He said:

“Because you have listened to the voice of your wife
and have eaten from the tree
of which I commanded you not to eat,
cursed is the ground because of you;
through toil you will eat of it
all the days of your life.
18Both thorns and thistles it will yield for you,
and you will eat the plants of the field.
19By the sweat of your brow
you will eat your bread,
until you return to the ground—
because out of it were you taken.
For dust you are,
and to dust you shall return.”

20And Adam named his wife Eve, because she would be the mother of all the living.

The Expulsion from Paradise

21And the LordGod made garments of skin for Adam and his wife, and He clothed them.

22Then the LordGod said, “Behold, the man has become like one of Us, knowing good and evil. And now, lest he reach out his hand and take also from the tree of life, and eat, and live forever...”

23Therefore the LordGod banished him from the Garden of Eden to work the ground from which he had been taken. 24So He drove out the man and stationed cherubim on the east side of the Garden of Eden, along with a whirling sword of flame to guard the way to the tree of life.

Study notes on this chapter · 5

Type / patternLinked by the New Testament

Adam, the first man, and Christ, the last Adam

Paul says Adam was 'a type of the one to come': through one man sin and death entered the world, and through one man grace and life come to many. In 1 Corinthians he contrasts the first man, from the dust, with the last Adam, a life-giving spirit, and ties resurrection to this pairing.

When: Genesis, the opening of human history; Romans, c. AD 57; 1 Corinthians, c. AD 55.

Original passageGenesis 2:7Genesis 3:1–19
Where the New Testament applies itRomans 5:12–211 Corinthians 15:21–221 Corinthians 15:45–49
Ways people read this (3)
  • Adam was a historical individual whose act affected all humanity, as Paul's parallel suggests (common in many traditions).
  • Adam is a representative figure for humanity, and Paul's point is about Christ's role more than Adam's historicity.
  • Traditions differ on how Adam's sin is transmitted (original sin, ancestral sin, inherited mortality and so on).

Type / patternWidely held reading

The tree of life restored

After the fall, humanity is barred from the tree of life in Eden. Revelation's final chapters picture the tree of life along the river in the new Jerusalem, open to those who overcome and wash their robes. Many readers see the Bible's storyline as beginning and ending in a garden-city.

When: Genesis; Revelation, c. AD 90s.

Original passageGenesis 2:9Genesis 3:22–24
Echoed inRevelation 2:7Revelation 22:1–2Revelation 22:14

Prophecy & fulfillmentTraditional reading

The offspring who will crush the serpent

After the fall, God tells the serpent that the woman's offspring will strike its head while it strikes his heel. Christian tradition has long read this as the first hint of a coming deliverer (sometimes called the protoevangelium). The New Testament alludes to it (Romans 16:20) but does not quote it as a prediction of Jesus.

When: Set at the beginning of human history; written down within the Torah.

Original passageGenesis 3:15
Echoed inRomans 16:20 — "The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet."
Ways people read this (2)
  • Traditional Christian reading: a promise of Christ's victory over evil.
  • Many Jewish and some modern Christian readers see a general statement about the ongoing conflict between humanity and evil.

Prophecy & fulfillmentDebated

The king of Tyre in Eden

Ezekiel's lament over the king of Tyre describes him as having been in Eden, the garden of God, a guardian cherub on the holy mountain, blameless until iniquity was found in him, and cast to the ground. The passage has been read as a description of a human king, of Adam, or of Satan.

When: Ezekiel, c. 586 BC.

Original passageEzekiel 28:11–19Ezekiel 28:1–10
Echoed inGenesis 3:24Luke 10:18
Ways people read this (4)
  • A poetic indictment of the king of Tyre, who has claimed to be a god, using Eden imagery (the plain historical reading).
  • A figure who also reflects Satan's fall, as early church writers and many later readers held.
  • A retelling of the Adam story, with the king as a failed Adam-figure.
  • Jewish tradition reads it as about a proud human king, with echoes of Adam.

Editorial note by BibleWay. Interpretations vary; dates are approximate scholarly estimates.

Prophecy & fulfillmentDebated

The woman and the dragon

John sees a woman clothed with the sun who gives birth to a male child destined to rule the nations, while a red dragon waits to devour him. The child is caught up to God and the woman flees to the wilderness. The passage draws on Genesis 3, Joseph's dream and Psalm 2.

When: Revelation, c. AD 90s.

Original passageRevelation 12:1–6Revelation 12:13–17
Echoed inGenesis 3:15Genesis 37:9–10Psalms 2:9
Ways people read this (4)
  • The woman is Israel (or the faithful remnant), from whom the Messiah came.
  • The woman is the church, persecuted by Satan, and the child represents Christ or the church's faithful.
  • The woman is Mary, the mother of Jesus (a common Catholic and Orthodox reading alongside others), with the larger picture being the church.
  • The woman is a figure for the whole people of God, both Israel and the church, from Eve to the end.
