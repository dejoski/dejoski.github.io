Abraham and Keturah

1 Chronicles 1:32–33

1Now Abraham had taken another wife, named Keturah, 2and she bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.

3Jokshan was the father of Sheba and Dedan. And the sons of Dedan were the Asshurites, the Letushites, and the Leummites.

4The sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah.

All these were descendants of Keturah.

5Abraham left everything he owned to Isaac. 6But while he was still alive, Abraham gave gifts to the sons of his concubines and sent them away from his son Isaac to the land of the east.

The Death of Abraham

7Abraham lived a total of 175 years. 8And at a ripe old age he breathed his last and died, old and contented, and was gathered to his people.

9His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah near Mamre, in the field of Ephron son of Zohar the Hittite. 10This was the field that Abraham had bought from the Hittites. Abraham was buried there with his wife Sarah.

11After Abrahamʼs death, God blessed his son Isaac, who lived near Beer-lahai-roi.

The Descendants of Ishmael

1 Chronicles 1:28–31

12This is the account of Abrahamʼs son Ishmael, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarahʼs maidservant, bore to Abraham. 13These are the names of the sons of Ishmael in the order of their birth: Nebaioth the firstborn of Ishmael, then Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam, 14Mishma, Dumah, Massa, 15Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah.

16These were the sons of Ishmael, and these were their names by their villages and encampments—twelve princes of their tribes. 17Ishmael lived a total of 137 years. Then he breathed his last and died, and was gathered to his people.

18Ishmaelʼs descendants settled from Havilah to Shur, which is near the border of Egypt as you go toward Asshur. And they lived in hostility toward all their brothers.

Jacob and Esau

Malachi 1:1–5; Romans 9:6–29

19This is the account of Abrahamʼs son Isaac. Abraham became the father of Isaac, 20and Isaac was forty years old when he married Rebekah, the daughter of Bethuel the Aramean from Paddan-aram and the sister of Laban the Aramean.

21Later, Isaac prayed to the Lordon behalf of his wife, because she was barren. And the Lordheard his prayer, and his wife Rebekah conceived.

22But the children inside her struggled with each other, and she said, “Why is this happening to me?” So Rebekah went to inquire of the Lord, 23and He declared to her:

“Two nations are in your womb,
and two peoples from within you will be separated;
one people will be stronger than the other,
and the older will serve the younger.”

24When her time came to give birth, there were indeed twins in her womb. 25The first one came out red, covered with hair like a fur coat; so they named him Esau. 26After this, his brother came out grasping Esauʼs heel; so he was named Jacob. And Isaac was sixty years old when the twins were born.

27When the boys grew up, Esau became a skillful hunter, a man of the field, while Jacob was a quiet man who stayed at home. 28Because Isaac had a taste for wild game, he loved Esau; but Rebekah loved Jacob.

Esau Sells His Birthright

29One day, while Jacob was cooking some stew, Esau came in from the field and was famished. 30He said to Jacob, “Let me eat some of that red stew, for I am famished.” (That is why he was also called Edom.)

31“First sell me your birthright,” Jacob replied.

32“Look,” said Esau, “I am about to die, so what good is a birthright to me?”

33“Swear to me first,” Jacob said.

So Esau swore to Jacob and sold him the birthright. 34Then Jacob gave some bread and lentil stew to Esau, who ate and drank and then got up and went away. Thus Esau despised his birthright.

Study notes on this chapter · 2

Prophecy & fulfillmentWidely held reading

Ishmael: twelve princes and a great nation

God promises Hagar that her son will have many descendants and live in hostility with his kin, and promises Abraham that Ishmael will father twelve princes. Genesis lists twelve sons of Ishmael, and later texts name Arab peoples linked with him.

When: Genesis (traditionally c. 1900 BC).

Where the New Testament applies itGenesis 25:12–18

Prophecy & fulfillmentWidely held reading

Two nations in your womb

Before Jacob and Esau are born, God tells Rebekah that two nations are in her womb and the older will serve the younger. Israel and Edom are the later nations, and David's conquest of Edom is usually seen as a first fulfillment. Malachi and Paul use the pair to speak of God's freedom in choosing; how Paul's argument is best read is disputed among traditions.

When: Genesis (traditionally c. 1900 BC); David's conquest of Edom, c. 1000 BC; Romans, c. AD 57.

Original passageGenesis 25:23Malachi 1:2–3
Where the New Testament applies it2 Samuel 8:13–14
Echoed inRomans 9:10–13
Ways people read this (3)
  • Paul uses Jacob and Esau to argue for God's sovereign election of individuals (Reformed and related traditions).
  • Paul is speaking of God's choice of peoples and their roles in his purposes, not the final destiny of individuals (many Arminian, Orthodox and Catholic readers).
  • Malachi's 'love' and 'hate' describe covenant preference in Semitic idiom, not emotion.
