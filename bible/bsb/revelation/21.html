A New Heaven and a New Earth

Isaiah 65:17–25

1Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and earth had passed away, and the sea was no more. 2I saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.

3And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying:

“Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man,
and He will dwell with them.
They will be His people,
and God Himself will be with them as their God.
4‘He will wipe away every tear from their eyes,’
and there will be no more death
or mourning or crying or pain,
for the former things have passed away.”

5And the One seated on the throne said, “Behold, I make all things new.” Then He said, “Write this down, for these words are faithful and true.” 6And He told me, “It is done! I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End. To the thirsty I will give freely from the spring of the water of life. 7The one who overcomes will inherit all things, and I will be his God, and he will be My son.

8But to the cowardly and unbelieving and abominable and murderers and sexually immoral and sorcerers and idolaters and all liars, their place will be in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur. This is the second death.”

The New Jerusalem

9Then one of the seven angels with the seven bowls full of the seven final plagues came and said to me, “Come, I will show you the bride, the wife of the Lamb.”

10And he carried me away in the Spirit to a mountain great and high, and showed me the holy city of Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God, 11shining with the glory of God. Its radiance was like a most precious jewel, like a jasper, as clear as crystal. 12The city had a great and high wall with twelve gates inscribed with the names of the twelve tribes of Israel, and twelve angels at the gates. 13There were three gates on the east, three on the north, three on the south, and three on the west. 14The wall of the city had twelve foundations bearing the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb.

15The angel who spoke with me had a golden measuring rod to measure the city and its gates and walls. 16The city lies foursquare, with its width the same as its length. And he measured the city with the rod, and all its dimensions were equal—12,000 stadia in length and width and height. 17And he measured its wall to be 144 cubits, by the human measure the angel was using.

18The wall was made of jasper, and the city itself of pure gold, as pure as glass. 19The foundations of the city walls were adorned with every kind of precious stone:

The first foundation was jasper,
the second sapphire,
the third chalcedony,
the fourth emerald,
20the fifth sardonyx,
the sixth carnelian,
the seventh chrysolite,
the eighth beryl,
the ninth topaz,
the tenth chrysoprase,
the eleventh jacinth,
and the twelfth amethyst.

21And the twelve gates were twelve pearls, with each gate consisting of a single pearl. The main street of the city was pure gold, as clear as glass.

22But I saw no temple in the city, because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple. 23And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it, because the glory of God illuminates the city, and the Lamb is its lamp. 24By its light the nations will walk, and into it the kings of the earth will bring their glory. 25Its gates will never be shut at the end of the day, because there will be no night there.

26And into the city will be brought the glory and honor of the nations. 27But nothing unclean will ever enter it, nor anyone who practices an abomination or a lie, but only those whose names are written in the Lambʼs Book of Life.

Study notes on this chapter · 6

Type / patternWidely held reading

The tabernacle and the Word made flesh

God's glory filled the tabernacle in the wilderness. John says the Word became flesh and 'dwelt' (literally 'tabernacled') among us, and we saw his glory. Revelation says the dwelling of God is with humanity in the new creation.

When: Exodus (c. 1446 or c. 1250 BC); John, c. AD 80–95.

Original passageExodus 40:34–38Exodus 25:8
Echoed inJohn 1:14Revelation 21:3

Prophecy & fulfillmentTraditional reading

Kings bring gifts to the king

Psalm 72 prays for the king of David's line, that he will judge with righteousness, and that kings of Sheba and Seba will bring gifts. Isaiah 60 pictures nations streaming to Zion's light with gold and frankincense. Christians have read these as background for the magi's gifts in Matthew 2, though Matthew does not quote either passage.

When: Psalm 72 (Solomon's era); Isaiah 60, c. 540 BC or earlier; the magi, c. 5–4 BC.

Original passagePsalms 72:10–11Isaiah 60:1–6
Echoed inMatthew 2:1–12Revelation 21:24

Prophecy & fulfillmentDebated

Swords into plowshares

Isaiah and Micah picture all nations streaming to the LORD's mountain to learn his ways, beating swords into plowshares and learning war no more. Christians and Jews agree it describes God's ultimate peace, but disagree on when and how it comes about.

When: Isaiah and Micah, c. 740–700 BC.

Original passageIsaiah 2:2–4Micah 4:1–5
Echoed inLuke 24:47Revelation 21:24–26
Ways people read this (4)
  • Fulfilled in part as the gospel goes out from Jerusalem to the nations (Acts 1:8; Luke 24:47) and will be completed at Christ's return.
  • Fulfilled in a future earthly kingdom or millennium, with Jerusalem as a center of worship.
  • A poetic picture of the age to come (the new creation), not tied to a specific earthly stage.
  • Jewish tradition reads it as a messianic age of universal peace, yet to come.

Prophecy & fulfillmentWidely held reading

The feast for all peoples

Isaiah says the LORD will prepare a feast for all peoples on his mountain, will swallow up death forever and wipe away tears. Jesus speaks of people coming from east and west to sit at table with Abraham in the kingdom, and Revelation pictures a wedding supper of the Lamb.

When: Isaiah, c. 740–700 BC; Jesus' teaching, c. AD 28–30; Revelation, c. AD 90s.

Original passageIsaiah 25:6–8
Echoed inLuke 13:28–30Revelation 19:6–9Revelation 21:4

Prophecy & fulfillmentLinked by the New Testament

New heavens and a new earth

Isaiah promises that God will create new heavens and a new earth, and former things will not be remembered. Peter says believers wait for these according to his promise, and John sees a new heaven and earth with no more sea. Christians differ on whether the new creation renews this world or replaces it.

When: Isaiah, c. 700–540 BC; 2 Peter, c. AD 60–68; Revelation, c. AD 90s.

Original passageIsaiah 65:17–25Isaiah 66:22
Where the New Testament applies it2 Peter 3:10–13Revelation 21:1–5
Ways people read this (3)
  • Renewal: the present creation is transformed and purified, as a resurrected body is continuous with the old.
  • Replacement: the present heavens and earth are destroyed and a new creation takes their place.
  • Isaiah 65:20 describes death and birth in the new age, which some take as symbolic and others as a stage before the final state.

Prophecy & fulfillmentDebated

Ezekiel's vision of the temple

In the twenty-fifth year of the exile Ezekiel is taken in vision to a high mountain and shown a temple with precise dimensions, the return of God's glory, and a river flowing from the threshold that turns the Dead Sea fresh. No such temple was built. Readers disagree about what it depicts.

When: Ezekiel, 573 BC; proposed fulfillments from the return from exile to the end of the age.

Original passageEzekiel 40:1–4Ezekiel 43:1–7Ezekiel 47:1–12
Echoed inRevelation 21:9–14Revelation 22:1–2
Ways people read this (4)
  • A temple to be built literally in a future age, with sacrifices as memorials (dispensational premillennial).
  • A blueprint for the returning exiles that was never carried out, because the people did not repent.
  • A symbolic vision of God's presence among his people, fulfilled in Christ and the church, with the river of Revelation 22 as its fulfillment.
  • Jewish tradition has long found these chapters difficult (the Talmud says Elijah will resolve their problems) and has looked to the messianic era for their meaning.
