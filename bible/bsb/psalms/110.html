Godʼs Faithful Messiah

Genesis 14:17–24; Hebrews 5:1–10

A Psalm of David.

1The Lord said to my Lord:
“Sit at My right hand
until I make Your enemies
a footstool for Your feet.”
2The Lord extends Your mighty scepter from Zion:
“Rule in the midst of Your enemies.”
3Your people shall be willing
on Your day of battle.
Arrayed in holy splendor, from the womb of the dawn,
to You belongs the dew of Your youth.
4The Lord has sworn
and will not change His mind:
“You are a priest forever
in the order of Melchizedek.”
5The Lord is at Your right hand;
He will crush kings in the day of His wrath.
6He will judge the nations, heaping up the dead;
He will crush the leaders far and wide.
7He will drink from the brook by the road;
therefore He will lift up His head.

Study notes on this chapter · 2

Type / patternLinked by the New Testament

Melchizedek, priest of God Most High

Melchizedek, king of Salem, appears suddenly in Genesis to bless Abram and receive a tenth. Psalm 110 addresses the coming king as 'a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek'. Hebrews reads these together to show that Christ's priesthood is greater than Levi's, because it is of a different order and permanent.

When: Genesis (traditionally c. 2000 BC); Psalm 110 (Davidic era); Hebrews, before AD 70 (most likely).

Original passageGenesis 14:17–20Psalms 110:4
Where the New Testament applies itHebrews 5:5–10Hebrews 6:19–20Hebrews 7:1–28
Ways people read this (3)
  • Melchizedek is a historical priest-king whose silence in the record makes him a fitting pattern for Christ.
  • Some early writers thought Melchizedek was an appearance of the Son or an angel; most interpreters read Hebrews as presenting him as a type, not as Christ himself.
  • Some Jewish texts from Qumran treat Melchizedek as a heavenly figure.

Prophecy & fulfillmentLinked by the New Testament

Sit at my right hand

David writes, 'The LORD says to my Lord: Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies your footstool.' Jesus uses this to raise the question of how the Messiah can be both David's son and David's Lord; Peter and Hebrews apply it to Jesus' exaltation.

When: Written by David (c. 1000 BC); quoted by Jesus in his final week (c. AD 30–33).
