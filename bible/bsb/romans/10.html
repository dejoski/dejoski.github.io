The Word Brings Salvation

Isaiah 65:1–16

1Brothers, my heartʼs desire and prayer to God for the Israelites is for their salvation. 2For I testify about them that they are zealous for God, but not on the basis of knowledge. 3Because they were ignorant of Godʼs righteousness and sought to establish their own, they did not submit to Godʼs righteousness. 4For Christ is the end of the law, to bring righteousness to everyone who believes.

5For concerning the righteousness that is by the law, Moses writes: “The man who does these things will live by them.” 6But the righteousness that is by faith says: “Do not say in your heart, ‘Who will ascend into heaven?’ (that is, to bring Christ down) 7or, ‘Who will descend into the Abyss?’ (that is, to bring Christ up from the dead).”

8But what does it say? “The word is near you; it is in your mouth and in your heart,” that is, the word of faith we are proclaiming: 9that if you confess with your mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved. 10For with your heart you believe and are justified, and with your mouth you confess and are saved.

11It is just as the Scripture says: “Anyone who believes in Him will never be put to shame.” 12For there is no difference between Jew and Greek: The same Lord is Lord of all, and gives richly to all who call on Him, 13for, “Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.”

14How then can they call on the One in whom they have not believed? And how can they believe in the One of whom they have not heard? And how can they hear without someone to preach? 15And how can they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: “How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!”

16But not all of them welcomed the good news. For Isaiah says, “Lord, who has believed our message?” 17Consequently, faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ.

18But I ask, did they not hear? Indeed they did:

“Their voice has gone out into all the earth,
their words to the ends of the world.”

19I ask instead, did Israel not understand? First, Moses says:

“I will make you jealous by those who are not a nation;
I will make you angry by a nation without understanding.”

20And Isaiah boldly says:

“I was found by those who did not seek Me;
I revealed Myself to those who did not ask for Me.”

21But as for Israel he says:

“All day long I have held out My hands
to a disobedient and obstinate people.”

Study notes on this chapter · 4

QuotationLinked by the New Testament

The word is near you

Moses tells Israel the commandment is not hidden in heaven or across the sea but near, in the mouth and heart. Paul applies this language to the word of faith in Christ: no one needs to ascend to heaven or descend to the deep to bring him. How Paul's use relates to Moses' original meaning is discussed by interpreters.

When: Deuteronomy (traditionally c. 1406 BC); Romans, c. AD 57.

Original passageDeuteronomy 30:11–14
Where the New Testament applies itRomans 10:5–10
Ways people read this (2)
  • Paul reads Moses' words as having always pointed to the righteousness of faith, now embodied in Christ.
  • Paul adapts the wording as a pattern (midrash-like) and does not claim it was Moses' meaning.

Prophecy & fulfillmentLinked by the New Testament

How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news

Isaiah pictures a messenger on the mountains announcing peace and salvation, and saying to Zion, 'Your God reigns'. Paul applies it to those who proclaim the gospel, in his argument that faith comes by hearing.

When: Isaiah, c. 700–540 BC; Romans, c. AD 57.

Original passageIsaiah 52:7Nahum 1:15
Where the New Testament applies itRomans 10:14–15
Echoed inEphesians 6:15

Prophecy & fulfillmentLinked by the New Testament

Who has believed our message?

John explains the crowds' unbelief despite Jesus' many signs by quoting Isaiah's question 'Who has believed our message?' and, with it, Isaiah's commission to a hardened people. Paul uses the same verse for Israel's response to the gospel.

When: Isaiah, c. 700–540 BC; John's gospel, c. AD 80–95.

Prophecy & fulfillmentLinked by the New Testament

The suffering servant

A servant despised and rejected, wounded for the transgressions of others, silent like a lamb before its shearers, who bears the sin of many and is 'numbered with the transgressors'. The New Testament quotes or alludes to this passage more than any other to explain Jesus' death: healing (Matthew 8), silence before accusers (Acts 8), substitutionary suffering (1 Peter 2), being counted among criminals (Luke 22).

When: Isaiah 40–55 (composition dated to c. 700 BC by some scholars, c. 540 BC by others); quoted in the New Testament c. AD 30–95.

Original passageIsaiah 53:1–12
Where the New Testament applies itMatthew 8:16–17John 12:37–38Acts 8:32–35Luke 22:371 Peter 2:21–25Romans 10:16
Ways people read this (3)
  • Christian reading: a prophecy of Jesus' atoning death.
  • Traditional Jewish reading: the servant is Israel, suffering for the nations.
  • Some scholars see both an original corporate meaning and a later individual reading.
