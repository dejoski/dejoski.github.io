The Year of the LORDʼs Favor

Luke 4:16–30
1The Spirit of the Lord God is on Me,
because the Lord has anointed Me
to preach good news to the poor.
He has sent Me to bind up the brokenhearted,
to proclaim liberty to the captives
and freedom to the prisoners,
2to proclaim the year of the Lordʼs favor
and the day of our Godʼs vengeance,
to comfort all who mourn,
3to console the mourners in Zion—
to give them a crown of beauty for ashes,
the oil of joy for mourning,
and a garment of praise for a spirit of despair.
So they will be called oaks of righteousness,
the planting of the Lord, that He may be glorified.
4They will rebuild the ancient ruins;
they will restore the places long devastated;
they will renew the ruined cities,
the desolations of many generations.
5Strangers will stand and feed your flocks,
and foreigners will be your plowmen and vinedressers.
6But you will be called the priests of the Lord;
they will speak of you as ministers of our God;
you will feed on the wealth of nations,
and you will boast in their riches.
7Instead of shame, My people will have a double portion,
and instead of humiliation, they will rejoice in their share;
and so they will inherit a double portion in their land,
and everlasting joy will be theirs.
8For I, the Lord, love justice;
I hate robbery and iniquity;
in My faithfulness I will give them their recompense
and make an everlasting covenant with them.
9Their descendants will be known among the nations,
and their offspring among the peoples.
All who see them will acknowledge
that they are a people the Lord has blessed.
10I will rejoice greatly in the Lord,
my soul will exult in my God;
for He has clothed me with garments of salvation
and wrapped me in a robe of righteousness,
as a bridegroom wears a priestly headdress,
as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.
11For as the earth brings forth its growth,
and as a garden enables seed to spring up,
so the Lord God will cause righteousness and praise
to spring up before all the nations.

  2. b v2 Or to proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD; cited in Luke 4:19

Study notes on this chapter · 3

Type / patternWidely held reading

The year of Jubilee

Every fiftieth year slaves were freed, debts forgiven and land returned. In the Nazareth synagogue Jesus reads Isaiah 61, which speaks of a proclamation of release and the year of the LORD's favor, in language that echoes the Jubilee, and says 'Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing'.

When: Leviticus (Sinai era); Jesus' Nazareth sermon, c. AD 27–28.

Original passageLeviticus 25:8–13Isaiah 61:1–2
Echoed inLuke 4:16–21
Ways people read this (3)
  • Jesus announces a spiritual Jubilee of forgiveness and freedom.
  • Some also take it as a mandate for social release (debt, poverty and oppression); others say the application is primarily spiritual.
  • It is uncertain how fully Israel actually kept the Jubilee.

Prophecy & fulfillmentLinked by the New Testament

The blind see, the lame walk

When John the Baptist asks from prison whether Jesus is the coming one, Jesus answers by listing what is happening: the blind see, the lame walk, the dead are raised, the poor hear good news. These are the signs Isaiah linked with God's coming to save.

When: Isaiah, c. 700 BC; Jesus' reply to John's disciples, c. AD 28–29.

Original passageIsaiah 35:5–6Isaiah 29:18–19Isaiah 61:1
Where the New Testament applies itMatthew 11:2–6Luke 7:18–23
