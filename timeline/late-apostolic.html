  1. AD 68 – AD 96
    Introduction and blessing
    Revelation 1:1–6· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    John introduces the book as a revelation of Jesus Christ given through an angel, pronounces a blessing on those who read and keep it, and greets the seven churches with grace and peace.

  2. AD 68 – AD 96
    Behold, he is coming
    Revelation 1:7–8· praise

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    John praises Jesus who loves us and freed us from sin, announces that he is coming with the clouds, and God declares himself the Alpha and Omega.

  3. AD 68 – AD 96
    John's vision of the risen Jesus
    Revelation 1:9–16· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    On Patmos John hears a loud voice and turns to see one like a son of man among seven golden lampstands, glorious and with a two-edged sword from his mouth. John falls as though dead.

  4. AD 68 – AD 96
    Do not be afraid: write what you see
    Revelation 1:17–20· speech

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus lays his right hand on John and tells him not to be afraid, for he is the first and the last, who was dead and lives, holding the keys of death. He tells John to write what he sees, and explains the stars and lampstands as the angels and churches.

  5. AD 68 – AD 96
    To Ephesus: you have left your first love
    Revelation 2:1–7· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus commends the Ephesians for hard work and testing false apostles, but tells them they have abandoned their first love and must repent, promising the tree of life to the one who conquers.

  6. AD 68 – AD 96
    To Smyrna: be faithful unto death
    Revelation 2:8–11· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus, who died and came to life, says he knows their suffering and poverty. He tells them not to fear coming imprisonment and promises the crown of life to those faithful to death.

  7. AD 68 – AD 96
    To Pergamum: hold fast, do not compromise
    Revelation 2:12–17· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus commends the Pergamum church for holding fast where Satan's throne is, but rebukes those who follow Balaam's teaching and the Nicolaitans. He promises hidden manna and a white stone.

  8. AD 68 – AD 96
    To Thyatira: I know your works
    Revelation 2:18–29· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus commends the church's love, faith, and growth but rebukes them for tolerating Jezebel, a false prophetess. He promises authority and the morning star to those who conquer.

  9. AD 68 – AD 96
    To Sardis: wake up
    Revelation 3:1–6· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus says Sardis has a reputation for being alive but is dead. He calls them to wake up, remember what they received, and repent, and promises white garments to the few who have not soiled theirs.

  10. AD 68 – AD 96
    To Philadelphia: an open door
    Revelation 3:7–13· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus, who holds the key of David, has set an open door before this church, which has little strength but kept his word. He promises to keep them in the hour of trial and to make them pillars in God's temple.

  11. AD 68 – AD 96
    To Laodicea: lukewarm and self-sufficient
    Revelation 3:14–22· letter

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Jesus says the Laodiceans are neither hot nor cold and think themselves rich when they are wretched. He counsels them to buy gold, white garments, and salve, standing at the door to knock, and promising a seat on his throne to the one who conquers.

  12. AD 68 – AD 96
    A door open in heaven
    Revelation 4:1–3· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    John sees an open door in heaven and is called up in the Spirit. He sees a throne with one seated on it, shining like jasper and carnelian, encircled by a rainbow.

  13. AD 68 – AD 96
    Twenty-four elders and the seven spirits
    Revelation 4:4–5· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Around the throne are twenty-four elders in white with gold crowns, with lightning and thunder coming from it, seven torches of fire, and a sea of glass like crystal.

  14. AD 68 – AD 96
    Four living creatures worship without ceasing
    Revelation 4:6–11· praise

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Four living creatures full of eyes never stop saying 'Holy, holy, holy, is the Lord God Almighty.' The twenty-four elders fall down, cast their crowns, and worship him as Creator.

  15. AD 68 – AD 96
    The sealed scroll and no one worthy
    Revelation 5:1–4· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    John sees a scroll with seven seals in the right hand of the One on the throne. A mighty angel asks who is worthy to open it, and John weeps when no one is found.

  16. AD 68 – AD 96
    The Lion who is a Lamb
    Revelation 5:5–7· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    An elder tells John to stop weeping: the Lion of Judah has conquered. But John sees a Lamb looking as though slain, with seven horns and eyes, who takes the scroll.

  17. AD 68 – AD 96
    A new song to the Lamb
    Revelation 5:8–10· praise

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    The creatures and elders fall before the Lamb with harps and bowls of incense and sing a new song: he is worthy because he was slain and ransomed people from every tribe and nation to be a kingdom and priests.

  18. AD 68 – AD 96
    Every creature joins in praise
    Revelation 5:11–14· praise

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Countless angels declare the Lamb worthy, then every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, and in the sea joins in blessing him. The elders fall down and worship.

  19. AD 68 – AD 96
    First four seals: four horsemen
    Revelation 6:1–8· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    As the Lamb opens the first four seals, four horsemen ride out: a white one conquering, a red one taking peace from the earth, a black one bringing famine, and a pale one whose name is Death.

  20. AD 68 – AD 96
    Fifth seal: martyrs under the altar
    Revelation 6:9–11· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Under the altar are the souls of those slain for God's word, crying 'How long, O Lord?' Each is given a white robe and told to wait a little longer until the number of their fellow servants is complete.

  21. AD 68 – AD 96
    Sixth seal: the day of wrath
    Revelation 6:12–17· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    A great earthquake shakes the earth, the sun goes black, the moon turns red, and stars fall. All people hide and call for the mountains to fall on them, since the great day of the wrath of God and the Lamb has come.

  22. AD 68 – AD 96
    The 144,000 sealed
    Revelation 7:1–8· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Four angels hold back the winds until the servants of God are sealed on their foreheads. John hears the number sealed, 144,000, from the twelve tribes of Israel, with a list of twelve thousand from each tribe.

  23. AD 68 – AD 96
    A great multitude before the throne
    Revelation 7:9–14· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    John sees a countless multitude from every nation, in white robes with palm branches, praising God and the Lamb. An elder says they came out of the great tribulation and washed their robes in the Lamb's blood.

  24. AD 68 – AD 96
    God will wipe away every tear
    Revelation 7:15–17· blessing

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    The elder says those in white serve God day and night in his temple, no longer hungering or thirsting, and the Lamb will shepherd them and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.

  25. AD 68 – AD 96

    Revelation 8:1–5· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

    Revelation 10:8–11· vision

    c. AD 95 – John on Patmos under Domitian (some scholars date it c. AD 68–69, under Nero) Traditional date

Events in this era

Big events in the wider world while these passages were happening.

  1. AD 70
    Rome destroys Jerusalem and burns the Temple

    The Temple where Jesus worshipped is gone for good; Jewish and Christian life both reshape around its loss.

  2. AD 73 – AD 74
    Masada, the last rebel stronghold, falls to Rome

    The desert fortress's fall ends the revolt; its ruins still overlook the Dead Sea.

  3. AD 79
    Mount Vesuvius erupts and buries Pompeii

    The disaster freezes a Roman city of exactly the world the first Christians lived in — its streets, shops and graffiti survive.

  4. AD 80 – AD 95
    Rabbi Yohanan ben Zakkai re-founds Jewish teaching at Yavneh

    With the Temple gone, Judaism rebuilds itself around teachers and the written and oral law — the path that leads to the Mishnah.
