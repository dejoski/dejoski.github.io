  1. 1010 BC – 444 BC
    Duties of the gatekeepers and Levites
    1 Chronicles 9:19–34· history

    Gatekeepers' duties traced to David and Samuel's day (c. 1000 BC) and continued after the exile Best estimate

    The gatekeepers' work is described: guarding the entrance to the Tent as their fathers did, standing watch through the night and handling the utensils. Other Levites baked bread, prepared spices and served as musicians.

  2. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Arise and shine: the light has come
    Isaiah 60:1–7· oracle of hope

    Vision of Zion's glory (return era c. 538–500 BC per many; Isaiah's vision per tradition) Best estimate

    Zion is told to arise and shine because the LORD's glory has risen upon her, though darkness covers other peoples. Nations and kings come to her light, bringing her scattered children and treasures such as gold and frankincense.

  3. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Ships and foreign kings serve Zion
    Isaiah 60:8–14· oracle of hope

    Vision of Zion's glory (return era c. 538–500 BC per many; Isaiah's vision per tradition) Best estimate

    Ships bring Zion's children and wealth from far away, and foreigners rebuild her walls. Gates stay open, and those who once oppressed her come bowing, calling her the City of the LORD.

  4. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Everlasting light and righteousness
    Isaiah 60:15–22· oracle of hope

    Vision of Zion's glory (return era c. 538–500 BC per many; Isaiah's vision per tradition) Best estimate

    God promises to turn Zion's shame into pride, with peace as her governor and violence gone. He will be her everlasting light, replacing sun and moon, and her righteous people will possess the land forever.

  5. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Good news to the poor and captives
    Isaiah 61:1–3· speech

    The anointed herald of good news and rebuilding (return era per many scholars) Best estimate

    A speaker says the Spirit of the Lord GOD is on him because he was anointed to bring good news to the poor, bind up the brokenhearted, and free captives. He proclaims the year of the LORD's favor and comforts mourners with beauty for ashes.

  6. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Rebuilding, priesthood and everlasting covenant
    Isaiah 61:4–9· oracle of hope

    The anointed herald of good news and rebuilding (return era per many scholars) Best estimate

    The mourners will rebuild the ancient ruins while foreigners tend their fields. The people will be called priests of the LORD, receive a double portion, and God will make an everlasting covenant with them.

  7. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Clothed in salvation
    Isaiah 61:10–11· praise

    The anointed herald of good news and rebuilding (return era per many scholars) Best estimate

    The speaker rejoices in the LORD, who has clothed him in garments of salvation like a bridegroom or bride. As a garden makes seeds sprout, God will make righteousness and praise spring up before all nations.

  8. 700 BC – 500 BC
    A new name for Zion
    Isaiah 62:1–5· oracle of hope

    Jerusalem's vindication; watchmen on her walls (return era per many scholars) Best estimate

    The speaker says he will not stay silent until Zion's righteousness shines like a torch. She will receive a new name, Hephzibah and Beulah, no longer Forsaken, because God delights in her as a bridegroom in his bride.

  9. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Watchmen who give God no rest
    Isaiah 62:6–9· exhortation

    Jerusalem's vindication; watchmen on her walls (return era per many scholars) Best estimate

    God posts watchmen on Jerusalem's walls who must never be silent, and those who call on the LORD are told not to rest until he makes Jerusalem the praise of the earth. God swears her grain and wine will no longer go to enemies.

  10. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Prepare the way: your Savior comes
    Isaiah 62:10–12· exhortation

    Jerusalem's vindication; watchmen on her walls (return era per many scholars) Best estimate

    People are told to go through the gates and build up the highway, because the LORD has announced that Zion's Savior comes with his reward. Her people will be called the Holy People and the city Sought Out, Not Forsaken.

  11. 700 BC – 500 BC
    The warrior returns from Edom
    Isaiah 63:1–6· vision

    Divine warrior returns from Edom (date debated) Best estimate

    A questioner asks who comes from Edom with garments stained red, and the answer says he is mighty to save. He describes trampling the nations alone as in a winepress, because the day of vengeance had come.

  12. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Remembering God's past kindness and Israel's rebellion
    Isaiah 63:7–14· prayer

    Lament recalling God's past kindness; temple in ruins (63:18) points after 586 BC Best estimate

    A prayer begins by recalling the LORD's love and how he saved his people in all their distress. They rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit, then remembered how he led them through the sea in Moses' day.

  13. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Look down: You are our Father
    Isaiah 63:15–19· lament

    Lament recalling God's past kindness; temple in ruins (63:18) points after 586 BC Best estimate

    The people ask God to look down from heaven, saying his zeal and compassion seem restrained. Though Abraham does not know them, God is their Father and Redeemer; they ask him to return because enemies have trampled the sanctuary.

  14. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Tear the heavens and come down
    Isaiah 64:1–7· lament

    Community lament over a ruined temple and cities (after 586 BC per many) Best estimate

    The people plead for God to tear open the heavens and come down so the nations tremble. They admit they sinned, are unclean, and their righteous acts are like filthy rags, and that no one calls on his name.

  15. 700 BC – 500 BC
    We are the clay; do not stay silent
    Isaiah 64:8–12· lament

    Community lament over a ruined temple and cities (after 586 BC per many) Best estimate

    The people say God is their Father and they are clay in the potter's hand, and ask him not to be angry forever. They describe the ruined holy cities and burned temple, and ask whether he will still hold back.

  16. 700 BC – 500 BC
    God held out his hands to a rebellious people
    Isaiah 65:1–7· oracle of judgment

    God's answer to a rebellious people; new heavens and new earth (date debated) Best estimate

    God answers by saying he was ready to be found by those who did not seek him, holding out his hands to a people who chose idolatry and unclean practices. He will repay their sins and those of their ancestors.

  17. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Servants blessed, rebels judged
    Isaiah 65:8–16· oracle of judgment

    God's answer to a rebellious people; new heavens and new earth (date debated) Best estimate

    God says he will not destroy all his people, as a good cluster is kept from spoiling, and his servants will inherit the land. Those who abandon him for Fortune and Destiny face the sword and hunger, while his servants will eat, drink and rejoice.

  18. 700 BC – 500 BC
    New heavens and a new earth
    Isaiah 65:17–25· oracle of hope

    God's answer to a rebellious people; new heavens and new earth (date debated) Best estimate

    God promises to create new heavens and a new earth and a joyful Jerusalem without weeping or early death. People will enjoy their houses and vineyards, God will answer before they call, and the wolf and lamb will feed together.

  19. 700 BC – 500 BC
    What house can humans build for God
    Isaiah 66:1–6· oracle of judgment

    Temple theme (66:1) often read as c. 520–515 BC, rebuilding; date debated Best estimate

    God says heaven is his throne and earth his footstool, so no house can contain him. He esteems the humble who tremble at his word and rejects empty rituals, promising to repay his enemies.

  20. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Zion gives birth and is comforted
    Isaiah 66:7–14· oracle of hope

    Final promise of Zion's comfort and gathering of nations (date debated) Best estimate

    Zion is pictured as giving birth to a nation in a single day, and those who love Jerusalem are invited to rejoice with her. God promises peace like a river and comfort like a mother's, and his hand will be revealed to his servants.

  21. 700 BC – 500 BC
    Fire, gathering of nations and endless worship
    Isaiah 66:15–24· oracle of judgment

    Final promise of Zion's comfort and gathering of nations (date debated) Best estimate

    The LORD comes with fire to judge those who rebel, then gathers all nations to see his glory and sends survivors to declare it. Exiles are brought back, all people worship him every Sabbath, and the rebels' fate is a lasting horror.

  22. 538 BC – 444 BC
    Exile and the first to resettle
    1 Chronicles 9:1–9· history

    After Judah's exile: the first to resettle, c. 538 BC on, in the lists of returned families Best estimate

    The chapter says all Israel was recorded in the genealogies, while Judah was exiled to Babylon for unfaithfulness. It lists the first settlers who returned to Jerusalem from Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim and Manasseh.

  23. 538 BC – 444 BC
    Priests in Jerusalem
    1 Chronicles 9:10–13· list

    Priests settled in Jerusalem after the return from exile Best estimate

    The priests who lived in Jerusalem are named, including Azariah, chief official of the temple. The priests numbered 1,760 able men for the work of the house of God.

  24. 538 BC – 444 BC
    Levites and gatekeepers
    1 Chronicles 9:14–18· list

    Levites and gatekeepers settled in Jerusalem after the return Best estimate

    Levites who settled around Jerusalem are named, along with the gatekeepers under Shallum. Some had served at the King's Gate on the east side.

  25. 538 BC
    Cyrus's decree to rebuild the temple
    2 Chronicles 36:22–23· history

    538 BC – Cyrus of Persia, after conquering Babylon, decrees the temple's rebuilding Well established

    In Cyrus of Persia's first year, God stirs him to issue a written proclamation. He says the LORD has appointed him to build a house in Jerusalem and invites any of God's people to go up.

  26. 538 BC
    Cyrus decrees the temple be rebuilt
    Ezra 1:1–4· history

    538 BC – Cyrus of Persia, first year after taking Babylon, decrees that the Jerusalem temple be rebuilt Well established

    God moves King Cyrus of Persia to announce that the exiles may go to Jerusalem to rebuild the LORD's house, and that neighbors should help them with gifts.

  27. 538 BC
    The exiles prepare and receive temple articles
    Ezra 1:5–11· narrative

    538 BC – the exiles prepare to go; Cyrus returns the temple articles to Sheshbazzar, prince of Judah Well established

    Family heads, priests, and Levites prepare to go, neighbors give generously, and Cyrus returns the temple articles Nebuchadnezzar had taken. Sheshbazzar receives them and carries them to Jerusalem.

  28. 538 BC – 537 BC
    Introducing those who returned
    Ezra 2:1–2· genealogy

    c. 538–537 BC – the roll of exiles who returned under Zerubbabel and Jeshua, counted by family and town Traditional date

    The chapter opens by introducing the people from the province who came back from Babylonian captivity, each to his own town, led by Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and others.

  29. 538 BC – 537 BC
    Returnees counted by family and town
    Ezra 2:3–35· list

    c. 538–537 BC – the roll of exiles who returned under Zerubbabel and Jeshua, counted by family and town Traditional date

    A count of laypeople who returned, listed first by ancestral families (such as Parosh and Pahath-moab) and then by hometowns such as Bethlehem and Jericho.

  30. 538 BC – 537 BC
    Priests, Levites, singers, and gatekeepers
    Ezra 2:36–42· list

    c. 538–537 BC – the priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers and temple servants who returned Traditional date

    The list turns to those serving in worship: four priestly families, a small number of Levites, the singers descended from Asaph, and the gatekeepers.

  31. 538 BC – 537 BC
    Temple servants and Solomon's servants
    Ezra 2:43–58· list

    c. 538–537 BC – the priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers and temple servants who returned Traditional date

    Two further groups who served at the temple are named: the temple servants and the descendants of Solomon's servants, totaling 392.

  32. 538 BC – 537 BC
    Those unable to prove their ancestry
    Ezra 2:59–63· narrative

    c. 538–537 BC – families who could not prove their ancestry are excluded from the priesthood Traditional date

    Some families could not show records of their Israelite descent, and some priestly families could not find their registration. These priests were excluded from priestly duty and from eating the most holy things until a priest could consult God by Urim and Thummim.

  33. 538 BC – 537 BC
    Totals of people and animals
    Ezra 2:64–67· list

    c. 538–537 BC – totals of returnees, servants and animals for the journey from Babylon Traditional date

    The whole assembly totals 42,360, besides servants, singers, and a large number of animals that they brought along.

  34. 538 BC – 444 BC
    Families and towns of the returnees
    Nehemiah 7:8–38· genealogy

    List from the first return c. 538 BC (under Cyrus), copied in Nehemiah's day c. 444 BC; compare Ezra 2 Traditional date

    The register lists the lay families and the men of various towns who returned, with counts for each group.

  35. 538 BC – 444 BC
    Priests, Levites, and temple staff
    Nehemiah 7:39–60· genealogy

    List from the first return c. 538 BC, preserved and recopied c. 444 BC; priests, Levites and temple staff (Ezra 2) Traditional date

    The list continues with the priestly families, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, temple servants, and descendants of Solomon's servants, with totals.

  36. 538 BC – 444 BC
    Those who could not prove their ancestry
    Nehemiah 7:61–65· narrative

    List from the first return c. 538 BC; families who could not prove their ancestry (copy in Nehemiah's day c. 444 BC) Traditional date

    Some families could not document that they were Israelites, and priests who lacked records were excluded from priestly duty. The governor ruled that they could not eat the most holy food until a priest could consult the Urim and Thummim.

  37. 538 BC – 444 BC
    Totals and gifts for the work
    Nehemiah 7:66–73· list

    Autumn 444 BC – totals of the register and gifts for the work at the first return and now Traditional date

    The whole group numbers 42,360, plus servants, singers, and animals. Leaders and people give gold, silver, and priestly garments for the work, and the people settle in their towns by the seventh month.

  38. 538 BC – 450 BC

    Nehemiah 12:1–11· list

    Lists of those who came with Zerubbabel and Jeshua c. 538 BC and later priestly succession to c. 450 BC Traditional date

    The chapter lists the priests and Levites who returned with Zerubbabel and Jeshua, and the line of high priests down to Jaddua.

  39. 538 BC – 400 BC

    Nehemiah 12:12–26· list

    The heads of priestly families under Joiakim, the recording of Levite families, and the leaders of praise and gatekeeping are listed. The Levites take turns in worship as David prescribed.

    Psalms 85:4–7· lament

    Uncertain – Korahite psalm after a restoration; often read as post-return (538 BC or later), though some place it earlier Best estimate

    Psalms 107:10–16· praise

    Uncertain – thanks from those gathered from the lands; probably after the return, 538 BC or later Best estimate

    The second group sat in darkness in chains because they rebelled against God's words. When they cried out, he freed them, breaking gates of bronze and bars of iron, and they are told to give thanks.

    Psalms 107:17–22· praise

    Uncertain – thanks from those gathered from the lands; probably after the return, 538 BC or later Best estimate

    The third group suffered illness because of their rebellious ways, and drew near to death. God sent his word and healed them, and they should give thanks and offer sacrifices of thanksgiving.

  46. 538 BC – 450 BC

    Psalms 107:23–32· praise

    Uncertain – thanks from those gathered from the lands; probably after the return, 538 BC or later Best estimate

    Merchants at sea saw God raise a storm that brought their skill to nothing. When they cried out he stilled the waves and guided them to harbor, so they should praise him in the assembly.

    Psalms 126:4–6· prayer

    c. 538 BC or later – joy at the return from Babylon (some read it as a later prayer) Best estimate

    Ezra 2:68–70· narrative

    c. 537 BC – the returnees give for the temple and settle in their towns Traditional date

  51. 537 BC

    Ezra 3:1–6· narrative

    Seventh month, c. 537 BC – Jeshua and Zerubbabel rebuild the altar and offer sacrifices during the Feast of Tabernacles Traditional date

    In the seventh month the people gather in Jerusalem, and Jeshua and Zerubbabel rebuild the altar. They offer daily sacrifices and keep the Feast of Tabernacles as the Law prescribes, despite fearing the surrounding peoples.

  52. 537 BC – 536 BC

    Ezra 3:7–9· narrative

    c. 537–536 BC – materials are gathered and Levites appointed to oversee the temple work Traditional date

    The returnees hire workers and arrange cedar from Lebanon by way of Tyre and Sidon. In the second year, Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the Levites begin the temple work.

  53. 536 BC
    The foundation laid amid praise and weeping
    Ezra 3:10–13· praise

    Second month of the second year, c. 536 BC – the temple foundation is laid amid praise and weeping Traditional date

    When the foundation is laid, priests and Levites praise the LORD as David had prescribed, and the people shout. Older people who remembered the first temple weep, and joy and weeping blend into one great sound.

  54. 536 BC – 520 BC

    Ezra 4:1–5· narrative

    c. 536–520 BC – neighbors offer help, are refused, and oppose the builders 'all the days of Cyrus' to Darius (4:5) Dated in the text

    People living in the land ask to join the temple building, but Zerubbabel and the leaders refuse. They then discourage and frighten the builders and hire counselors against them for many years.

  55. 536 BC – 535 BC

    c. 536–535 BC – Cyrus's third year: Daniel's vision by the Tigris, soon after the first exiles returned Dated in the text

    After three weeks of mourning, Daniel sees a man dressed in linen by the Tigris River, shining like lightning and bronze. His companions flee in terror, and Daniel is left powerless and falls face down.

  56. 536 BC – 535 BC

    Daniel 10:10–14· dialogue

    c. 536–535 BC – Cyrus's third year: Daniel's vision by the Tigris, soon after the first exiles returned Dated in the text

  57. 536 BC – 535 BC

    Daniel 10:15–21· dialogue

    c. 536–535 BC – Cyrus's third year: Daniel's vision by the Tigris, soon after the first exiles returned Dated in the text

    Daniel, speechless and drained, is touched and strengthened again and told not to be afraid. The messenger says he must return to fight the prince of Persia, then the prince of Greece, but will first tell what is written in the Book of Truth.

  58. 536 BC – 535 BC

  59. 536 BC – 535 BC

    Daniel 11:5–20· prophecy

    The messenger describes a long series of conflicts, alliances and marriages between the kings of the South and the North, with invasions of the Beautiful Land. Several rulers rise and fall, and their plans fail.

  60. 536 BC – 535 BC

    Daniel 11:21–28· prophecy

Events in this era

Big events in the wider world while these passages were happening.

  1. 538 BC
    Cyrus lets the exiles return home and rebuild

    The Persian king's decree ends the exile; the prophets had named Cyrus by name as the one who would do it.

  2. 515 BC
    The rebuilt Temple in Jerusalem is finished

    Worship restarts on the old site; this second Temple, later hugely enlarged by Herod, is the Temple of Jesus's day.

  3. 458 BC – 445 BC
    Ezra and Nehemiah rebuild Jerusalem's community and walls

    Under Persian rule the returned community is re-formed around the Law, and the city's broken walls are rebuilt in 52 days.
