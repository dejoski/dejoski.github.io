Order: by date · by book

Showing 1–60 of 603 passages; pick a book above to see all of its passages, or switch to “by book”.

  1. AD 30
    Luke recalls Jesus' final teaching and promise
    Acts 1:1–5· narrative

    Spring AD 30 – forty days between Jesus' resurrection and ascension (some date the crucifixion AD 33) Traditional date

    Luke opens by tying this book to his earlier one, then recalls how the risen Jesus proved He was alive over forty days. He tells the apostles to wait in Jerusalem for the Holy Spirit.

  2. AD 30
    The apostles ask about the kingdom
    Acts 1:6–8· dialogue

    c. May AD 30 – on the Mount of Olives, the apostles ask about the kingdom (some date it AD 33) Traditional date

    The apostles ask whether Jesus will now restore Israel's kingdom. He says the timing is the Father's business and instead promises Spirit-given power to be His witnesses, spreading outward from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth.

  3. AD 30
    Jesus ascends and angels promise His return
    Acts 1:9–11· narrative

    c. May AD 30 – Jesus ascends from the Mount of Olives, forty days after his resurrection (some: AD 33) Traditional date

    Jesus is lifted up and hidden by a cloud while the apostles watch. Two men in white ask why they keep staring at the sky and say Jesus will return in the same way.

  4. AD 30
    The believers gather to pray
    Acts 1:12–14· narrative

    c. May AD 30 – in Jerusalem, in the ten days before Pentecost (some date it AD 33) Traditional date

    The apostles return to Jerusalem and meet in an upper room, listing the eleven by name. They pray together with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus, and Jesus' brothers.

  5. AD 30
    Peter proposes replacing Judas
    Acts 1:15–22· speech

    c. May–June AD 30 – Peter proposes a replacement for Judas among about 120 believers (some: AD 33) Traditional date

    Peter tells about 120 believers that Scripture foretold Judas's betrayal and his fate, including the purchase of a field. He says another eyewitness of Jesus' ministry must take Judas's place as a witness to the resurrection.

  6. AD 30
    Matthias is chosen by lot
    Acts 1:23–26· prayer

    c. May–June AD 30 – Matthias chosen by lot to join the Twelve, before Pentecost (some: AD 33) Traditional date

    The group puts forward two candidates and prays for God to show whom He has chosen. They cast lots, and Matthias is counted among the eleven apostles.

  7. AD 30
    The Spirit comes at Pentecost
    Acts 2:1–4· miracle

    Pentecost, c. AD 30 – the Spirit comes in Jerusalem (some date it AD 33) Traditional date

    On the day of Pentecost, a sound like rushing wind fills the house and flame-like tongues rest on each believer. All are filled with the Holy Spirit and begin speaking in other languages.

  8. AD 30
    The crowd hears their own languages
    Acts 2:5–13· narrative

    Pentecost, c. AD 30 – pilgrims from across the Roman and Parthian world hear the gospel in their own languages Traditional date

    Jewish pilgrims from many nations gather, amazed to hear Galileans declaring God's wonders in each person's native language. Some are baffled and ask what it means, while others mock them as drunk.

  9. AD 30
    Peter explains it through Joel's prophecy
    Acts 2:14–21· speech

    Pentecost, c. AD 30 – Peter cites Joel to the crowd (some date it AD 33) Traditional date

    Peter denies that the believers are drunk, since it is only morning. He says this is what the prophet Joel foretold: God pouring out His Spirit on all people, with salvation for everyone who calls on the Lord.

  10. AD 30
    Peter proclaims the risen Jesus
    Acts 2:22–36· speech

    Pentecost, c. AD 30 – Peter proclaims the risen Jesus (some date it AD 33) Traditional date

    Peter says God accredited Jesus by miracles, that they had Him crucified, and that God raised Him. Using Psalms of David, he argues Jesus is the exalted Lord and Christ.

  11. AD 30
    The crowd asks what to do
    Acts 2:37–41· dialogue

    Pentecost, c. AD 30 – about three thousand respond and are baptized (some: AD 33) Traditional date

    Deeply moved, the listeners ask what they should do. Peter urges them to repent and be baptized, and about three thousand accept his message and are baptized that day.

  12. AD 30
    Life together in the first church
    Acts 2:42–47· narrative

    c. AD 30–31 – the first months of the Jerusalem church (some date it AD 33–34) Traditional date

    The new believers devote themselves to the apostles' teaching, fellowship, shared meals and prayer. They share their possessions with those in need, worship in the temple and gain favor with the people as more join.

  13. AD 30 – AD 31
    Peter and John heal a lame beggar
    Acts 3:1–10· miracle

    c. AD 30–31 – soon after Pentecost, the healing at the temple's Beautiful Gate (some date it AD 33–34) Traditional date

    Going to the temple at the hour of prayer, Peter and John meet a man lame from birth who begs at the Beautiful Gate. Peter commands him to walk in Jesus' name, and he leaps and praises God while onlookers recognize him.

  14. AD 30 – AD 31
    Peter credits Jesus, not himself
    Acts 3:11–16· speech

    c. AD 30–31 – Peter speaks in Solomon's Colonnade at the temple (some date it AD 33–34) Traditional date

    As a crowd gathers in Solomon's Colonnade, Peter says the healing came not from his own power but through the name of Jesus. He charges the people with rejecting Jesus, whom God raised.

  15. AD 30 – AD 31
    Peter calls the people to repent
    Acts 3:17–26· speech

    c. AD 30–31 – Peter calls Jerusalem to repent (some date it AD 33–34) Traditional date

    Peter says they acted in ignorance and that God foretold the Christ's suffering. He urges them to repent so their sins are wiped away, pointing to Moses and Abraham's promise as pointing to Jesus.

  16. AD 30 – AD 32
    Peter and John are arrested
    Acts 4:1–4· narrative

    c. AD 30–32 – Peter and John arrested by the temple authorities (Caiaphas high priest, AD 18–36) Traditional date

    Temple authorities, upset that the apostles are preaching the resurrection, seize Peter and John and hold them overnight. Meanwhile many who heard the message believe, and the group grows to about five thousand men.

  17. AD 30 – AD 32
    Peter speaks before the Sanhedrin
    Acts 4:5–12· speech

    c. AD 30–32 – Peter before the Sanhedrin under Annas and Caiaphas Traditional date

    Asked by what power the man was healed, Peter, filled with the Spirit, says it was by Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom they crucified and God raised. He declares there is salvation in no one else.

  18. AD 30 – AD 32
    The council warns them to stay silent
    Acts 4:13–22· dialogue

    c. AD 30–32 – the council orders the apostles to stop preaching Traditional date

    The leaders are struck by the boldness of these ordinary men and cannot deny the miracle. They forbid them to speak in Jesus' name, but Peter and John say they cannot stop speaking about what they have seen, and are released.

  19. AD 30 – AD 32
    The church prays for boldness
    Acts 4:23–31· prayer

    c. AD 30–32 – the Jerusalem church prays for boldness Traditional date

    Freed, Peter and John report back, and the believers pray together to the Creator, quoting Psalm 2 about opposition to God's Anointed. They ask for boldness and healing signs; the place shakes and they speak God's word boldly.

  20. AD 30 – AD 32
    Believers share everything, including Barnabas
    Acts 4:32–37· narrative

    c. AD 30–32 – believers share possessions; Barnabas introduced Traditional date

    The believers are united and share their possessions so no one is in need, while the apostles preach the resurrection with power. Barnabas is highlighted as one who sold a field and gave the money to the apostles.

  21. AD 31 – AD 33
    Ananias lies and dies
    Acts 5:1–6· narrative

    c. AD 31–33 – Ananias and Sapphira in the Jerusalem church Traditional date

    Ananias and his wife Sapphira sell property but secretly keep back part of the money while presenting the rest as the full amount. Peter says Ananias has lied to God, and Ananias falls dead and is buried.

  22. AD 31 – AD 33
    Sapphira shares her husband's fate
    Acts 5:7–11· narrative

    c. AD 31–33 – Sapphira dies; the church is gripped by fear Traditional date

    About three hours later Sapphira arrives unaware, and Peter asks whether the price they reported was the real one. She repeats the lie and also dies, and great fear comes over the church.

  23. AD 31 – AD 33
    Signs and healings draw the crowds
    Acts 5:12–16· narrative

    c. AD 31–33 – signs and healings draw crowds to the apostles in Jerusalem Traditional date

    The apostles perform many signs, and believers meet in Solomon's Colonnade. People are held in awe, more join, and crowds from surrounding towns bring the sick, all of whom are healed.

  24. AD 31 – AD 33
    An angel frees the apostles from jail
    Acts 5:17–21· miracle

    c. AD 31–33 – the high priest jails the apostles; an angel frees them Traditional date

    The jealous high priest and his Sadducee colleagues arrest the apostles, but during the night an angel opens the jail and tells them to keep preaching in the temple. At daybreak they are teaching there.

  25. AD 31 – AD 33
    The apostles answer the Sanhedrin
    Acts 5:22–32· dialogue

    c. AD 31–33 – the apostles answer the Sanhedrin: obey God rather than men Traditional date

    The officers find the jail empty and the authorities are perplexed when the apostles are found teaching. Brought before the council, Peter says they must obey God rather than men and testifies to Jesus' resurrection.

  26. AD 31 – AD 33
    Gamaliel's advice and the apostles' joy
    Acts 5:33–42· narrative

    c. AD 31–33 – Gamaliel counsels the Sanhedrin to wait; the apostles are flogged and released Traditional date

    Enraged council members want the apostles killed, but the Pharisee Gamaliel argues that if this work is merely human it will fail, and if it is from God they cannot stop it. The apostles are flogged and released, and leave rejoicing and still teaching.

  27. AD 32 – AD 34
    Seven men chosen to serve
    Acts 6:1–7· narrative

    c. AD 32–34 – the Hellenist widows dispute; seven men appointed (dates approximate) Best estimate

    Greek-speaking Jewish believers complain that their widows are overlooked in the daily food distribution. The Twelve have the community choose seven Spirit-filled men to handle it so they can focus on prayer and the word, and the church keeps growing.

  28. AD 33 – AD 35
    Stephen is accused and brought before the council
    Acts 6:8–15· narrative

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen accused and brought before the Sanhedrin (dates approximate) Best estimate

    Stephen does great wonders, but men from a synagogue debate him and lose. They persuade others to claim he blasphemed against Moses and the temple, and he is seized and taken before the Sanhedrin with a face like an angel's.

  29. AD 33 – AD 35
    Stephen begins with Abraham
    Acts 7:1–8· speech

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen's speech to the Sanhedrin begins with Abraham (retold history, c. 2000 BC) Best estimate

    Asked if the charges are true, Stephen begins retelling Israel's story. He recounts God calling Abraham out of Mesopotamia, promising the land, and giving the covenant of circumcision.

  30. AD 33 – AD 35
    Joseph, rejected by his brothers, saves them
    Acts 7:9–16· speech

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen retells the Joseph story to the Sanhedrin (patriarchal era, c. 1900–1700 BC) Best estimate

    Stephen recalls that the patriarchs sold Joseph into Egypt out of jealousy, yet God was with him and made him ruler. Joseph later saved his family from famine, and they settled in Egypt.

  31. AD 33 – AD 35
    Moses is born and rejected
    Acts 7:17–29· speech

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen retells Moses' birth and rejection (Egypt, early date c. 1500–1446 BC) Best estimate

    Stephen describes the Israelites' oppression in Egypt and Moses' upbringing in Pharaoh's house. When Moses tried to help his people, they rejected him, and he fled to Midian.

  32. AD 33 – AD 35
    God sends the rejected Moses as deliverer
    Acts 7:30–38· speech

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen retells the burning bush and the Exodus to the Sanhedrin Best estimate

    Stephen recounts God appearing to Moses in the burning bush and sending him back. He stresses that the very man Israel rejected was the one God sent to lead them out and give living words.

  33. AD 33 – AD 35
    Israel's idolatry and God's true dwelling
    Acts 7:39–50· speech

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen retells Israel's idolatry and Solomon's temple Best estimate

    Stephen says the ancestors refused to obey Moses, made a golden calf, and later worshiped other gods. He then notes the tabernacle and temple, insisting the Most High does not dwell in buildings made by human hands.

  34. AD 33 – AD 35
    Stephen accuses his hearers
    Acts 7:51–53· speech

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen accuses the council of resisting the Spirit Best estimate

    Stephen turns directly on the council, saying they resist the Holy Spirit like their ancestors, who persecuted the prophets. He says they have betrayed and murdered the Righteous One and not kept the law.

  35. AD 33 – AD 35
    Stephen is stoned
    Acts 7:54–60· narrative

    c. AD 33–35 – Stephen stoned outside Jerusalem; Saul looks on (dates approximate) Best estimate

    Enraged, the council members hear Stephen say he sees Jesus standing at God's right hand, and they drag him out and stone him. As he dies he prays for Jesus to receive his spirit and for his killers to be forgiven, while young Saul watches over the witnesses' coats.

  36. AD 33 – AD 35
    Persecution scatters the church
    Acts 8:1–4· narrative

    c. AD 33–35 – persecution after Stephen's death scatters the church; Saul ravages it Best estimate

    Saul approves of Stephen's death, and a great persecution scatters believers throughout Judea and Samaria, except the apostles. Saul goes house to house dragging believers to prison, while those scattered preach wherever they go.

  37. AD 33 – AD 36
    Saul is struck down on the Damascus road
    Acts 9:1–9· narrative

    c. AD 33–36 – Saul struck down near Damascus (Paul's conversion; dated from his own chronology in Galatians) Traditional date

    Saul sets out for Damascus with letters to arrest followers of the Way. A heavenly light stops him, and Jesus asks why he persecutes Him; Saul is left blind for three days.

  38. AD 33 – AD 36
    Ananias restores Saul's sight
    Acts 9:10–19· narrative

    c. AD 33–36 – Ananias restores Saul's sight in Damascus Traditional date

    The Lord sends a reluctant disciple, Ananias, to Saul, explaining Saul is His chosen instrument to the Gentiles. Ananias lays hands on him, his sight returns, and he is baptized.

  39. AD 33 – AD 36
    Paul's gospel came by revelation
    Galatians 1:11–17· argument

    c. AD 33–36 – Paul's conversion near Damascus and time in Arabia, recalled in the letter (Acts 9) Traditional date

    Paul says he received his gospel not from people but from a revelation of Jesus. He recalls his former zeal in Judaism and how God called him to preach Christ among the Gentiles.

  40. AD 34 – AD 36
    Philip preaches in Samaria
    Acts 8:5–13· narrative

    c. AD 34–36 – Philip preaches in Samaria (dates approximate) Best estimate

    Philip proclaims Christ in a Samaritan city, where healings and exorcisms bring great joy. Many are baptized, including Simon, a sorcerer who had long amazed the people.

  41. AD 34 – AD 36
    Peter rebukes Simon's attempt to buy the Spirit
    Acts 8:14–25· narrative

    c. AD 34–36 – Peter and John in Samaria; Simon the magician (dates approximate) Best estimate

    Peter and John come from Jerusalem, pray, and the Samaritan believers receive the Holy Spirit. Simon offers money for this power, and Peter sharply rebukes him and urges him to repent.

  42. AD 34 – AD 36
    Philip and the Ethiopian official
    Acts 8:26–40· narrative

    c. AD 34–36 – Philip meets the Ethiopian official on the Gaza road (dates approximate) Best estimate

    Guided by an angel and the Spirit, Philip meets an Ethiopian court official reading Isaiah. Philip explains that the passage points to Jesus and baptizes him, after which the Spirit takes Philip away and he preaches on toward Caesarea.

  43. AD 34 – AD 62
    Paul counts his credentials as loss
    Philippians 3:4–11· teaching

    c. AD 60–62 – Paul recalls his Pharisee past and conversion on the Damascus road (c. AD 33–36), now written from prison Traditional date

    Paul lists his own impressive background, then says he counts it all as loss compared with knowing Christ. He wants righteousness by faith in Christ, to know the power of His resurrection and share His sufferings.

  44. AD 35 – AD 38
    Saul preaches Jesus and escapes a plot
    Acts 9:20–25· narrative

    c. AD 35–38 – Saul preaches in Damascus and escapes over the wall (Aretas IV's governor, cf. 2 Cor 11:32) Traditional date

    Saul begins proclaiming in the synagogues that Jesus is the Son of God, astonishing those who knew him as a persecutor. When Jews plot to kill him, his disciples lower him over the wall in a basket.

  45. AD 35 – AD 40
    Peter heals Aeneas at Lydda
    Acts 9:32–35· miracle

    c. AD 35–40 – Peter's ministry on the coastal plain Best estimate

    Traveling among the believers, Peter finds Aeneas, paralyzed for eight years, and tells him Jesus Christ heals him. He gets up, and many in the area turn to the Lord.

  46. AD 35 – AD 40
    Peter raises Tabitha at Joppa
    Acts 9:36–43· miracle

    c. AD 35–40 – Peter's ministry on the coastal plain Best estimate

    Tabitha, a disciple known for kindness to widows, dies in Joppa, and two men fetch Peter from nearby Lydda. He prays and tells her to get up, and she opens her eyes and is presented alive.

  47. AD 35 – AD 44
    The gospel reaches Greeks at Antioch
    Acts 11:19–26· narrative

    c. AD 35–44 – Greeks reached at Antioch; Barnabas fetches Saul from Tarsus; 'Christians' (dates approximate) Best estimate

    Believers scattered after Stephen's death preach mostly to Jews, but some speak to Greeks in Antioch, and many believe. Jerusalem sends Barnabas, who fetches Saul, and the disciples are first called Christians there.

  48. AD 35 – AD 37
    First visit to Jerusalem
    Galatians 1:18–24· narrative

    c. AD 35–37 – Paul's first Jerusalem visit, three years after conversion; he meets Peter and James Traditional date

    After three years Paul visited Jerusalem for fifteen days and met Peter and James, then went to Syria and Cilicia, where churches in Judea only heard that their former persecutor was now preaching the faith.

  49. AD 36 – AD 38
    Barnabas introduces Saul in Jerusalem
    Acts 9:26–31· narrative

    c. AD 36–38 – Saul's first Jerusalem visit, about three years after his conversion (Gal 1:18) Traditional date

    The disciples in Jerusalem fear Saul until Barnabas vouches for him. After hostile debates, believers send him to Tarsus, and the church enjoys peace and growth.

  50. AD 36 – AD 37
    Boasting in weakness; escape from Damascus
    2 Corinthians 11:30–33· narrative

    c. AD 36–37 – Paul's escape from Damascus (under King Aretas IV), recalled in a letter of c. AD 54–56 Traditional date

    Paul says he will boast of what shows his weakness and swears he is not lying. He recounts being lowered in a basket through a wall window to escape arrest in Damascus.

  51. AD 37 – AD 41
    An angel directs Cornelius to Peter
    Acts 10:1–8· narrative

    c. AD 37–41 – Peter and Cornelius at Caesarea (date uncertain) Best estimate

    Cornelius, a devout Roman centurion in Caesarea, sees an angel who says his prayers and gifts to the poor are remembered by God. He is told to send for Peter in Joppa, and he sends three men.

  52. AD 37 – AD 41
    Peter's vision of the sheet
    Acts 10:9–16· narrative

    c. AD 37–41 – Peter and Cornelius at Caesarea (date uncertain) Best estimate

    On a rooftop in Joppa, hungry Peter sees a sheet of all kinds of animals and hears a voice tell him to kill and eat. He refuses as they are unclean, but is told not to call impure what God has made clean, three times.

  53. AD 37 – AD 41
    The Spirit sends Peter with the visitors
    Acts 10:17–23· narrative

    c. AD 37–41 – Peter and Cornelius at Caesarea (date uncertain) Best estimate

    While Peter ponders the vision, Cornelius's men arrive, and the Spirit tells him to go with them without hesitation. Peter welcomes them as guests, and next day sets out with some believers from Joppa.

  54. AD 37 – AD 41
    Peter and Cornelius meet
    Acts 10:24–33· dialogue

    c. AD 37–41 – Peter and Cornelius at Caesarea (date uncertain) Best estimate

    Cornelius greets Peter with a crowd gathered, and Peter declines his worship, explaining that God has shown him not to call anyone unclean. Cornelius retells his angelic vision and says they are ready to hear all Peter has been told.

  55. AD 37 – AD 41
    Peter preaches Jesus to Gentiles
    Acts 10:34–43· speech

    c. AD 37–41 – Peter and Cornelius at Caesarea (date uncertain) Best estimate

    Peter says he now understands God accepts people from every nation. He summarizes Jesus' ministry, death and resurrection, and says everyone who believes in Him receives forgiveness of sins.

  56. AD 37 – AD 41
    The Spirit falls on the Gentiles
    Acts 10:44–48· narrative

    c. AD 37–41 – Peter and Cornelius at Caesarea (date uncertain) Best estimate

    While Peter is still speaking, the Holy Spirit comes on the listeners, who speak in tongues and praise God. Astonished, Peter orders that they be baptized, since they received the Spirit just as the Jewish believers had.

  57. AD 37 – AD 41
    Peter defends himself in Jerusalem
    Acts 11:1–18· speech

    c. AD 37–41 – Peter reports to Jerusalem Best estimate

    Jewish believers criticize Peter for eating with uncircumcised men. Peter retells the vision, the Spirit's command and the Spirit coming on the Gentiles, asking who he was to hinder God, and the critics accept it and praise God.

  58. AD 41 – AD 44
    Herod kills James and jails Peter
    Acts 12:1–5· narrative

    AD 41–44 – Herod Agrippa I reigns; James killed and Peter jailed at Passover, most likely AD 44 Well established

    King Herod has James the brother of John killed and, seeing it pleases people, arrests Peter during Passover week. Peter is heavily guarded while the church prays fervently for him.

  59. AD 41 – AD 44
    An angel leads Peter out of prison
    Acts 12:6–11· miracle

    AD 41–44 – Herod Agrippa I's reign; Peter's angelic rescue (Passover, most likely AD 44) Well established

    The night before his trial, an angel wakes Peter, his chains fall off, and he is led past the guards through a gate that opens by itself. Once outside, Peter realizes the rescue was real.

  60. AD 41 – AD 44
    Peter at Mary's house
    Acts 12:12–17· narrative

    AD 41–44 – Herod Agrippa I's reign; Peter at Mary's house after the escape Well established

    Peter goes to the house where believers are praying, and the servant Rhoda is so happy she forgets to open the gate. The group doubts her until they see him, and he tells them what happened before leaving.

Events in this era

Big events in the wider world while these passages were happening.

  1. AD 45 – AD 60
    Paul's journeys carry the message across the Roman world

    Travelling Roman roads and shipping lanes, Paul plants churches from Syria to Greece — and writes the letters that fill much of the New Testament.

  2. AD 49
    Emperor Claudius expels the Jews from Rome

    Among those who leave are Aquila and Priscilla, who meet Paul in Corinth and become his close partners.

  3. AD 64
    Rome burns, and Nero blames the Christians

    The first imperial persecution of Christians; tradition holds that Peter and Paul both died in Rome in these years.

  4. AD 66 – AD 70
    Judea revolts against Rome

    The uprising Jesus's followers had been warned about ends in catastrophe for Jerusalem.
