  1. Undated
    In the beginning: formless earth
    Genesis 1:1–2· narrative

    The beginning – Genesis gives no calendar date; readers differ widely on how to date creation Best estimate

    The Bible opens with God creating the heavens and the earth. The earth is at first formless and dark, with God's Spirit hovering over the waters.

  2. Undated
    Day one: light
    Genesis 1:3–5· narrative

    Creation week, day one – Genesis counts days, not years; no calendar date is given Best estimate

    God speaks light into being, calls it good, and separates it from darkness, naming them day and night. The first evening and morning mark the first day.

  3. Undated
    Day two: sky separates the waters
    Genesis 1:6–8· narrative

    Creation week, day two – no calendar date is given Best estimate

    God makes an expanse to divide the waters above from the waters below and calls it sky. This completes the second day.

  4. Undated
    Day three: land, seas, and plants
    Genesis 1:9–13· narrative

    Creation week, day three – no calendar date is given Best estimate

    God gathers the waters so dry land appears, naming land and seas, then commands the earth to produce plants and fruit trees, each reproducing according to its kind.

  5. Undated
    Day four: sun, moon, and stars
    Genesis 1:14–19· narrative

    Creation week, day four – no calendar date is given Best estimate

    God places lights in the sky to separate day from night and to mark seasons, days, and years. The greater light rules the day, the lesser the night, and the stars are made too.

  6. Undated
    Day five: sea creatures and birds
    Genesis 1:20–23· narrative

    Creation week, day five – no calendar date is given Best estimate

    God fills the waters and sky with living creatures and birds, and blesses them to be fruitful and multiply.

  7. Undated
    Day six: land animals
    Genesis 1:24–25· narrative

    Creation week, day six – land animals; no calendar date is given Best estimate

    God commands the earth to bring forth livestock, crawling creatures, and wild beasts, and sees that this is good.

  8. Undated
    Humanity made in God's image
    Genesis 1:26–31· narrative

    Creation week, day six – humanity made in God's image; no calendar date is given Best estimate

    God decides to make humankind in his image, male and female, to rule over the animals. He blesses them, gives plants for food, and calls all he has made very good.

  9. Undated
    God rests on the seventh day
    Genesis 2:1–3· narrative

    The first humans in Eden – before any datable history; no date is given Best estimate

    Creation is complete, and God rests on the seventh day, blessing it and setting it apart as holy.

  10. Undated

    Genesis 2:4–7· narrative

    The first humans in Eden – before any datable history; no date is given Best estimate

    A second account begins, zooming in on the earth before plants grew. God forms the first man from the ground's dust and breathes life into him.

  11. Undated
    The garden of Eden and its rivers
    Genesis 2:8–14· narrative

    The first humans in Eden – before any datable history; no date is given Best estimate

    God plants a garden in Eden and puts the man there. Trees grow, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and a river divides into four branches.

  12. Undated
    God's command about the trees
    Genesis 2:15–17· narrative

    The first humans in Eden – before any datable history; no date is given Best estimate

    God places the man in the garden to work and care for it. He allows eating from every tree but forbids the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, warning of death.

  13. Undated
    The man names the animals
    Genesis 2:18–20· narrative

    The first humans in Eden – before any datable history; no date is given Best estimate

    God observes it is not good for the man to be alone and brings the animals to him to name. Among them, no suitable helper is found for him.

  14. Undated
    The woman is made
    Genesis 2:21–25· narrative

    The first humans in Eden – before any datable history; no date is given Best estimate

    God makes a woman from the man's rib and brings her to him. The man celebrates her as his own kind, and the narrator says a man and wife become one flesh, unashamed.

  15. Undated
    The serpent questions God's word
    Genesis 3:1–5· dialogue

    The first humans' disobedience and expulsion from Eden – undated, before any datable history Best estimate

    A crafty serpent asks the woman whether God really forbade eating from any tree. She restates the command, and the serpent contradicts it, saying God is withholding godlike knowledge.

  16. Undated
    The fruit is eaten
    Genesis 3:6–8· narrative

    The first humans' disobedience and expulsion from Eden – undated, before any datable history Best estimate

    The woman eats the fruit and gives some to her husband, who eats. Their eyes are opened, they realize they are naked, sew fig leaves, and hide from God.

  17. Undated
    God questions the man and woman
    Genesis 3:9–13· dialogue

    The first humans' disobedience and expulsion from Eden – undated, before any datable history Best estimate

    God calls out to the man, who admits fear and hiding. When asked if he ate, the man blames the woman, and she blames the serpent.

  18. Undated
    Consequences spoken over serpent, woman, man
    Genesis 3:14–19· oracle of judgment

    The first humans' disobedience and expulsion from Eden – undated, before any datable history Best estimate

    God pronounces consequences: the serpent is cursed with a coming conflict, the woman will face pain and strained relations, and the man will toil against a cursed ground until he returns to dust.

  19. Undated
    Clothing and expulsion from Eden
    Genesis 3:20–24· narrative

    The first humans' disobedience and expulsion from Eden – undated, before any datable history Best estimate

    Adam names his wife Eve, and God clothes them in skins. He then sends them out of the garden and posts cherubim and a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree of life.

  20. Undated
    Cain and Abel's offerings
    Genesis 4:1–7· narrative

    Adam and Eve's first sons – Cain and Abel; undated early generations Best estimate

    Eve bears Cain and Abel. Both bring offerings, and God favors Abel's but not Cain's. God warns the angry Cain that sin is crouching at his door but that he can master it.

  21. Undated
    Cain kills Abel
    Genesis 4:8–12· narrative

    Cain kills Abel – undated early generations after Adam Best estimate

    Cain lures Abel to the field and kills him. When God asks where Abel is, Cain answers evasively, and God declares that Abel's blood cries out and Cain will be a wanderer.

  22. Undated
    Cain's protection and departure
    Genesis 4:13–16· narrative

    Cain leaves for Nod – undated early generations after Adam Best estimate

    Cain protests his punishment is too great. God places a mark on him so no one will kill him, and Cain settles in Nod, east of Eden.

  23. Undated
    Cain's descendants and Lamech's boast
    Genesis 4:17–24· genealogy

    Cain's line – city-building, herding, music and metalwork; undated Best estimate

    Cain's line builds a city and produces herdsmen, musicians, and metalworkers. Lamech takes two wives and boasts of killing a man, claiming far greater vengeance than Cain.

  24. Undated
    Seth is born; people call on the Lord
    Genesis 4:25–26· narrative

    Seth born to Adam and Eve – undated; after Abel's death Best estimate

    Adam and Eve have another son, Seth, in place of Abel. Seth has a son Enosh, and at that time people begin to call on the name of the Lord.

  25. Undated
    The book of Adam's generations
    Genesis 5:1–5· genealogy

    Genealogy from Adam to Noah's sons – ages are given, but no fixed calendar dates Best estimate

    This section begins with a reminder that God made humankind in his likeness, then records Adam's age at Seth's birth, his later years, and his death at 930.

  26. Undated
    From Seth to Jared
    Genesis 5:6–20· genealogy

    Genealogy from Adam to Noah's sons – ages are given, but no fixed calendar dates Best estimate

    A repeated pattern lists each man's age at his son's birth, the years he lived afterward, and his death: Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, and Jared.

  27. Undated
    Enoch walked with God
    Genesis 5:21–24· genealogy

    Genealogy from Adam to Noah's sons – ages are given, but no fixed calendar dates Best estimate

    The pattern breaks with Enoch, who walks with God for 300 years and does not die like the others, because God takes him.

  28. Undated
    From Methuselah to Noah's sons
    Genesis 5:25–32· genealogy

    Genealogy from Adam to Noah's sons – ages are given, but no fixed calendar dates Best estimate

    The list continues through Methuselah and Lamech. Lamech names his son Noah, hoping for comfort from the cursed ground's toil, and Noah later has Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

  29. Undated

    Genesis 6:1–4· narrative

    Noah's generation before the flood – undated; Noah is 500 years old at his sons' births Best estimate

    As people multiply, the 'sons of God' take wives from the daughters of men. God limits human days to 120 years, and the Nephilim and mighty men of old are mentioned.

    Genesis 6:5–8· narrative

    Noah's generation before the flood – undated; Noah is 500 years old at his sons' births Best estimate

    God sees that human hearts are wholly evil and regrets making man, grieved. He resolves to blot out life from the earth, but Noah finds favor in his eyes.

  31. Undated
    Noah's righteousness and the coming end
    Genesis 6:9–13· narrative

    Noah's generation before the flood – undated; Noah is 500 years old at his sons' births Best estimate

    Noah is described as righteous and blameless, walking with God, with three sons. The earth is corrupt and violent, and God tells Noah he will destroy it.

    Noah's generation before the flood – undated; Noah is 500 years old at his sons' births Best estimate

    God tells Noah to build an ark of specified size and design, promises a covenant, and instructs him to bring his family and pairs of animals with food aboard. Noah obeys.

    Genesis 7:6–10· narrative

    The flood year – dated in Noah's 600th year, not by any calendar year we can fix Best estimate

    The flood year – dated in Noah's 600th year, not by any calendar year we can fix Best estimate

    The deep's fountains burst open and the heavens' floodgates open, with rain for forty days. All those on the ark enter, and the Lord shuts Noah in.

    Genesis 7:17–24· narrative

    The flood year – dated in Noah's 600th year, not by any calendar year we can fix Best estimate

    The waters swell, lift the ark, and cover the mountains. Every living thing on dry land dies, and only Noah and those with him survive.

    God sends a wind and closes the springs of the deep and the heavens' floodgates. The waters recede, the ark rests on the mountains of Ararat, and mountaintops become visible later.

    The flood recedes – dated in Noah's 600th–601st years, not by a fixed calendar year Best estimate

    Noah sends out a raven and then a dove three times. The dove returns with an olive leaf and then does not return. Noah sees the ground dry and the earth fully dry by the second month.

    Genesis 8:15–19· narrative

    The flood recedes – dated in Noah's 600th–601st years, not by a fixed calendar year Best estimate

    God tells Noah to come out with his family and the animals so they can spread over the earth and multiply. They all leave the ark.

    Genesis 8:20–22· narrative

    The flood recedes – dated in Noah's 600th–601st years, not by a fixed calendar year Best estimate

    Noah builds an altar and offers burnt offerings. The Lord is pleased and resolves never again to curse the ground or destroy all life, as long as the seasons of the earth continue.

    After the flood – Noah's family; undated, before the spread of the nations Best estimate

    Genesis 9:18–23· narrative

    After the flood – Noah's family; undated, before the spread of the nations Best estimate

    Noah plants a vineyard, becomes drunk, and lies uncovered. Ham tells his brothers, while Shem and Japheth cover their father without looking.

    After the flood – Noah's family; undated, before the spread of the nations Best estimate

    After the flood – Noah's family; undated, before the spread of the nations Best estimate

  47. Undated

    Genesis 10:6–20· genealogy

    Ham's sons are listed: Cush, Mizraim, Put, and Canaan. Nimrod, a mighty hunter, builds kingdoms in Shinar and Assyria, and the Canaanite peoples and their borders are described.

  48. Undated

    Genesis 10:21–32· genealogy

    The table of nations – peoples descended from Noah's sons; undated Best estimate

    Shem's sons and grandsons are listed, including the line of Eber and his son Peleg. The chapter closes noting that from Noah's sons the nations spread across the earth.

    Genesis 11:1–4· narrative

    Early humanity at Shinar, the Babel story – undated, between Noah and Abraham Best estimate

    The whole world speaks one language. People settle in Shinar, make bricks, and decide to build a city and tower to make a name for themselves and avoid being scattered.

    Genesis 11:5–9· narrative

    The Lord scatters the builders of Babel – undated, between Noah and Abraham Best estimate

    The Lord comes down, sees the project, and confuses their language. The people are scattered across the earth and stop building, and the place is named Babel.

    Genesis 11:10–26· genealogy

    Shem to Terah – ten generations bridging the flood and Abraham; no fixed dates Best estimate

    The line of Shem is traced from Arphaxad through Eber, Peleg, and others down to Terah, who fathers Abram, Nahor, and Haran.

    The chronicler lists the descendants of Japheth and then of Ham, including Cush, Mizraim and Canaan. Notes identify Nimrod as a mighty man and the Philistines as coming from the Casluhites.
