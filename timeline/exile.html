  1. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Comfort for Jerusalem and a road for the LORD
    Isaiah 40:1–5· oracle of hope

    Comfort for exiles (traditional: foretold c. 700 BC; critical: c. 550 BC, late exile) Best estimate

    God tells his messengers to comfort Jerusalem because her punishment is finished and her sin pardoned. A voice calls for a highway to be prepared in the wilderness for the LORD's glory to be revealed.

  2. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Grass withers but God's word stands
    Isaiah 40:6–8· teaching

    Comfort for Jerusalem; word of God endures (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    A voice commands the prophet to cry out that all people are like grass and flowers that fade when the LORD's breath blows on them. Only the word of God stands forever.

  3. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Announcing the arrival of God
    Isaiah 40:9–11· oracle of hope

    Comfort for Jerusalem; word of God endures (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    Zion and Jerusalem are told to shout the good news to Judah's cities that their God is coming with might. He is also pictured as a shepherd who gathers lambs in his arms.

  4. 700 BC – 539 BC
    God's greatness beyond human understanding
    Isaiah 40:12–17· poetry

    God's greatness over Babylon's idols (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    A series of questions shows that no one measures, counsels or teaches God. The nations are like a drop in a bucket and a speck of dust before him.

  5. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Idols cannot compare to God
    Isaiah 40:18–24· argument

    God's greatness over Babylon's idols (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    The passage asks what could be compared to God and mocks idols made by craftsmen. It says God sits above the earth and brings rulers to nothing.

  6. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Those who wait on the LORD renew strength
    Isaiah 40:25–31· teaching

    God's greatness over Babylon's idols (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God asks who is his equal and points to the stars he names and keeps. To a discouraged Israel he says he never grows weary, and those who wait on him will renew their strength.

  7. 700 BC – 539 BC
    The LORD summons the nations to judgment
    Isaiah 41:1–7· oracle of judgment

    Nations summoned before God as Persia's Cyrus rises (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God calls the distant peoples to court and asks who raised up a conqueror from the east; he answers that he did, as the first and the last. The nations react by fearfully strengthening each other and their idols.

  8. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Do not fear, Israel my servant
    Isaiah 41:8–16· oracle of hope

    Promise to Israel the servant (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God reassures Israel, his chosen servant and Abraham's descendant, that he is with them and will help them. Their enemies will vanish, and Israel will become like a threshing sledge that crushes mountains.

  9. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Water for the poor and needy
    Isaiah 41:17–20· oracle of hope

    Promise to Israel the servant (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God promises to answer the thirsty poor by opening rivers and fountains and turning the desert into pools and planted trees. The purpose is that all will know God has done it.

  10. 700 BC – 539 BC
    The idols challenged to prove themselves
    Isaiah 41:21–29· argument

    Idols challenged to predict events (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God challenges the idols and their followers to declare what will happen and prove they are gods. They cannot, so he calls them nothing, contrasting them with his own foretelling of what he raises up from the north and east.

  11. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 42:1–4· oracle of hope

    First Servant Song (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God introduces his Servant, whom he upholds and whose Spirit is on him. He will bring justice gently, not crushing the bruised reed, and will not be discouraged until justice is established.

  12. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 42:5–9· oracle of hope

    First Servant Song (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God, the Creator, tells the one he has called that he will hold his hand and make him a covenant for the people and a light for the nations. He will open blind eyes and free prisoners, and he will not share his glory with idols.

  13. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 42:10–13· praise

    New song of God the warrior, ahead of Babylon's fall (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    The whole earth, from sea to desert to mountains, is called to sing a new song of praise to the LORD. He is pictured going out like a warrior who shouts in triumph over his enemies.

  14. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 42:14–17· oracle of hope

    New song of God the warrior, ahead of Babylon's fall (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God says that after long silence he will now cry out like a woman in labor and reshape the land. He will lead the blind along unfamiliar paths and put those who trust idols to shame.

  15. 700 BC – 539 BC
    A deaf and blind servant Israel
    Isaiah 42:18–25· oracle of judgment

    Israel as a blind and deaf servant in exile (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God calls Israel deaf and blind, having seen much without understanding. Their plunder and imprisonment came because they would not obey him, and even when judgment burned them they did not take it to heart.

  16. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 43:1–7· oracle of hope

    Promise of redemption and a 'new exodus' from exile (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God tells Israel not to fear because he has redeemed them, called them by name, and will be with them through water and fire. He will gather his sons and daughters from every direction.

  17. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 43:8–13· argument

    Promise of redemption and a 'new exodus' from exile (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    Nations are brought before God as if in court, and Israel is named as his witness. God declares that there is no god before or after him and no savior but him.

  18. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 43:14–21· oracle of hope

    Promise of redemption and a 'new exodus' from exile (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God promises to bring Babylon down and recalls how he once opened a path through the sea. He tells Israel not to dwell on the past because he is doing something new, making a way in the wilderness.

  19. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 43:22–28· oracle of judgment

    Promise of redemption and a 'new exodus' from exile (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God says Israel has not called on him and has burdened him with sins rather than offerings, yet he blots out their transgressions for his own sake. He then invites them to argue their case, but their sin has brought disgrace.

  20. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 44:1–5· oracle of hope

    God tells Jacob, his chosen servant, not to fear, and promises to pour water on the dry ground and his Spirit on their descendants. Many will then claim to belong to the LORD.

  21. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 44:6–8· teaching

    Spirit outpoured; folly of idol-making in Babylonian setting (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    The LORD, Israel's King and Redeemer, says he is the first and last and challenges anyone to declare the future like him. He tells Israel not to fear, as they are his witnesses and there is no other Rock.

  22. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 44:9–20· argument

    Idol makers are shown as foolish: a smith and a woodworker labor to make gods from the same materials they burn for warmth and cooking. They bow before a block of wood without seeing the deception.

  23. 700 BC – 539 BC
    Return to me and sing
    Isaiah 44:21–23· exhortation

    Spirit outpoured; folly of idol-making in Babylonian setting (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God tells Israel to remember that he made them, has forgiven their sins like a cloud that vanishes, and will not forget them. He calls them to return, and heaven, earth and mountains to sing that he has redeemed Jacob.

  24. 700 BC – 539 BC
    The Creator who names Cyrus
    Isaiah 44:24–28· oracle of hope

    God, who formed Israel and made all things, exposes false prophets and confirms the words of his messengers. He says Jerusalem will be rebuilt, and he names Cyrus as the shepherd who will carry out his purpose.

  25. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 45:1–7· oracle of hope

    God addresses Cyrus as his anointed, promising to go before him, open gates and give him hidden treasures. This is for Israel's sake, so that people everywhere will know the LORD alone is God.

  26. 700 BC – 539 BC

  27. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 45:14–17· oracle of hope

    Nations like Egypt, Cush and Seba will come to Israel in submission and say God is with them and there is no other. The prophet marvels at a God who hides himself, and idol makers are shamed while Israel is saved forever.

  28. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 45:18–25· exhortation

    God alone is Creator and Savior, Cyrus as his agent (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    The Creator, who has not spoken in secret, invites survivors from the nations to turn to him and be saved, since he alone is God. He swears that every knee will bow before him.

  29. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 46:1–7· oracle of judgment

    The Babylonian gods Bel and Nebo are hauled off on animals, unable to save themselves. By contrast, God says he has carried Israel from birth to old age, and idols made by goldsmiths cannot answer or save.

  30. 700 BC – 539 BC

    Isaiah 46:8–13· exhortation

    Babylon's gods (Bel, Nebo) carried off as Persia advances (Isaiah c. 700 BC foretelling, or c. 550 BC per many) Best estimate

    God tells the stubborn to remember he alone is God and declares the end from the beginning. He will summon a bird of prey from the east to fulfill his purpose and is bringing salvation to Zion.

    Babylon, pictured as a pampered young woman, is told to sit in the dust and do slave's work, her shame exposed. God, Israel's Redeemer, will take vengeance, and she will no longer be called queen of kingdoms.

  32. 700 BC – 539 BC

Events in this era

Big events in the wider world while these passages were happening.

  1. 586 BC
    Babylon destroys Jerusalem and the Temple

    The city and Solomon's Temple are burned and Judah's people are carried to Babylon — the disaster the prophets had warned about.

  2. 539 BC
    Cyrus of Persia conquers Babylon

    A new, more tolerant empire takes over — and for the exiles, the way home suddenly opens.
