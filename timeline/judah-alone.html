  1. 925 BC – 586 BC
    Singing of God's covenant with David
    Psalms 89:1–4· praise

    Uncertain – Ethan the Ezrahite on God's covenant with David; the lament suggests a Davidic king's defeat, c. 925–586 BC Best estimate

    The psalmist promises to sing of God's loving devotion and faithfulness forever, and recalls God's oath to David that his descendants and throne would last for all generations.

  2. 925 BC – 586 BC
    The Lord's power over heaven, sea and earth
    Psalms 89:5–14· praise

    Uncertain – Ethan the Ezrahite on God's covenant with David; the lament suggests a Davidic king's defeat, c. 925–586 BC Best estimate

    The psalmist praises God as unmatched among heavenly beings, ruler of the raging sea, victor over Rahab, and creator of heaven and earth. His throne rests on righteousness and justice.

  3. 925 BC – 586 BC
    Blessed are those who worship
    Psalms 89:15–18· praise

    Uncertain – Ethan the Ezrahite on God's covenant with David; the lament suggests a Davidic king's defeat, c. 925–586 BC Best estimate

    Those who know the joyful sound and walk in God's light are blessed. The psalmist says God is their strength and that Israel's shield, its king, belongs to the Holy One.

  4. 925 BC – 586 BC
    God's promise to David restated
    Psalms 89:19–29· oracle of hope

    Uncertain – Ethan the Ezrahite on God's covenant with David; the lament suggests a Davidic king's defeat, c. 925–586 BC Best estimate

    Recalling a vision, the psalmist quotes God: he has chosen and anointed David, will strengthen him and defeat his foes, will be a father to him, and will keep his covenant and his line forever.

  5. 925 BC – 586 BC
    Discipline but not abandonment
    Psalms 89:30–37· oracle of hope

    Uncertain – Ethan the Ezrahite on God's covenant with David; the lament suggests a Davidic king's defeat, c. 925–586 BC Best estimate

    God adds that if David's sons abandon his law he will discipline them, but he will never withdraw his loving devotion or break his covenant. He swears by his holiness that David's line will endure like the sun and moon.

  6. 925 BC – 586 BC
    The covenant seems broken
    Psalms 89:38–45· lament

    Uncertain – Ethan the Ezrahite on God's covenant with David; the lament suggests a Davidic king's defeat, c. 925–586 BC Best estimate

    The tone reverses: the psalmist says God has rejected his anointed king, torn down his defenses, let enemies plunder him, and ended his throne. The king is left shamed and weak.

  7. 925 BC – 586 BC
    How long, Lord? A closing plea
    Psalms 89:46–52· lament

    Uncertain – Ethan the Ezrahite on God's covenant with David; the lament suggests a Davidic king's defeat, c. 925–586 BC Best estimate

    The psalmist asks how long God will hide himself, reminds him that life is brief, and asks where his old faithful love to David has gone. He ends with a line of blessing on God, and the whole third book of Psalms ends here.

  8. 735 BC – 701 BC
    God charges His rebellious children
    Isaiah 1:2–9· oracle of judgment

    c. 701 BC, or earlier c. 734 (scholars differ) – Judah's land desolate, cities burned; God charges his rebellious people Best estimate

    God calls heaven and earth to witness that His children have rebelled and do not know Him. Isaiah describes Judah as a wounded, devastated land spared from total destruction only by a few survivors.

  9. 735 BC – 701 BC
    God rejects empty worship
    Isaiah 1:10–17· speech

    c. 735–701 BC – Isaiah in Jerusalem under Ahaz or Hezekiah; temple sacrifices without justice Best estimate

    God says He is sickened by sacrifices, festivals and prayers from people whose hands are violent. He calls them to wash themselves, stop evil, and defend the fatherless and widow.

  10. 735 BC – 701 BC
    An invitation to reason together
    Isaiah 1:18–20· speech

    c. 735–701 BC – God's invitation to reason: scarlet sins made white, in Ahaz's or Hezekiah's Jerusalem Best estimate

    God invites the people to reason with Him: sins as red as crimson can be made white as snow. The choice is between obedience and eating well, or rebellion and the sword.

  11. 735 BC – 701 BC
    The faithful city has become corrupt
    Isaiah 1:21–26· lament

    c. 735–701 BC – Jerusalem's corrupt rulers; Assyria's rise threatens Judah Best estimate

    Isaiah mourns that Jerusalem, once full of justice, now has murderers, watered-down wine and bribe-loving rulers. God promises to purge her like metal and restore honest judges.

  12. 735 BC – 701 BC
    Redemption for the repentant, ruin for rebels
    Isaiah 1:27–31· oracle of judgment

    c. 735–701 BC – Isaiah warns of ruin for idolaters and rebels, with Assyria looming Best estimate

    Zion will be redeemed through justice, but rebels will be shattered. Those who trusted in sacred trees and gardens will become like withered oaks, burned up together.

  13. 735 BC – 700 BC
    Moab's sudden ruin and grief
    Isaiah 15:1–9· lament

    c. 735–700 BC – Moab's sudden ruin, probably by Assyria in the late 8th century BC Best estimate

    An oracle describes the cities of Moab laid waste in a night, with people weeping, shaving their heads and fleeing. The prophet himself grieves over the devastation and the dried-up land.

  14. 735 BC – 700 BC
    Moab's plea for refuge
    Isaiah 16:1–5· dialogue

    c. 735–700 BC – Moabite refugees seek shelter in Judah from Assyrian advance Best estimate

    Moabite refugees ask Judah for shelter and a decision on their behalf. The passage says that when oppression ends, a faithful throne will be established in David's tent.

  15. 735 BC – 700 BC
    Moab's pride and empty prayer
    Isaiah 16:6–12· lament

    c. 735–700 BC – Moab's pride and withered vineyards under Assyrian pressure Best estimate

    Moab's arrogance cannot save its vineyards, which wither. Isaiah weeps for the ruined harvest and says Moab's prayers at the high places will be useless.

  16. 735 BC – 701 BC
    Woe to land-grabbers; the LORD plans disaster
    Micah 2:1–5· oracle of judgment

    c. 735–701 BC, Judah under Ahaz or Hezekiah: land-grabbing elites condemned Best estimate

    Micah condemns people who plot at night to seize fields and homes from others. The LORD says he plans a disaster for them, and they will lose their own land with no one to restore it.

  17. 735 BC – 701 BC
    Do not preach: the people reject the message
    Micah 2:6–11· dialogue

    c. 735–701 BC, Judah: the powerful silence Micah; Assyrian pressure looms Best estimate

    The people tell prophets to stop preaching, but Micah replies that God's words bring good to the upright. He accuses them of robbing the vulnerable, tells them to leave, and says they would prefer a preacher of wine.

  18. 735 BC – 701 BC
    Rulers who devour the people
    Micah 3:1–4· oracle of judgment

    c. 735–701 BC, Judah: corrupt rulers devour the people Best estimate

    Micah confronts Israel's leaders, who should know justice but hate good and love evil. He compares their exploitation of the people to butchering them, and says the LORD will not answer when they cry out.

  19. 735 BC – 701 BC
    Misleading prophets go silent; Micah is empowered
    Micah 3:5–8· oracle of judgment

    c. 735–701 BC, Judah: paid prophets mislead; Micah speaks by the Spirit Best estimate

    The LORD says prophets who lead people astray, promising peace to those who pay them, will lose all visions and answers. Micah contrasts himself, filled with the Spirit to declare Israel's sin.

  20. 729 BC – 715 BC
    Hezekiah opens the temple and calls the Levites
    2 Chronicles 29:1–11· speech

    c. 715 BC – first year of Hezekiah (some scholars date it c. 729 BC) Traditional date

    Hezekiah becomes king and in his first month reopens the temple doors. He tells the Levites to consecrate themselves and cleanse the temple because his fathers' unfaithfulness brought God's anger.

  21. 729 BC – 715 BC
    The temple is cleansed
    2 Chronicles 29:12–19· narrative

    c. 715 BC – first year of Hezekiah (some scholars date it c. 729 BC) Traditional date

    The Levites, listed by family, consecrate themselves and clear the unclean things from the temple, finishing in sixteen days. They report that the altar and utensils are ready.

  22. 729 BC – 715 BC
    Sin offerings and restored worship
    2 Chronicles 29:20–30· ritual instruction

    c. 715 BC – first year of Hezekiah (some scholars date it c. 729 BC) Traditional date

    Hezekiah brings sacrifices for the kingdom and sanctuary. As the burnt offering begins, singers and trumpets join, and the assembly worships with David's and Asaph's songs.

  23. 729 BC – 715 BC
    Thank offerings and rejoicing
    2 Chronicles 29:31–36· narrative

    c. 715 BC – first year of Hezekiah (some scholars date it c. 729 BC) Traditional date

    The people bring sacrifices and thank offerings in great numbers. Levites help the too few priests, and the people rejoice at how quickly God had prepared everything.

  24. 725 BC – 700 BC
    Tyre's fall shocks the seafaring world
    Isaiah 23:1–7· lament

    c. 725–700 BC – Tyre's fall; Assyria besieges Tyre under Shalmaneser V and again under Sennacherib (701 BC) Best estimate

    Ships of Tarshish are told to wail because Tyre has been destroyed. Sidon and the trading world grieve over a once-jubilant city.

  25. 725 BC – 700 BC
    The LORD planned Tyre's fall
    Isaiah 23:8–14· oracle of judgment

    c. 725–700 BC – the LORD planned Tyre's fall; Assyria's arrival and the land of the Chaldeans Best estimate

    The LORD planned Tyre's fall to humble the pride of a city whose merchants were like princes. Its allies have no answer and the harbor is gone.

  26. 725 BC – 700 BC
    Tyre forgotten, then restored
    Isaiah 23:15–18· oracle of hope

    c. 725–700 BC – Tyre forgotten for seventy years and then restored (oracle spoken in Assyria's age) Best estimate

    Tyre will be forgotten for seventy years and then restored, returning to trade. Its profits will finally be dedicated to the LORD and to those who live before Him.

  27. 725 BC – 701 BC
    Micah mourns the incurable wound
    Micah 1:8–9· lament

    After 722 BC: the wound from Assyria reaches Judah; likely Sennacherib's 701 BC invasion up to Jerusalem's gate Best estimate

    Micah says he will wail and go barefoot and naked like a mourner. Samaria's wound is incurable and has reached the gates of Jerusalem.

  28. 722 BC – 700 BC
    Foreign settlers and mixed worship in Samaria
    2 Kings 17:24–33· narrative

    After 722 BC – Assyria resettles foreign peoples in Samaria (Sargon II and later kings) Best estimate

    Assyria settles peoples from other lands in Samaria. When lions attack them, a priest is sent back to teach them to worship the LORD, but each nation keeps its own gods as well.

  29. 722 BC – 700 BC
    Covenant remembered, but not followed
    2 Kings 17:34–41· teaching

    After 722 BC – the narrator's reflection on the broken covenant and mixed worship in Samaria Best estimate

    The narrator quotes the commands of the covenant: worship the LORD alone and do not fear other gods. The settlers still keep their former customs alongside the worship of the LORD.

  30. 722 BC – 701 BC
    Samaria falls; Sennacherib takes Judah's cities
    2 Kings 18:9–16· history

    722 and 701 BC – Samaria falls; Sennacherib invades Judah in Hezekiah's 14th year Well established

    Assyria finishes its siege of Samaria and exiles Israel because of its disobedience. Years later Sennacherib captures Judah's fortified cities, and Hezekiah pays heavy tribute, stripping the temple's silver and gold.

  31. 720 BC – 700 BC
    The LORD musters an army against Babylon
    Isaiah 13:1–5· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–700 BC – oracle on Babylon, while Assyria rules; Babylon's later fall was to Persia and Media (539 BC); some see an exilic date Best estimate

    A heading introduces a message about Babylon. God summons a banner, warriors and a multinational army from far away to carry out His judgment on the country.

  32. 720 BC – 700 BC
    The Day of the LORD brings terror
    Isaiah 13:6–16· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–700 BC – the Day of the LORD on Babylon; fulfilment looked to 539 BC (Medes and Persians) Best estimate

    The Day of the LORD is pictured as cruel destruction, with people paralyzed by fear, stars dark and the earth shaking. God says He will punish the world's arrogance and its people will flee or be killed.

  33. 720 BC – 700 BC
    The Medes will overthrow Babylon for good
    Isaiah 13:17–22· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–700 BC – the Medes will overthrow Babylon, as Cyrus's army did in 539 BC Best estimate

    God will stir up the Medes, who cannot be bought off, to overthrow Babylon like Sodom and Gomorrah. The city will never be lived in again and only wild animals will dwell there.

  34. 720 BC – 700 BC
    Israel restored, Babylon's king mocked
    Isaiah 14:1–3· oracle of hope

    c. 720–700 BC – Israel's restoration promised; a taunt against Babylon's king (some see an Assyrian or later exilic setting) Best estimate

    God will have compassion on Jacob and bring them home, with foreigners joining them. When they find rest, they will sing a taunt song against the king of Babylon.

  35. 720 BC – 700 BC
    The taunt: the oppressor is silenced
    Isaiah 14:4–11· poetry

    c. 720–700 BC – the taunt song over the fallen tyrant, probably Babylon's king Best estimate

    The taunt song celebrates that the tyrant's rule is broken and the whole earth sings. Even the dead in Sheol rise to greet the fallen king, who is now as weak as they are.

  36. 720 BC – 700 BC
    How you have fallen, day star
    Isaiah 14:12–15· poetry

    c. 720–700 BC – "How you have fallen, day star": the king's pride brought low Best estimate

    The king who aspired to rise above the stars and be like the Most High is brought down to the lowest depths of the pit. The images show how far his pride reaches and how far it falls.

  37. 720 BC – 700 BC
    A dishonored end for the tyrant
    Isaiah 14:16–21· poetry

    c. 720–700 BC – the tyrant's dishonored end; his captives kept from home Best estimate

    Onlookers stare at the fallen ruler who ruined cities and kept captives from going home. Unlike other kings, he is denied an honorable burial and his line is cut off.

  38. 720 BC – 700 BC
    God's plan will stand
    Isaiah 14:22–27· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–700 BC – God vows to break Assyria in his land; Sennacherib's army fell in 701 BC Best estimate

    God vows to wipe out Babylon's name and also to break Assyria in His land. No one can thwart the plan God has purposed for the whole earth.

  39. 720 BC – 701 BC
    The LORD comes to Egypt
    Isaiah 19:1–4· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–701 BC – oracle against Egypt, divided and weak in the Kushite and Saite age Best estimate

    The LORD rides on a swift cloud to Egypt, whose idols tremble. God will stir up civil strife and hand Egypt to a harsh ruler.

  40. 720 BC – 701 BC
    The Nile dries up
    Isaiah 19:5–10· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–701 BC – the Nile dries up and Egypt's industries fail Best estimate

    The Nile dries out, and so do the reeds, fields, fishing and linen work that depend on it. Workers across Egypt are dismayed.

  41. 720 BC – 701 BC
    Egypt's wise men are fools
    Isaiah 19:11–15· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–701 BC – the wise counselors of Zoan and Memphis become fools Best estimate

    The princes of Zoan and Memphis give foolish advice, and God has poured a spirit of confusion into them. Egypt staggers like a drunk and no one in the country can help.

  42. 720 BC – 701 BC
    Egypt will fear Judah
    Isaiah 19:16–17· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–701 BC – Egypt will fear Judah Best estimate

    On that day the Egyptians will tremble at what the LORD of Hosts has planned, and the mere mention of Judah will terrify them.

  43. 720 BC – 701 BC
    Egypt and Assyria will worship the LORD
    Isaiah 19:18–25· oracle of hope

    c. 720–701 BC – a future hope: Egypt and Assyria worship the LORD beside Israel Best estimate

    On that day Egypt will speak the language of Canaan, build an altar to the LORD and be healed after being struck. Egypt, Assyria and Israel will share in blessing, with God calling Egypt 'My people' and Assyria 'My handiwork'.

  44. 720 BC – 700 BC
    In the last days, nations stream to Zion
    Micah 4:1–5· prophecy

    c. 720–700 BC, Judah: promise of the last days with nations streaming to Zion Best estimate

    Micah foresees the LORD's house raised up and many nations coming to learn his ways. He will settle disputes, weapons will be turned into farm tools, and each person will live in peace, while Israel walks in the LORD's name.

  45. 720 BC – 700 BC
    The lame and outcast become a remnant
    Micah 4:6–8· oracle of hope

    c. 720–700 BC: promise of a gathered remnant, the lame and outcast Best estimate

    The LORD promises to gather the lame and outcast into a strong nation and rule over them in Zion forever. Former dominion will be restored to Jerusalem.

  46. 720 BC – 700 BC
    A ruler from Bethlehem
    Micah 5:2–5· prophecy

    c. 720–700 BC: promise of a ruler from Bethlehem while Judah's king is humiliated Best estimate

    A ruler for Israel will come from small Bethlehem, with origins from ancient times. Israel is abandoned until his birth, and then he will shepherd his flock in the LORD's strength and be their peace.

  47. 720 BC – 700 BC
    The LORD removes horses, idols, and cities
    Micah 5:10–15· oracle of judgment

    c. 720–700 BC: the LORD will remove horses, idols and fortified cities Best estimate

    The LORD says he will remove Israel's military strength, sorcery, carved images, and Asherah poles so they no longer trust their own works. He will take vengeance on nations that have not obeyed him.

  48. 717 BC – 701 BC
    Assyria, the rod in God's hand
    Isaiah 10:5–11· oracle of judgment

    c. 717–701 BC – Assyria as God's rod; its boast recalls Samaria, Damascus and Carchemish (taken 717 by Sargon II) Best estimate

    God calls Assyria the rod of His anger, sent against a godless nation. But Assyria boasts in its own power and plans to destroy Jerusalem as it did Samaria.

  49. 717 BC – 701 BC
    Assyria's pride will be punished
    Isaiah 10:12–19· oracle of judgment

    c. 717–701 BC – Assyria's king will be punished for his pride, after God's work on Jerusalem Best estimate

    When God has finished with Jerusalem, He will punish Assyria's king for boasting of his own strength. An axe does not lift itself above the one who wields it, so Assyria's forests will burn.

  50. 716 BC – 714 BC
    Hezekiah invites all Israel to Passover
    2 Chronicles 30:1–5· narrative

    c. 715 BC – Hezekiah's first year; Passover invitation sent north to Israel, newly weakened by Assyria Traditional date

    King Hezekiah and his assembly decide to keep the Passover in the second month, since the priests and people were not ready in the first. He sends word to both Judah and the northern tribes inviting everyone to Jerusalem.

  51. 716 BC – 714 BC
    The couriers' letter: return to the LORD
    2 Chronicles 30:6–9· letter

    c. 715 BC – Hezekiah's couriers carry a letter to the survivors of Israel after Samaria's fall (722 BC) Traditional date

    The royal letter urges the survivors of Israel to turn back to God and not repeat their fathers' unfaithfulness. It promises that God is gracious and merciful, and that repentance would bring mercy to their captive relatives.

  52. 716 BC – 714 BC
    Some scorn the invitation, others humble themselves
    2 Chronicles 30:10–12· narrative

    c. 715 BC – northern tribes (Asher, Manasseh, Zebulun) respond unevenly to Hezekiah's invitation Traditional date

    In most of the northern towns the couriers are laughed at and mocked, though some from Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun come to Jerusalem. In Judah, God gives the people one heart to obey the king's command.

  53. 716 BC – 714 BC
    A great assembly clears away the altars
    2 Chronicles 30:13–14· narrative

    c. 715 BC – second-month Passover gathering; Jerusalem's pagan altars removed Traditional date

    A very large crowd gathers in Jerusalem in the second month for the Feast of Unleavened Bread. They first remove the altars and incense altars in the city and throw them into the Kidron Valley.

  54. 716 BC – 714 BC
    The Passover kept, and Hezekiah's prayer for the unclean
    2 Chronicles 30:15–20· narrative

    c. 715 BC – Hezekiah's first year; Passover kept a month late for lack of consecrated priests Traditional date

    The Passover lambs are slaughtered, and the ashamed priests and Levites consecrate themselves. Many worshippers had not purified themselves yet ate the meal, so Hezekiah prays that God would pardon all who set their hearts on seeking Him, and God heals the people.

  55. 716 BC – 714 BC
    Seven days of joy, then seven more
    2 Chronicles 30:21–27· praise

    c. 715 BC – Hezekiah's first year; the Feast of Unleavened Bread extended a second week Traditional date

    The people celebrate the Feast of Unleavened Bread with great joy and music, and agree to extend it another seven days, helped by generous gifts from the king and officials. The celebration is described as unmatched since Solomon, and the priests and Levites bless the people.

  56. 716 BC – 714 BC
    Idols destroyed and worship reorganized
    2 Chronicles 31:1–3· narrative

    c. 715 BC – Hezekiah's first year; Judah and Israelite pilgrims smash idols and high places Traditional date

    After the festival, the people go out and smash sacred pillars, Asherah poles, and high places across Judah and the northern tribal lands. Hezekiah reestablishes the priestly and Levite divisions and pays for the regular offerings from his own possessions.

  57. 716 BC – 715 BC

    Isaiah 14:28–32· oracle of judgment

    In the year King Ahaz died, a warning comes to Philistia not to celebrate the breaking of the rod that struck them, since something worse will come from the north. The poor of Zion will find refuge in the LORD.

  58. 715 BC

    2 Kings 16:19–20· history

    Ahaz dies and his son Hezekiah becomes king.

  59. 715 BC

    2 Kings 18:1–8· history

    Hezekiah becomes king of Judah, removes the high places and destroys the bronze snake Moses made because people burned incense to it. He trusts the LORD, prospers, and rebels against Assyria.

  60. 715 BC – 686 BC

    1 Chronicles 4:34–43· history

Events in this era

Big events in the wider world while these passages were happening.

  1. 722 BC
    Assyria destroys the northern kingdom and carries its people away

    Samaria falls and Israel's ten northern tribes are scattered into exile; only Judah, in the south, remains.

  2. 701 BC
    Assyria's king Sennacherib invades Judah

    Jerusalem survives against the odds in Hezekiah's day; the tunnel he dug for the siege can still be walked today.

  3. 622 BC
    King Josiah renews the covenant after a scroll is found in the Temple

    The book found during Temple repairs drives the last great reform of Judah before Babylon comes.

  4. 612 BC
    Babylon brings down Assyria; Nineveh falls

    The empire that terrified Israel and Judah is gone, and Babylon is the new power Judah has to fear.

  5. 609 BC
    Pharaoh Necho kills King Josiah at Megiddo

    Judah's best king dies trying to block Egypt's march north, and Judah passes under foreign control for good.

  6. 605 BC
    Babylon defeats Egypt at Carchemish

    Nebuchadnezzar's victory makes Babylon master of the whole region, Judah included.

  7. 597 BC
    Nebuchadnezzar captures Jerusalem and takes its king into exile

    The first deportation to Babylon begins the exile; the prophet Ezekiel is among those taken.
