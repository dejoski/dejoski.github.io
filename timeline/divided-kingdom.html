  1. 931 BC
    Rehoboam rejects the elders' advice
    1 Kings 12:1–15· narrative

    c. 931 BC – Rehoboam at Shechem rejects the elders' advice Traditional date

    Israel comes to Shechem and asks Rehoboam to lighten Solomon's heavy burden. He rejects his elders' advice and threatens to make it heavier, which the narrator says was part of God's plan.

  2. 931 BC
    Israel splits from David's house
    1 Kings 12:16–24· narrative

    c. 931 BC – the northern tribes break from David's house; Jeroboam made king of Israel Traditional date

    The northern tribes reject David's house and stone Adoram. They make Jeroboam king. Rehoboam prepares for war but obeys God's word through Shemaiah and turns back.

  3. 931 BC – 925 BC
    Jeroboam's golden calves
    1 Kings 12:25–33· narrative

    c. 931–925 BC – early in Jeroboam's reign: fortified Shechem; golden calves at Bethel and Dan Best estimate

    Jeroboam, afraid people would return to Jerusalem, sets up golden calves at Bethel and Dan, appoints non-Levite priests and creates his own festival.

  4. 931 BC – 930 BC
    Israel asks Rehoboam for relief
    2 Chronicles 10:1–5· narrative

    c. 931 BC – Rehoboam at Shechem; the kingdom splits Traditional date

    Rehoboam goes to Shechem to be made king, and Jeroboam returns from Egypt. The people ask him to lighten the heavy burden Solomon put on them in return for their service; he asks for three days.

  5. 931 BC – 930 BC
    Two sets of advice
    2 Chronicles 10:6–11· dialogue

    c. 931 BC – Rehoboam at Shechem; the kingdom splits Traditional date

    Rehoboam first asks his father's elders, who urge kindness. He rejects their advice and asks his young companions, who tell him to answer harshly and promise even heavier burdens.

  6. 931 BC – 930 BC
    Rehoboam answers harshly
    2 Chronicles 10:12–15· narrative

    c. 931 BC – Rehoboam at Shechem; the kingdom splits Traditional date

    After three days Rehoboam gives the harsh reply the young men suggested. The narrator says this turn came from God to fulfill Ahijah's earlier word to Jeroboam.

  7. 931 BC – 930 BC
    Israel breaks away
    2 Chronicles 10:16–19· narrative

    c. 931 BC – Rehoboam at Shechem; the kingdom splits Traditional date

    The northern tribes reject David's house and go home, leaving Rehoboam ruling only Judah's cities. When he sends Hadoram to them, they stone him and Rehoboam flees to Jerusalem.

  8. 931 BC
    God forbids a war against Israel
    2 Chronicles 11:1–4· narrative

    c. 931 BC – after the split, Shemaiah forbids Rehoboam's war against Israel (traditional dates) Traditional date

    Rehoboam gathers 180,000 warriors to retake the northern tribes. God sends the prophet Shemaiah to tell them not to fight their brothers, and they turn back.

  9. 930 BC – 920 BC
    A man of God confronts Jeroboam
    1 Kings 13:1–10· narrative

    c. 930–920 BC – early in Jeroboam's reign: a man of God from Judah confronts him at Bethel Best estimate

    A man of God from Judah denounces the altar at Bethel and predicts Josiah. The altar splits, Jeroboam's hand withers and is healed, and the man refuses to eat there.

  10. 930 BC – 920 BC
    The old prophet deceives him
    1 Kings 13:11–19· narrative

    c. 930–920 BC – the old prophet of Bethel deceives the man of God Best estimate

    An old prophet of Bethel goes after the man of God and lies, claiming an angel told him to bring the man home to eat. The man of God goes with him.

  11. 930 BC – 920 BC
    Judgment and burial of the man of God
    1 Kings 13:20–32· narrative

    c. 930–920 BC – judgment and burial of the man of God Best estimate

    God speaks through the old prophet to announce judgment on the man of God. He is killed by a lion on the road and the old prophet buries him.

  12. 930 BC – 910 BC
    Jeroboam does not repent
    1 Kings 13:33–34· history

    c. 930–910 BC – Jeroboam's reign: he persists in idolatrous priesthood Best estimate

    Despite these events, Jeroboam keeps appointing priests for the high places. This becomes the sin that leads to his house's destruction.

  13. 930 BC – 586 BC
    The kings of Judah after Solomon
    1 Chronicles 3:10–16· genealogy

    Judah's kings from Rehoboam (c. 930 BC) to Zedekiah; Jerusalem falls to Babylon in 586 BC Well established

    The line of kings is traced father to son from Solomon's son Rehoboam down to Josiah. His sons are listed, ending with the last kings before the exile.

  14. 930 BC – 927 BC
    Rehoboam fortifies Judah
    2 Chronicles 11:5–12· list

    c. 930–927 BC – Rehoboam fortifies Judah; priests from Israel settle there; his family (three years of strength, v. 17) Best estimate

    Rehoboam builds up fifteen cities for defense in Judah and Benjamin, stocking them with officers, food, shields, and spears.

  15. 930 BC – 927 BC
    Priests and Levites join Judah
    2 Chronicles 11:13–17· narrative

    c. 930–927 BC – Rehoboam fortifies Judah; priests from Israel settle there; his family (three years of strength, v. 17) Best estimate

    Priests, Levites, and other faithful Israelites leave the north for Jerusalem because Jeroboam has rejected the Levites and set up his own high places and calf idols. For three years they strengthen Judah.

  16. 930 BC – 927 BC
    Rehoboam's family
    2 Chronicles 11:18–23· genealogy

    c. 930–927 BC – Rehoboam fortifies Judah; priests from Israel settle there; his family (three years of strength, v. 17) Best estimate

    The passage lists Rehoboam's wives and children, noting his favorite wife Maacah and his choice of her son Abijah as chief prince. He spreads his other sons through the fortified cities.

  17. 926 BC – 910 BC
    Jeroboam's wife goes to Ahijah
    1 Kings 14:1–6· narrative

    Later in Jeroboam's reign (ends c. 910 BC) – his wife consults Ahijah about their sick son Best estimate

    When Jeroboam's son Abijah falls ill, Jeroboam sends his wife in disguise to the prophet Ahijah. God has already told Ahijah who is coming.

  18. 926 BC – 910 BC
    Ahijah's message against Jeroboam's house
    1 Kings 14:7–16· oracle of judgment

    Later in Jeroboam's reign (ends c. 910 BC) – Ahijah's message against Jeroboam's house Best estimate

    Ahijah says Jeroboam has done more evil than any before him. God will cut off his house, the child will die, and Israel will be uprooted from the land for its sins.

  19. 926 BC – 910 BC
    The boy dies; Jeroboam's reign ends
    1 Kings 14:17–20· narrative

    c. 926–910 BC – the boy dies; Jeroboam's 22-year reign ends Best estimate

    The boy dies as she reaches home, and all Israel mourns. Jeroboam reigns twenty-two years and is succeeded by Nadab.

  20. 926 BC – 913 BC
    Rehoboam and Shishak's invasion
    1 Kings 14:21–31· history

    Rehoboam's reign (931–913 BC); Shishak of Egypt invades in his 5th year, c. 926–925 BC Dated in the text

    Judah under Rehoboam does evil with high places and shrine prostitutes. Shishak of Egypt attacks Jerusalem and takes the temple treasures, and Rehoboam replaces the gold shields with bronze.

  21. 926 BC – 925 BC
    Judah forsakes the Law and Egypt invades
    2 Chronicles 12:1–4· narrative

    c. 926 BC – Rehoboam's fifth year; Pharaoh Shishak invades Judah Well established

    Once secure, Rehoboam and all Israel abandon the Law of the LORD. In his fifth year Shishak of Egypt attacks with a vast army and captures Judah's fortified cities.

  22. 926 BC – 925 BC
    Humility and a limited deliverance
    2 Chronicles 12:5–8· dialogue

    c. 926 BC – Rehoboam's fifth year; Pharaoh Shishak invades Judah Well established

    The prophet Shemaiah tells the leaders that God has abandoned them to Shishak. They humble themselves, and God promises not to destroy them but says they will become Shishak's servants.

  23. 926 BC – 925 BC
    Shishak takes the treasures
    2 Chronicles 12:9–12· narrative

    c. 926 BC – Rehoboam's fifth year; Pharaoh Shishak invades Judah Well established

    Shishak seizes the temple and palace treasures, including Solomon's gold shields. Rehoboam has bronze shields made instead, and because he humbled himself God's anger turns from him.

  24. 926 BC – 913 BC
    Rehoboam's reign ends
    2 Chronicles 12:13–16· history

    c. 926–913 BC – end of Rehoboam's seventeen-year reign Traditional date

    Rehoboam reigns seventeen years, but the summary says he did evil because he did not set his heart to seek the LORD. He dies and his son Abijah reigns.

  25. 913 BC – 911 BC
    Abijam king of Judah
    1 Kings 15:1–8· history

    c. 913–911 BC – Abijam's 3-year reign in Judah, from the 18th year of Jeroboam Dated in the text

    Abijam reigns three years and follows his father's sins. God preserves his line for David's sake.

  26. 913 BC – 911 BC
    Abijah and Jeroboam prepare for war
    2 Chronicles 13:1–3· narrative

    c. 913–911 BC – Abijah of Judah at war with Jeroboam of Israel Traditional date

    Abijah becomes king of Judah and goes to battle against Jeroboam of Israel. Abijah leads 400,000 men against Jeroboam's 800,000.

  27. 913 BC – 911 BC
    Abijah's speech from Mount Zemaraim
    2 Chronicles 13:4–12· speech

    c. 913–911 BC – Abijah of Judah at war with Jeroboam of Israel Traditional date

    Abijah argues that God gave David's line the kingdom by covenant, that Jeroboam rebelled and set up golden calves, and that Judah still worships properly. He warns Israel not to fight the LORD.

  28. 913 BC – 911 BC
    God routes Israel
    2 Chronicles 13:13–18· narrative

    c. 913–911 BC – Abijah of Judah at war with Jeroboam of Israel Traditional date

    Jeroboam's troops ambush Judah from behind, but Judah cries to the LORD and the priests blow trumpets. God routes Israel, and 500,000 of their men fall because Judah relied on the LORD.

  29. 913 BC – 911 BC
    Aftermath and Abijah's family
    2 Chronicles 13:19–22· history

    c. 913–911 BC – Abijah of Judah at war with Jeroboam of Israel Traditional date

    Abijah captures Bethel and other cities, and Jeroboam never regains power. Abijah grows strong and has many wives and children.

  30. 911 BC – 870 BC
    Asa's reforms and treaty with Aram
    1 Kings 15:9–24· history

    c. 911–870 BC – Asa's 41-year reign in Judah: reforms and the treaty with Aram Traditional date

    Asa does right, removes idols and his grandmother's Asherah pole. When Baasha threatens him he buys help from Aram's king, and he later takes Baasha's building materials.

  31. 911 BC – 897 BC
    Asa's reforms and building
    2 Chronicles 14:1–8· narrative

    c. 911–897 BC – early reign of Asa; a decade of peace Traditional date

    Asa becomes king and does what is right, removing foreign altars and idols and telling Judah to seek the LORD. The land has peace, so he builds fortified cities and gathers a large army.

  32. 910 BC – 886 BC
    Nadab and Baasha of Israel
    1 Kings 15:25–34· history

    c. 910–886 BC – Nadab of Israel (2nd year of Asa) killed by Baasha, who reigns 24 years Dated in the text

    Nadab reigns two years before Baasha kills him and destroys Jeroboam's family, as Ahijah had said. Baasha then continues Jeroboam's sin.

  33. 897 BC – 896 BC
    Asa prays against Zerah
    2 Chronicles 14:9–11· prayer

    c. 897 BC – Zerah the Cushite attacks Asa (date uncertain) Best estimate

    Zerah the Cushite invades with a huge army, and Asa meets him at Mareshah. Asa prays that only God can help the powerless against the mighty and asks for help.

  34. 897 BC – 896 BC
    God defeats the Cushites
    2 Chronicles 14:12–15· narrative

    c. 897 BC – Zerah the Cushite attacks Asa (date uncertain) Best estimate

    God strikes down the Cushites before Asa and Judah. Judah pursues them to Gerar, plunders the cities there, and returns to Jerusalem with much loot.

  35. 896 BC – 895 BC
    Azariah's message to Asa
    2 Chronicles 15:1–7· oracle of hope

    c. 896 BC – Asa's fifteenth year; covenant renewal Dated in the text

    The Spirit of God comes on Azariah, who tells Asa and Judah that God is with them if they seek Him and will forsake them if they forsake Him. He recalls Israel's troubled past and urges them to be strong.

  36. 896 BC – 895 BC
    Asa renews the covenant
    2 Chronicles 15:8–15· narrative

    c. 896 BC – Asa's fifteenth year; covenant renewal Dated in the text

    Encouraged, Asa removes idols and repairs the altar. In the third month of his fifteenth year the people offer sacrifices and covenant to seek the LORD with all their heart, and God gives them rest.

  37. 896 BC – 876 BC
    Maacah removed and a peaceful reign
    2 Chronicles 15:16–19· narrative

    c. 896–876 BC – Asa removes Maacah; Judah has peace until his 35th year (recorded as 15:19; traditional dates) Best estimate

    Asa removes his grandmother Maacah as queen mother for making an Asherah pole, and destroys the pole. He restores dedicated items to God's house, and there is no war until his thirty-fifth year.

  38. 890 BC – 886 BC
    Jehu's message against Baasha
    1 Kings 16:1–7· oracle of judgment

    Late in Baasha's reign (ends c. 886 BC) – Jehu son of Hanani condemns his house Best estimate

    God tells Baasha through Jehu that his house will end like Jeroboam's, because he followed Jeroboam's sin. Baasha dies and his son Elah reigns.

  39. 886 BC
    Zimri kills Elah
    1 Kings 16:8–14· history

    c. 886 BC – 26th year of Asa: Zimri kills Elah of Israel Dated in the text

    While Elah is drunk, Zimri kills him and wipes out Baasha's family, fulfilling the prophecy.

  40. 886 BC – 881 BC
    Zimri and Omri
    1 Kings 16:15–22· history

    c. 886–881 BC – Zimri's seven days; Omri prevails over Tibni (27th–31st years of Asa) Dated in the text

    Zimri reigns seven days before the army makes Omri king. Zimri burns the palace over himself, and Omri overcomes his rival Tibni.

  41. 886 BC – 875 BC
    Asa hires Aram against Baasha
    2 Chronicles 16:1–6· narrative

    c. 886–875 BC – Asa and Baasha (the '36th year' is hard to reconcile with Baasha's dates) Best estimate

    Baasha of Israel fortifies Ramah to cut off Judah. Asa takes silver and gold from the temple and palace to pay Ben-hadad of Aram to break his treaty with Israel, and Baasha withdraws.

  42. 886 BC – 875 BC
    Hanani rebukes Asa
    2 Chronicles 16:7–10· oracle of judgment

    c. 886–875 BC – Hanani rebukes Asa (dating uncertain) Best estimate

    The seer Hanani tells Asa he relied on Aram instead of the LORD, unlike when he trusted God against the Cushites. He warns Asa that God supports those fully devoted to Him and that Asa will now face war. Asa puts Hanani in prison.

  43. 881 BC – 875 BC
    Omri builds Samaria
    1 Kings 16:23–28· history

    c. 881–875 BC – Omri's reign: he builds Samaria as Israel's capital Traditional date

    Omri reigns twelve years, buys the hill of Samaria and builds a capital there. The narrator says he did more evil than any before him.

  44. 874 BC – 853 BC
    Ahab's reign and Baal worship
    1 Kings 16:29–34· history

    c. 874–853 BC – Ahab's 22-year reign in Israel: Jezebel and Baal worship Traditional date

    Ahab marries Jezebel and sets up an altar for Baal. Hiel rebuilds Jericho at the cost of his sons.

  45. 871 BC – 866 BC
    Elijah announces drought
    1 Kings 17:1–7· narrative

    c. 871–866 BC – Ahab's reign: Elijah announces a drought and is fed at the Cherith Best estimate

    Elijah tells Ahab there will be no rain except at his word. God sends him to hide by the Brook of Cherith, where ravens feed him, until it dries up.

  46. 871 BC – 870 BC
    Asa's illness and death
    2 Chronicles 16:11–14· history

    c. 870 BC – Asa dies in his forty-first year Traditional date

    In his thirty-ninth year Asa's feet become diseased, and he seeks only physicians and not the LORD. He dies in his forty-first year and is buried in a tomb he had prepared.

  47. 870 BC – 866 BC
    Elijah and the widow of Zarephath
    1 Kings 17:8–16· narrative

    c. 870–866 BC – during the drought in Ahab's reign: Elijah and the widow of Zarephath Best estimate

    God sends Elijah to a widow in Zarephath, who has almost no food. She feeds him, and her flour and oil do not run out.

  48. 870 BC – 866 BC
    Elijah raises the widow's son
    1 Kings 17:17–24· miracle

    c. 870–866 BC – during the drought: Elijah raises the widow's son at Zarephath Best estimate

    The widow's son dies, and Elijah prays over him until his life returns. The woman says she now knows Elijah is a man of God.

  49. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Elijah sent to Ahab; famine grips Samaria
    1 Kings 18:1–6· narrative

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    In the third year of drought God tells Elijah to go to Ahab and promises rain. Meanwhile Ahab and his palace manager Obadiah, who had secretly saved a hundred prophets, split up to search for grass.

  50. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Elijah persuades fearful Obadiah
    1 Kings 18:7–16· dialogue

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    Elijah meets Obadiah and tells him to announce him to Ahab. Obadiah fears Ahab will kill him if Elijah vanishes again, but Elijah swears he will appear that day, so Obadiah goes.

  51. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Ahab and Elijah trade accusations
    1 Kings 18:17–20· dialogue

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    Ahab calls Elijah the troubler of Israel; Elijah replies that Ahab and his family are the real cause because they abandoned God's commands for the Baals. He demands a gathering on Mount Carmel, and Ahab complies.

  52. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Elijah challenges Israel to choose
    1 Kings 18:21–24· speech

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    Elijah asks the silent crowd how long they will waver between the LORD and Baal, then proposes a test: each side prepares a bull, and the God who answers by fire is God.

  53. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Prophets of Baal call in vain
    1 Kings 18:25–29· narrative

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    Baal's prophets go first, calling on him from morning until evening, dancing and cutting themselves. Elijah mocks them, but there is no answer.

  54. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Elijah rebuilds the altar and drenches it
    1 Kings 18:30–35· narrative

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    Elijah repairs the LORD's fallen altar with twelve stones for the twelve tribes, digs a trench, and has water poured over the offering three times until the trench is full, making the coming fire clearly miraculous.

  55. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Fire falls and the people confess
    1 Kings 18:36–40· miracle

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    Elijah prays briefly that the LORD would show he is God, and fire falls and consumes everything. The people declare the LORD is God, and Elijah has Baal's prophets seized and killed at the Kishon Valley.

  56. 870 BC – 860 BC
    Rain returns after Elijah prays
    1 Kings 18:41–46· narrative

    c. 870–860 BC – Ahab king of Israel (874–853); Elijah, the long drought and Carmel (Jezebel of Tyre's Baal worship) Best estimate

    Elijah tells Ahab a heavy rain is coming, prays on Carmel, and sends his servant to watch the sea seven times until a small cloud appears. The rain arrives and Elijah runs ahead of Ahab's chariot to Jezreel.

  57. 870 BC – 855 BC
    Jezebel threatens; Elijah flees and despairs
    1 Kings 19:1–4· narrative

    c. 865–860 BC – Elijah's flight and call of Elisha, in Ahab's reign Best estimate

    Ahab reports everything to Jezebel, who vows to kill Elijah within a day. Elijah runs to Beersheba, leaves his servant, and sits under a broom tree praying to die.

  58. 870 BC – 855 BC
    An angel feeds Elijah
    1 Kings 19:5–8· narrative

    c. 865–860 BC – Elijah's flight and call of Elisha, in Ahab's reign Best estimate

    An angel twice wakes Elijah and gives him bread and water. Strengthened, he travels forty days and nights to Horeb, the mountain of God.

  59. 870 BC – 855 BC
    God's quiet voice at Horeb
    1 Kings 19:9–14· dialogue

    c. 865–860 BC – Elijah's flight and call of Elisha, in Ahab's reign Best estimate

    God asks what Elijah is doing there, and Elijah says he alone remains faithful. God's presence is not in wind, earthquake, or fire but in a still, small voice, and Elijah repeats the same complaint.

  60. 870 BC – 855 BC
    God gives Elijah new tasks
    1 Kings 19:15–18· speech

    c. 865–860 BC – Elijah's flight and call of Elisha, in Ahab's reign Best estimate

    God sends Elijah to anoint Hazael, Jehu, and Elisha, and says judgment will follow. He also reveals that seven thousand in Israel have not bowed to Baal, so Elijah is not alone.
