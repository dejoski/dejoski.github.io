  1. 2000 BC – 1000 BC
    Genealogy from Abraham to David
    Matthew 1:1–6· genealogy

    c. 2000–1000 BC – Jesus' ancestry from Abraham to King David (traditional dates for the patriarchs and David) Traditional date

    Matthew opens his Gospel by tracing Jesus' family line from Abraham through Judah to King David. It presents Jesus as heir to the promises made to both men, and it names several women, including Tamar, Rahab and Ruth.

  2. 1010 BC – 586 BC
    Genealogy through the kings to the exile
    Matthew 1:7–11· genealogy

    c. 1010–586 BC – the line of Judah's kings from David and Solomon to the Babylonian exile Well established

    The list continues down the royal line from Solomon to Josiah and his sons, ending at the time Judah was carried off to Babylon.

  3. 586 BC – 6 BC
    Genealogy after the exile to Jesus
    Matthew 1:12–17· genealogy

    586–c. 6 BC – from the exile in Babylon through the post-exilic period down to Jesus' birth Traditional date

    The line continues from Jeconiah after the exile through Zerubbabel down to Joseph, husband of Mary, of whom Jesus was born. Matthew sums it up as three sets of fourteen generations.

  4. 7 BC – 5 BC
    A childless, righteous priestly couple
    Luke 1:5–7· narrative

    c. 7–5 BC – 'in the days of Herod king of Judea' (Herod died 4 BC); priest Zechariah and Elizabeth Dated in the text

    Zechariah, a priest, and his wife Elizabeth are introduced as faithful people who have no children and are now elderly.

  5. 7 BC – 5 BC
    Zechariah meets an angel at the altar
    Luke 1:8–12· narrative

    c. 7–5 BC – Herod's reign: Gabriel appears to Zechariah during his division's temple service Traditional date

    Chosen by lot to burn incense in the temple, Zechariah is startled by an angel standing beside the altar while the people pray outside.

  6. 7 BC – 5 BC
    The angel promises a son named John
    Luke 1:13–17· speech

    c. 7–5 BC – Herod's reign: Gabriel appears to Zechariah during his division's temple service Traditional date

    The angel says Zechariah's prayer is heard: Elizabeth will bear a son, John, who will be filled with the Holy Spirit and will prepare Israel for the Lord in the spirit of Elijah.

  7. 7 BC – 5 BC
    Zechariah doubts and is silenced
    Luke 1:18–25· dialogue

    c. 7–5 BC – Herod's reign: Gabriel appears to Zechariah during his division's temple service Traditional date

    Zechariah asks for proof because of their age, and Gabriel makes him unable to speak until the birth. Zechariah returns home, and Elizabeth conceives and praises God for removing her disgrace.

  8. 7 BC – 5 BC
    Gabriel announces Jesus to Mary
    Luke 1:26–38· dialogue

    c. 7–5 BC – Herod's reign: Gabriel's announcement to Mary in Nazareth, six months after Elizabeth's Traditional date

    Gabriel tells the young virgin Mary she will conceive a son named Jesus, who will be called Son of the Most High and reign on David's throne forever. Mary asks how, is told the Holy Spirit will come upon her, and accepts.

  9. 7 BC – 5 BC
    Mary visits Elizabeth
    Luke 1:39–45· narrative

    c. 7–5 BC – Herod's reign: Mary visits Elizabeth in Judea's hill country Traditional date

    Mary hurries to Elizabeth, whose baby leaps at her greeting. Filled with the Holy Spirit, Elizabeth blesses Mary and calls her the mother of her Lord.

  10. 7 BC – 5 BC
    Mary's song of praise
    Luke 1:46–56· praise

    c. 7–5 BC – Herod's reign: Mary visits Elizabeth in Judea's hill country Traditional date

    Mary praises God for looking on her humble state and celebrates how He has shown mercy, humbled the proud, lifted the lowly, and kept His promise to Abraham. She then stays about three months and returns home.

  11. 6 BC – 5 BC
    Joseph learns of Mary's pregnancy
    Matthew 1:18–21· narrative

    c. 6–5 BC – before Jesus' birth, late in the reign of Herod the Great (Herod died 4 BC) Best estimate

    Mary, engaged to Joseph, is found to be pregnant through the Holy Spirit. Joseph plans to end the engagement quietly, but an angel tells him in a dream to take Mary as his wife and name the child Jesus, who will save his people from their sins.

  12. 6 BC – 5 BC
    Fulfillment of Immanuel and Joseph's obedience
    Matthew 1:22–25· narrative

    c. 6–5 BC – before Jesus' birth, late in the reign of Herod the Great (Herod died 4 BC) Best estimate

    Matthew says these events fulfilled a prophecy that a virgin would bear a son called Immanuel, 'God with us.' Joseph obeys the angel, takes Mary as his wife, and names the child Jesus after he is born.

  13. 6 BC – 5 BC
    Magi ask Herod about the newborn king
    Matthew 2:1–6· narrative

    c. 6–5 BC – Magi reach Jerusalem and ask Herod the Great about a newborn king (Herod d. 4 BC) Best estimate

    Magi from the east arrive in Jerusalem asking where the newborn King of the Jews is, because they saw his star. Herod, troubled, asks the chief priests and scribes, who point to Bethlehem based on the prophet's words.

  14. 6 BC – 4 BC
    The Magi find and worship Jesus
    Matthew 2:7–12· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – the Magi find Jesus at Bethlehem; Herod the Great still reigning Best estimate

    Herod secretly sends the Magi to Bethlehem, asking them to report back. They follow the star, find the child with Mary, worship him and give gifts, then return home by another route after a warning in a dream.

  15. 6 BC – 4 BC
    John is born and named
    Luke 1:57–66· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – Herod's reign: John born about nine months after Zechariah's vision Traditional date

    Elizabeth gives birth, and at the circumcision she insists the boy be called John. Zechariah confirms it in writing, his speech returns, and the neighbors wonder what the child will become.

  16. 6 BC – 4 BC
    Zechariah's prophecy over Israel and John
    Luke 1:67–79· prophecy

    c. 6–4 BC – Herod's reign: John born about nine months after Zechariah's vision Traditional date

    Filled with the Spirit, Zechariah blesses God for raising up salvation from David's house as promised to Abraham. He then tells John he will prepare the way of the Lord and bring knowledge of forgiveness.

  17. 6 BC – 4 BC
    Jesus is born in Bethlehem
    Luke 2:1–7· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – Jesus born at Bethlehem during a Roman census (Herod the Great d. 4 BC; dating debated) Traditional date

    A Roman census sends Joseph and the expectant Mary from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the city of David. There she gives birth to her firstborn son and lays Him in a manger.

  18. 6 BC – 4 BC
    Angels announce the birth to shepherds
    Luke 2:8–14· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – angels and shepherds near Bethlehem on the night of Jesus' birth Traditional date

    An angel tells terrified shepherds that a Savior, Christ the Lord, has been born and that they will find Him in a manger. A crowd of angels then praises God.

  19. 6 BC – 4 BC
    The shepherds find the child
    Luke 2:15–20· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – angels and shepherds near Bethlehem on the night of Jesus' birth Traditional date

    The shepherds go to Bethlehem, find the baby as told, and spread the message, while Mary quietly treasures these things. They return praising God.

  20. 6 BC – 4 BC
    Jesus is named and presented to the Lord
    Luke 2:21–24· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – Jesus circumcised on the eighth day, presented in the Jerusalem temple at forty days Traditional date

    At eight days Jesus is circumcised and named as the angel directed. Later His parents bring Him to Jerusalem to be presented and to offer the sacrifice the Law requires.

  21. 6 BC – 4 BC
    Simeon blesses the child
    Luke 2:25–35· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – Simeon and Anna meet the infant Jesus in the temple Traditional date

    Simeon, told by the Spirit he would see the Lord's Christ, takes Jesus in his arms and praises God for salvation for all peoples. He warns Mary that the child will divide many and that a sword will pierce her own soul.

  22. 6 BC – 4 BC
    Anna speaks about the child
    Luke 2:36–40· narrative

    c. 6–4 BC – Simeon and Anna meet the infant Jesus in the temple Traditional date

    The elderly prophetess Anna gives thanks and tells everyone awaiting redemption about the child. The family then returns to Nazareth, where Jesus grows in wisdom.

  23. 5 BC – 4 BC
    Flight to Egypt
    Matthew 2:13–15· narrative

    c. 5–4 BC – flight to Egypt; Herod the Great's killing of Bethlehem's boys before his death in 4 BC Best estimate

    An angel warns Joseph in a dream that Herod will search for the child to kill him. Joseph takes Mary and Jesus by night to Egypt, where they stay until Herod dies, which Matthew links to 'Out of Egypt I called My Son.'

  24. 5 BC – 4 BC
    Herod kills the boys of Bethlehem
    Matthew 2:16–18· narrative

    c. 5–4 BC – flight to Egypt; Herod the Great's killing of Bethlehem's boys before his death in 4 BC Best estimate

    Furious at being outwitted, Herod orders the killing of all boys in Bethlehem aged two and under. Matthew ties the grief of that moment to Jeremiah's words about Rachel weeping for her children.

  25. 5 BC – AD 28
    John grows up in the wilderness
    Luke 1:80· narrative

    c. 5 BC – AD 28 – John's hidden years until his public appearance (Luke 3:1) Traditional date

    A single verse sums up John's childhood: he grew strong in spirit and lived in the wilderness until he appeared publicly to Israel.

  26. 4 BC
    Return from Egypt to Nazareth
    Matthew 2:19–23· narrative

    c. 4 BC – after Herod the Great's death; Archelaus rules Judea, so the family settles in Nazareth Traditional date

    After Herod dies, an angel tells Joseph in a dream to go back to Israel. Fearing Herod's son Archelaus, Joseph is warned in a dream and settles the family in Nazareth in Galilee.

  27. AD 6 – AD 8
    The boy Jesus in the temple
    Luke 2:41–52· narrative

    c. AD 6–8 – Jesus at twelve visits Jerusalem for Passover (age twelve, if born c. 6–4 BC) Best estimate

    Jesus stays behind in Jerusalem, and His parents find Him after three days among the teachers. He says he had to be in His Father's house, though they do not understand, and He returns home and grows.

  28. AD 27 – AD 29
    John the Baptist preaches in the wilderness
    Matthew 3:1–6· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – John the Baptist preaches by the Jordan (Luke: Tiberius' 15th year) Traditional date

    John the Baptist appears in the wilderness calling people to repent because the kingdom of heaven is near. Matthew links him to Isaiah's 'voice in the wilderness,' and crowds come to confess their sins and be baptized in the Jordan.

  29. AD 27 – AD 29
    John warns the Pharisees and Sadducees
    Matthew 3:7–12· speech

    c. AD 27–29 – John the Baptist preaches by the Jordan (Luke: Tiberius' 15th year) Traditional date

    John sharply challenges the Pharisees and Sadducees to show real repentance and not rely on being descended from Abraham. He warns of coming judgment and announces a more powerful One who will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire.

  30. AD 27 – AD 29
    Jesus is baptized
    Matthew 3:13–17· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – Jesus' baptism by John, at the start of his ministry Traditional date

    Jesus comes from Galilee to be baptized by John, who hesitates but consents when Jesus says it fits God's purposes. As Jesus comes up from the water, the Spirit descends like a dove and a voice from heaven calls him the beloved Son.

  31. AD 27 – AD 29
    Jesus is tempted in the wilderness
    Matthew 4:1–11· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – Jesus tempted in the wilderness, right after his baptism Traditional date

    After fasting forty days, Jesus is tempted by the devil to turn stones to bread, to throw himself from the temple, and to worship the devil for the world's kingdoms. Each time Jesus answers with Scripture, and the devil leaves.

  32. AD 27 – AD 29
    Opening and the promised messenger
    Mark 1:1–3· prophecy

    c. AD 27–29 – John the Baptist preaches in the Judean wilderness (Luke 3:1 dates his start to Tiberius' 15th year, AD 28–29) Traditional date

    Mark opens by naming his book the gospel of Jesus Christ and quoting prophecy about a messenger who will prepare the Lord's way in the wilderness.

  33. AD 27 – AD 29
    John baptizes and points ahead
    Mark 1:4–8· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – John the Baptist preaches in the Judean wilderness (Luke 3:1 dates his start to Tiberius' 15th year, AD 28–29) Traditional date

    John the Baptist preaches repentance and baptizes crowds in the Jordan, living simply in the wilderness. He announces that a more powerful one is coming who will baptize with the Holy Spirit.

  34. AD 27 – AD 29
    Jesus is baptized and named Son
    Mark 1:9–11· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – John baptizes Jesus in the Jordan, under Pontius Pilate (prefect AD 26–36) and Herod Antipas Traditional date

    Jesus comes from Nazareth and is baptized by John. The Spirit descends like a dove and a voice from heaven calls him the beloved Son.

  35. AD 27 – AD 29
    Tempted in the wilderness
    Mark 1:12–13· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – just after his baptism, forty days of testing in the Judean wilderness Traditional date

    The Spirit sends Jesus into the wilderness, where he spends forty days being tempted by Satan, with wild animals around and angels serving him.

  36. AD 27 – AD 29
    John's witness and the Light's reception
    John 1:6–13· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – John the Baptist's ministry begins (Luke dates it to Tiberius's 15th year, AD 28–29) Traditional date

    A man named John is sent to testify about the true Light. The Light enters the world but is not recognized or received by many, though those who receive Him become children of God.

  37. AD 27 – AD 29
    John denies being the Christ
    John 1:19–28· dialogue

    c. AD 27–29 – at Bethany beyond the Jordan, John questioned by priests and Levites from Jerusalem Traditional date

    Priests, Levites and Pharisees from Jerusalem question John. He says he is not the Christ, Elijah or the Prophet, calls himself a voice from Isaiah, and points to One coming after him.

  38. AD 27 – AD 29
    Behold the Lamb of God
    John 1:29–34· speech

    c. AD 27–29 – the day after: John sees Jesus and names him the Lamb of God Traditional date

    Seeing Jesus approach, John calls Him the Lamb of God who takes away the world's sin. He describes seeing the Spirit descend on Him and testifies that He is the Son of God.

  39. AD 27 – AD 29
    First disciples follow Jesus
    John 1:35–42· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – next day: Andrew and another follow Jesus Traditional date

    Two of John's disciples, one being Andrew, follow Jesus and spend the day with Him. Andrew brings his brother Simon, whom Jesus renames Cephas, meaning Peter.

  40. AD 27 – AD 29
    Philip and Nathanael are called
    John 1:43–51· narrative

    c. AD 27–29 – the following day: Jesus calls Philip and Nathanael Traditional date

    Jesus calls Philip, who brings Nathanael. Nathanael doubts Nazareth but then confesses Jesus as Son of God and King of Israel, and Jesus promises he will see greater things.

  41. AD 27 – AD 28
    Water turned to wine at Cana
    John 2:1–11· miracle

    c. AD 27–28 – early in Jesus' ministry: first sign at Cana in Galilee Traditional date

    At a wedding in Cana the wine runs out. At His mother's prompting, Jesus has servants fill jars with water, which becomes fine wine. John calls this the first sign, revealing His glory.

  42. AD 27 – AD 28
    Jesus clears the temple
    John 2:12–22· narrative

    Passover, c. AD 27–28 – first Passover of Jesus' ministry; temple 'built forty-six years' (from Herod's start c. 20 BC) Traditional date

    At Passover Jesus drives merchants and money changers from the temple courts. Asked for a sign, He speaks of raising the temple in three days, which John explains refers to His body.

  43. AD 27 – AD 28
    Jesus knows what is in people
    John 2:23–25· narrative

    Passover, c. AD 27–28 – people believe in Jesus at the feast in Jerusalem Traditional date

    Many believe because of the signs, but Jesus does not entrust Himself to them because He knows human nature without needing anyone to tell Him.

  44. AD 27 – AD 28
    Nicodemus and the new birth
    John 3:1–8· dialogue

    c. AD 27–28 – at night in Jerusalem, Nicodemus comes to Jesus Traditional date

    Nicodemus, a Pharisee and Jewish leader, visits Jesus by night. Jesus says one must be born again, or born of water and Spirit, and compares the Spirit's working to the wind.

  45. AD 27 – AD 28
    Earthly and heavenly things
    John 3:9–15· teaching

    c. AD 27–28 – at night in Jerusalem, Nicodemus comes to Jesus Traditional date

    Puzzled, Nicodemus asks how this can be. Jesus rebukes his lack of understanding, speaks of testifying to what He has seen, and says the Son of Man must be lifted up like the serpent in the wilderness.

  46. AD 27 – AD 28
    God so loved the world
    John 3:16–21· teaching

    c. AD 27–28 – at night in Jerusalem, Nicodemus comes to Jesus Traditional date

    Jesus (or John's reflection on His words) says God gave His Son so that believers would have eternal life, that He came to save rather than condemn, and that people avoid the Light because of their deeds.

  47. AD 27 – AD 29
    John: he must increase
    John 3:22–30· narrative

    c. AD 28–29 – Jesus baptizes in Judea while John, not yet jailed, baptizes at Aenon Best estimate

    Jesus and His disciples baptize in Judea while John also baptizes nearby. John's disciples worry that everyone is going to Jesus, and John replies that he is only the bridegroom's friend and Jesus must increase.

  48. AD 27 – AD 29
    The One from above
    John 3:31–36· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – Jesus baptizes in Judea while John, not yet jailed, baptizes at Aenon Best estimate

    Speaking of the One who comes from heaven, the speaker says He testifies to what He has seen, speaks God's words with the Spirit without limit, and those who believe in the Son have eternal life.

  49. AD 28 – AD 29
    Jesus moves to Capernaum and begins preaching
    Matthew 4:12–17· narrative

    c. AD 28–29 – after John's arrest, Jesus begins preaching and calling disciples in Galilee Best estimate

    Hearing that John was imprisoned, Jesus withdraws to Galilee and settles in Capernaum. Matthew sees Isaiah's words about a great light for Galilee fulfilled, and Jesus begins preaching the same message John did: repent, for the kingdom of heaven is near.

  50. AD 28 – AD 29
    Jesus calls the first four disciples
    Matthew 4:18–22· narrative

    c. AD 28–29 – after John's arrest, Jesus begins preaching and calling disciples in Galilee Best estimate

    Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus calls two pairs of fishermen brothers, Simon Peter and Andrew, then James and John. They immediately leave their nets, boat and family to follow him.

  51. AD 28 – AD 29
    Jesus teaches and heals across Galilee
    Matthew 4:23–25· narrative

    c. AD 28–29 – after John's arrest, Jesus begins preaching and calling disciples in Galilee Best estimate

    Jesus travels throughout Galilee teaching in synagogues, proclaiming the good news of the kingdom and healing many kinds of sickness. His fame spreads and large crowds from many regions follow him.

  52. AD 28 – AD 29
    The Beatitudes
    Matthew 5:1–12· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus opens his teaching by declaring who is blessed: the poor in spirit, mourners, the meek, the merciful, the pure in heart, peacemakers and the persecuted. He tells his followers to rejoice, since prophets before them were also persecuted.

  53. AD 28 – AD 29
    Salt and light
    Matthew 5:13–16· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus calls his followers the salt of the earth and the light of the world. Their good deeds should be visible so that others will glorify the Father in heaven.

  54. AD 28 – AD 29
    Jesus and the Law and the Prophets
    Matthew 5:17–20· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus says he has come not to abolish the Law and the Prophets but to fulfill them. He says the Law's commands still matter and that his followers' righteousness must exceed that of the scribes and Pharisees.

  55. AD 28 – AD 29
    On anger and reconciliation
    Matthew 5:21–26· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Quoting the command against murder, Jesus says anger and insults toward a brother also bring judgment. He urges settling disputes quickly, even before offering a gift at the altar.

  56. AD 28 – AD 29
    On lust and adultery
    Matthew 5:27–30· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus says that looking at a woman with lust is already adultery in the heart. He uses vivid exaggeration, about gouging out an eye or cutting off a hand, to stress how seriously sin should be dealt with.

  57. AD 28 – AD 29
    On divorce
    Matthew 5:31–32· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus recalls the rule about giving a certificate of divorce and says that divorcing a wife, except for sexual immorality, leads to adultery. It is a strict standard meant to protect the woman's position.

  58. AD 28 – AD 29
    On oaths
    Matthew 5:33–37· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus tells his followers not to swear by heaven, earth, Jerusalem or their own head. Their plain 'Yes' and 'No' should be enough.

  59. AD 28 – AD 29
    On retaliation
    Matthew 5:38–42· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus cites 'eye for eye' and tells his followers not to resist an evil person by force: turn the other cheek, give the cloak also, go the second mile and give to those who ask.

  60. AD 28 – AD 29
    Love your enemies
    Matthew 5:43–48· teaching

    c. AD 28–29 – the Sermon on the Mount, early in the Galilean ministry (Luke places a parallel sermon later) Best estimate

    Jesus tells his hearers to love their enemies and pray for persecutors, imitating a Father who gives sun and rain to good and bad alike. Loving only those who love you is nothing special, and they are to be perfect as the heavenly Father is perfect.

Events in this era

Big events in the wider world while these passages were happening.

  1. 37 BC – 4 BC
    Herod the Great rules Judea for Rome

    The brilliant, ruthless king of the Christmas story — builder of the Temple mount, and the ruler who tries to kill the newborn Jesus.

  2. 27 BC – AD 14
    Augustus is emperor of the Roman world

    Jesus is born in Augustus's empire, under the census Luke describes; Roman peace and Roman roads later carry the Gospel.

  3. AD 30 – AD 33
    Jesus is crucified under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate

    Dated to about AD 30 or 33 — the event at the centre of the Christian faith, carried out by Rome's standard punishment for rebels.
