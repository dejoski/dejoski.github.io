  1. Noah's words about his sons
    Genesis 9:24–27 · prophecy

    Waking, Noah curses Canaan, Ham's son, as a servant to his brothers, and blesses the Lord, the God of Shem. He asks that Japheth's territory expand and that Canaan serve them.

    Sin and the fall
  2. Descendants of Japheth
    Genesis 10:1–5 · genealogy

    The chapter begins a record of Noah's three sons' descendants, listing Japheth's sons and grandsons, from whom maritime peoples spread into their territories.

  3. Descendants of Ham
    Genesis 10:6–20 · genealogy

    Ham's sons are listed: Cush, Mizraim, Put, and Canaan. Nimrod, a mighty hunter, builds kingdoms in Shinar and Assyria, and the Canaanite peoples and their borders are described.

  4. Descendants of Shem and the nations
    Genesis 10:21–32 · genealogy

    Shem's sons and grandsons are listed, including the line of Eber and his son Peleg. The chapter closes noting that from Noah's sons the nations spread across the earth.

  5. The Lord confuses the language at Babel
    Genesis 11:5–9 · narrative

    The Lord comes down, sees the project, and confuses their language. The people are scattered across the earth and stop building, and the place is named Babel.

    Justice and judgmentPride and humility
  6. Lot's daughters and the origin of Moab and Ammon
    Genesis 19:30–38 · narrative · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Living in a cave, Lot's daughters fear they will have no children. They get their father drunk and each conceive a son, Moab and Ben-ammi, ancestors of the Moabites and Ammonites.

    Sin and the fallFamily and marriage
  7. God renews the name Israel and the promise
    Genesis 35:9–15 · speech · 1900 BC – 1895 BC

    God appears again to Jacob at Bethel, confirming his new name, Israel, and telling him that a nation and kings will come from him and that the land promised to Abraham and Isaac will be his descendants'.

    PromiseCovenant
  8. Esau's wives, sons, and move to Seir
    Genesis 36:1–8 · genealogy · 1900 BC – 1880 BC

    This account of Esau (Edom) lists his wives and the sons born to him in Canaan. His possessions grow too large to share land with Jacob, so he settles in the hill country of Seir.

    Family and marriage
  9. Esau's descendants and chiefs
    Genesis 36:9–19 · genealogy · 1900 BC – 1700 BC

    This section lists Esau's sons and grandsons, who became chiefs in the land of Edom.

    Family and marriage
  10. The Horite families of Seir
    Genesis 36:20–30 · genealogy · 1900 BC – 1700 BC

    The passage lists the sons and chiefs of Seir the Horite, the people already living in the land before Esau's family settled among them.

  11. Kings and chiefs of Edom
    Genesis 36:31–43 · list · 1800 BC – 1050 BC

    The chapter closes by listing the kings who ruled Edom before Israel had a king, followed by a list of the chiefs of Edom by clan and territory.

    Kings and kingship
  12. Respect for elders, foreigners, and honest measures
    Leviticus 19:32–37 · law · 1445 BC

    The Israelites are to honor the elderly, treat foreigners as native-born and love them as themselves, and use honest weights and measures. The LORD reminds them he brought them out of Egypt.

    Loving othersJustice for the poor and oppressed
  13. Be holy and different from the nations
    Leviticus 20:22–27 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The Israelites are to keep all the LORD's statutes so the land does not vomit them out. They are to be holy because he has set them apart. Mediums and spiritists are to be put to death.

    HolinessThe promised land
  14. A second-month Passover, open to foreigners
    Numbers 9:9–14 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD allows those unclean or traveling to keep the Passover a month later. Those who neglect it while clean are cut off, and foreigners may join under the same rules.

    Sabbath and feastsMercy and compassion
  15. Moses asks Hobab to guide them
    Numbers 10:29–32 · dialogue · 1445 BC

    Moses invites his brother-in-law Hobab to come with Israel and share in the LORD's good promises. Hobab first refuses, and Moses presses him to stay.

    Promise
  16. Edom refuses Israel passage
    Numbers 20:14–21 · dialogue · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses sends messengers asking the king of Edom for safe passage along the King's Highway, promising not to take water or crops. Edom refuses twice and threatens war, so Israel turns away.

  17. Balak of Moab summons Balaam
    Numbers 22:1–8 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel camps by the Jordan. Alarmed by their numbers, Balak king of Moab sends elders with fees to Balaam, asking him to curse Israel. Balaam asks them to stay overnight while he seeks the LORD's answer.

    Wealth and poverty
  18. Short oracles on other nations
    Numbers 24:20–25 · oracle of judgment · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balaam briefly pronounces the fate of Amalek, the Kenites and the far-off sea peoples, foretelling destruction. Balaam and Balak then go their separate ways.

    Justice and judgment
  19. Passing by Edom
    Deuteronomy 2:1–8 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Moses recalls that after years wandering near Mount Seir, God told Israel to pass peacefully through the land of their relatives, Esau's descendants, and to pay for food and water.

    God's sovereigntyThe promised land
  20. Passing by Moab; the old generation dies
    Deuteronomy 2:9–15 · narrative · 1407 BC

    God tells Israel not to provoke Moab, the descendants of Lot. A note explains earlier peoples of the area, and the crossing of the Zered marks thirty-eight years since Kadesh, when the old generation had died.

    God's sovereigntyDeath and mortality
  21. Passing by Ammon and the order to fight Sihon
    Deuteronomy 2:16–25 · narrative · 1407 BC

    With the fighting men dead, God tells Israel to respect Ammon's land, then to cross the Arnon and begin taking the land of Sihon, the Amorite king, promising that the nations will fear Israel.

    God's sovereigntyWar and conflict
  22. The defeat of Sihon king of Heshbon
    Deuteronomy 2:26–37 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Moses offered Sihon peaceful passage, but Sihon refused. God gave him and his army to Israel at Jahaz, and Israel captured all his cities while keeping away from Ammon's land.

    God's sovereigntyWar and conflict
  23. The defeat of Og king of Bashan
    Deuteronomy 3:1–11 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Israel defeated Og, king of Bashan, and captured his sixty fortified cities after God told Moses not to fear him. A note mentions Og's huge iron bed as evidence he was among the last of the Rephaim.

    War and conflictGod's sovereignty
  24. Obey the law and show wisdom to the nations
    Deuteronomy 4:1–8 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses calls Israel to listen and keep the law without adding or subtracting, recalling how those who followed Baal of Peor died. Keeping the law will show other nations Israel's wisdom and the nearness of God.

    Law and commandmentsWisdomObedience
  25. No treaties or intermarriage with the nations
    Deuteronomy 7:1–6 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    When God brings Israel into the land and drives out seven nations, Israel must not make treaties or intermarry, because this would lead them to other gods. They are to tear down the nations' altars and idols, as they are God's holy people.

    IdolatryFamily and marriage
  26. Do not fear the nations; avoid their idols
    Deuteronomy 7:17–26 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses tells Israel not to fear stronger nations, since God defeated Egypt and will drive them out gradually. They must burn the idols and not take the silver and gold on them.

    FaithIdolatry
  27. Who may enter the assembly
    Deuteronomy 23:1–8 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses lists those excluded from the assembly of the LORD, including Ammonites and Moabites, who did not help Israel and hired Balaam. Israel is not to despise Edomites or Egyptians, whose descendants may enter after three generations.

    Worship and praiseHoliness
  28. Blessings for obedience
    Deuteronomy 28:1–6 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses promises that if Israel obeys the LORD, he will set them above the nations. Blessings are listed on city and country, children, crops, livestock, and daily comings and goings.

    ObediencePromise
  29. Victory, prosperity, and a name before the nations
    Deuteronomy 28:7–14 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The LORD will defeat Israel's enemies, bless their barns and work, and make them his holy people. Other nations will see and stand in awe, provided Israel does not turn to other gods.

    PromiseWork and restElection and calling
  30. A fierce nation and the horrors of siege
    Deuteronomy 28:49–57 · oracle of judgment · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    A distant nation with an unknown language will swoop down like an eagle and besiege Israel's cities. The famine will be so severe that people will resort to eating their own children.

    War and conflictSufferingJustice and judgment
