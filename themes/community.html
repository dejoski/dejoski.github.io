Community and friendship

25 passages · Jesus and the church

Ruth · 21 Samuel · 6Job · 1Psalms · 1Ecclesiastes · 1Acts · 2Romans · 21 Corinthians · 12 Corinthians · 1Galatians · 2Philippians · 11 Thessalonians · 12 Timothy · 1Philemon · 11 John · 13 John · 1
  1. Naomi's plea; Orpah leaves, Ruth clings
    Ruth 1:11–15 · dialogue · 1125 BC – 1100 BC

    Naomi argues that she has no more sons to offer them and that the LORD's hand has gone against her. Orpah kisses her goodbye and turns back, but Ruth clings to Naomi.

    FaithfulnessFamily and marriage
  2. Ruth's vow of loyalty
    Ruth 1:16–18 · speech · 1125 BC – 1100 BC

    Ruth refuses to leave, pledging to go where Naomi goes, adopt her people and God, and stay with her even in death. Seeing her determination, Naomi stops arguing.

    FaithfulnessFaith
  3. Jonathan befriends David
    1 Samuel 18:1–5 · narrative · 1020 BC – 1015 BC

    Jonathan loves David as himself and makes a covenant with him, giving him his robe, sword, bow and belt. David prospers as Saul sets him over the men of war.

    CovenantLove
  4. Jonathan defends David to Saul
    1 Samuel 19:1–7 · narrative · 1018 BC – 1012 BC

    Saul orders Jonathan and his servants to kill David, but Jonathan warns David and speaks up for him. Saul swears David will not be killed, and David returns to court.

    Loving others
  5. David asks Jonathan for help
    1 Samuel 20:1–10 · dialogue · 1017 BC – 1012 BC

    David asks Jonathan what he has done wrong, since Saul wants him dead. Jonathan doubts it, but David proposes a test at the New Moon feast.

    Opposition and persecution
  6. Jonathan and David renew their covenant
    1 Samuel 20:11–23 · dialogue · 1017 BC – 1012 BC

    In the field, Jonathan promises to tell David the truth about Saul's mood and asks David to show loyal love to his family. They agree on a signal with three arrows and a boy.

    Covenant
  7. Jonathan signals; the friends part
    1 Samuel 20:35–42 · narrative · 1017 BC – 1012 BC

    Jonathan shoots arrows beyond the boy, telling David to flee. After sending the boy away, the two weep, and Jonathan sends David off in peace.

    LoveCovenant
  8. Jonathan strengthens David's hand in God
    1 Samuel 23:14–18 · narrative · 1015 BC – 1011 BC

    David stays in the wilderness of Ziph, where Saul keeps looking for him. Jonathan comes and encourages David, saying he will be king, and they make a covenant.

    FaithCovenant
  9. Three friends arrive and sit in silence
    Job 2:11–13 · narrative · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar hear of Job's trouble and come to comfort him. They barely recognize him, weep, mourn, and sit with him seven days without speaking.

    Suffering
  10. The blessing of unity
    Psalms 133:1–3 · wisdom · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    David says how good and pleasant it is when God's people live together in unity, comparing it to fragrant oil on Aaron's head and to dew from Hermon falling on Zion, where the LORD commands blessing.

    Unity
  11. The lonely worker and the value of companions
    Ecclesiastes 4:7–12 · wisdom

    He describes a man with no family who works endlessly and never feels he has enough. He then praises companionship, since two can help, warm and defend each other.

    Work and restUnity
  12. Life together in the first church
    Acts 2:42–47 · narrative · AD 30

    The new believers devote themselves to the apostles' teaching, fellowship, shared meals and prayer. They share their possessions with those in need, worship in the temple and gain favor with the people as more join.

    Generosity and givingWorship and praiseThe church
  13. Believers share everything, including Barnabas
    Acts 4:32–37 · narrative · AD 30 – AD 32

    The believers are united and share their possessions so no one is in need, while the apostles preach the resurrection with power. Barnabas is highlighted as one who sold a field and gave the money to the apostles.

    Generosity and givingUnity
  14. Greetings to many friends
    Romans 16:3–16 · list · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul sends personal greetings to a long list of named people in Rome, including Priscilla and Aquila, and notes their labors, sufferings, and service. He asks them to greet one another with a holy kiss.

    The church
  15. Greetings from Paul's companions
    Romans 16:21–24 · list · AD 56 – AD 57

    Timothy and other companions, including Tertius who wrote down the letter, send their greetings to the Romans.

    The church
  16. Greetings and closing blessing
    1 Corinthians 16:19–24 · letter · AD 54 – AD 55

    Paul passes on greetings from the churches in Asia, Aquila and Prisca, and the brothers, adds a greeting in his own hand, and ends with a call for the Lord to come and blessings of grace and love.

    The churchLove
  17. Titus brings comfort in Macedonia
    2 Corinthians 7:5–7 · narrative · AD 55 – AD 56

    Paul says that on reaching Macedonia he had conflict outside and fear within. God comforted him through Titus's arrival and his report of the Corinthians' longing and zeal.

    Joy and thanksgiving
  18. Personal plea: remember our friendship
    Galatians 4:12–20 · exhortation · AD 48 – AD 55

    Paul reminds them how warmly they welcomed him when he first preached to them in weakness, and asks if he has become their enemy by telling the truth. He is in agony until Christ is formed in them.

    Loving others
  19. Restore gently and carry each other's burdens
    Galatians 6:1–5 · exhortation · AD 48 – AD 55

    Paul tells spiritual believers to restore anyone caught in a sin gently, watching themselves, and to carry each other's burdens, which fulfills the law of Christ. Each should test their own work.

    Loving othersPride and humility
  20. Thankful for their partnership
    Philippians 1:3–8 · letter · AD 50 – AD 62

    Paul thanks God for the Philippians' partnership in the gospel from the start, confident God will complete what He began in them. He tells them how much he longs for them.

    Gospel and missionJoy and thanksgiving
  21. Community life and short commands
    1 Thessalonians 5:12–22 · exhortation · AD 50 – AD 51

    Paul asks them to respect their leaders, live in peace and care for the unruly, fainthearted and weak. He then gives quick commands: rejoice always, pray continually, give thanks in everything, test all things, and hold to what is good.

    The churchLeadershipJoy and thanksgiving
  22. Final greetings and farewell
    2 Timothy 4:19–22 · letter · AD 64 – AD 67

    Paul sends greetings to Prisca, Aquila, and Onesiphorus's household, gives news of Erastus and Trophimus, and asks Timothy again to come before winter. He closes with greetings from friends and a blessing.

    The church
  23. Greeting to Philemon and the house church
    Philemon 1:1–3 · letter · AD 60 – AD 62

    Paul and Timothy greet Philemon, two co-workers, and the church that meets in Philemon's home, wishing them grace and peace from God and Jesus.

    The church
  24. God is light: walk in the light
    1 John 1:5–7 · teaching · AD 85 – AD 95

    The message is that God is light with no darkness. Claiming fellowship with Him while walking in darkness is a lie, but walking in the light brings fellowship and the cleansing of Jesus' blood.

    Who God isSin and the fall
  25. Closing: hope to visit soon
    3 John 1:13–14 · letter · AD 85 – AD 95

    The elder says he has more to write but hopes to speak face to face soon, then sends peace and greetings from friends and asks Gaius to greet friends there by name.
