Wealth and poverty

  1. Abram and Lot outgrow the land
    Genesis 13:1–7 · narrative · 2088 BC – 2085 BC

    Abram returns from Egypt very wealthy and worships at his old altar. He and Lot both have so many possessions that the land cannot support them, and their herdsmen quarrel.

    Worship and praise
  2. Abram lets Lot choose
    Genesis 13:8–13 · dialogue · 2088 BC – 2085 BC

    Abram proposes peaceful separation and offers Lot first choice. Lot picks the well-watered Jordan plain and settles near Sodom, whose people are wicked.

    Peace and reconciliationGenerosity and giving
  3. Abram refuses the king of Sodom's reward
    Genesis 14:21–24 · dialogue · 2085 BC – 2082 BC

    The king of Sodom offers Abram the goods in exchange for the people. Abram, swearing by God Most High, refuses to take anything so no one can say he made Abram rich.

    Faith
  4. Ephron and Abraham bargain
    Genesis 23:10–16 · dialogue · 2030 BC – 2028 BC

    Ephron publicly offers the field and cave as a gift, but Abraham insists on paying. Ephron names four hundred shekels, and Abraham weighs out the silver.

    The promised land
  5. Isaac prospers and digs wells
    Genesis 26:12–22 · narrative · 1985 BC – 1965 BC

    Isaac farms, grows very rich, and the Philistines become envious and block his father's wells. After moving and reopening the wells, he moves on until he digs one that is not disputed.

    Faithfulness
  6. Jacob asks to leave; Laban bargains
    Genesis 30:25–36 · dialogue · 1915 BC – 1909 BC

    After Joseph's birth, Jacob asks permission to go home. Laban wants him to stay, and Jacob proposes that his wages be the speckled and dark animals. Laban agrees, then removes those animals and moves them away.

    Work and rest
  7. Jacob's flocks grow
    Genesis 30:37–43 · narrative · 1915 BC – 1909 BC

    Jacob uses peeled branches at the troughs so that the flocks bear streaked and spotted young, and he takes the stronger animals for himself. He becomes very wealthy.

    God's sovereignty
  8. Famine: Joseph buys Egypt for Pharaoh
    Genesis 47:13–26 · narrative · 1876 BC – 1872 BC

    As famine drains Egypt's money, Joseph takes livestock, then land and people, in exchange for food. Pharaoh gains ownership, and a fifth of produce is set aside for him.

    Leadership
  9. Burnt offering of birds
    Leviticus 1:14–17 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A poorer worshiper may bring a turtledove or young pigeon. The priest prepares it at the altar and burns it, and the text again calls it a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

    Atonement and sacrificeWorship and praise
  10. Offerings for those who cannot afford a lamb
    Leviticus 5:7–13 · law · 1445 BC

    A person too poor for a lamb may bring two birds, one for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering. If even that is too much, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour, without oil or frankincense, is accepted.

    Atonement and sacrificeMercy and compassion
  11. The offering for purification
    Leviticus 12:6–8 · law · 1445 BC

    When the days are complete, the woman brings a lamb and a bird to the priest, who offers them for her cleansing. If she cannot afford a lamb, she brings two birds instead.

    Atonement and sacrificeMercy and compassion
  12. Offerings for the poor
    Leviticus 14:21–32 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A person who cannot afford all these offerings brings one lamb, less flour, and two birds. The ritual is the same, and the passage concludes it is the law for someone who cannot afford full cleansing.

    Atonement and sacrificeMercy and compassion
  13. Fair dealing and trust in provision
    Leviticus 25:14–22 · law · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Land sales must be priced by the number of harvests remaining until Jubilee, and no one may cheat a neighbor. God promises a triple harvest in the sixth year for those who obey.

    FaithJustice and judgment
  14. Balak of Moab summons Balaam
    Numbers 22:1–8 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel camps by the Jordan. Alarmed by their numbers, Balak king of Moab sends elders with fees to Balaam, asking him to curse Israel. Balaam asks them to stay overnight while he seeks the LORD's answer.

    The nations
  15. A second delegation and God's permission
    Numbers 22:15–21 · dialogue · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balak sends more distinguished princes with promises of great honor. Balaam says no amount of wealth can make him disobey God, but asks them to wait, and God then lets him go if he speaks only what God says.

    Obedience
  16. Dividing the plunder
    Numbers 31:25–47 · law · 1406 BC

    God instructs that the plunder be counted and split between the soldiers and the rest of the congregation, with a tribute to the priests and Levites. The chapter lists the animals and people in each share.

    War and conflict
  17. Reuben and Gad ask for land east of the Jordan
    Numbers 32:1–5 · dialogue · 1406 BC

    The tribes of Reuben and Gad, with large herds, see that Jazer and Gilead are good grazing land. They ask Moses and the leaders to give them the land as their possession rather than making them cross the Jordan.

    The promised land
  18. Cancel debts every seventh year
    Deuteronomy 15:1–6 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Every seventh year creditors must cancel loans made to fellow Israelites, though foreigners can still be charged. God promises that obedience will leave no poor among them.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedForgiveness
  19. Runaway slaves, interest, vows, and neighbors' crops
    Deuteronomy 23:15–25 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses gives rules against returning a fugitive slave, shrine prostitution, and charging interest to a fellow Israelite. He also says vows must be kept and neighbors' fields may be eaten from but not carried away.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedWords and speech
  20. Protecting newlyweds and a debtor's livelihood
    Deuteronomy 24:5–6 · law · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    A newly married man is exempt from war and other duties for a year to bring joy to his wife. Lenders may not take millstones as security, since that would take away a family's means of making bread.

    Family and marriage
  21. Honest weights and measures
    Deuteronomy 25:13–16 · law · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Israelites must not keep two sets of weights or measures, one heavy and one light. Honest dealing is tied to long life in the land, and dishonesty is detestable to the LORD.

    Justice and judgmentLaw and commandments
  22. Samson's riddle at the wedding feast
    Judges 14:10–14 · dialogue · 1072 BC – 1060 BC

    At the seven-day wedding feast, Samson bets thirty companions thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothes that they cannot solve his riddle. They fail to answer for three days.

    Words and speech
  23. Samuel warns what a king will take
    1 Samuel 8:10–18 · speech · 1052 BC – 1050 BC

    Samuel lists the costs of kingship: sons drafted, daughters and fields taken, a tenth of harvests and flocks claimed, and the people made servants. He says they will cry out but God will not answer.

    Kings and kingship
  24. Samuel dies; Nabal and Abigail introduced
    1 Samuel 25:1–3 · narrative · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Samuel dies and Israel mourns him. David moves to the Wilderness of Paran, where a wealthy man named Nabal lives; he is harsh, while his wife Abigail is intelligent and beautiful.

    Death and mortality
  25. David asks Nabal for provisions
    1 Samuel 25:4–8 · speech · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Hearing that Nabal is shearing sheep, David sends ten young men to greet him. They are to say David's men protected Nabal's shepherds and to ask for whatever he can spare at feast time.

    Words and speech
  26. Ziba brings supplies and accuses Mephibosheth
    2 Samuel 16:1–4 · dialogue · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Ziba brings donkeys and food and claims Mephibosheth is staying in Jerusalem hoping to regain the kingdom. David gives Ziba all Mephibosheth's property.

    Sin and the fallLeadership
  27. Mephibosheth's account
    2 Samuel 19:24–30 · dialogue · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Mephibosheth, unwashed and mourning, says Ziba deceived him. David declares that he and Ziba divide the land, and Mephibosheth says Ziba may take all.

    Justice and judgmentLeadership
  28. Temple offerings and the fleet
    1 Kings 9:25–28 · narrative · 946 BC – 931 BC

    Solomon offers sacrifices three times a year and builds a fleet at Ezion-geber. Hiram's sailors help bring gold from Ophir.

    Worship and praise
  29. The queen of Sheba visits Solomon
    1 Kings 10:1–13 · narrative · 946 BC – 935 BC

    The queen of Sheba tests Solomon with hard questions and sees his wisdom and wealth. She praises the LORD, gives gifts and returns home.

    WisdomThe nations
  30. Solomon's gold, shields and throne
    1 Kings 10:14–22 · history · 946 BC – 931 BC

    Solomon receives great amounts of gold each year and makes gold shields, an ivory and gold throne, and gold drinking vessels.

    Kings and kingship
