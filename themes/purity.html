Purity and sexual ethics

  1. Angels at Lot's house and the mob
    Genesis 19:1–11 · narrative · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Two angels arrive at Sodom and Lot insists they stay with him. The men of the city surround the house demanding the visitors, and Lot offers his daughters instead. The angels pull him in and strike the crowd with blindness.

    Angels and spiritual beingsSin and the fall
  2. Shechem seizes Dinah and asks to marry her
    Genesis 34:1–12 · narrative · 1907 BC – 1900 BC

    Shechem assaults Dinah, then wants her as his wife. His father Hamor asks Jacob's family to intermarry and settle among them, and Shechem offers any price.

    Sin and the fallFamily and marriage
  3. Potiphar's wife accuses Joseph
    Genesis 39:7–20 · narrative · 1890 BC – 1889 BC

    Potiphar's wife repeatedly urges Joseph to sleep with her, and he refuses, saying it would be a sin against God. When she grabs his cloak, he flees, and she uses the cloak to accuse him falsely, so he is imprisoned.

    Temptation and spiritual warfare
  4. Seduction and serious offenses
    Exodus 22:16–20 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    A man who seduces an unengaged virgin must pay the bridal price. The passage also names sorcery, bestiality, and sacrificing to other gods as capital offenses.

    Law and commandments
  5. Uncleanness after childbirth
    Leviticus 12:1–5 · law · 1445 BC

    A woman who bears a son is unclean for seven days and then remains in purification for thirty-three more. The son is circumcised on the eighth day. After a daughter, the periods are fourteen days and sixty-six days.

    HolinessFamily and marriage
  6. Examining a possible skin disease
    Leviticus 13:1–8 · law · 1445 BC

    A person with a swelling, rash, or bright spot is brought to the priest, who examines the skin and hair. If unclear, he isolates the person for seven days, then re-examines to declare the person clean or unclean.

    HolinessPriesthood
  7. Chronic disease and raw flesh
    Leviticus 13:9–17 · law · 1445 BC

    A white swelling with white hair and raw flesh marks a chronic condition, and the priest declares the person unclean. If the disease covers the whole body and turns white, the person is declared clean.

    Holiness
  8. Skin problems after a boil
    Leviticus 13:18–23 · law · 1445 BC

    If a white swelling or reddish-white spot appears where a boil healed, the priest checks it. If it is deeper than the skin with white hair or is spreading, it is unclean, but a stable spot is only a scar.

    Holiness
  9. Skin problems after a burn
    Leviticus 13:24–28 · law · 1445 BC

    When a burn turns reddish-white or white, the priest examines it in the same way. White hair and depth mean it is unclean, and a spot that does not spread is only a scar from the burn.

    Holiness
  10. Outbreaks on the head or beard
    Leviticus 13:29–37 · law · 1445 BC

    An outbreak on the head or beard is judged by whether it is deeper than the skin and has thin yellow hair. The person shaves except the affected area and is isolated, and the priest checks after seven days.

    Holiness
  11. Harmless rashes and baldness
    Leviticus 13:38–44 · law · 1445 BC

    Dull white spots on the skin are a harmless rash and the person is clean. A bald man is also clean, but a reddish-white sore on a bald head marks an infectious disease.

    Holiness
  12. Living apart while unclean
    Leviticus 13:45–46 · law · 1445 BC

    A person with the infectious disease wears torn clothes, lets his hair hang loose, covers his lower face, and calls out 'Unclean!' He lives alone outside the camp as long as the disease lasts.

    HolinessSuffering
  13. Mildew in clothing and leather
    Leviticus 13:47–52 · law · 1445 BC

    Green or red marks in wool, linen, or leather are shown to the priest and the item is isolated for seven days. If the mark has spread, it is a persistent mildew and the item is burned.

    Holiness
  14. When the mildew fades or returns
    Leviticus 13:53–59 · law · 1445 BC

    If the mark has not spread, the item is washed and isolated a further seven days. If it fades, the spot is cut out, and if it reappears the item is burned, while a clean item is washed again and declared clean.

    Holiness
  15. Cleansing a person healed of skin disease
    Leviticus 14:1–9 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A priest examines the healed person outside the camp. Two birds, cedar wood, scarlet yarn, and hyssop are used: one bird is killed over water, and the live bird is dipped in the blood and set free. The person then washes and shaves, and waits seven days.

    Atonement and sacrificeHolinessPriesthood
  16. Mildew in a house
    Leviticus 14:33–47 · law · 1445 BC

    When the Israelites are in Canaan and the LORD puts mildew in a house, the owner tells the priest. The priest has the house emptied and inspected, and closes it for seven days. Contaminated stones and plaster are removed, and a house where it keeps spreading is torn down.

    HolinessThe promised land
  17. Cleansing a house after mildew
    Leviticus 14:48–53 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    If the mildew does not return after the house is re-plastered, the priest declares it clean. He purifies it with the same ritual of two birds, cedar, scarlet yarn, and hyssop.

    Atonement and sacrificeHoliness
  18. Unclean discharges in men
    Leviticus 15:1–15 · law · 1445 BC

    A man with a bodily discharge is unclean, and so are his bed, seat, and anything he touches. People who touch these must wash and stay unclean until evening. After healing he waits seven days and brings two birds on the eighth day.

    Holiness
  19. Emission of semen
    Leviticus 15:16–18 · law · 1445 BC

    A man who has an emission of semen bathes and is unclean until evening. The same applies to clothing it touches, and to a couple who lie together.

    HolinessFamily and marriage
  20. A woman's monthly period
    Leviticus 15:19–24 · law · 1445 BC

    A woman is unclean for seven days during her menstrual period, and so is anything she lies or sits on. Anyone touching those things must wash and stays unclean until evening.

    Holiness
  21. Long-lasting discharge of blood in women
    Leviticus 15:25–30 · law · 1445 BC

    A woman with a discharge of blood beyond her usual period is unclean, along with what she lies on or sits on. After it ends she waits seven days and on the eighth brings two birds for a sin and a burnt offering.

    HolinessAtonement and sacrifice
  22. Forbidden sexual relations with relatives
    Leviticus 18:6–18 · law · 1445 BC

    This is a list of forbidden sexual relationships within the family, such as with a parent, sibling, grandchild, aunt, daughter-in-law, brother's wife, or both a woman and her daughter.

    Family and marriageLaw and commandments
  23. Other forbidden sexual and religious practices
    Leviticus 18:19–23 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD forbids sex during a woman's period, adultery, giving children to Molech, sex between men, and sex with animals. These acts are said to defile those who do them.

    IdolatryHoliness
  24. The land vomits out its defilers
    Leviticus 18:24–30 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The LORD warns that the nations he is driving out defiled the land through these acts, and the land will vomit out the Israelites if they do the same. They must keep his charge and avoid these customs.

    The promised landJustice and judgment
  25. Penalties for cursing parents and sexual offenses
    Leviticus 20:9–13 · law · 1445 BC

    Cursing one's parents is punishable by death. So are adultery, relations with a father's wife or daughter-in-law, and sex between men.

    Family and marriageJustice and judgment
  26. Further penalties: mother-in-law and animals
    Leviticus 20:14–16 · law · 1445 BC

    A man who marries both a woman and her mother is to be burned with them. Relations with an animal, by a man or a woman, bring death for the person and the animal.

    Justice and judgment
  27. Other forbidden unions
    Leviticus 20:17–21 · law · 1445 BC

    This section covers relations with a sister, a menstruating woman, an aunt, an uncle's wife, or a brother's wife. The penalties include being cut off from the people or remaining childless.

    Family and marriageLaw and commandments
  28. Israel worships Baal of Peor
    Numbers 25:1–5 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    At Shittim, Israelite men engage in sexual immorality with Moabite women and join in sacrifices to their gods. God's anger burns, and He orders that the leaders be executed and the guilty put to death.

    IdolatryWrath
  29. A husband's false accusation
    Deuteronomy 22:13–21 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    If a man accuses his new wife of not being a virgin, her parents present evidence to the elders. If the charge is false, he is fined and cannot divorce her; if true, she is stoned.

    Justice and judgmentFamily and marriage
  30. Laws on adultery and sexual offenses
    Deuteronomy 22:22–30 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses gives laws on adultery, on a betrothed woman assaulted in the city or in the open country, on rape of an unbetrothed woman, and on a man marrying his father's wife.

    Justice and judgmentFamily and marriage
