  1. God calls Abram with a promise
    Genesis 12:1–3 · speech · 2092 BC – 2090 BC

    The Lord tells Abram to leave his country and family for a land he will show him. He promises to make Abram a great nation, bless him, and bless all families of the earth through him.

    PromiseFaith
  2. The Lord decides to look into Sodom
    Genesis 18:16–21 · speech · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    As the men leave, the Lord reflects that he has chosen Abraham to teach his household justice. He says the outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah is great and he will go see for himself.

    Justice and judgmentCovenant
  3. Rebekah's struggling twins are born
    Genesis 25:19–26 · narrative · 2006 BC

    Isaac prays because Rebekah is barren, and she conceives twins who struggle in her womb. God tells her two nations are within her and the older will serve the younger; Esau and Jacob are born.

    PrayerPromise
  4. Jacob wrestles and is renamed Israel
    Genesis 32:24–32 · narrative · 1909 BC – 1908 BC

    Left alone at night, Jacob wrestles with a man until daybreak. He refuses to let go until blessed, is given the name Israel, is left limping, and names the place Peniel, saying he saw God face to face.

    FaithPromise
  5. Jacob adopts Ephraim and Manasseh
    Genesis 48:1–7 · speech · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    Hearing Jacob is ill, Joseph brings his two sons. Jacob recalls God's promise at Luz and declares Ephraim and Manasseh to be his own sons, then remembers Rachel's death.

    PromiseFamily and marriage
  6. Jacob blesses the boys, crossing his hands
    Genesis 48:8–16 · narrative · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    Jacob kisses the boys and blesses them, crossing his hands so his right hand rests on the younger Ephraim. He prays that God, who has shepherded him, will bless them.

    Pride and humility
  7. Moses meets God at the burning bush
    Exodus 3:1–6 · narrative · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    While shepherding, Moses sees a bush burning without being consumed. God calls to him from it, tells him to remove his sandals on holy ground, and identifies himself as the God of Moses' ancestors.

    Dreams, visions and revelationHoliness
  8. God sends Moses to free his people
    Exodus 3:7–12 · dialogue · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    God says he has seen Israel's suffering and will rescue them to a good land, and sends Moses to Pharaoh. Moses asks who he is to go, and God answers that he will be with him.

    Exodus and deliveranceRedemption
  9. Moses objects and Aaron is appointed
    Exodus 4:10–17 · dialogue · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Moses says he is slow of speech, and God replies that he made the mouth and will help him. When Moses asks him to send someone else, God grows angry and appoints his brother Aaron as spokesman.

    Words and speechLeadership
  10. Moses and Aaron sent again to Pharaoh
    Exodus 6:26–30 · narrative · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The text stresses that these are the Moses and Aaron the LORD told to bring Israel out. It repeats the day God told Moses to speak to Pharaoh and Moses' worry that he speaks poorly.

    Leadership
  11. God offers Israel a covenant at Sinai
    Exodus 19:1–8 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    Israel camps before Sinai and the LORD tells Moses to remind them how he carried them on eagles' wings. If they obey his covenant they will be his treasured possession, a kingdom of priests, and they answer that they will do all he says.

    CovenantObedience
  12. The breastpiece of judgment
    Exodus 28:15–30 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God describes a breastpiece with four rows of gemstones for the twelve tribes, attached to the ephod by chains and rings. Aaron will carry the tribes' names over his heart, along with the Urim and Thummim.

    Priesthood
  13. Bezalel and Oholiab called as craftsmen
    Exodus 31:1–11 · other · 1446 BC

    God announces that he has chosen Bezalel of Judah and Oholiab of Dan, filled with his Spirit and skill, to make the tent, furnishings, priestly garments, oil and incense.

    The Holy SpiritWork and rest
  14. Bezalel and Oholiab named
    Exodus 35:30–35 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses tells the people that the LORD has called Bezalel and Oholiab, filled them with skill and given them the ability to teach others, so they can do every kind of craft.

    The Holy SpiritWork and rest
  15. Making the breastpiece
    Exodus 39:8–21 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The workers make the breastpiece with its twelve gems for the tribes, its gold chains and rings, and tie it to the ephod with a blue cord.

    Priesthood
  16. Mediums and being holy
    Leviticus 20:6–8 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The LORD will set his face against anyone who turns to mediums. He calls the people to consecrate themselves and keep his statutes, since he is the one who makes them holy.

    IdolatryHoliness
  17. Aaron's staff blossoms overnight
    Numbers 17:6–9 · miracle · 1445 BC – 1407 BC

    Moses collects twelve staffs and places them in the Tent. Next day Aaron's has sprouted, blossomed and produced almonds, and Moses shows all the staffs to the people.

    Miracles and signsPriesthood
  18. First oracle: Israel cannot be cursed
    Numbers 23:7–12 · oracle of hope · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balaam says Balak asked him to curse Israel, but he cannot curse those whom God has not cursed. He sees a numerous people who live apart, and Balak angrily complains that Balaam has blessed them instead.

    Promise
  19. Why God chose Israel
    Deuteronomy 7:7–11 · teaching · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses says God chose Israel not because they were numerous but because He loved them and kept His oath. God is faithful to those who love Him and repays those who hate Him, so they should obey.

    LoveCovenant
  20. What God asks of Israel
    Deuteronomy 10:12–22 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses asks what God requires: to fear Him, walk in His ways, love and serve Him. He says God owns everything, cares for the fatherless, widow, and foreigner, and Israel should love foreigners too. He urges them to circumcise their hearts.

    LoveObedienceJustice for the poor and oppressed
  21. You are God's people; do not mourn like the nations
    Deuteronomy 14:1–2 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses says Israel are sons of the LORD and holy, so they must not cut themselves or shave their foreheads in mourning for the dead.

    HolinessDeath and mortality
  22. Israel and the LORD affirm the covenant
    Deuteronomy 26:16–19 · exhortation · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses urges Israel to keep the statutes wholeheartedly. Israel has declared the LORD its God and the LORD has declared Israel his treasured people, promising them honor above the nations as a holy people.

    CovenantObedience
  23. Israel is now the LORD's people
    Deuteronomy 27:9–10 · exhortation · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses and the priests call Israel to be silent and listen, since they have become the LORD's people. They are to obey his voice and commands.

    CovenantObedience
  24. Victory, prosperity, and a name before the nations
    Deuteronomy 28:7–14 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The LORD will defeat Israel's enemies, bless their barns and work, and make them his holy people. Other nations will see and stand in awe, provided Israel does not turn to other gods.

    PromiseThe nationsWork and rest
  25. Everyone enters the covenant today
    Deuteronomy 29:9–15 · exhortation · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Every group of Israel, from leaders to children and resident foreigners, stands before the LORD to enter his covenant and oath. It also binds future generations who are not present.

    Covenant
  26. Blessings on Reuben, Judah, and Levi
    Deuteronomy 33:6–11 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses asks that Reuben live and be many, that the LORD hear Judah and help him against enemies, and that Levi keep the Urim and Thummim and teach God's law. Levi is praised for loyalty at Massah and Meribah.

    LeadershipPriesthood
  27. Blessings on Benjamin and Joseph
    Deuteronomy 33:12–17 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Benjamin is called beloved of the LORD, resting securely under God's protection. Joseph is blessed with rich land, dew, harvest, and strength like a wild ox to push nations.

    LoveThe promised land
  28. Blessings on Zebulun, Gad, Dan, Naphtali, Asher
    Deuteronomy 33:18–25 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses blesses Zebulun and Issachar in their work, Gad's fierce strength, Dan as a lion's cub, Naphtali with favor, and Asher with abundance and security.

    The promised landWork and rest
  29. There is none like the God of Israel
    Deuteronomy 33:26–29 · praise · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses closes by praising the God who rides the heavens to help Israel, whose everlasting arms carry them. He declares Israel blessed as a people saved by the LORD, who will drive out their enemies and live in security.

    Who God isSalvationFaithfulness
  30. God recalls Abraham's call and the patriarchs
    Joshua 24:2–4 · speech · 1390 BC – 1385 BC

    Joshua relays God's words: Abraham's ancestors worshiped other gods beyond the Euphrates, but God led Abraham into Canaan and gave him Isaac, Jacob and Esau. Jacob's family then went down to Egypt.

    PromiseIdolatry
