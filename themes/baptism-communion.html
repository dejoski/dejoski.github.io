Baptism and the Lord's Supper

28 passages · Jesus and the church

Matthew · 3Mark · 4Luke · 5John · 2Acts · 7Romans · 11 Corinthians · 4Galatians · 11 Peter · 1
  1. Jesus is baptized
    Matthew 3:13–17 · narrative · AD 27 – AD 29

    Jesus comes from Galilee to be baptized by John, who hesitates but consents when Jesus says it fits God's purposes. As Jesus comes up from the water, the Spirit descends like a dove and a voice from heaven calls him the beloved Son.

    Father, Son and SpiritObedience
  2. The Last Supper
    Matthew 26:26–30 · ritual instruction · AD 30 – AD 33

    During the meal Jesus gives the disciples bread as his body and a cup as his blood of the covenant, poured out for the forgiveness of sins, and says he will not drink again until the kingdom. They sing a hymn and go out.

    Atonement and sacrificeCovenant
  3. The Great Commission
    Matthew 28:16–20 · speech · AD 30 – AD 33

    On a mountain in Galilee the eleven see Jesus; they worship, though some doubt. He says all authority is his and sends them to make disciples of all nations, baptizing and teaching, promising to be with them always.

    Gospel and missionDiscipleshipThe nations
  4. John baptizes and points ahead
    Mark 1:4–8 · narrative · AD 27 – AD 29

    John the Baptist preaches repentance and baptizes crowds in the Jordan, living simply in the wilderness. He announces that a more powerful one is coming who will baptize with the Holy Spirit.

    RepentanceForgiveness
  5. Jesus is baptized and named Son
    Mark 1:9–11 · narrative · AD 27 – AD 29

    Jesus comes from Nazareth and is baptized by John. The Spirit descends like a dove and a voice from heaven calls him the beloved Son.

    Father, Son and SpiritThe Holy Spirit
  6. The Lord's Supper
    Mark 14:22–26 · ritual instruction · AD 30 – AD 33

    Jesus breaks bread and gives the cup, calling them his body and his blood of the covenant poured out for many, and says he will not drink again until the kingdom. They sing a hymn and go out to the Mount of Olives.

    Atonement and sacrificeCovenant
  7. The commission and ascension
    Mark 16:15–20 · speech · AD 30 – AD 33

    Jesus tells the Eleven to preach the gospel to everyone, promising that those who believe and are baptized will be saved and that signs will accompany believers. He is then taken up to heaven, and the disciples preach everywhere.

    Gospel and missionMiracles and signs
  8. John begins preaching in the wilderness
    Luke 3:1–6 · narrative · AD 28 – AD 29

    Luke dates John's ministry by Roman and Jewish rulers and says God's word came to John. John preaches repentance and baptism for forgiveness, as Isaiah foretold.

    RepentanceForgivenessProphecy
  9. The coming One and John's imprisonment
    Luke 3:15–20 · speech · AD 28 – AD 29

    People wonder whether John is the Christ, but he says someone more powerful is coming to baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire. Luke then notes that Herod imprisoned John for rebuking him.

    The Holy SpiritOpposition and persecution
  10. Jesus is baptized
    Luke 3:21–22 · narrative · AD 28 – AD 29

    As Jesus prays after being baptized, heaven opens, the Spirit descends like a dove, and a voice declares Him God's beloved Son.

    Father, Son and SpiritThe Holy Spirit
  11. Fire and division
    Luke 12:49–53 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus says He came to cast fire on the earth and has a baptism to undergo, and that His coming will bring division, even within families.

    The crossOpposition and persecution
  12. The Last Supper
    Luke 22:14–20 · ritual instruction · AD 30

    At the Passover meal Jesus says he has longed to eat it with them before he suffers. He gives them bread as his body and a cup as the new covenant in his blood, telling them to do this in remembrance of him.

    CovenantAtonement and sacrificeSabbath and feasts
  13. John: he must increase
    John 3:22–30 · narrative · AD 27 – AD 29

    Jesus and His disciples baptize in Judea while John also baptizes nearby. John's disciples worry that everyone is going to Jesus, and John replies that he is only the bridegroom's friend and Jesus must increase.

    Pride and humility
  14. Eating flesh and drinking blood
    John 6:52–59 · teaching · AD 29

    The Jews argue about how He can give His flesh to eat. Jesus says that eating His flesh and drinking His blood brings eternal life and abiding in Him, unlike the manna.

    Eternal lifeThe cross
  15. The crowd asks what to do
    Acts 2:37–41 · dialogue · AD 30

    Deeply moved, the listeners ask what they should do. Peter urges them to repent and be baptized, and about three thousand accept his message and are baptized that day.

    RepentanceForgivenessThe Holy Spirit
  16. Ananias restores Saul's sight
    Acts 9:10–19 · narrative · AD 33 – AD 36

    The Lord sends a reluctant disciple, Ananias, to Saul, explaining Saul is His chosen instrument to the Gentiles. Ananias lays hands on him, his sight returns, and he is baptized.

    Election and callingDreams, visions and revelationThe Holy Spirit
  17. The Spirit falls on the Gentiles
    Acts 10:44–48 · narrative · AD 37 – AD 41

    While Peter is still speaking, the Holy Spirit comes on the listeners, who speak in tongues and praise God. Astonished, Peter orders that they be baptized, since they received the Spirit just as the Jewish believers had.

    The Holy SpiritThe nationsUnity
  18. Apollos taught more accurately in Ephesus
    Acts 18:24–28 · narrative · AD 52 – AD 53

    Apollos, an eloquent scholar of Scripture from Alexandria, teaches about Jesus but knows only John's baptism. Priscilla and Aquila explain the way of God more fully, and he goes on to Achaia to help believers and debate publicly.

    Gospel and missionWisdomThe church
  19. Twelve disciples receive the Holy Spirit
    Acts 19:1–7 · narrative · AD 52 – AD 53

    At Ephesus Paul finds about twelve disciples who only knew John's baptism and had not heard of the Holy Spirit. After Paul explains, they are baptized in Jesus's name, and the Spirit comes on them when he lays hands on them.

    The Holy SpiritFaith
  20. Paul's account of meeting Jesus on the road
    Acts 22:6–16 · speech · AD 57

    Paul recounts the bright light near Damascus, the voice of Jesus, and his blindness. He tells how Ananias restored his sight and told him he would be God's witness to everyone and should be baptized.

    Dreams, visions and revelationElection and callingGospel and mission
  21. Sailors' escape attempt and the shared meal
    Acts 27:27–38 · narrative · AD 59

    On the fourteenth night the sailors sense land and try to flee in the lifeboat, but Paul tells the soldiers to stop them. At dawn Paul urges everyone to eat, gives thanks over bread, and the 276 aboard are encouraged.

    FaithLeadershipGod's sovereignty
  22. Shall we keep sinning so grace abounds?
    Romans 6:1–4 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul rejects the idea that believers should continue in sin so grace may increase. He says that in baptism they were united with Christ in his death so they might live a new life.

    GraceSin and the fall
  23. Flee idolatry: the Lord's table
    1 Corinthians 10:14–22 · exhortation · AD 54 – AD 55

    Paul tells them to flee idolatry, pointing out that the bread and cup are a participation in Christ, and that pagan sacrifices involve demons. They cannot share both the Lord's table and the table of demons.

    IdolatryUnity
  24. Divisions at the Lord's Supper
    1 Corinthians 11:17–22 · exhortation · AD 54 – AD 55

    Paul says their gatherings do more harm than good: divisions appear, some eat ahead without sharing, and some go hungry while others get drunk. Paul says this is not really the Lord's Supper.

    UnityWealth and poverty
  25. What Jesus instituted
    1 Corinthians 11:23–26 · teaching · AD 54 – AD 55

    Paul recounts what he received and passed on: on the night He was betrayed, Jesus took bread and the cup, gave them as His body and the new covenant in His blood, and told them to do this in remembrance. Doing so proclaims the Lord's death until He comes.

    CovenantThe cross
  26. Examine yourselves; wait for one another
    1 Corinthians 11:27–34 · exhortation · AD 54 – AD 55

    Paul warns against eating and drinking in an unworthy manner, urging self-examination and recognizing the body. He links present weakness and illness to this and tells them to wait for one another.

    Final judgmentUnity
  27. All one in Christ Jesus
    Galatians 3:26–29 · argument · AD 48 – AD 55

    Through faith, all are sons of God in Christ, baptized into him. In Christ there is no Jew or Greek, slave or free, male and female, and if they belong to Christ they are Abraham's offspring and heirs.

    UnityFaith
  28. Christ's suffering, Noah and baptism
    1 Peter 3:18–22 · teaching · AD 62 – AD 65

    Peter says Christ suffered once for sins to bring us to God, and was made alive in the Spirit, and then compares the flood saving Noah's family with baptism. Christ is now at God's right hand with angels and powers under Him.

    The crossSalvationSuffering
