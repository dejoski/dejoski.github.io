Discipleship

113 passages · Jesus and the church

  1. Elisha leaves his plow to follow
    1 Kings 19:19–21 · narrative · 870 BC – 855 BC

    Elijah finds Elisha plowing and throws his cloak over him. Elisha says goodbye to his family, slaughters his oxen for a farewell meal, and follows Elijah as his attendant.

    Election and calling
  2. Elisha refuses to leave Elijah
    2 Kings 2:1–6 · dialogue · 852 BC

    On the way from Gilgal, Elijah three times asks Elisha to stay behind, and each time Elisha vows not to leave him. Groups of prophets at Bethel and Jericho tell Elisha that Elijah will be taken that day.

    Leadership
  3. Jesus calls the first four disciples
    Matthew 4:18–22 · narrative · AD 28 – AD 29

    Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus calls two pairs of fishermen brothers, Simon Peter and Andrew, then James and John. They immediately leave their nets, boat and family to follow him.

  4. The Beatitudes
    Matthew 5:1–12 · teaching · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus opens his teaching by declaring who is blessed: the poor in spirit, mourners, the meek, the merciful, the pure in heart, peacemakers and the persecuted. He tells his followers to rejoice, since prophets before them were also persecuted.

    Kingdom of God
  5. Salt and light
    Matthew 5:13–16 · teaching · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus calls his followers the salt of the earth and the light of the world. Their good deeds should be visible so that others will glorify the Father in heaven.

    Gospel and mission
  6. Giving in secret
    Matthew 6:1–4 · teaching · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus warns against doing good deeds to be seen by others. Giving to the needy should be done quietly, and the Father who sees in secret will reward it.

    Generosity and givingWorship and praise
  7. Fasting in secret
    Matthew 6:16–18 · teaching · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus tells his followers not to make a show of fasting. They should look normal outwardly, and the Father who sees in secret will reward them.

    Worship and praiseLament and fasting
  8. The narrow and wide gates
    Matthew 7:13–14 · teaching · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus contrasts a wide gate and broad road that lead to destruction with a small gate and narrow way that lead to life. Few find the second.

    Salvation
  9. Two builders and the crowd's reaction
    Matthew 7:24–29 · parable · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus compares those who hear and obey his words to a wise man building on rock, and those who hear and do not obey to a fool building on sand. When he finishes, the crowds are amazed because he teaches with authority, unlike their scribes.

    ObedienceParables
  10. The cost of following Jesus
    Matthew 8:18–22 · dialogue · AD 28 – AD 29

    A scribe says he will follow Jesus anywhere, and Jesus says the Son of Man has no place to lay his head. Another disciple wants to bury his father first, and Jesus tells him to follow him now.

  11. Matthew's call and dinner with sinners
    Matthew 9:9–13 · narrative · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus calls Matthew, a tax collector, to follow him, and Matthew does. At dinner with tax collectors and sinners, Jesus answers the Pharisees' complaint by saying he has come to call sinners, not the righteous, and quotes 'I desire mercy, not sacrifice.'

    Mercy and compassion
  12. Question about fasting
    Matthew 9:14–17 · dialogue · AD 28 – AD 29

    John's disciples ask why Jesus' disciples do not fast. Jesus says guests do not mourn while the bridegroom is present, and that new wine needs new wineskins, suggesting something new has begun.

    Lament and fastingJoy and thanksgiving
  13. The twelve apostles named
    Matthew 10:1–4 · list · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus gives his twelve disciples authority over unclean spirits and disease, and Matthew lists their names, ending with Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him.

    Gospel and mission
  14. Warning of persecution
    Matthew 10:16–23 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus warns that he sends them like sheep among wolves, and they will be handed over to councils and brought before governors and kings. They are told the Spirit will speak through them, and to endure to the end or flee to the next town.

    Opposition and persecution
  15. Jesus brings division
    Matthew 10:34–39 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus says he has come with a sword, not peace, and that loyalty to him may divide households, using words from the prophet Micah. Loving family more than him, or refusing to take up a cross, makes one unworthy of him, and losing one's life for him means finding it.

    Family and marriage
  16. Whoever receives you receives me
    Matthew 10:40–42 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus tells the twelve that welcoming them is welcoming him and the Father who sent him. Even a cup of cold water given to a disciple will not lose its reward.

    Gospel and mission
  17. Jesus' true family
    Matthew 12:46–50 · narrative · AD 29

    When told his mother and brothers are outside, Jesus points to his disciples and says they are his mother and brothers. Whoever does the will of his Father in heaven belongs to his family.

    Family and marriage
  18. Beware the yeast of the Pharisees
    Matthew 16:5–12 · dialogue · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus warns the disciples to beware of the yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees, and they think he is talking about bread. He reminds them of the two feedings, and they realize he meant those leaders' teaching.

    False teaching
  19. The cost of following Jesus
    Matthew 16:24–28 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus says anyone who wants to follow him must deny himself and take up his cross, and that losing one's life for him means finding it. He asks what profit there is in gaining the world but losing one's soul, and says the Son of Man will come in glory.

    The cross
  20. Who is greatest? Become like a child
    Matthew 18:1–5 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    The disciples ask who is greatest in the kingdom, and Jesus sets a child among them. He says they must change and become like children, and that whoever humbles himself is greatest and welcoming such a child welcomes him.

    Pride and humilityKingdom of God
  21. Warning against causing little ones to stumble
    Matthew 18:6–11 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus warns that causing one of his little ones to stumble is so grave that a millstone around the neck would be better. He uses hard sayings about cutting off a hand or eye to show how far to go to avoid sin, and says not to despise the little ones.

    Sin and the fall
  22. Jesus blesses the little children
    Matthew 19:13–15 · narrative · AD 30

    People bring children for Jesus to bless, and the disciples rebuke them. Jesus tells them to let the children come, because the kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these, and he lays his hands on them.

    Kingdom of God
  23. Reward for leaving everything
    Matthew 19:27–30 · dialogue · AD 30

    Peter says the disciples have left everything and asks what they will get. Jesus promises that they will sit on twelve thrones in the renewal of all things and that all who leave much for him will receive a hundredfold and eternal life, adding that many who are first will be last.

    Eternal life
  24. Request for places, and the call to serve
    Matthew 20:20–28 · dialogue · AD 30 – AD 33

    The mother of James and John asks for the top places for her sons. Jesus says they will share his cup but the places are not his to grant, then teaches that greatness means serving, as he came to give his life as a ransom.

    Pride and humilityLeadership
  25. No one knows the day; be ready
    Matthew 24:36–44 · teaching · AD 30 – AD 33

    Jesus says only the Father knows the day or hour. He compares the coming to the days of Noah and to a thief in the night, and tells his followers to keep watch and be ready.

    The end timesHope and endurance
  26. The faithful and the wicked servant
    Matthew 24:45–51 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    Jesus contrasts a faithful servant, who cares for the household while the master is away, with a wicked one who abuses others and is caught unprepared. The first is rewarded and the second is severely punished.

    Final judgmentThe end times
  27. Parable of the ten virgins
    Matthew 25:1–13 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    Ten young women wait for a bridegroom; five wise ones bring extra oil and five foolish ones do not. When he arrives at midnight the unready are shut out. Jesus ends with the call to keep watch.

    ParablesThe end times
  28. Parable of the talents
    Matthew 25:14–30 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    A master entrusts money to three servants before traveling. Two double it and are praised, but the third buries his and is condemned as lazy and afraid. The master takes it away and casts him out.

    ParablesFinal judgment
  29. Jesus predicts the disciples' desertion
    Matthew 26:31–35 · dialogue · AD 30 – AD 33

    On the way to the Mount of Olives Jesus says all will fall away, quoting a prophecy of the scattered sheep, and that he will go ahead to Galilee. Peter insists he never will, but Jesus says he will deny him three times.

    FaithfulnessTemptation and spiritual warfare
  30. The Great Commission
    Matthew 28:16–20 · speech · AD 30 – AD 33

    On a mountain in Galilee the eleven see Jesus; they worship, though some doubt. He says all authority is his and sends them to make disciples of all nations, baptizing and teaching, promising to be with them always.

    Gospel and missionBaptism and the Lord's SupperThe nations
