  1. Cain and Abel's offerings
    Genesis 4:1–7 · narrative

    Eve bears Cain and Abel. Both bring offerings, and God favors Abel's but not Cain's. God warns the angry Cain that sin is crouching at his door but that he can master it.

    Sin and the fall
  2. Seth is born; people call on the Lord
    Genesis 4:25–26 · narrative

    Adam and Eve have another son, Seth, in place of Abel. Seth has a son Enosh, and at that time people begin to call on the name of the Lord.

    Family and marriage
  3. Noah's offering and God's promise
    Genesis 8:20–22 · narrative

    Noah builds an altar and offers burnt offerings. The Lord is pleased and resolves never again to curse the ground or destroy all life, as long as the seasons of the earth continue.

    Atonement and sacrificePromise
  4. Abram travels through Canaan
    Genesis 12:4–9 · narrative · 2091 BC – 2090 BC

    Abram, seventy-five, leaves with Sarai and Lot. At Shechem the Lord promises the land to his offspring, and Abram builds altars there and near Bethel.

    PromiseThe promised land
  5. Abram and Lot outgrow the land
    Genesis 13:1–7 · narrative · 2088 BC – 2085 BC

    Abram returns from Egypt very wealthy and worships at his old altar. He and Lot both have so many possessions that the land cannot support them, and their herdsmen quarrel.

    Wealth and poverty
  6. Melchizedek blesses Abram
    Genesis 14:17–20 · narrative · 2085 BC – 2082 BC

    Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, brings bread and wine and blesses Abram. Abram gives him a tenth of everything.

    Priesthood
  7. Abraham and Abimelech's treaty at Beersheba
    Genesis 21:22–34 · narrative · 2063 BC – 2050 BC

    Abimelech asks Abraham to swear fair dealing. After settling a dispute over a well, they make a covenant with seven ewe lambs as witness, and the place is named Beersheba.

    Covenant
  8. God appears and Abimelech makes a treaty
    Genesis 26:23–33 · narrative · 1975 BC – 1960 BC

    At Beersheba God reassures Isaac, who builds an altar. Abimelech comes seeking a peace agreement, having seen that God is with Isaac, and they swear an oath and share a feast.

    Peace and reconciliationCovenant
  9. Jacob names Bethel and makes a vow
    Genesis 28:16–22 · narrative · 1929 BC

    Jacob wakes in awe, sets up the stone as a pillar, and names the place Bethel. He vows that if God stays with him and brings him home safely, the Lord will be his God and he will give a tenth.

    PromiseFaith
  10. Return to Bethel
    Genesis 35:1–8 · narrative · 1900 BC – 1895 BC

    God tells Jacob to go to Bethel and build an altar. Jacob has his household remove their foreign gods, and at Bethel he builds an altar; Rebekah's nurse Deborah dies there.

    IdolatryObedience
  11. Aaron joins Moses and the people believe
    Exodus 4:27–31 · narrative · 1446 BC

    Aaron meets Moses at the mountain, and Moses tells him everything. They gather Israel's elders, perform the signs, and the people believe and bow in worship.

    Faith
  12. Moses and Israel sing of victory
    Exodus 15:1–10 · praise · 1446 BC

    In song, Israel praises the LORD as strength, salvation, and warrior. They celebrate how he threw Pharaoh's horse and rider into the sea and how the enemy's boast ended.

    SalvationWar and conflict
  13. Miriam leads the women in song
    Exodus 15:19–21 · praise · 1446 BC

    The narrator summarizes the crossing again. Miriam the prophetess takes up a tambourine and leads the women in dancing and singing the refrain of the song.

  14. Jethro visits and praises the LORD
    Exodus 18:1–12 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Moses' father-in-law Jethro brings Moses' wife and sons to him. Moses tells him all the LORD has done, and Jethro rejoices, says the LORD is greater than all gods, and offers sacrifices.

    Exodus and deliverance
  15. Rules for altars and worship
    Exodus 20:22–26 · law · 1446 BC

    God reminds Israel he has spoken from heaven, and forbids gods of silver or gold. He tells them to build altars of earth or uncut stone and to avoid steps up to the altar.

    Idolatry
  16. The table for the bread
    Exodus 25:23–30 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God describes a gold-covered table with rings and poles, along with gold plates and bowls. The Bread of the Presence is to be kept on it before God at all times.

    Temple and tabernacle
  17. The golden lampstand
    Exodus 25:31–40 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God details a lampstand of hammered gold with six branches, almond-blossom cups and seven lamps, made from one talent of gold. He reminds Moses to follow the pattern shown on the mountain.

    Temple and tabernacle
  18. Daily offerings and God dwelling among them
    Exodus 29:38–46 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God commands two lambs to be offered daily, morning and twilight, with flour, oil and wine. He promises to meet the people there, consecrate the place by his glory and dwell among them as their God who brought them out of Egypt.

    PromiseTemple and tabernacle
  19. The altar of incense
    Exodus 30:1–10 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God describes a small gold-covered altar to be placed before the veil, where Aaron will burn incense every morning and twilight. No unauthorized offerings may be made on it, and once a year atonement is made on its horns.

    Temple and tabernaclePrayer
  20. The holy incense
    Exodus 30:34–38 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God gives a formula for fragrant incense of four spices with salt, to be placed before the Testimony. It is holy and must not be copied for personal enjoyment.

    Holiness
  21. The tent outside the camp
    Exodus 33:7–11 · narrative · 1446 BC

    Moses regularly pitches a tent of meeting outside the camp. When he enters, the pillar of cloud descends and the LORD speaks with him face to face like a friend, while the people watch and worship.

    PrayerLeadership
  22. The LORD introduces offerings to Israel
    Leviticus 1:1–2 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD calls to Moses from the Tent of Meeting and tells him to instruct the Israelites about bringing animal offerings from the herd or flock.

    Atonement and sacrificeTemple and tabernacle
  23. Burnt offering from the herd
    Leviticus 1:3–9 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    An unblemished bull is brought to the entrance, the worshiper lays a hand on it, and it is slaughtered. The priests splash the blood on the altar and burn the whole animal as a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

    Atonement and sacrifice
  24. Burnt offering from the flock
    Leviticus 1:10–13 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    If the worshiper brings a sheep or goat instead, it is also an unblemished male, killed on the north side of the altar and burned whole in much the same way.

    Atonement and sacrifice
  25. Burnt offering of birds
    Leviticus 1:14–17 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A poorer worshiper may bring a turtledove or young pigeon. The priest prepares it at the altar and burns it, and the text again calls it a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

    Atonement and sacrificeWealth and poverty
  26. Grain offering of fine flour
    Leviticus 2:1–3 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A grain offering is made of fine flour with oil and frankincense. The priest burns a handful as a memorial portion, and the rest goes to Aaron and his sons as a most holy share.

    Priesthood
  27. Baked, griddle, and pan grain offerings
    Leviticus 2:4–10 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    Grain offerings may be baked in an oven, cooked on a griddle, or made in a pan, always unleavened and with oil. The priest burns the memorial portion and keeps the remainder.

    Priesthood
  28. No leaven or honey, but always salt
    Leviticus 2:11–13 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    Grain offerings must not contain leaven or honey when burned on the altar, though such items may be brought as firstfruits. Every grain offering is to be seasoned with the salt of the covenant.

    Covenant
  29. Grain offering of firstfruits
    Leviticus 2:14–16 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    An offering of the first new grain is to be crushed, roasted heads of grain with oil and frankincense. The priest burns a memorial portion of it to the LORD.

    Sabbath and feasts
  30. Peace offering from the herd
    Leviticus 3:1–5 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A male or female animal from the herd, without blemish, is presented as a peace offering. The worshiper lays a hand on it, and the priests burn its fat, kidneys, and liver lobe on the altar.

    Atonement and sacrifice
