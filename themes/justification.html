Justification and righteousness

33 passages · Salvation

Genesis · 3Psalms · 3Isaiah · 2Hosea · 1Habakkuk · 1Zechariah · 1Luke · 1Acts · 1Romans · 111 Corinthians · 1Galatians · 2Philippians · 2Titus · 1Hebrews · 1James · 11 John · 1
  1. Noah's righteousness and the coming end
    Genesis 6:9–13 · narrative

    Noah is described as righteous and blameless, walking with God, with three sons. The earth is corrupt and violent, and God tells Noah he will destroy it.

    Faith
  2. God's promise of offspring and Abram's faith
    Genesis 15:1–6 · dialogue · 2083 BC – 2081 BC

    The Lord tells Abram not to fear and that he is his shield. Abram, still childless, questions this, and God promises a son and offspring as many as the stars. Abram believes, and it is counted as righteousness.

    FaithPromise
  3. Judah acknowledges Tamar's righteousness
    Genesis 38:24–30 · narrative · 1888 BC – 1878 BC

    Told that Tamar is pregnant, Judah orders her burned, but she produces his seal, cord, and staff. He says she is more righteous than he is, and she bears twins, Perez and Zerah.

    RepentanceFamily and marriage
  4. Blessed is the forgiven
    Psalms 32:1–2 · wisdom · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The psalm opens by calling blessed the person whose sins are forgiven and covered, and whose spirit has no deceit.

    Forgiveness
  5. Asking to enter the gates to give thanks
    Psalms 118:19–21 · praise

    The speaker asks for the gates of righteousness to be opened so he can enter the LORD's house and give thanks. He states that only the righteous enter and thanks the LORD for answering him.

    Worship and praiseTemple and tabernacle
  6. Hear my prayer; my spirit faints
    Psalms 143:1–4 · prayer · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    David asks the LORD to listen in His faithfulness and not to judge him, since no living person is righteous before Him. The enemy has crushed him, and his spirit is faint and his heart appalled.

    PrayerFaithfulness
  7. The LORD's will and the Servant's reward
    Isaiah 53:10–12 · poetry · 700 BC – 538 BC

    It was the LORD's will to crush him and make his life a guilt offering, yet he will see offspring, prolong his days and justify many. God says he will give him a portion with the great because he poured out his life and bore the sin of many.

    Atonement and sacrificeResurrection
  8. Clothed in salvation
    Isaiah 61:10–11 · praise · 700 BC – 500 BC

    The speaker rejoices in the LORD, who has clothed him in garments of salvation like a bridegroom or bride. As a garden makes seeds sprout, God will make righteousness and praise spring up before all nations.

    Joy and thanksgivingSalvation
  9. Sow righteousness; you reaped injustice
    Hosea 10:11–15 · exhortation · 728 BC – 722 BC

    God says Ephraim is a heifer that loves threshing but He will place a yoke on her. He urges them to sow righteousness and seek Him until He rains righteousness, but they plowed wickedness and will see fortresses destroyed and the king cut off.

    Repentance
  10. Waiting for God's answer
    Habakkuk 2:1–5 · speech · 605 BC – 598 BC

    Habakkuk says he will stand watch to see what God says. God tells him to write the vision plainly, promising it will surely come in its appointed time, and that the righteous will live by faith while the proud are never satisfied.

    FaithHope and endurance
  11. Joshua the high priest is cleansed
    Zechariah 3:1–5 · vision · 519 BC

    Zechariah sees the high priest Joshua in filthy clothes with Satan accusing him. The LORD rebukes Satan, has Joshua's filthy garments removed, and dresses him in fine robes and a clean turban.

    ForgivenessPriesthoodDreams, visions and revelation
  12. The Pharisee and the tax collector
    Luke 18:9–14 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    A Pharisee prays thanking God he is better than others, while a tax collector asks only for mercy. Jesus says the tax collector went home justified, because the humble will be exalted.

    ParablesPride and humilityPrayer
  13. Forgiveness through Jesus and a warning
    Acts 13:38–41 · speech · AD 47

    Paul announces that forgiveness of sins and justification for all who believe are offered through Jesus, beyond what the law of Moses could provide. He closes with a warning from the prophets not to scoff.

    ForgivenessFaithLaw and commandments
  14. The gospel reveals God's righteousness
    Romans 1:16–17 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul states the theme of the letter: he is not ashamed of the gospel, which is God's power to save everyone who believes, and it reveals a righteousness received by faith, as Scripture says.

    FaithGospel and missionSalvation
  15. No one is righteous
    Romans 3:9–20 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul concludes that Jews and Gentiles alike are under sin, and strings together Scripture quotations describing human sinfulness. He says the law brings knowledge of sin rather than a way to be justified by works.

    Sin and the fall
  16. Righteousness through faith in Jesus
    Romans 3:21–26 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul says God's righteousness is now shown apart from the law, given through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe. All have sinned, and are justified freely by grace through the redemption in Christ.

    FaithGraceAtonement and sacrifice
  17. Boasting excluded; faith upholds the law
    Romans 3:27–31 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul says boasting is ruled out because justification comes by faith, not works. God is God of Gentiles as well as Jews, and this faith upholds the law rather than abolishing it.

    FaithLaw and commandments
  18. Abraham was justified by faith
    Romans 4:1–8 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul uses Abraham as an example: he was counted righteous because he believed God, not because of his works. Paul adds David's words about the blessing of those whose sins are forgiven.

    FaithForgiveness
  19. Peace with God and hope in suffering
    Romans 5:1–5 · teaching · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul says those justified by faith have peace with God and a hope of sharing his glory. Even suffering produces endurance, character, and hope, because God's love has been poured into their hearts through the Holy Spirit.

    Peace and reconciliationHope and enduranceSuffering
  20. The gift is greater than the trespass
    Romans 5:15–21 · teaching · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul contrasts Adam's trespass, which led to condemnation, with Christ's act of obedience, which brings justification and life. Where sin increased, grace increased all the more.

    GraceEternal life
  21. No condemnation in Christ Jesus
    Romans 8:1–4 · teaching · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul declares that there is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. What the law could not do, God did by sending his Son, so that the law's requirement is fulfilled in those who live by the Spirit.

    FreedomThe Holy SpiritThe incarnation
  22. Israel stumbled over faith
    Romans 9:30–33 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul says Gentiles who did not pursue righteousness obtained it by faith, while Israel, pursuing it by the law, did not attain it because they pursued it by works and stumbled over the stone laid in Zion.

    FaithLaw and commandments
  23. Zeal without knowledge
    Romans 10:1–4 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul says his heart's desire and prayer is for Israel to be saved. They have zeal for God, but by ignoring God's righteousness and seeking their own, they missed that Christ is the end of the law for righteousness for everyone who believes.

    Law and commandmentsSalvation
  24. The word of faith is near
    Romans 10:5–13 · argument · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul contrasts righteousness by the law with righteousness by faith, saying that whoever confesses Jesus as Lord and believes in his resurrection will be saved. This offer is for Jew and Gentile alike, for 'everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.'

    FaithSalvation
  25. You were washed and justified
    1 Corinthians 6:9–11 · teaching · AD 54 – AD 55

    Paul warns that the unrighteous, listing various vices, will not inherit God's kingdom. He reminds them that some of them were once like that but have been washed, sanctified, and justified in Christ and by the Spirit.

    Purity and sexual ethicsThe Holy SpiritSin and the fall
  26. Justified by faith in Christ, not works of the law
    Galatians 2:14–21 · argument · AD 47 – AD 52

    Paul tells Peter that Jews and Gentiles alike are justified by faith in Christ, not by works of the law. He says he has been crucified with Christ and now lives by faith in the Son of God, who loved him.

    FaithLaw and commandmentsThe cross
  27. Abraham's faith and the blessing for all nations
    Galatians 3:6–9 · argument · AD 48 – AD 55

    Paul says Abraham believed God and it was counted as righteousness, so those who have faith are his sons. Scripture foresaw that the Gentiles would be justified by faith and blessed with Abraham.

    PromiseThe nations
  28. Rejoice and beware false teachers
    Philippians 3:1–3 · exhortation · AD 60 – AD 62

    Paul tells them to rejoice in the Lord and warns against those who insist on outward circumcision. He says believers are those who worship by God's Spirit and put no confidence in the flesh.

    False teachingJoy and thanksgiving
  29. Paul counts his credentials as loss
    Philippians 3:4–11 · teaching · AD 34 – AD 62

    Paul lists his own impressive background, then says he counts it all as loss compared with knowing Christ. He wants righteousness by faith in Christ, to know the power of His resurrection and share His sufferings.

    FaithResurrection
  30. Saved by mercy, not our own works
    Titus 3:3–7 · teaching · AD 62 – AD 65

    Paul recalls that believers were once foolish and enslaved to desires, but God's kindness appeared and saved them by his mercy through new birth by the Holy Spirit. They are justified by grace and made heirs of eternal life.

    SalvationMercy and compassionThe Holy Spirit
