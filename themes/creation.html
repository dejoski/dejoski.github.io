Creation

104 passages · Humanity

  1. In the beginning: formless earth
    Genesis 1:1–2 · narrative

    The Bible opens with God creating the heavens and the earth. The earth is at first formless and dark, with God's Spirit hovering over the waters.

    God's sovereignty
  2. Day one: light
    Genesis 1:3–5 · narrative

    God speaks light into being, calls it good, and separates it from darkness, naming them day and night. The first evening and morning mark the first day.

    Who God is
  3. Day two: sky separates the waters
    Genesis 1:6–8 · narrative

    God makes an expanse to divide the waters above from the waters below and calls it sky. This completes the second day.

  4. Day three: land, seas, and plants
    Genesis 1:9–13 · narrative

    God gathers the waters so dry land appears, naming land and seas, then commands the earth to produce plants and fruit trees, each reproducing according to its kind.

  5. Day four: sun, moon, and stars
    Genesis 1:14–19 · narrative

    God places lights in the sky to separate day from night and to mark seasons, days, and years. The greater light rules the day, the lesser the night, and the stars are made too.

  6. Day five: sea creatures and birds
    Genesis 1:20–23 · narrative

    God fills the waters and sky with living creatures and birds, and blesses them to be fruitful and multiply.

  7. Day six: land animals
    Genesis 1:24–25 · narrative

    God commands the earth to bring forth livestock, crawling creatures, and wild beasts, and sees that this is good.

  8. Humanity made in God's image
    Genesis 1:26–31 · narrative

    God decides to make humankind in his image, male and female, to rule over the animals. He blesses them, gives plants for food, and calls all he has made very good.

    Image of God
  9. God rests on the seventh day
    Genesis 2:1–3 · narrative

    Creation is complete, and God rests on the seventh day, blessing it and setting it apart as holy.

    Sabbath and feasts
  10. Creation retold: man from the dust
    Genesis 2:4–7 · narrative

    A second account begins, zooming in on the earth before plants grew. God forms the first man from the ground's dust and breathes life into him.

    Image of God
  11. The garden of Eden and its rivers
    Genesis 2:8–14 · narrative

    God plants a garden in Eden and puts the man there. Trees grow, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and a river divides into four branches.

    Work and rest
  12. The flood begins
    Genesis 7:11–16 · narrative

    The deep's fountains burst open and the heavens' floodgates open, with rain for forty days. All those on the ark enter, and the Lord shuts Noah in.

    Justice and judgment
  13. Blessing and rules for life after the flood
    Genesis 9:1–7 · speech

    God blesses Noah's family to be fruitful and fills the earth. He gives animals as food but forbids eating meat with its lifeblood and declares that human life is precious, made in God's image.

    Law and commandmentsFamily and marriage
  14. Do not destroy fruit trees
    Deuteronomy 20:19–20 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    When besieging a city for a long time, Israel must not cut down its fruit trees, since the trees are not enemies. Only non-fruit trees may be used for siege works.

    War and conflict
  15. Song: the Lord is above all gods
    1 Chronicles 16:23–33 · praise · 1000 BC – 997 BC

    The song calls the whole earth to sing, since the Lord is great and made the heavens, unlike the idols of the nations. Creation is urged to rejoice because he is coming to judge the earth.

    Worship and praiseIdolatryGlory
  16. God as Creator, Abraham's God, and Redeemer
    Nehemiah 9:6–15 · prayer · 444 BC

    The prayer praises God as Creator of all, who chose Abram, made a covenant with him, rescued Israel from Egypt, and gave the Law at Sinai. It also recalls how He provided bread and water in the wilderness.

    CovenantExodus and deliveranceFaithfulness
  17. God is too great to contend with
    Job 9:1–12 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job agrees that no mortal is righteous before God. He describes God's overwhelming power over mountains, sun, stars and sea, and says no one can question what God does.

    God's sovereigntyJustice and judgment
  18. You made me, so why destroy me?
    Job 10:8–17 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job recalls that God shaped him like clay and cared for him, but thinks God secretly planned to watch for his sin. Whether guilty or innocent, he feels hunted and overwhelmed.

    Suffering
  19. Even animals know God's hand is at work
    Job 12:7–12 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says anyone, even animals, birds and the earth itself, could teach that the LORD's hand is behind all life. He notes that wisdom is thought to come with age.

    God's sovereignty
  20. God's power over the cosmos
    Job 26:5–14 · poetry · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job describes God's power over the realm of the dead, the earth hung on nothing, the clouds, the horizon and the sea, and says these are only the outskirts of His ways.

    God's sovereigntyWho God is
  21. Fair treatment of servants
    Job 31:13–15 · speech · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Job says he never ignored a servant's complaint, since God made both master and servant in the womb and will hold him to account.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedJustice and judgment
  22. Elihu invites Job to answer him
    Job 33:1–7 · speech · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Elihu asks Job to listen, claims to speak sincerely, and says he is also a creature made of clay, so Job need not fear him.

    Words and speech
  23. God's power in rain and storm
    Job 36:27–33 · poetry · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Elihu describes how God draws up water into rain, and controls clouds, thunder and lightning to judge nations and provide food.

    God's sovereignty
  24. Thunder as the voice of God
    Job 37:1–5 · poetry · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Elihu is stirred by thunder and lightning, which he describes as God's voice doing great things people cannot understand.

    GloryWho God is
  25. Snow, rain and ice at God's command
    Job 37:6–13 · poetry · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Elihu says God commands snow, rain and ice, sends people and animals indoors, and directs the clouds for correction or for blessing on the land.

    God's sovereignty
  26. Where were you at creation?
    Job 38:4–11 · speech · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    God asks Job where he was when the earth's foundations were laid and the sea was set within its bounds. This shows how vast God's role is compared with Job's.

    God's sovereignty
  27. Dawn, the deep and the gates of death
    Job 38:12–21 · speech · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    God asks whether Job commands the morning, has explored the ocean depths, the gates of death, or the home of light and darkness.

    Death and mortalityGod's sovereignty
  28. Snow, rain, lightning and ice
    Job 38:22–30 · speech · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    God asks Job about His storehouses of snow and hail, who directs rain to empty land, and who is the source of rain, dew, ice and frost.

    God's sovereignty
  29. The stars and the heavens
    Job 38:31–38 · speech · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    God asks whether Job can control the constellations, command the clouds or send lightning, and who gives wisdom to people.

    Wisdom
  30. God feeds lions and ravens
    Job 38:39–41 · speech · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    God asks whether Job hunts for lions and feeds ravens. This starts a series of questions about wild animals which depend on God's care.

    Who God is
