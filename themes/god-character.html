Who God is

83 passages · God

  1. Day one: light
    Genesis 1:3–5 · narrative

    God speaks light into being, calls it good, and separates it from darkness, naming them day and night. The first evening and morning mark the first day.

    Creation
  2. God reveals his name
    Exodus 3:13–15 · dialogue · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Moses asks what name to give the Israelites. God answers 'I AM WHO I AM' and says he is the LORD, the God of their fathers, the name by which he is to be remembered forever.

    Names of God
  3. God leads them by cloud and fire
    Exodus 13:17–22 · narrative · 1446 BC

    God avoids the shorter road through Philistine land so the people will not lose heart at war. Moses carries Joseph's bones, and the LORD goes before them in a pillar of cloud by day and fire by night.

    Providence
  4. None is like the LORD; he will reign
    Exodus 15:11–18 · poetry · 1446 BC

    The song asks who among the gods is like the LORD, then looks ahead to God leading his people to his holy dwelling while the nations tremble. It ends with the LORD reigning forever.

    Kings and kingship
  5. The first two commandments
    Exodus 20:1–6 · law · 1446 BC

    God says he is the LORD who brought Israel out of slavery. Israel is to have no other gods and make no idols, and God describes himself as jealous but showing love to those who love him.

    Law and commandmentsIdolatry
  6. Be holy, for I am holy
    Leviticus 11:41–47 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The people must not eat creatures that swarm on the ground or defile themselves. The LORD grounds the rules in who he is: he brought them out of Egypt to be their God, so they are to be holy as he is holy.

    HolinessExodus and deliverance
  7. The cloud guides Israel's travels
    Numbers 9:15–23 · narrative · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    A cloud covers the tabernacle, looking like fire at night. Israel camps while it stays and moves when it lifts, whether for a night or a long time.

    Obedience
  8. The people rebel out of fear
    Deuteronomy 1:26–33 · speech · 1445 BC

    Moses reminds Israel that they refused to go up, grumbled that God hated them, and feared the people of the land. He urged them not to be afraid since God had carried them, yet they did not trust Him.

    FaithSin and the fall
  9. Encouragement before battle
    Deuteronomy 20:1–9 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    When Israel goes to war against a larger army, they are not to fear, because God goes with them. The priest encourages the army, and the officers release men with new houses, vineyards, or engagements, and the fearful.

    War and conflictFaith
  10. Song opens: God is faithful, Israel is not
    Deuteronomy 32:1–6 · poetry · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses' song calls heaven and earth to listen and praises the LORD as the Rock, perfect and just. It contrasts his faithfulness with Israel's corrupt behavior and asks whether this is how they repay their Father.

    FaithfulnessSin and the fall
  11. Moses' blessing begins with the LORD at Sinai
    Deuteronomy 33:1–5 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Before his death, Moses begins a blessing on Israel by describing the LORD coming from Sinai with holy ones. He notes God's love for his people, the law given through Moses, and the LORD as King in Jeshurun.

    CovenantLaw and commandments
  12. There is none like the God of Israel
    Deuteronomy 33:26–29 · praise · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses closes by praising the God who rides the heavens to help Israel, whose everlasting arms carry them. He declares Israel blessed as a people saved by the LORD, who will drive out their enemies and live in security.

    SalvationFaithfulnessElection and calling
  13. God tells how the Jordan will stop
    Joshua 3:7–13 · speech · 1406 BC

    The LORD promises to exalt Joshua and tells him how the crossing will work. Joshua announces that when the priests' feet touch the water, the river will stand up in a heap, showing that God is among them.

    PromiseLeadership
  14. The waters return
    Joshua 4:15–18 · miracle · 1406 BC

    The LORD tells Joshua to have the priests come up from the Jordan. As soon as their feet touch dry land, the river flows over its banks again.

    Miracles and signs
  15. David refuses armor and faces Goliath
    1 Samuel 17:38–47 · narrative · 1022 BC – 1015 BC

    Saul's armor does not fit David, so he takes his staff, five stones and sling. Goliath curses him, and David answers that he comes in the name of the LORD, so all will know the battle is the LORD's.

    FaithWar and conflict
  16. David praises the LORD as his rescuer
    2 Samuel 22:1–7 · praise · 1010 BC – 975 BC

    David sings that the LORD is his rock, fortress and deliverer. He recalls calling out from death's snares and being heard.

    SalvationFaith
  17. Solomon blesses the people
    1 Kings 8:12–21 · speech · 959 BC – 958 BC

    Solomon says the LORD chose to dwell in a cloud and that he has built a house for him. He blesses the assembly and recalls how God promised David's son would build the house.

    Temple and tabernaclePromise
  18. Fire falls and the people confess
    1 Kings 18:36–40 · miracle · 870 BC – 860 BC

    Elijah prays briefly that the LORD would show he is God, and fire falls and consumes everything. The people declare the LORD is God, and Elijah has Baal's prophets seized and killed at the Kishon Valley.

    Miracles and signsPrayerIdolatry
  19. God's quiet voice at Horeb
    1 Kings 19:9–14 · dialogue · 870 BC – 855 BC

    God asks what Elijah is doing there, and Elijah says he alone remains faithful. God's presence is not in wind, earthquake, or fire but in a still, small voice, and Elijah repeats the same complaint.

    Dreams, visions and revelationSuffering
  20. Israel defeats Aram at Aphek
    1 Kings 20:26–30 · narrative · 860 BC – 856 BC

    In spring Aram gathers at Aphek. A man of God says the LORD will give the vast army into Israel's hand to show he is not merely a god of the hills, and after seven days Israel wins decisively.

    War and conflictGod's sovereignty
  21. Naaman's confession and request
    2 Kings 5:15–19 · dialogue · 852 BC – 841 BC

    Healed, Naaman declares that there is no God except in Israel and offers a gift, which Elisha refuses. Naaman asks for soil to worship the LORD alone and for pardon when accompanying his king in a pagan temple, and Elisha tells him to go in peace.

    FaithThe nations
  22. David's prayer of wonder
    1 Chronicles 17:16–22 · prayer · 998 BC – 990 BC

    David sits before the Lord and asks who he is to be so honored. He praises God as unique and celebrates how God redeemed Israel from Egypt and made them his own people.

    PrayerPride and humility
  23. Solomon begins to pray for David's line
    2 Chronicles 6:12–17 · prayer · 959 BC – 958 BC

    Standing on a bronze platform with hands spread, Solomon praises God as unique and faithful to His covenant. He asks God to keep His promise that David's descendants will always sit on the throne.

    PrayerCovenant
  24. Will God dwell on earth?
    2 Chronicles 6:18–21 · prayer · 959 BC – 958 BC

    Solomon acknowledges that not even the highest heavens can contain God, let alone this temple. He asks God to hear prayers made toward it and to forgive.

    PrayerTemple and tabernacle
  25. Insults against God and threats to the wall
    2 Chronicles 32:16–19 · narrative · 701 BC

    Sennacherib's servants keep speaking against the LORD, and he writes letters mocking Him. The Assyrians shout in Hebrew to the people on the wall to frighten them, comparing the God of Jerusalem to the idols of other nations.

    War and conflictPride and humility
  26. God's wisdom is beyond human reach
    Job 11:7–12 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Zophar says the deep things of God are higher than heaven and deeper than Sheol, and God sees all deceit. He insists a foolish person cannot become wise.

    Wisdom
  27. God is hidden yet knows my way
    Job 23:8–17 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says he cannot find God east, west, north, or south, but he trusts that God knows his way and that he will come out as gold. Still, he is terrified by God's unchangeable will.

    Faith
  28. God's power over the cosmos
    Job 26:5–14 · poetry · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job describes God's power over the realm of the dead, the earth hung on nothing, the clouds, the horizon and the sea, and says these are only the outskirts of His ways.

    God's sovereigntyCreation
  29. Only God knows wisdom's place
    Job 28:20–28 · wisdom

    Wisdom is hidden from every living creature, and even Death has only heard a rumor. God alone understands it, and He tells people that fearing the Lord is wisdom.

    WisdomGod's sovereignty
  30. Job accuses God of turning on him
    Job 30:16–23 · lament · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Job describes constant pain and says God grabs and throws him down. He cries for help, but he feels God does not answer and expects death.

    Lament and fastingSuffering
