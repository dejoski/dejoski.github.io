  2. Sayings on Zebulun, Issachar, Dan, Gad, Asher, Naphtali
    Genesis 49:13–21 · poetry · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    Jacob gives brief images for six sons: Zebulun by the sea, Issachar the strong laborer, Dan the judge and viper, Gad the raider, Asher with rich food, Naphtali the free doe.

    Promise
  3. God makes Moses like God to Pharaoh
    Exodus 7:1–7 · speech · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The LORD says Moses will speak to Pharaoh with Aaron as his prophet. God will harden Pharaoh's heart and bring judgments so that Egypt will know he is the LORD, and Moses and Aaron obey.

    God's sovereignty
  4. The Spirit on the elders, Eldad and Medad
    Numbers 11:24–30 · narrative · 1445 BC

    God puts His Spirit on seventy elders, who prophesy. Eldad and Medad prophesy in the camp, and Moses tells Joshua he wishes all God's people were prophets.

    The Holy SpiritLeadership
  5. The LORD defends Moses
    Numbers 12:4–9 · speech · 1445 BC

    God summons the three to the Tent of Meeting. He says Moses is unique among prophets, faithful and spoken to face to face, and asks why they did not fear to speak against him.

    Dreams, visions and revelationLeadership
  6. Third oracle: How lovely are your tents
    Numbers 24:1–9 · oracle of hope · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Seeing Israel camped by tribes, Balaam is seized by the Spirit of God. He describes Israel's tents as beautiful, prosperous like gardens, strong like a lion, and says those who bless Israel will be blessed and those who curse it cursed.

    The Holy SpiritPromise
  7. Fourth oracle: A star out of Jacob
    Numbers 24:15–19 · prophecy · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balaam speaks of a distant future in which a star and scepter rise from Israel, defeating Moab and Edom. A ruler from Jacob will overcome their enemies.

    The MessiahKings and kingshipWar and conflict
  8. A false prophet with true signs
    Deuteronomy 13:1–5 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    If a prophet or dreamer performs a sign but urges Israel to follow other gods, they must not listen. God is testing their love, and such a prophet is to be put to death.

    False teachingIdolatry
  9. A prophet like Moses
    Deuteronomy 18:15–22 · prophecy · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses says God will raise up a prophet like him whom Israel must heed, because the people asked at Horeb not to hear God's voice directly. A prophet who speaks falsely, or whose word does not come true, is not from God.

    The MessiahLaw and commandments
  10. Joshua succeeds; no prophet like Moses
    Deuteronomy 34:9–12 · history · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Joshua, filled with wisdom after Moses laid hands on him, leads Israel, and the people obey. The book ends by saying no later prophet equaled Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face, or his signs and wonders.

    LeadershipObedience
  11. Deborah summons Barak to fight
    Judges 4:4–10 · dialogue · 1220 BC – 1125 BC

    Deborah, a prophetess who judges Israel, tells Barak that the LORD commands him to gather ten thousand men at Mount Tabor. Barak will go only if she comes, so she agrees but says the honor of victory will go to a woman.

    LeadershipWar and conflict
  12. Midian oppresses Israel; a prophet rebukes them
    Judges 6:1–10 · narrative · 1180 BC – 1160 BC

    Israel does evil and the LORD gives them to Midian for seven years. Midianite raiders devastate the crops and livestock until Israel cries out, and the LORD sends a prophet who reminds them of the exodus and that they did not obey Him.

    Sin and the fallOpposition and persecution
  13. A man of God announces judgment on Eli's house
    1 Samuel 2:27–36 · oracle of judgment · 1095 BC – 1080 BC

    An unnamed man of God tells Eli that because he honored his sons above God, his family will lose strength, his sons will die on one day, and a faithful priest will be raised up in their place.

    Justice and judgmentPriesthood
  14. The LORD calls Samuel three times
    1 Samuel 3:1–9 · narrative · 1095 BC – 1085 BC

    In a time when God's word was rare, Samuel hears a voice in the night and thinks it is Eli. After the third time, Eli realizes it is the LORD and tells Samuel how to answer.

    Dreams, visions and revelation
  15. Samuel tells Eli everything
    1 Samuel 3:15–18 · dialogue · 1095 BC – 1080 BC

    Afraid at first, Samuel obeys when Eli insists on knowing the message and hides nothing. Eli accepts it, saying the LORD may do what is good in his eyes.

    Obedience
  16. Samuel is recognized as the LORD's prophet
    1 Samuel 3:19–21 · narrative · 1095 BC – 1080 BC

    The LORD is with Samuel and none of his words fail. All Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, knows he is a confirmed prophet, and God continues to reveal himself at Shiloh.

    Faithfulness
  17. The servant suggests asking the seer
    1 Samuel 9:6–10 · dialogue · 1052 BC – 1050 BC

    The servant proposes consulting a respected man of God in the nearby city and offers a quarter shekel of silver as a gift. Saul agrees.

  18. Young women direct them to Samuel
    1 Samuel 9:11–14 · dialogue · 1052 BC – 1050 BC

    Young women drawing water tell Saul the seer has just arrived for a sacrifice at the high place. Saul and his servant enter the city and meet Samuel coming toward them.

  19. The signs happen and Saul prophesies
    1 Samuel 10:9–16 · narrative · 1052 BC – 1050 BC

    God changes Saul's heart, and the signs happen that day. Saul prophesies with a group of prophets, which sparks the saying 'Is Saul also among the prophets?', but he tells his uncle nothing about the kingdom.

    The Holy Spirit
  20. David flees to Samuel; Saul prophesies
    1 Samuel 19:18–24 · narrative · 1018 BC – 1012 BC

    David goes to Samuel at Naioth in Ramah. Three sets of Saul's messengers are overcome by the Spirit and prophesy, and finally Saul himself does.

    The Holy Spirit
  21. Ahijah's prophecy to Jeroboam
    1 Kings 11:26–40 · prophecy · 935 BC – 931 BC

    The prophet Ahijah tears a cloak into twelve pieces and tells Jeroboam that God will give him ten tribes because Solomon worshiped other gods. Solomon tries to kill Jeroboam, who flees to Egypt.

    Kings and kingship
  22. A man of God confronts Jeroboam
    1 Kings 13:1–10 · narrative · 930 BC – 920 BC

    A man of God from Judah denounces the altar at Bethel and predicts Josiah. The altar splits, Jeroboam's hand withers and is healed, and the man refuses to eat there.

    IdolatryOpposition and persecution
  23. Jeroboam's wife goes to Ahijah
    1 Kings 14:1–6 · narrative · 926 BC – 910 BC

    When Jeroboam's son Abijah falls ill, Jeroboam sends his wife in disguise to the prophet Ahijah. God has already told Ahijah who is coming.

  24. Ahijah's message against Jeroboam's house
    1 Kings 14:7–16 · oracle of judgment · 926 BC – 910 BC

    Ahijah says Jeroboam has done more evil than any before him. God will cut off his house, the child will die, and Israel will be uprooted from the land for its sins.

    Justice and judgmentIdolatry
  25. Jehu's message against Baasha
    1 Kings 16:1–7 · oracle of judgment · 890 BC – 886 BC

    God tells Baasha through Jehu that his house will end like Jeroboam's, because he followed Jeroboam's sin. Baasha dies and his son Elah reigns.

    Justice and judgmentIdolatry
  26. Elijah announces drought
    1 Kings 17:1–7 · narrative · 871 BC – 866 BC

    Elijah tells Ahab there will be no rain except at his word. God sends him to hide by the Brook of Cherith, where ravens feed him, until it dries up.

    ProvidenceFaith
  27. Elijah's message of death for Ahaziah
    2 Kings 1:3–8 · dialogue · 852 BC

    The angel of the LORD sends Elijah to intercept the messengers and ask why they consult Baal-zebub instead of God. Elijah announces that Ahaziah will die, and the king recognizes the messenger as Elijah from their description.

    Angels and spiritual beingsIdolatry
  28. Fire consumes two captains of fifty
    2 Kings 1:9–14 · miracle · 852 BC

    Ahaziah sends three captains with fifty men each to summon Elijah. Fire from heaven consumes the first two companies, and the third captain begs for mercy on his knees.

    Miracles and signsOpposition and persecution
  29. Elijah confronts Ahaziah and the king dies
    2 Kings 1:15–18 · narrative · 852 BC

    The angel tells Elijah to go with the third captain without fear. Elijah repeats the message to Ahaziah's face, the king dies as foretold, and his brother Jehoram succeeds him.

    Death and mortalityGod's sovereignty
  30. Boys jeer at Elisha near Bethel
    2 Kings 2:23–25 · narrative · 852 BC – 851 BC

    Boys from Bethel mock Elisha as he goes up the road. He curses them in the LORD's name, and two bears attack forty-two of them, after which Elisha continues to Carmel and Samaria.

    Justice and judgmentPride and humility
