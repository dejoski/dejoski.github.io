Kingdom of God

69 passages · Kingdom

  1. All nations will turn to the LORD
    Psalms 22:27–31 · prophecy · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The psalm looks ahead to all the earth remembering the LORD and every family of the nations bowing to Him. Future generations will be told of what God has done.

    The nationsWorship and praise
  2. The king's throne, righteousness and royal court
    Psalms 45:6–9 · poetry

    The poet declares that the king's throne endures and rests on justice. God has anointed him for loving righteousness, and the scene shows his fragrant robes, ivory palaces and the queen at his side.

    Kings and kingshipJustice and judgment
  3. I will exalt You every day
    Psalms 145:1–3 · praise · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    David vows to exalt God as his King and to bless His name every day forever, because the LORD is great and greatly to be praised and His greatness cannot be fully searched out.

    Worship and praise
  4. Gracious, compassionate, and everlasting King
    Psalms 145:8–13 · praise · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    David praises the LORD as gracious, compassionate, slow to anger, and good to all He has made. All creation will thank Him and His people will speak of His kingdom, which is everlasting.

    GraceMercy and compassionWho God is
  5. All nations stream to the LORD's mountain
    Isaiah 2:1–5 · oracle of hope · 750 BC – 735 BC

    Isaiah sees a future day when all nations go up to the LORD's house to learn His ways, and He settles their disputes so that swords become plowshares. He then urges Jacob to walk in that light.

    The nationsPeace and reconciliationWorship and praise
  6. The LORD reigns in Zion
    Isaiah 24:21–23 · oracle of hope

    God will punish the powers in heaven and kings on earth, who will be locked up like prisoners. Then the LORD of Hosts will reign on Mount Zion with glory, and the moon and sun will be ashamed.

    GloryGod's sovereignty
  7. Good news: your God reigns
    Isaiah 52:7–12 · praise · 700 BC – 538 BC

    The feet of the messenger who announces peace and says 'Your God reigns' are called beautiful. Watchmen sing as the LORD returns to Zion, and the exiles are told to depart in purity.

    Gospel and missionPeace and reconciliationSalvation
  8. The statue, the stone, and its meaning
    Daniel 2:31–45 · prophecy · 604 BC – 603 BC

    Daniel tells the king the dream of a statue of gold, silver, bronze, iron and clay, struck by a stone that becomes a mountain. He explains that the head of gold is Nebuchadnezzar, followed by other kingdoms, and God will set up an unending kingdom.

    Dreams, visions and revelationProphecyGod's sovereignty
  9. Darius honors the God of Daniel
    Daniel 6:25–28 · praise · 538 BC – 537 BC

    Darius writes to all nations commanding reverence for the living God of Daniel, whose kingdom will never end. Daniel prospers under both Darius and Cyrus.

    Worship and praiseThe nations
  10. The Ancient of Days and the Son of Man
    Daniel 7:9–14 · vision · 553 BC – 552 BC

    Daniel sees thrones set, the Ancient of Days seated in judgment, and the boastful beast destroyed. One like a son of man comes with the clouds and is given an everlasting dominion over all peoples.

    Final judgmentThe Messiah
  11. An attendant explains the beasts
    Daniel 7:15–18 · dialogue · 553 BC – 552 BC

    Alarmed, Daniel asks one of those standing there for the meaning. He is told the four beasts are four kings, but the holy ones of the Most High will receive the kingdom forever.

    Dreams, visions and revelationThe nations
  12. Israel possesses the land; the kingdom is the LORD's
    Obadiah 1:19–21 · oracle of hope · 586 BC – 500 BC

    The prophecy ends with restored Israelites taking possession of surrounding lands, including Edom's mountains. Deliverers go up Mount Zion to rule, and the kingdom belongs to the LORD.

    The promised landExile and return
  13. In the last days, nations stream to Zion
    Micah 4:1–5 · prophecy · 720 BC – 700 BC

    Micah foresees the LORD's house raised up and many nations coming to learn his ways. He will settle disputes, weapons will be turned into farm tools, and each person will live in peace, while Israel walks in the LORD's name.

    Peace and reconciliationThe nationsLaw and commandments
  14. Living water and the LORD as king
    Zechariah 14:6–11 · prophecy · 518 BC – 480 BC

    On that day there is a unique light at evening, and living water flows out of Jerusalem east and west all year. The LORD will be king over all the earth, and Jerusalem will be raised up and live in safety.

    New creation
  15. John the Baptist preaches in the wilderness
    Matthew 3:1–6 · narrative · AD 27 – AD 29

    John the Baptist appears in the wilderness calling people to repent because the kingdom of heaven is near. Matthew links him to Isaiah's 'voice in the wilderness,' and crowds come to confess their sins and be baptized in the Jordan.

    RepentanceGospel and mission
  16. Jesus moves to Capernaum and begins preaching
    Matthew 4:12–17 · narrative · AD 28 – AD 29

    Hearing that John was imprisoned, Jesus withdraws to Galilee and settles in Capernaum. Matthew sees Isaiah's words about a great light for Galilee fulfilled, and Jesus begins preaching the same message John did: repent, for the kingdom of heaven is near.

    Gospel and missionProphecy
  17. Jesus teaches and heals across Galilee
    Matthew 4:23–25 · narrative · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus travels throughout Galilee teaching in synagogues, proclaiming the good news of the kingdom and healing many kinds of sickness. His fame spreads and large crowds from many regions follow him.

    Miracles and signsGospel and mission
  18. The Beatitudes
    Matthew 5:1–12 · teaching · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus opens his teaching by declaring who is blessed: the poor in spirit, mourners, the meek, the merciful, the pure in heart, peacemakers and the persecuted. He tells his followers to rejoice, since prophets before them were also persecuted.

    Discipleship
  19. The Lord's Prayer
    Matthew 6:9–15 · prayer · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus gives a pattern for prayer: honoring God's name, asking for his kingdom and will, and requesting daily bread, forgiveness and deliverance from evil. He adds that God's forgiveness is tied to forgiving others.

    PrayerForgiveness
  20. Instructions for the mission
    Matthew 10:5–15 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus sends the twelve to the lost sheep of Israel to preach that the kingdom is near, heal the sick and cast out demons freely. They are to travel simply, rely on hospitality, and shake off the dust of towns that reject them.

    Gospel and mission
  21. Accused of using Satan's power
    Matthew 12:22–30 · dialogue · AD 29

    After Jesus heals a blind, mute demon-possessed man, the crowds wonder if he is the Son of David, but the Pharisees say he uses Beelzebul's power. Jesus answers that a divided kingdom cannot stand, and casting out demons shows God's kingdom has come.

    Demons and deliveranceOpposition and persecution
  22. The parable of the sower
    Matthew 13:1–9 · parable · AD 29

    Sitting in a boat, Jesus tells of a farmer whose seed falls on the path, rocky ground, thorns and good soil, with only the good soil producing a large crop. He ends by saying whoever has ears should hear.

    ParablesFaith
  23. Why Jesus speaks in parables
    Matthew 13:10–17 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus says the secrets of the kingdom are given to the disciples but not to others, and quotes Isaiah about people who see and hear but do not understand. He calls his disciples blessed for seeing what many prophets longed to see.

    Parables
  24. Jesus explains the sower
    Matthew 13:18–23 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus explains that the seed is the message of the kingdom and the soils are different responses to it. Some hearers lose it, some fall away under trouble, some are choked by worry and wealth, and some bear fruit.

    Parables
  25. The parable of the weeds
    Matthew 13:24–30 · parable · AD 29

    A man sows good seed but an enemy sows weeds among the wheat. The owner refuses to pull the weeds early, since that would uproot the wheat, and says both will grow together until harvest when they will be separated.

    ParablesFinal judgment
  26. Mustard seed and yeast
    Matthew 13:31–35 · parable · AD 29

    Jesus says the kingdom is like a tiny mustard seed that grows into a large plant, and like yeast a woman mixes into flour until it all rises. Matthew notes Jesus spoke only in parables to the crowds, fulfilling a psalm.

    Parables
  27. Jesus explains the weeds
    Matthew 13:36–43 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus explains that the sower is the Son of Man, the field is the world and the weeds are the sons of the evil one. At the end of the age the angels will gather the wicked for judgment, while the righteous shine like the sun.

    Final judgment
  28. Treasure, pearl, net and the storeroom
    Matthew 13:44–52 · parable · AD 29

    Jesus compares the kingdom to a hidden treasure and a fine pearl, each worth giving up everything to have, and to a net that catches good and bad fish to be sorted at the end. He closes by likening a trained scribe to a homeowner bringing out new and old treasures.

    Parables
  29. Who is greatest? Become like a child
    Matthew 18:1–5 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    The disciples ask who is greatest in the kingdom, and Jesus sets a child among them. He says they must change and become like children, and that whoever humbles himself is greatest and welcoming such a child welcomes him.

    Pride and humilityDiscipleship
  30. Jesus blesses the little children
    Matthew 19:13–15 · narrative · AD 30

    People bring children for Jesus to bless, and the disciples rebuke them. Jesus tells them to let the children come, because the kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these, and he lays his hands on them.

    Discipleship
