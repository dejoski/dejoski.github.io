Final judgment

  1. Creation rejoices at his coming
    Psalms 96:11–13 · praise

    Heavens, earth, sea, fields and trees are told to rejoice because the Lord is coming to judge the world in righteousness and faithfulness.

    Joy and thanksgivingCreationKings and kingship
  2. A priest-king who judges nations
    Psalms 110:4–7 · oracle of judgment · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    God swears the king is a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek. The Lord stands at his right hand, crushing kings and judging the nations, and the king is refreshed and lifts his head.

    PriesthoodThe MessiahThe nations
  3. Rejoice in youth, and remember judgment
    Ecclesiastes 11:7–10 · exhortation

    He tells the young to enjoy their youth, but to remember that God will bring them to judgment. The days of darkness will be many and youth is fleeting.

    Joy and thanksgivingDeath and mortality
  4. The conclusion: fear God
    Ecclesiastes 12:13–14 · exhortation · 970 BC – 931 BC

    The book ends with the conclusion that people should fear God and keep his commandments, since God will judge every deed.

    ObedienceWorship and praise
  5. The Day of the LORD brings terror
    Isaiah 13:6–16 · oracle of judgment · 720 BC – 700 BC

    The Day of the LORD is pictured as cruel destruction, with people paralyzed by fear, stars dark and the earth shaking. God says He will punish the world's arrogance and its people will flee or be killed.

    The Day of the LordWrath
  6. The whole earth is laid waste
    Isaiah 24:1–6 · oracle of judgment

    Here the focus widens from nations to the whole earth, which God will empty and ruin. No class of people is spared, because people have broken the everlasting covenant.

    Sin and the fall
  7. Joy is silenced in the ruined city
    Isaiah 24:7–13 · lament

    Wine dries up, music stops and the city of chaos lies shattered and shut. Only a few are left, like olives after a harvest.

    Suffering
  8. Praise from afar, but the earth staggers
    Isaiah 24:14–20 · oracle of judgment

    Some sing praises from the west and east, but Isaiah cries out that he wastes away over treachery. The earth is broken and staggers like a drunkard, and there is no escape from terror, pit and snare.

    Worship and praise
  9. Hide until the wrath passes
    Isaiah 26:20–21 · exhortation

    God tells His people to shut themselves in their rooms for a little while until His anger passes. The LORD will come out to punish the earth's iniquity and the earth will reveal its slain.

    Wrath
  10. God will slay Leviathan
    Isaiah 27:1 · prophecy

    On that day the LORD will punish Leviathan, the coiling serpent, and slay the dragon of the sea with His sword.

    God's sovereignty
  11. Edom left to wild creatures
    Isaiah 34:11–17 · oracle of judgment · 712 BC – 690 BC

    Edom becomes an empty wasteland, overgrown with thorns and inhabited only by owls, jackals and other wild animals. The reader is told to check the scroll of the LORD, because none of this will fail to happen.

    The nations
  12. Isaiah foretells Babylon's conquest
    Isaiah 39:5–8 · oracle of judgment · 704 BC – 702 BC

    Isaiah tells Hezekiah that everything in his palace, and some of his descendants, will be carried off to Babylon. Hezekiah calls the word good, thinking there will at least be peace in his own days.

    ProphecyExile and return
  13. Sorcerers and astrologers cannot save
    Isaiah 47:12–15 · oracle of judgment · 700 BC – 539 BC

    God mocks Babylon's spells and astrologers as no better than stubble that will burn. Those she trusted will wander off, and none can save her.

    IdolatryWisdom
  14. Trust in the LORD, not your own fire
    Isaiah 50:10–11 · exhortation · 700 BC – 538 BC

    A closing word invites those who fear the LORD and walk in darkness to trust in his name. Those who light their own fires for light will instead lie down in torment.

    FaithHope and endurance
  15. Charges of idolatry and false worship
    Isaiah 57:3–13 · oracle of judgment · 700 BC – 500 BC

    God calls the idolaters offspring of sorcerers and adulterers who worship among the oaks, sacrifice children and make offerings to stones. He says their efforts will not save them, but those who take refuge in him will inherit the land.

    IdolatrySin and the fall
  16. Servants blessed, rebels judged
    Isaiah 65:8–16 · oracle of judgment · 700 BC – 500 BC

    God says he will not destroy all his people, as a good cluster is kept from spoiling, and his servants will inherit the land. Those who abandon him for Fortune and Destiny face the sword and hunger, while his servants will eat, drink and rejoice.

    Justice and judgmentFaithfulness
  17. Fire, gathering of nations and endless worship
    Isaiah 66:15–24 · oracle of judgment · 700 BC – 500 BC

    The LORD comes with fire to judge those who rebel, then gathers all nations to see his glory and sends survivors to declare it. Exiles are brought back, all people worship him every Sabbath, and the rebels' fate is a lasting horror.

    The nationsGloryWorship and praise
  18. Executioners and the man in linen
    Ezekiel 9:1–3 · vision · 592 BC

    God summons six armed men and a man in linen with a writing kit, and they stand by the bronze altar as the glory of God moves to the temple threshold.

    Angels and spiritual beingsJustice and judgment
  19. A ruin for the wicked prince of Israel
    Ezekiel 21:25–27 · oracle of judgment · 590 BC – 588 BC

    God tells the profane prince, whose day of final punishment has come, that the turban and crown will be removed and the low lifted up. It will be a ruin until the rightful one comes.

    Kings and kingshipJustice and judgment
  20. A mob will stone them and end lewdness
    Ezekiel 23:45–49 · oracle of judgment · 591 BC – 588 BC

    God says righteous men will judge them, a crowd will be brought against them, and lewdness will end so all women take warning and they know he is the LORD.

    Justice and judgmentIdolatry
  21. The Ancient of Days and the Son of Man
    Daniel 7:9–14 · vision · 553 BC – 552 BC

    Daniel sees thrones set, the Ancient of Days seated in judgment, and the boastful beast destroyed. One like a son of man comes with the clouds and is given an everlasting dominion over all peoples.

    Kingdom of GodThe Messiah
  22. The fourth beast and the little horn
    Daniel 7:19–27 · prophecy · 553 BC – 552 BC

    Daniel asks more about the fourth beast and the horn that wages war on the holy ones. The attendant explains that this king will speak against the Most High and oppress His people for a time, but the court will end his rule and give the kingdom to the holy ones.

    Opposition and persecutionThe end times
  23. Deliverance, resurrection and sealed words
    Daniel 12:1–4 · prophecy · 536 BC – 535 BC

    Michael will arise in a time of unparalleled distress, and those whose names are written in the book will be delivered. Many who sleep in the dust will awake, some to everlasting life and some to shame, and the wise will shine. Daniel is told to seal the book.

    ResurrectionEternal lifeAngels and spiritual beings
  24. No escape in that day
    Amos 2:13–16 · oracle of judgment · 762 BC – 753 BC

    God says He will crush Israel like a cart heavy with grain. The swift, strong, archer and horseman will not escape, and even the bravest will flee.

    Justice and judgment
  25. Prepare to meet your God
    Amos 4:12–13 · oracle of judgment · 760 BC – 753 BC

    Since Israel has not returned, God says He will do to them what He has warned, and tells them to prepare to meet Him. He is the maker of mountains and wind and Lord of Hosts.

    CreationWho God is
  26. The Day of the LORD is darkness
    Amos 5:18–20 · oracle of judgment · 760 BC – 753 BC

    Amos warns those who long for the Day of the LORD that it will be darkness and not light, like fleeing a lion only to meet a bear.

    The Day of the Lord
  27. God abhors the pride of Jacob
    Amos 6:8–11 · oracle of judgment · 760 BC – 753 BC

    God swears by Himself that He will deliver up the city, and even ten people left in a house will die. Survivors will be too afraid to speak the LORD's name, and great and small houses will be smashed.

    Pride and humility
  28. No one can escape
    Amos 9:1–4 · vision · 760 BC – 753 BC

    Amos sees the Lord standing by the altar, ordering the temple to be shaken and the people struck. Those who flee, however far, will be found: in Sheol, heaven, Carmel or the sea.

    Dreams, visions and revelationGod's sovereignty
  29. Second call; Jonah preaches in Nineveh
    Jonah 3:1–4 · narrative · 793 BC – 753 BC

    God calls Jonah a second time, and this time he obeys, entering the huge city of Nineveh and announcing that it will be overturned in forty days.

    ObedienceGospel and mission
  30. The great day of wrath
    Zephaniah 1:14–18 · oracle of judgment · 640 BC – 612 BC

    The Day of the LORD is described as near and terrible: a day of wrath, darkness, and battle cries. Neither silver nor gold can save anyone on it.

    The Day of the LordWrath
