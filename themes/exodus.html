Exodus and deliverance

71 passages · Kingdom

  1. The covenant and the prophecy of slavery
    Genesis 15:12–21 · prophecy · 2083 BC – 2081 BC

    In a deep sleep, Abram is told his descendants will be enslaved four hundred years, then leave with possessions. A flaming torch passes between the animal halves, and God promises the land from Egypt's river to the Euphrates.

    CovenantPromise
  2. God sends Moses to free his people
    Exodus 3:7–12 · dialogue · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    God says he has seen Israel's suffering and will rescue them to a good land, and sends Moses to Pharaoh. Moses asks who he is to go, and God answers that he will be with him.

    RedemptionElection and calling
  3. God's plan for the elders and Pharaoh
    Exodus 3:16–22 · speech · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    God tells Moses to gather the elders and ask Pharaoh for a three-day journey to sacrifice. He warns that Pharaoh will refuse until compelled by wonders, after which the Israelites will leave with Egyptian valuables.

    God's sovereignty
  4. The LORD announces one more plague
    Exodus 11:1–3 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD says one more plague will make Pharaoh drive Israel out. He tells the people to ask their neighbors for silver and gold, and gives them favor with the Egyptians.

    Justice and judgment
  5. The firstborn die and Pharaoh releases Israel
    Exodus 12:29–36 · narrative · 1446 BC

    At midnight the LORD strikes down every firstborn in Egypt, and Pharaoh summons Moses and Aaron by night and tells Israel to go. The Egyptians urge them to hurry and give them silver, gold, and clothing.

    Justice and judgment
  6. Israel leaves Egypt
    Exodus 12:37–42 · narrative · 1446 BC

    About 600,000 men on foot, plus women, children, a mixed crowd, and livestock, set out from Rameses. The text notes their unleavened bread and the 430 years of the stay, and calls it a night of vigil.

    Faithfulness
  7. Remember the day with unleavened bread
    Exodus 13:3–10 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses tells the people to remember the day of their release and to eat no leavened bread. In the promised land they are to keep the feast each year and explain it to their children.

    Sabbath and feasts
  8. The cloud separates the two camps
    Exodus 14:15–20 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to go forward, lift his staff, and divide the sea. The angel of God and the pillar of cloud move behind Israel, giving light to them and darkness to Egypt.

    Miracles and signs
  9. Israel crosses on dry ground
    Exodus 14:21–25 · miracle · 1446 BC

    Moses stretches out his hand and a strong east wind divides the sea so Israel goes through with walls of water on both sides. The Egyptians follow, and at morning watch the LORD throws them into confusion.

    SalvationMiracles and signs
  10. Jethro visits and praises the LORD
    Exodus 18:1–12 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Moses' father-in-law Jethro brings Moses' wife and sons to him. Moses tells him all the LORD has done, and Jethro rejoices, says the LORD is greater than all gods, and offers sacrifices.

    Worship and praise
  11. Be holy, for I am holy
    Leviticus 11:41–47 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The people must not eat creatures that swarm on the ground or defile themselves. The LORD grounds the rules in who he is: he brought them out of Egypt to be their God, so they are to be holy as he is holy.

    HolinessWho God is
  12. The Feast of Tabernacles
    Leviticus 23:33–44 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    For seven days beginning on the fifteenth of the seventh month, Israel is to celebrate the harvest by rejoicing and living in booths, remembering the exodus from Egypt. The chapter closes by summarizing the feasts and noting that Moses announced them.

    Sabbath and feastsJoy and thanksgiving
  13. Servitude and redemption of people
    Leviticus 25:39–55 · law · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    An Israelite who sells himself must be treated as a hired worker and released at Jubilee, because Israel belongs to God. Slaves may be taken from surrounding nations, and an Israelite sold to a foreigner may be redeemed by relatives or himself.

    FreedomRedemption
  14. Israel keeps the Passover at Sinai
    Numbers 9:1–5 · narrative · 1445 BC

    In the first month of the second year, the LORD tells Moses to keep the Passover at twilight on the fourteenth day, and Israel does so.

    Sabbath and feastsObedience
  15. The craving for meat
    Numbers 11:4–9 · narrative · 1445 BC

    The rabble and the Israelites weep for meat, remembering Egypt's food and complaining about manna. The passage describes the manna's look and taste.

    Sin and the fall
  16. Passover and Unleavened Bread offerings
    Numbers 28:16–25 · ritual instruction · 1406 BC

    Passover is on the fourteenth day of the first month, followed by seven days of unleavened bread. Each day carries a set offering, with a sacred assembly on the first and seventh days.

    Sabbath and feastsWorship and praise
  17. Introducing the record of the journeys
    Numbers 33:1–4 · history · 1446 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses writes down each stage of Israel's journey out of Egypt at the LORD's command. It begins with leaving Rameses the day after Passover as the Egyptians buried their firstborn.

    Faithfulness
  18. From Egypt to Sinai
    Numbers 33:5–15 · list · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The stages from Rameses through the sea, Marah, Elim, the Desert of Sin and Rephidim to the Wilderness of Sinai are listed one after another.

  19. From Sinai to Kadesh
    Numbers 33:16–36 · list · 1445 BC – 1407 BC

    The camps from Sinai through Kibroth-hattaavah, Hazeroth and many more to Ezion-geber and then Kadesh are listed in order.

  20. From Kadesh to the plains of Moab
    Numbers 33:37–49 · list · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    The journey continues to Mount Hor, where Aaron dies at age 123, then on past Edom and Moab to the plains of Moab by the Jordan.

    Death and mortality
  21. The LORD alone is God
    Deuteronomy 4:32–40 · speech · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses asks whether any people has ever heard God speak from fire or been rescued by such signs as Israel was. He concludes that the LORD alone is God and calls them to keep His commands.

    Names of GodLove
  22. Do not forget God when you prosper
    Deuteronomy 6:10–19 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses warns that in a land full of blessings they did not build, Israel must not forget the God who freed them. They should serve Him alone, avoid other gods, and not test Him as at Massah.

    IdolatryPride and humility
  23. Explaining the law to your children
    Deuteronomy 6:20–25 · teaching · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses says that when children ask about the meaning of the laws, parents should tell the story of the exodus from Egypt and explain that keeping the commands is for Israel's good and counts as righteousness.

    Family and marriageLaw and commandments
  24. Remember what you have seen
    Deuteronomy 11:1–9 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses calls Israel to love God and keep His commands, reminding them that they themselves saw the acts of God in Egypt, at the Red Sea, and in the wilderness. Obedience will give them strength to take the land.

    ObedienceLove
  25. Passover and Unleavened Bread
    Deuteronomy 16:1–8 · ritual instruction · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel is to celebrate the Passover in the month of Abib with a sacrifice at the chosen place, and eat unleavened bread for seven days to remember the exodus from Egypt.

    Sabbath and feastsWorship and praise
  26. Leaving the gleanings for the needy
    Deuteronomy 24:19–22 · law · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Farmers must leave forgotten sheaves, leftover olives, and leftover grapes for foreigners, orphans, and widows. Israel is again reminded that it was once enslaved in Egypt.

    Generosity and givingJustice for the poor and oppressed
  27. The confession over the firstfruits
    Deuteronomy 26:5–11 · prayer · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The worshiper recites Israel's story: a wandering forefather, slavery in Egypt, God's rescue, and the gift of the land. He presents the firstfruits, bows down, and rejoices with the Levite and the foreigner.

    Worship and praiseFaithfulnessRedemption
  28. Moses recalls what Israel has seen
    Deuteronomy 29:1–8 · speech · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses gathers all Israel in Moab for a covenant beyond the one at Horeb. He reminds them of the wonders in Egypt, forty years of care in the wilderness, and victories over Sihon and Og, yet says they still lack understanding.

    CovenantFaithfulness
  29. The Jordan stops and Israel crosses
    Joshua 3:14–17 · miracle · 1406 BC

    The priests carrying the ark step into the flooded Jordan and the water stops far upstream. The priests stand on dry ground while the whole nation crosses.

    Miracles and signsThe promised landFaithfulness
  30. The stones are set up and the crossing ends
    Joshua 4:8–14 · narrative · 1406 BC

    The men carry twelve stones to the camp, and Joshua sets up another twelve in the river. The priests leave the riverbed after everyone has crossed, and the people honor Joshua as they did Moses.

    Obedience
