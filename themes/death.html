Death and mortality

  1. God's command about the trees
    Genesis 2:15–17 · narrative

    God places the man in the garden to work and care for it. He allows eating from every tree but forbids the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, warning of death.

    ObedienceWork and rest
  2. Consequences spoken over serpent, woman, man
    Genesis 3:14–19 · oracle of judgment

    God pronounces consequences: the serpent is cursed with a coming conflict, the woman will face pain and strained relations, and the man will toil against a cursed ground until he returns to dust.

    Justice and judgmentSin and the fall
  3. From Seth to Jared
    Genesis 5:6–20 · genealogy

    A repeated pattern lists each man's age at his son's birth, the years he lived afterward, and his death: Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, and Jared.

    Family and marriage
  4. Enoch walked with God
    Genesis 5:21–24 · genealogy

    The pattern breaks with Enoch, who walks with God for 300 years and does not die like the others, because God takes him.

    Faith
  5. The waters rise and cover the earth
    Genesis 7:17–24 · narrative

    The waters swell, lift the ark, and cover the mountains. Every living thing on dry land dies, and only Noah and those with him survive.

    Justice and judgment
  6. Noah's death
    Genesis 9:28–29 · narrative

    Noah lives 350 more years after the flood and dies at 950.

  7. Sarah dies at Hebron
    Genesis 23:1–2 · narrative · 2030 BC – 2028 BC

    Sarah dies at 127 in Hebron, and Abraham mourns and weeps for her.

    Family and marriage
  8. Abraham buries Sarah in Machpelah
    Genesis 23:17–20 · narrative · 2030 BC – 2028 BC

    The field and cave are formally transferred to Abraham before the Hittites, and he buries Sarah there. It becomes his first owned piece of the promised land.

    The promised landPromise
  9. Abraham's later children and death
    Genesis 25:1–11 · narrative · 2029 BC – 1991 BC

    Abraham has more sons through Keturah and other concubines, gives them gifts and sends them east, and leaves everything to Isaac. He dies at 175 and Isaac and Ishmael bury him beside Sarah.

    PromiseFamily and marriage
  10. Rachel dies giving birth to Benjamin
    Genesis 35:16–20 · narrative · 1899 BC – 1897 BC

    On the way to Ephrath, Rachel has a hard labor and dies after giving birth to a son. She names him Ben-oni, but Jacob calls him Benjamin, and he sets up a pillar at her grave.

    Family and marriage
  11. Judah's sons and Tamar
    Genesis 38:1–11 · narrative · 1897 BC – 1885 BC

    Judah leaves his brothers, marries a Canaanite woman, and has three sons. Tamar marries the eldest, Er, and after Er dies she is given to Onan, who also dies, and Judah sends Tamar home to wait for Shelah.

    Family and marriageSin and the fall
  12. The baker's dream and the outcomes
    Genesis 40:16–23 · narrative · 1887 BC – 1886 BC

    The baker recounts his dream of three baskets and birds, and Joseph says he will be executed in three days. Both come true on Pharaoh's birthday, but the cupbearer forgets Joseph.

    Dreams, visions and revelation
  13. Jacob's burial charge and death
    Genesis 49:28–33 · narrative · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    The narrator notes the twelve tribes and Jacob's blessings. Jacob then instructs his sons to bury him in the cave of Machpelah with Abraham and Isaac, and he dies.

    FaithThe promised land
  14. Joseph mourns and gets Pharaoh's permission
    Genesis 50:1–6 · narrative · 1859 BC – 1858 BC

    Joseph weeps over his father and has him embalmed. After the mourning period, he asks Pharaoh's court for leave to bury his father in Canaan, and Pharaoh agrees.

    Family and marriage
  15. The great funeral procession to Canaan
    Genesis 50:7–14 · narrative · 1859 BC – 1858 BC

    A large Egyptian and Israelite company accompanies Joseph. They mourn at Atad, and Jacob's sons bury him in the cave at Machpelah before returning to Egypt.

    Family and marriage
  16. Joseph's last words and death
    Genesis 50:22–26 · narrative · 1859 BC – 1805 BC

    Joseph lives to 110, sees great-grandchildren, and tells his brothers God will bring them to the promised land. He has them swear to carry his bones there, and dies.

    FaithPromise
  17. Keeping Israel from defiling the sanctuary
    Leviticus 15:31–33 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The Israelites are to be kept apart from their uncleanness so they do not die by defiling the LORD's dwelling among them. A summary then repeats the types of discharge these laws cover.

    HolinessTemple and tabernacle
  18. Draining the blood of game
    Leviticus 17:13–16 · law · 1445 BC

    An animal or bird hunted for food must have its blood poured out and covered with earth. Eating an animal found dead requires washing, and anyone who does not wash bears the consequences.

    Law and commandmentsHoliness
  19. Avoiding pagan practices
    Leviticus 19:26–31 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD forbids eating meat with blood in it, divination, sorcery, shaving the hair in certain ways, cutting the body for the dead, and turning to mediums. They must also keep his Sabbaths and honor the sanctuary.

    IdolatryLaw and commandments
  20. Priests and the dead
    Leviticus 21:1–6 · law · 1445 BC

    A priest is not to make himself unclean for the dead, except for close family. He must not make bald spots or cuts on his body, because he presents the food of God and must be holy.

    PriesthoodHoliness
  21. Rules for the high priest
    Leviticus 21:10–15 · law · 1445 BC

    The high priest, on whom the anointing oil has been poured, must not go near any dead body, even his parents'. He must not leave the sanctuary and may marry only a virgin from his own people.

    PriesthoodHoliness
  22. A warning to protect the Kohathites
    Numbers 4:17–20 · speech · 1445 BC

    The LORD says the Kohathites must not look at the holy objects even briefly, or they will die. Aaron's sons will assign each man his load.

    Holiness
  23. Corpse uncleanness requires purification
    Numbers 19:11–13 · law · 1445 BC – 1407 BC

    Touching a dead body makes a person unclean for seven days. He must be purified with the water on the third and seventh days, or he defiles the tabernacle and is cut off from Israel.

    HolinessAtonement and sacrifice
  24. How the purification water is used
    Numbers 19:14–22 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1407 BC

    God details uncleanness from death in a tent, in the open field or by a grave, and explains how a clean man sprinkles the ash-water with hyssop on day three and seven. Neglecting it defiles the sanctuary.

    HolinessAtonement and sacrifice
  25. Aaron dies on Mount Hor
    Numbers 20:22–29 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    At Mount Hor, God announces Aaron will die because of Meribah. Moses takes Aaron's priestly garments and puts them on Eleazar, Aaron dies on the mountain, and Israel mourns thirty days.

    PriesthoodLeadership
  26. Moses is told he will die
    Numbers 27:12–14 · dialogue · 1406 BC

    God tells Moses to go up the Abarim range to see the land, after which he will be gathered to his people like Aaron, because of the rebellion at Meribah.

    LeadershipSin and the fall
  27. From Kadesh to the plains of Moab
    Numbers 33:37–49 · list · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    The journey continues to Mount Hor, where Aaron dies at age 123, then on past Edom and Moab to the plains of Moab by the Jordan.

    Exodus and deliverance
  28. Passing by Moab; the old generation dies
    Deuteronomy 2:9–15 · narrative · 1407 BC

    God tells Israel not to provoke Moab, the descendants of Lot. A note explains earlier peoples of the area, and the crossing of the Zered marks thirty-eight years since Kadesh, when the old generation had died.

    God's sovereigntyThe nations
  29. You are God's people; do not mourn like the nations
    Deuteronomy 14:1–2 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses says Israel are sons of the LORD and holy, so they must not cut themselves or shave their foreheads in mourning for the dead.

    HolinessElection and calling
  30. Burial of a hanged body
    Deuteronomy 21:22–23 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    A body hanged on a tree after execution must be buried the same day, because someone hanged is under God's curse and the land must not be defiled.

    The crossJustice and judgment
