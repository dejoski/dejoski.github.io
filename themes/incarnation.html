The incarnation

24 passages · Jesus and the church

Matthew · 2Luke · 4John · 6Romans · 1Galatians · 1Philippians · 1Colossians · 21 Timothy · 1Hebrews · 21 John · 32 John · 1
  1. Joseph learns of Mary's pregnancy
    Matthew 1:18–21 · narrative · 6 BC – 5 BC

    Mary, engaged to Joseph, is found to be pregnant through the Holy Spirit. Joseph plans to end the engagement quietly, but an angel tells him in a dream to take Mary as his wife and name the child Jesus, who will save his people from their sins.

    The Holy SpiritDreams, visions and revelationObedience
  2. Fulfillment of Immanuel and Joseph's obedience
    Matthew 1:22–25 · narrative · 6 BC – 5 BC

    Matthew says these events fulfilled a prophecy that a virgin would bear a son called Immanuel, 'God with us.' Joseph obeys the angel, takes Mary as his wife, and names the child Jesus after he is born.

    ProphecyObedience
  3. Gabriel announces Jesus to Mary
    Luke 1:26–38 · dialogue · 7 BC – 5 BC

    Gabriel tells the young virgin Mary she will conceive a son named Jesus, who will be called Son of the Most High and reign on David's throne forever. Mary asks how, is told the Holy Spirit will come upon her, and accepts.

    Angels and spiritual beingsThe MessiahThe Holy Spirit
  4. Jesus is born in Bethlehem
    Luke 2:1–7 · narrative · 6 BC – 4 BC

    A Roman census sends Joseph and the expectant Mary from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the city of David. There she gives birth to her firstborn son and lays Him in a manger.

    ProvidenceKings and kingship
  5. Jesus is named and presented to the Lord
    Luke 2:21–24 · narrative · 6 BC – 4 BC

    At eight days Jesus is circumcised and named as the angel directed. Later His parents bring Him to Jerusalem to be presented and to offer the sacrifice the Law requires.

    ObedienceLaw and commandments
  6. Jesus' genealogy back to Adam
    Luke 3:23–38 · genealogy · AD 28 – AD 29

    Luke says Jesus was about thirty when He began and traces His line through Joseph back through David, Abraham and Noah to Adam, the son of God.

    Promise
  7. The Word, life, and light
    John 1:1–5 · poetry

    John opens by describing the Word as existing in the beginning, with God and as God, the agent of creation, and the source of life and light that darkness has not overcome.

    CreationFather, Son and SpiritGlory
  8. The Word becomes flesh
    John 1:14–18 · teaching

    The Word takes on human life and lives among people, revealing glory, grace and truth. John the Baptist's testimony is recalled, and the Son is said to make the unseen God known.

    GloryMercy and compassion
  9. Earthly and heavenly things
    John 3:9–15 · teaching · AD 27 – AD 28

    Puzzled, Nicodemus asks how this can be. Jesus rebukes his lack of understanding, speaks of testifying to what He has seen, and says the Son of Man must be lifted up like the serpent in the wilderness.

    FaithThe cross
  10. Grumbling over 'come down from heaven'
    John 6:41–51 · teaching · AD 29

    The Jews object that they know Jesus' family. He says no one comes unless the Father draws them, and repeats that He is the living bread that came down from heaven, given for the world's life.

    Election and calling
  11. I am the light of the world
    John 8:12–20 · dialogue · AD 29

    Jesus says He is the light of the world. The Pharisees say His self-testimony is invalid, and He replies that He and the Father testify together and that they know neither.

    Names of GodOpposition and persecution
  12. Philip asks to see the Father
    John 14:8–14 · dialogue · AD 30 – AD 33

    Philip asks Jesus to show them the Father. Jesus replies that seeing him is seeing the Father, points to his words and works, and promises that believers will do great works and that prayer in his name will be answered.

    Father, Son and SpiritFaith
  13. No condemnation in Christ Jesus
    Romans 8:1–4 · teaching · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul declares that there is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. What the law could not do, God did by sending his Son, so that the law's requirement is fulfilled in those who live by the Spirit.

    Justification and righteousnessFreedomThe Holy Spirit
  14. From slaves to sons
    Galatians 4:1–7 · teaching · AD 48 – AD 55

    Paul compares the time before Christ to an heir under guardians. When the time was right, God sent his Son to redeem those under the law, so believers are adopted and God's Spirit cries 'Abba, Father' in their hearts.

    RedemptionFreedomFather, Son and Spirit
  15. The mind of Christ
    Philippians 2:5–11 · poetry · AD 60 – AD 62

    Paul points to Christ, who was in the form of God but emptied Himself, took a servant's form and died on a cross. God then exalted Him so that every knee will bow and every tongue confess Jesus Christ is Lord.

    Pride and humilityThe cross
  16. Christ is supreme over all creation
    Colossians 1:15–20 · poetry · AD 60 – AD 62

    In an exalted passage, Paul describes the Son as the image of the invisible God, through whom all things were created and hold together. He is head of the church, first to rise from the dead, and through His cross God reconciles all things.

    CreationGloryFather, Son and Spirit
  17. Live in Christ, not empty philosophy
    Colossians 2:6–15 · exhortation · AD 60 – AD 62

    Paul urges them to keep living as they began, rooted in Christ, and not be captured by human philosophy. In Christ dwells the fullness of God and they share in His fullness, having been buried and raised with Him and forgiven, as Christ disarmed the powers at the cross.

    False teachingFaith
  18. Why Paul writes: the mystery of godliness
    1 Timothy 3:14–16 · letter · AD 62 – AD 65

    Paul explains that he writes so Timothy will know how to behave in God's household, the church. He closes with what appears to be an early confession about Christ's appearance, vindication and glory.

    The churchGlory
  19. Jesus crowned after suffering
    Hebrews 2:5–9 · teaching · AD 60 – AD 69

    Quoting a psalm about humanity's place, the author says everything is not yet visibly under human control. But Jesus, made briefly lower than the angels, is now crowned with glory because he suffered death for everyone.

    SufferingGlory
  20. Jesus shares in our humanity
    Hebrews 2:10–18 · teaching · AD 60 – AD 69

    The author says it was fitting to make Jesus perfect through suffering, and that he calls believers his brothers. He shared flesh and blood to defeat death's power and free people from fear, becoming a merciful and faithful high priest who can help those who are tempted.

    Death and mortalityPriesthoodTemptation and spiritual warfare
  21. The Word of life we have seen
    1 John 1:1–4 · letter · AD 85 – AD 95

    The author says he and others heard, saw and touched the Word of life, the eternal life that was with the Father. They announce it so readers may share fellowship with them, the Father and Jesus, and so their joy is complete.

    Eternal lifeJoy and thanksgiving
  22. Test the spirits
    1 John 4:1–6 · exhortation · AD 85 – AD 95

    John tells readers to test spirits, since many false prophets have gone out. A spirit that confesses Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God, and believers have overcome the false ones because greater is He who is in them.

    False teachingThe Holy Spirit
  23. The testimony about the Son
    1 John 5:6–12 · teaching · AD 85 – AD 95

    Jesus came by water and blood, and the Spirit testifies to this, so the Spirit, the water and the blood agree. God's testimony is greater than human testimony, and it is that God gave eternal life in His Son.

    Father, Son and SpiritThe Holy Spirit
  24. Beware of deceivers who deny Christ
    2 John 1:7–11 · exhortation · AD 85 – AD 95

    Many deceivers have gone out who deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. The readers are warned to remain in Christ's teaching and not to welcome or greet those who bring a different teaching.

    False teachingDiscipleship
