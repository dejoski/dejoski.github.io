  1. Moses complains to the LORD
    Exodus 5:22–23 · prayer · 1446 BC

    Moses asks the LORD why he has brought trouble on the people, since his going to Pharaoh has only made things worse and no rescue has come.

  2. Moses' desperate complaint
    Numbers 11:10–15 · prayer · 1445 BC

    Hearing the weeping, Moses is troubled and asks God why He put the burden of all the people on him. He says he cannot carry them alone and asks to die.

    PrayerLeadership
  3. Joshua's prayer of distress
    Joshua 7:6–9 · prayer · 1406 BC – 1405 BC

    Joshua and the elders mourn before the ark. Joshua asks the LORD why He brought Israel across the Jordan, and he worries about what the defeat will do to God's name.

    Prayer
  4. Return to Bethlehem: call me Mara
    Ruth 1:19–22 · dialogue · 1125 BC – 1100 BC

    The two women reach Bethlehem, and the townswomen are stirred at seeing Naomi. She asks to be called Mara, meaning bitter, saying the LORD has brought her back empty; they arrive as the barley harvest begins.

    Suffering
  5. Israel gathers at Mizpah to confess
    1 Samuel 7:5–6 · ritual instruction · 1060 BC – 1050 BC

    Samuel gathers Israel at Mizpah, where they pour out water, fast and confess that they have sinned against the LORD, and Samuel judges them there.

  6. David mourns and executes the messenger
    2 Samuel 1:11–16 · narrative · 1010 BC

    David and his men tear their clothes and fast in grief. David then condemns the Amalekite, who admitted killing the LORD's anointed, and has him put to death.

  7. David's lament, the Song of the Bow
    2 Samuel 1:17–27 · lament · 1010 BC

    David sings a lament for Saul and Jonathan, mourning the fall of Israel's heroes and asking that the Philistines not rejoice. He praises both men and grieves especially for his close friend Jonathan.

    Death and mortalityWar and conflict
  8. David publicly mourns Abner
    2 Samuel 3:31–39 · narrative · 1005 BC – 1003 BC

    David orders mourning, walks behind the bier, and sings a short lament at Abner's tomb. He fasts, and the people become convinced he had no part in the murder, though David admits the sons of Zeruiah are too fierce for him.

    Justice and judgmentKings and kingship
  9. The child's illness and death
    2 Samuel 12:16–23 · narrative · 989 BC – 985 BC

    The child falls ill and David fasts and lies on the ground pleading for him. When the child dies after seven days, David washes, worships, and eats, explaining that he can no longer bring the child back.

    Death and mortalityPrayer
  10. David's grief for Absalom
    2 Samuel 18:33 · lament · 972 BC – 971 BC

    David weeps aloud, wishing he had died in Absalom's place.

    2 Chronicles 20:1–4 · narrative · 853 BC – 848 BC

    Moabites, Ammonites, and others come against Judah. Alarmed, Jehoshaphat proclaims a fast, and people from all the towns of Judah gather to seek the LORD.

  12. A fast for a safe journey
    Ezra 8:21–23 · prayer · 458 BC

    Ezra proclaims a fast so the group can humble themselves before God and ask for protection on the road. He explains he was ashamed to ask the king for soldiers after saying God protects those who seek Him.

    PrayerFaith
  13. The people weep; Shecaniah proposes a covenant
    Ezra 10:1–6 · dialogue · 458 BC

    A large crowd gathers and weeps as Ezra prays. Shecaniah admits the unfaithfulness but says there is still hope, and urges a covenant to send away the foreign wives and children. Ezra makes the leaders swear to do so.

    RepentanceCovenant
  14. Nehemiah weeps, fasts, and prays
    Nehemiah 1:4 · narrative · 445 BC

    The news breaks Nehemiah's heart. He sits down, weeps, mourns for days and fasts, turning to God in prayer.

    PrayerRepentance
  15. Confession and a call to bless God
    Nehemiah 9:1–5 · ritual instruction · 444 BC

    On the twenty-fourth day, the Israelites gather fasting in sackcloth, separate from foreigners, confess their sins, and read the Law. The Levites then call the people to stand and bless God's glorious name.

    Esther 4:1–3 · narrative · 474 BC

    On learning of the decree, Mordecai tears his clothes and wails in sackcloth and ashes, stopping at the king's gate. Jews in every province fast, weep, and lament.

  17. Esther calls for a fast and resolves to act
    Esther 4:15–17 · speech · 474 BC

    Esther asks Mordecai to gather the Jews of Susa to fast for three days with her and her maidens. She then says she will go to the king despite the law, and Mordecai carries out her instructions.

    FaithLeadership
  18. Job curses the day he was born
    Job 3:1–10 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    After the seven days of silence, Job speaks and wishes the day of his birth had never happened. He piles up images of darkness to erase that day and night.

  19. Why was I not stillborn?
    Job 3:11–19 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job asks why he did not die at birth. He imagines death as a place of quiet rest where kings, the wicked, captives and slaves all lie at peace.

    Death and mortalitySuffering
  20. Why is life given to the bitter?
    Job 3:20–26 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job asks why light and life are given to people in misery who long for death. He says the trouble he always dreaded has come, and he has no rest.

    SufferingDeath and mortality
  21. Job complains to God about sleepless terror
    Job 7:11–16 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says he will not hold back his complaint. He asks why God guards him like a sea monster, and says nightmares terrify him until he would rather die.

    SufferingPrayer
  22. Job asks God why He prosecutes him
    Job 10:1–7 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says he loathes his life and speaks to God directly, asking not to be condemned and to be told the charge. He asks whether God's view is limited like human sight.

    Justice and judgmentPrayer
  23. Why was I born? Leave me a little comfort
    Job 10:18–22 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job again asks why God brought him out of the womb and wishes he had died. He asks for a little relief before he goes to the dark land of death.

    Death and mortalitySuffering
  24. Job longs for God's earlier blessing
    Job 29:1–6 · lament · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Job looks back wistfully on the days when God protected him, his family was around him and life was abundant.

    Suffering
  25. Job accuses God of turning on him
    Job 30:16–23 · lament · 2100 BC – 1800 BC

    Job describes constant pain and says God grabs and throws him down. He cries for help, but he feels God does not answer and expects death.

    Psalms 6:1–3 · lament · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The psalmist begs the LORD not to rebuke him in anger and asks for mercy and healing. He describes his bones and soul as deeply troubled and asks how long this will last.

  28. Deliver me; I am worn out with weeping
    Psalms 6:4–7 · lament · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He asks God to turn to him and save him out of loving devotion, noting that the dead cannot praise God. He describes nights of crying that leave his eyes weak with sorrow because of his enemies.

    SufferingDeath and mortality
  29. Why do you stand far off?
    Psalms 10:1–2 · lament

    The psalmist asks why the LORD seems distant in times of trouble while the wicked chase the poor. He asks that the wicked be caught in their own schemes.

    SufferingJustice for the poor and oppressed
  30. Help: the faithful have vanished
    Psalms 12:1–4 · lament · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The psalmist asks the LORD for help because honest people seem gone and everyone lies with flattery. He asks God to cut off boastful lips of those who think their words make them masters.
