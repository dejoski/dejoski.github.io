  1. God's command about the trees
    Genesis 2:15–17 · narrative

    God places the man in the garden to work and care for it. He allows eating from every tree but forbids the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, warning of death.

    Work and restDeath and mortality
  2. God's instructions for the ark
    Genesis 6:14–22 · ritual instruction

    God tells Noah to build an ark of specified size and design, promises a covenant, and instructs him to bring his family and pairs of animals with food aboard. Noah obeys.

    SalvationCovenant
  3. God calls Noah into the ark
    Genesis 7:1–5 · narrative

    God tells Noah to enter the ark with his family because he is righteous, with seven pairs of clean animals and birds and a pair of the unclean. Noah does as commanded.

    Salvation
  4. Noah and the animals enter
    Genesis 7:6–10 · narrative

    Noah, his family, and the animals go into the ark two by two, as commanded, and after seven days the floodwaters come.

    Salvation
  5. God tells Noah to leave the ark
    Genesis 8:15–19 · narrative

    God tells Noah to come out with his family and the animals so they can spread over the earth and multiply. They all leave the ark.

    New creation
  6. Circumcision as the covenant sign
    Genesis 17:9–14 · law · 2068 BC – 2066 BC

    God commands every male in Abraham's household to be circumcised at eight days old as the sign of the covenant. Any male who is not is cut off from his people.

    Covenant
  7. Abraham circumcises his household
    Genesis 17:23–27 · narrative · 2068 BC – 2066 BC

    Abraham obeys that very day, circumcising Ishmael, aged thirteen, himself at ninety-nine, and every male in his household.

    Covenant
  8. God tests Abraham
    Genesis 22:1–8 · narrative · 2056 BC – 2040 BC

    God tells Abraham to offer Isaac as a burnt offering on Moriah. Abraham sets out early. Isaac asks where the lamb is, and Abraham answers that God will provide.

    FaithAtonement and sacrifice
  9. Abraham makes his servant swear an oath
    Genesis 24:1–9 · dialogue · 2027 BC – 2026 BC

    Aging Abraham has his chief servant swear not to choose a Canaanite wife for Isaac but to find one among Abraham's own relatives. Isaac must not be taken back to the old homeland, and God's promise of the land is the reason.

    Family and marriage
  10. Return to Bethel
    Genesis 35:1–8 · narrative · 1900 BC – 1895 BC

    God tells Jacob to go to Bethel and build an altar. Jacob has his household remove their foreign gods, and at Bethel he builds an altar; Rebekah's nurse Deborah dies there.

    Worship and praiseIdolatry
  11. The midwives defy Pharaoh's order
    Exodus 1:15–22 · narrative · 1530 BC – 1526 BC

    Pharaoh orders the Hebrew midwives to kill baby boys, but they fear God and let them live. When questioned, they give an excuse, and Pharaoh then orders all newborn Hebrew boys thrown into the Nile.

    Opposition and persecution
  12. Moses heads back to Egypt
    Exodus 4:18–23 · narrative · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Moses gets Jethro's blessing and sets out with his family and the staff of God. The LORD warns that Pharaoh's heart will be hardened and gives the message to deliver: let my firstborn son Israel go.

    God's sovereignty
  13. The LORD meets Moses on the road
    Exodus 4:24–26 · narrative · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    At a lodging place the LORD confronts Moses and is about to kill him. Zipporah circumcises her son and touches Moses' feet with the foreskin, and the LORD lets him alone.

    Covenant
  14. The Feast of Unleavened Bread
    Exodus 12:14–20 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    The day is to be a memorial and a lasting statute. For seven days no leaven is to be eaten, and there are to be sacred assemblies on the first and last days.

    Sabbath and feasts
  15. The LORD promises bread and meat
    Exodus 16:4–12 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD says he will rain down bread from heaven and test whether they will follow his instructions, with a double portion on the sixth day. Moses says their grumbling is against the LORD, whose glory then appears in the cloud.

    Glory
  16. The Sabbath and the double portion
    Exodus 16:22–30 · narrative · 1446 BC

    On the sixth day the people gather twice as much, and Moses explains that the next day is a holy Sabbath. What is kept keeps fresh, some go out looking on the seventh day and find nothing, and the people rest.

    Sabbath and feasts
  17. God offers Israel a covenant at Sinai
    Exodus 19:1–8 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    Israel camps before Sinai and the LORD tells Moses to remind them how he carried them on eagles' wings. If they obey his covenant they will be his treasured possession, a kingdom of priests, and they answer that they will do all he says.

    CovenantElection and calling
  18. The people are afraid and stand at a distance
    Exodus 20:18–21 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The people tremble at the thunder and smoke and ask Moses to speak instead of God. Moses says God has come to test them and keep them from sinning, and he approaches the thick darkness.

    Holiness
  19. The angel and a promise of blessing
    Exodus 23:20–26 · speech · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    God says he is sending an angel ahead to guard them and lead them to the promised place. If they obey, God will be an enemy to their enemies, bless their food and water, and remove sickness.

    Angels and spiritual beingsPromise
  20. Covenant sealed with blood and vow
    Exodus 24:1–8 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    The LORD summons Moses and leaders to worship from a distance. Moses relays the laws, the people promise to obey, and Moses writes them down, builds an altar, offers sacrifices, reads the Book of the Covenant and sprinkles blood on the people.

    CovenantAtonement and sacrifice
  21. More than enough is given
    Exodus 36:1–7 · narrative · 1446 BC

    Bezalel, Oholiab and skilled workers receive the offerings from Moses. The people keep bringing gifts every morning until the workers report there is too much, and Moses has the people stopped from giving more.

    Generosity and giving
  22. Making the veil and entrance curtain
    Exodus 36:35–38 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The workers make the cherub-embroidered veil with its gold-covered posts and silver bases, and the entrance curtain with its five posts and bronze bases.

    Temple and tabernacle
  23. The inventory and its makers
    Exodus 38:21–23 · list · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The tabernacle's inventory was recorded by the Levites under Ithamar. Bezalel made everything the LORD commanded, with Oholiab as his assistant.

    Temple and tabernacle
  24. Making the ephod
    Exodus 39:1–7 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The workers weave the priestly garments for ministry. Bezalel makes the ephod with gold thread and colored yarn, and the onyx stones engraved with the names of Israel's sons are fastened to its shoulders.

    Priesthood
  25. Work finished and inspected
    Exodus 39:32–43 · narrative · 1445 BC

    The Israelites complete all the work and bring every part of the tabernacle and garments to Moses. He inspects it, sees it was done as the LORD commanded, and blesses them.

    Temple and tabernacleWork and rest
  26. Moses sets up the tabernacle
    Exodus 40:16–33 · narrative · 1445 BC

    On the first day of the first month of the second year, Moses assembles the tabernacle, places the ark, table, lamps, altars and basin, and hangs the curtains. The text repeats that he did each thing as the LORD commanded.

    Temple and tabernacle
  27. Moses assembles Israel for the ordination
    Leviticus 8:1–5 · narrative · 1445 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to gather Aaron, his sons, their garments, the oil, and the animals at the entrance of the Tent. Moses obeys and tells the assembled congregation what the LORD has commanded.

    Priesthood
  28. The ordination meal and seven days
    Leviticus 8:31–36 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Moses tells Aaron and his sons to eat the ordination meat and bread at the entrance and burn what is left. They must stay there seven days, and they do everything the LORD commanded.

    Priesthood
  29. Moses commands offerings on the eighth day
    Leviticus 9:1–6 · narrative · 1445 BC

    On the eighth day Moses summons Aaron, his sons, and the elders, and lists the animals for Aaron and the people to offer. He says the LORD will appear to them that day.

    PriesthoodGlory
  30. Nadab and Abihu offer unauthorized fire
    Leviticus 10:1–7 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu offer fire the LORD had not commanded, and fire from the LORD kills them. Moses has their cousins carry the bodies out of the camp and tells Aaron and his other sons not to mourn openly.

    HolinessWrath
