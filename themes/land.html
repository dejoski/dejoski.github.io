  1. Abram travels through Canaan
    Genesis 12:4–9 · narrative · 2091 BC – 2090 BC

    Abram, seventy-five, leaves with Sarai and Lot. At Shechem the Lord promises the land to his offspring, and Abram builds altars there and near Bethel.

    PromiseWorship and praise
  2. God renews the promise to Abram
    Genesis 13:14–18 · speech · 2088 BC – 2085 BC

    After Lot leaves, God tells Abram to look in every direction, promising all the land to him and his offspring forever, as numerous as the dust. Abram moves to Hebron and builds an altar.

    Promise
  3. The covenant ritual prepared
    Genesis 15:7–11 · ritual instruction · 2083 BC – 2081 BC

    God says he brought Abram to give him the land. When Abram asks how he will know, God has him prepare animals, which Abram cuts in halves and guards from birds of prey.

    Covenant
  4. Abraham asks the Hittites for a burial place
    Genesis 23:3–9 · dialogue · 2030 BC – 2028 BC

    Abraham, calling himself a foreigner, asks for burial land. The Hittites respond respectfully, and he asks them to approach Ephron for the cave of Machpelah at full price.

    Faith
  5. Ephron and Abraham bargain
    Genesis 23:10–16 · dialogue · 2030 BC – 2028 BC

    Ephron publicly offers the field and cave as a gift, but Abraham insists on paying. Ephron names four hundred shekels, and Abraham weighs out the silver.

    Wealth and poverty
  6. Abraham buries Sarah in Machpelah
    Genesis 23:17–20 · narrative · 2030 BC – 2028 BC

    The field and cave are formally transferred to Abraham before the Hittites, and he buries Sarah there. It becomes his first owned piece of the promised land.

    PromiseDeath and mortality
  7. Joseph objects; Jacob explains and looks ahead
    Genesis 48:17–22 · dialogue · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    Joseph objects that Manasseh is the firstborn, but Jacob insists Ephraim will be greater. He assures Joseph that God will bring the family back to Canaan and gives him a portion of land.

    PromiseFaith
  8. Jacob's burial charge and death
    Genesis 49:28–33 · narrative · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    The narrator notes the twelve tribes and Jacob's blessings. Jacob then instructs his sons to bury him in the cave of Machpelah with Abraham and Isaac, and he dies.

    Death and mortalityFaith
  9. Driving out the nations and no covenants
    Exodus 23:27–33 · speech · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    God promises to send terror and the hornet ahead of them and drive nations out little by little. He sets borders for the land and warns Israel not to make covenants with them or their gods.

    CovenantIdolatry
  10. Covenant renewed: no other gods
    Exodus 34:10–17 · law · 1446 BC

    God promises wonders and driving out the peoples of Canaan. He commands Israel not to make treaties with them, to destroy their altars, and to worship no other god or make molten idols.

    IdolatryCovenant
  11. Mildew in a house
    Leviticus 14:33–47 · law · 1445 BC

    When the Israelites are in Canaan and the LORD puts mildew in a house, the owner tells the priest. The priest has the house emptied and inspected, and closes it for seven days. Contaminated stones and plaster are removed, and a house where it keeps spreading is torn down.

    Purity and sexual ethicsHoliness
  12. The land vomits out its defilers
    Leviticus 18:24–30 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The LORD warns that the nations he is driving out defiled the land through these acts, and the land will vomit out the Israelites if they do the same. They must keep his charge and avoid these customs.

    Purity and sexual ethicsJustice and judgment
  13. Fruit of new trees
    Leviticus 19:23–25 · law · 1445 BC

    When the Israelites plant fruit trees in the land, they are to treat the fruit as forbidden for three years and dedicate the fourth-year fruit to the LORD. In the fifth year they may eat it.

    PromiseWorship and praise
  14. Be holy and different from the nations
    Leviticus 20:22–27 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The Israelites are to keep all the LORD's statutes so the land does not vomit them out. They are to be holy because he has set them apart. Mediums and spiritists are to be put to death.

    HolinessThe nations
  15. Firstfruits of the harvest
    Leviticus 23:9–14 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Once in the land, Israelites must bring a sheaf of the first grain to the priest, who waves it before the LORD with a lamb and other offerings. No new grain may be eaten until this is done.

    Sabbath and feastsWorship and praise
  16. The Sabbath year for the land
    Leviticus 25:1–7 · law · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Once in the land, Israel is to farm for six years and let the land rest completely in the seventh. What grows on its own may be eaten by everyone, including servants and animals.

    Sabbath and feastsFaith
  17. The Year of Jubilee
    Leviticus 25:8–13 · law · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    After seven cycles of seven years, the fiftieth year is announced with a horn on the Day of Atonement. Liberty is proclaimed and each family returns to its property.

    FreedomRedemption
  18. Redeeming land and property
    Leviticus 25:23–34 · law · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Land belongs to God and cannot be sold permanently, so a poor Israelite's relative may redeem it, and it returns at Jubilee. Houses in walled cities are treated differently, and Levite property is always redeemable.

    Redemption
  19. Valuing people vowed to the LORD
    Leviticus 27:1–8 · law · 1445 BC

    When someone makes a special vow involving a person's value, the payment is set by age and sex in sanctuary shekels. A person too poor to pay may have the priest set a lower amount.

    Worship and praise
  20. Consecrated fields and the Jubilee
    Leviticus 27:16–25 · law · 1445 BC

    A field given to God is valued by the seed it needs and by the years left until Jubilee, and the owner may redeem it by adding a fifth. If not redeemed, it becomes the priests' property at Jubilee.

    Worship and praiseSabbath and feasts
  21. Moses appoints twelve spies
    Numbers 13:1–16 · list · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to send one leader from each tribe to explore Canaan. The twelve are named, and Moses renames Hoshea as Joshua.

    Leadership
  22. The spies explore the land
    Numbers 13:17–24 · narrative · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    Moses tells them what to look for, and they travel through the land to Hebron and the Valley of Eshcol, where they cut a huge cluster of grapes.

    Faith
  23. The spies' report
    Numbers 13:25–29 · speech · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    After forty days the spies return to Kadesh. They show the fruit and say the land is rich, but its people are strong and its cities are large.

    Faith
  24. Forty years in the wilderness
    Numbers 14:26–38 · oracle of judgment · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    God says the adults who grumbled will die in the wilderness and their children will enter the land after forty years. The spies who gave the bad report die by plague.

    Justice and judgment
  25. Grain and drink offerings with sacrifices
    Numbers 15:1–16 · ritual instruction · 1444 BC – 1406 BC

    God gives Moses rules for the grain and wine offered with animal sacrifices once Israel is in the land, and states the same rules apply to foreigners.

    Worship and praiseAtonement and sacrifice
  26. Offering the first of the dough
    Numbers 15:17–21 · ritual instruction · 1444 BC – 1406 BC

    When Israel eats the food of the land, they are to give the first of their dough as an offering to the LORD.

    Worship and praise
  27. The Levites' inheritance is the tithe
    Numbers 18:20–24 · law · 1445 BC – 1407 BC

    God tells Aaron the priests receive no land, since God Himself is their portion. The Levites likewise get no land but receive Israel's tithes in return for their service.

    PriesthoodWorship and praise
  28. Camps along the way and a well song
    Numbers 21:10–20 · history · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel travels stage by stage toward Moab, quoting the Book of the Wars of the LORD. At Beer they sing a song celebrating the well that leaders dug, before continuing to the valley below Pisgah.

    Worship and praise
  29. Sihon of the Amorites defeated
    Numbers 21:21–26 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel asks Sihon for passage along the King's Highway. Sihon refuses and attacks, but Israel defeats him and settles in his cities, including Heshbon.

    War and conflict
  30. Og of Bashan defeated
    Numbers 21:31–35 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel takes villages near Jazer and moves up to Bashan, where King Og meets them at Edrei. God tells Moses not to fear, and Israel destroys Og's army and takes his land.

    War and conflictPromise
