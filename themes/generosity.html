  1. Abram lets Lot choose
    Genesis 13:8–13 · dialogue · 2088 BC – 2085 BC

    Abram proposes peaceful separation and offers Lot first choice. Lot picks the well-watered Jordan plain and settles near Sodom, whose people are wicked.

  2. Rebekah gives water and is identified
    Genesis 24:15–27 · narrative · 2026 BC

    Before he finishes praying, Rebekah arrives and generously waters all ten camels. The servant gives her gifts, learns she is Abraham's relative, and worships God for guiding him.

    ProvidencePrayer
  3. Offering for a sanctuary
    Exodus 25:1–9 · law · 1446 BC

    God asks the Israelites to give freewill offerings of metals, yarn, skins, wood, oil, spices and gems. These materials will build a sanctuary so that God may dwell among them, following a pattern he will show.

    Temple and tabernacle
  4. Call for offerings and skilled workers
    Exodus 35:4–19 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses tells the people what the LORD commands: bring willing offerings of materials and let skilled craftsmen come make everything on the list, from the tabernacle to the priestly garments.

    Temple and tabernacleWork and rest
  5. The people give freely
    Exodus 35:20–29 · narrative · 1446 BC

    The people go and bring gold jewelry, yarn, linen, skins, wood, metals, gems, spices and oil. Skilled women spin yarn, and all whose hearts are willing, men and women, give.

    Work and rest
  6. More than enough is given
    Exodus 36:1–7 · narrative · 1446 BC

    Bezalel, Oholiab and skilled workers receive the offerings from Moses. The people keep bringing gifts every morning until the workers report there is too much, and Moses has the people stopped from giving more.

    Obedience
  7. Leaving harvest for the poor
    Leviticus 19:9–10 · law · 1445 BC

    Farmers are not to harvest to the edges of their fields or strip their vineyards bare. They are to leave gleanings for the poor and the foreigner.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedLoving others
  8. The Feast of Weeks and gleaning
    Leviticus 23:15–22 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Seven weeks, fifty days, after the firstfruits sheaf, Israel is to offer two loaves of leavened bread with animal offerings as a sacred assembly. The passage ends by telling farmers to leave the edges of their fields for the poor and foreigners.

    Sabbath and feastsLoving others
  9. Helping a poor countryman
    Leviticus 25:35–38 · law · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    An Israelite who becomes destitute is to be supported like a resident foreigner and not charged interest or profit. God grounds this in having brought Israel out of Egypt.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedLoving others
  10. The tithe
    Leviticus 27:30–34 · law · 1445 BC

    A tenth of the land's produce and of every tenth animal belongs to the LORD. Someone who wants to buy back part of the tithe must add a fifth, and the book ends by noting these commands were given at Sinai.

    Worship and praiseObedience
  11. Carts and oxen given to the Levites
    Numbers 7:1–9 · narrative · 1445 BC

    When the tabernacle is finished and consecrated, the twelve leaders bring six carts and twelve oxen. Moses gives them to the Gershonites and Merarites, but none to the Kohathites, who carry sacred objects on their shoulders.

    Worship and praiseLeadership
  12. Dedication gifts begin: Judah on day one
    Numbers 7:10–17 · narrative · 1445 BC

    The leaders bring offerings for the altar one day at a time. Nahshon of Judah goes first, with silver and gold vessels, burnt, sin and peace offerings.

    Worship and praise
  13. Days two to five of the dedication gifts
    Numbers 7:18–41 · list · 1445 BC

    Issachar, Zebulun, Reuben and Simeon's leaders each bring the same offering on their day, one after the other.

    Worship and praise
  14. Days six to nine of the dedication gifts
    Numbers 7:42–65 · list · 1445 BC

    Gad, Ephraim, Manasseh and Benjamin's leaders present exactly the same gifts on their days.

    Worship and praise
  15. Days ten to twelve of the dedication gifts
    Numbers 7:66–83 · list · 1445 BC

    The leaders of Dan, Asher and Naphtali complete the twelve days with the same offering.

    Worship and praise
  16. Totals of the altar dedication offerings
    Numbers 7:84–88 · list · 1445 BC

    The total of silver, gold and animals from the twelve leaders is added up as the dedication offering for the altar.

    Worship and praise
  17. Tithes and the poor
    Deuteronomy 14:22–29 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel is to set aside a tenth of its produce each year and eat it at God's chosen place, or exchange it for money if too far. Every third year the tithe is stored in the towns for the Levites, foreigners, orphans, and widows.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedJoy and thanksgiving
  18. Be generous to the poor
    Deuteronomy 15:7–11 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses commands Israel not to harden their hearts against the poor as the release year nears, but to lend freely and give generously. There will always be poor in the land, so they are to open their hands.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedMercy and compassion
  19. Freeing Hebrew servants
    Deuteronomy 15:12–18 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    A Hebrew servant must be set free in the seventh year and sent away with generous supplies, remembering Israel's own slavery in Egypt. A servant who wishes to stay may become a servant for life.

    FreedomRedemption
  20. Leaving the gleanings for the needy
    Deuteronomy 24:19–22 · law · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Farmers must leave forgotten sheaves, leftover olives, and leftover grapes for foreigners, orphans, and widows. Israel is again reminded that it was once enslaved in Egypt.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedExodus and deliverance
  21. The tithe declaration in the third year
    Deuteronomy 26:12–15 · ritual instruction · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Every third year the tithe is given to the Levite, foreigner, orphan, and widow. The giver then declares before God that he has done this faithfully and asks for God's blessing on the people and land.

    Worship and praiseObedience
  22. Meal, extra grain, and a full ephah
    Ruth 2:14–17 · narrative · 1124 BC – 1100 BC

    At mealtime Boaz shares food with Ruth and quietly orders his men to leave extra stalks for her. She gleans until evening and gathers about an ephah of barley.

    Loving others
  23. Abigail sets out with gifts
    1 Samuel 25:18–22 · narrative · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Abigail quickly loads donkeys with bread, wine, meat, grain, raisins and figs and sends them ahead without telling Nabal. Meanwhile David, riding toward her, has just vowed to kill every male of Nabal's household by morning.

    Leadership
