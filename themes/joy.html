Joy and thanksgiving

  1. Joseph meets Benjamin and hosts a feast
    Genesis 43:26–34 · narrative · 1876 BC – 1875 BC

    Joseph asks about his father, blesses Benjamin, and withdraws to weep. He then seats his brothers by age and gives Benjamin a portion five times larger.

    Family and marriage
  2. Jacob learns Joseph is alive
    Genesis 45:25–28 · narrative · 1876 BC – 1875 BC

    The brothers tell Jacob that Joseph is alive and ruling Egypt. Jacob doubts, but the wagons revive his spirit, and he resolves to go see Joseph before he dies.

    Family and marriage
  3. Jacob and Joseph reunite in Goshen
    Genesis 46:28–34 · narrative · 1876 BC – 1875 BC

    Judah goes ahead, and Joseph meets his father in Goshen with tears. Jacob says he can now die in peace, and Joseph coaches his brothers on how to answer Pharaoh.

    Family and marriage
  4. Law of the peace offering
    Leviticus 7:11–21 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A peace offering of thanksgiving comes with cakes and must be eaten the same day. Vow or freewill offerings can be eaten for two days, and anyone unclean who eats the meat is cut off from the people.

    Worship and praiseHoliness
  5. The Feast of Tabernacles
    Leviticus 23:33–44 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    For seven days beginning on the fifteenth of the seventh month, Israel is to celebrate the harvest by rejoicing and living in booths, remembering the exodus from Egypt. The chapter closes by summarizing the feasts and noting that Moses announced them.

    Sabbath and feastsExodus and deliverance
  6. Tithes and the poor
    Deuteronomy 14:22–29 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel is to set aside a tenth of its produce each year and eat it at God's chosen place, or exchange it for money if too far. Every third year the tithe is stored in the towns for the Levites, foreigners, orphans, and widows.

    Generosity and givingJustice for the poor and oppressed
  7. Feast of Weeks, Tabernacles, and appearing before God
    Deuteronomy 16:9–17 · ritual instruction · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel is to keep the Feast of Weeks seven weeks after harvest begins, and the Feast of Tabernacles for seven days after the harvest. All must rejoice with their households, the Levites, foreigners, and the poor.

    Sabbath and feastsWorship and praise
  8. Deborah and Barak begin their song
    Judges 5:1–5 · praise · 1220 BC – 1125 BC

    Deborah and Barak begin a victory song, calling on the LORD to be blessed and on kings and princes to listen. They recall how the LORD marched out from Seir so that the earth shook, before the God of Sinai.

    Worship and praiseWar and conflict
  9. A son is born to Naomi
    Ruth 4:13–17 · narrative · 1122 BC – 1095 BC

    Boaz marries Ruth and she bears a son. The women bless the LORD for giving Naomi a redeemer, and the child, named Obed, is the grandfather of David.

    Family and marriageMercy and compassion
  10. The ark enters Jerusalem with rejoicing
    2 Samuel 6:12–19 · narrative · 1002 BC – 1000 BC

    Hearing that Obed-edom was blessed, David brings the ark up with sacrifices, dancing, shouting and the ram's horn. He offers sacrifices, blesses the people, and gives everyone food.

    Worship and praiseSabbath and feasts
  11. Sacrifices and the festival
    1 Kings 8:62–66 · ritual instruction · 959 BC – 958 BC

    Solomon and Israel offer huge numbers of sacrifices and keep a feast for fourteen days. The people go home joyful.

    Worship and praiseSabbath and feasts
  12. Three days of feasting at Hebron
    1 Chronicles 12:38–40 · narrative · 1003 BC

    All the men came determined to make David king over all Israel and shared a three-day feast. Neighbors brought abundant food, and there was joy in Israel.

    UnityKings and kingship
  13. The ark leaves Kiriath-jearim
    1 Chronicles 13:6–8 · narrative · 1003 BC – 1000 BC

    David and Israel go to Kiriath-jearim and set the ark on a new cart. With Uzzah and Ahio guiding it, the people celebrate before God with songs and instruments.

    Worship and praiseObedience
  14. The ark enters Jerusalem with joy
    1 Chronicles 15:25–29 · narrative · 1000 BC – 997 BC

    David, the elders and commanders bring the ark from Obed-edom with sacrifices, shouting, trumpets and music. As it enters the City of David, David dances in a linen ephod and Michal despises him in her heart.

    Worship and praise
  15. Song: give thanks and cry for salvation
    1 Chronicles 16:34–36 · praise · 1000 BC – 997 BC

    The song ends with thanks for God's goodness and lasting love, and a prayer to gather and save Israel from the nations. It closes with a blessing, and the people answer Amen and Praise the Lord.

    Mercy and compassionPrayerSalvation
  16. Blessing and peace
    2 Chronicles 20:26–30 · narrative · 853 BC – 848 BC

    On the fourth day the people bless the LORD in the Valley of Beracah and return joyfully to the temple with instruments. Surrounding kingdoms fear God and Jehoshaphat's kingdom has rest.

    Peace and reconciliationWorship and praise
  17. The chest and the repair work
    2 Chronicles 24:8–14 · narrative · 830 BC – 800 BC

    A chest is placed at the temple gate and the people joyfully bring the tax set by Moses. The money pays workers to restore the temple, and leftover funds make gold and silver articles.

    Generosity and givingTemple and tabernacle
  18. Thank offerings and rejoicing
    2 Chronicles 29:31–36 · narrative · 729 BC – 715 BC

    The people bring sacrifices and thank offerings in great numbers. Levites help the too few priests, and the people rejoice at how quickly God had prepared everything.

    Worship and praise
  19. Seven days of joy, then seven more
    2 Chronicles 30:21–27 · praise · 716 BC – 714 BC

    The people celebrate the Feast of Unleavened Bread with great joy and music, and agree to extend it another seven days, helped by generous gifts from the king and officials. The celebration is described as unmatched since Solomon, and the priests and Levites bless the people.

    Worship and praise
  20. The foundation laid amid praise and weeping
    Ezra 3:10–13 · praise · 536 BC

    When the foundation is laid, priests and Levites praise the LORD as David had prescribed, and the people shout. Older people who remembered the first temple weep, and joy and weeping blend into one great sound.

    Temple and tabernacleWorship and praise
  21. Passover and Unleavened Bread celebrated
    Ezra 6:19–22 · narrative · 515 BC

    The priests and Levites purify themselves, and the exiles keep Passover and then a joyful seven-day Feast of Unleavened Bread. The writer credits the LORD with giving them joy.

    Sabbath and feastsHoliness
  22. Do not weep: the joy of the LORD
    Nehemiah 8:9–12 · speech · 444 BC

    Hearing the Law, the people weep. Nehemiah, Ezra, and the Levites tell them the day is holy and to celebrate with food and sharing, for the joy of the LORD is their strength, and the people rejoice because they understood.

    Law and commandments
  23. Preparing for the wall's dedication
    Nehemiah 12:27–30 · narrative · 444 BC – 432 BC

    The Levites and singers are brought to Jerusalem for a joyful dedication of the wall with music. The priests and Levites purify themselves, and then the people, the gates, and the wall.

    Worship and praiseTemple and tabernacle
  24. Two thanksgiving choirs process on the wall
    Nehemiah 12:31–43 · narrative · 444 BC – 432 BC

    Nehemiah leads two large choirs in opposite directions along the top of the wall, with priests and musicians. They meet at the temple, offer sacrifices, and rejoice so loudly that Jerusalem is heard from afar.

    Worship and praise
  25. Joy among the Jews
    Esther 8:15–17 · narrative · 474 BC

    Mordecai leaves the king in royal robes and a gold crown, and Susa rejoices. Jews everywhere celebrate with feasting, and many people of the land join them out of fear.

    SalvationSabbath and feasts
  26. Mordecai establishes Purim
    Esther 9:20–28 · narrative · 473 BC – 472 BC

    Mordecai writes to all Jews to observe the fourteenth and fifteenth of Adar yearly as days of feasting, gifts, and giving to the poor. The name Purim comes from Pur, the lot Haman cast.

    Sabbath and feastsGenerosity and giving
  27. Joy and peaceful sleep in God
    Psalms 4:6–8 · praise · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    Many ask who can show them good, but the psalmist asks the LORD to let His face shine on them. He says God has given him deeper joy than a good harvest, and he will lie down in peace because God keeps him safe.

    Peace and reconciliationPrayer
  28. Those who take refuge will rejoice
    Psalms 5:11–12 · blessing · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The psalm ends by asking that all who take refuge in God be glad and sing for joy. It affirms that the LORD blesses the righteous and covers them with His favor like a shield.

    Faith
  29. I trust your loving devotion
    Psalms 13:5–6 · praise · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The mood changes to trust: the psalmist says he trusts God's loving devotion and will rejoice in salvation and sing to the LORD for His goodness.

    FaithSalvation
  30. The LORD is my portion
    Psalms 16:5–8 · praise · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He calls the LORD his chosen portion and says his inheritance is pleasant. He blesses God for counsel and keeps Him always before him, so he will not be shaken.

    FaithPromise
