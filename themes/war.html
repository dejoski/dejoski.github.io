  1. War of the kings and Lot's capture
    Genesis 14:1–12 · narrative · 2085 BC – 2082 BC

    A coalition of four kings led by Chedorlaomer campaigns against five rebel kings, including Sodom and Gomorrah's. They win, take goods, and carry off Lot.

  2. Abram rescues Lot
    Genesis 14:13–16 · narrative · 2085 BC – 2082 BC

    Told of Lot's capture, Abram leads 318 trained men in pursuit, splits his forces by night, defeats the raiders, and recovers Lot and the goods.

    Leadership
  3. Moses and Israel sing of victory
    Exodus 15:1–10 · praise · 1446 BC

    In song, Israel praises the LORD as strength, salvation, and warrior. They celebrate how he threw Pharaoh's horse and rider into the sea and how the enemy's boast ended.

    Worship and praiseSalvation
  4. Israel fights the Amalekites
    Exodus 17:8–13 · narrative · 1446 BC

    The Amalekites attack, and Joshua leads the fight while Moses, Aaron, and Hur are on the hill. Israel wins while Moses' hands are raised, and Aaron and Hur hold them up when he tires.

    PrayerLeadership
  5. A memorial and an altar
    Exodus 17:14–16 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to write the event on a scroll and tell Joshua that Amalek will be wiped out. Moses builds an altar called The LORD Is My Banner.

  6. God orders a census of fighting men
    Numbers 1:1–4 · speech · 1445 BC

    At Sinai, in the second year after leaving Egypt, the LORD tells Moses to count every man twenty or older who can serve in the army, with a tribal head helping.

    Leadership
  7. Tribe-by-tribe count of the men
    Numbers 1:20–43 · list · 1445 BC

    Each tribe's number of men twenty or older able to serve is recorded in turn, from Reuben's 46,500 to Naphtali's 53,400.

  8. The census total: 603,550
    Numbers 1:44–46 · history · 1445 BC

    The count is summed up as carried out by Moses, Aaron and the twelve leaders, with a total of 603,550 men.

    Promise
  9. Reuben's camp to the south
    Numbers 2:10–16 · speech · 1445 BC

    Reuben, Simeon and Gad camp on the south side, numbering 151,450, and march second.

  10. Dan's camp to the north; Israel obeys
    Numbers 2:25–34 · speech · 1445 BC

    Dan, Asher and Naphtali camp on the north and march last. The chapter closes with the total of 603,550 and Israel's obedience.

    Obedience
  11. The ark leads; Moses' prayers
    Numbers 10:33–36 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Israel journeys three days with the ark ahead and the cloud above. When the ark set out, Moses called on the LORD to rise and scatter enemies, and when it rested, to return to Israel.

    Prayer
  12. A failed attempt to enter the land
    Numbers 14:39–45 · narrative · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    The people mourn, then try to invade against Moses' warning. The Amalekites and Canaanites defeat them at Hormah.

    ObedienceSin and the fall
  13. Victory over the king of Arad
    Numbers 21:1–3 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    A Canaanite king attacks Israel and takes prisoners. Israel vows to the LORD to devote the enemy's cities to destruction, and God grants victory; the place is named Hormah.

    Faithfulness
  14. Sihon of the Amorites defeated
    Numbers 21:21–26 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel asks Sihon for passage along the King's Highway. Sihon refuses and attacks, but Israel defeats him and settles in his cities, including Heshbon.

    The promised land
  15. A victory song about Heshbon
    Numbers 21:27–30 · poetry · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    The poets' song is quoted, telling how Sihon's fire consumed Moab's Ar and how Heshbon was taken. It celebrates the reversal, as Israel has now overthrown Sihon.

    Worship and praise
  16. Og of Bashan defeated
    Numbers 21:31–35 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel takes villages near Jazer and moves up to Bashan, where King Og meets them at Edrei. God tells Moses not to fear, and Israel destroys Og's army and takes his land.

    The promised landPromise
  17. Fourth oracle: A star out of Jacob
    Numbers 24:15–19 · prophecy · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balaam speaks of a distant future in which a star and scepter rise from Israel, defeating Moab and Edom. A ruler from Jacob will overcome their enemies.

    ProphecyThe MessiahKings and kingship
  18. God rewards Phinehas and orders action on Midian
    Numbers 25:10–18 · oracle of hope · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    God grants Phinehas a covenant of peace and lasting priesthood for his zeal. The slain pair are named Zimri and Cozbi, and God commands Moses to attack the Midianites for seducing Israel.

    CovenantPeace and reconciliationPriesthood
  19. God orders a second census
    Numbers 26:1–4 · narrative · 1406 BC

    After the plague, God tells Moses and Eleazar to count all men twenty and older able to serve in the army, by their families. They issue the order on the plains of Moab.

    Leadership
  20. War against Midian
    Numbers 31:1–12 · narrative · 1406 BC

    God tells Moses to take vengeance on Midian, after which he will die. Twelve thousand men, a thousand per tribe, go with Phinehas, killing every male including five kings and Balaam, and bring back captives and plunder.

    Justice and judgment
  21. Moses' anger and purification rules
    Numbers 31:13–24 · dialogue · 1406 BC

    Moses is angry that the army spared the women who had led Israel astray at Peor. He orders that certain captives be killed and girls who have not known a man spared, and that soldiers and goods be purified.

    HolinessIdolatry
  22. Dividing the plunder
    Numbers 31:25–47 · law · 1406 BC

    God instructs that the plunder be counted and split between the soldiers and the rest of the congregation, with a tribute to the priests and Levites. The chapter lists the animals and people in each share.

    Wealth and poverty
  23. The tribes' pledge and Moses' conditions
    Numbers 32:16–27 · dialogue · 1406 BC

    The tribes promise to build folds and cities for their families but to arm themselves and go ahead of Israel until everyone has their land. Moses agrees on condition they keep this promise.

    UnityObedience
  24. A failed attempt to invade
    Deuteronomy 1:41–46 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Moses recalls that the people then said they had sinned and would fight anyway, ignoring God's warning. The Amorites routed them, and Israel remained at Kadesh a long time.

    Sin and the fallObedience
  25. Passing by Ammon and the order to fight Sihon
    Deuteronomy 2:16–25 · narrative · 1407 BC

    With the fighting men dead, God tells Israel to respect Ammon's land, then to cross the Arnon and begin taking the land of Sihon, the Amorite king, promising that the nations will fear Israel.

    God's sovereigntyThe nations
  26. The defeat of Sihon king of Heshbon
    Deuteronomy 2:26–37 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Moses offered Sihon peaceful passage, but Sihon refused. God gave him and his army to Israel at Jahaz, and Israel captured all his cities while keeping away from Ammon's land.

    God's sovereigntyThe nations
  27. The defeat of Og king of Bashan
    Deuteronomy 3:1–11 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Israel defeated Og, king of Bashan, and captured his sixty fortified cities after God told Moses not to fear him. A note mentions Og's huge iron bed as evidence he was among the last of the Rephaim.

    God's sovereigntyThe nations
  28. Encouragement before battle
    Deuteronomy 20:1–9 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    When Israel goes to war against a larger army, they are not to fear, because God goes with them. The priest encourages the army, and the officers release men with new houses, vineyards, or engagements, and the fearful.

    FaithWho God is
  29. Rules for attacking cities
    Deuteronomy 20:10–18 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Israel is to offer peace to distant cities before attacking. If they refuse, males are to be killed and the rest taken as plunder; the nations in Canaan itself are to be completely destroyed so they cannot lead Israel into idolatry.

    Justice and judgmentMercy and compassion
  30. Do not destroy fruit trees
    Deuteronomy 20:19–20 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    When besieging a city for a long time, Israel must not cut down its fruit trees, since the trees are not enemies. Only non-fruit trees may be used for siege works.

    Creation
