  1. The man names the animals
    Genesis 2:18–20 · narrative

    God observes it is not good for the man to be alone and brings the animals to him to name. Among them, no suitable helper is found for him.

    Image of God
  2. The woman is made
    Genesis 2:21–25 · narrative

    God makes a woman from the man's rib and brings her to him. The man celebrates her as his own kind, and the narrator says a man and wife become one flesh, unashamed.

    Romantic love
  3. Cain's descendants and Lamech's boast
    Genesis 4:17–24 · genealogy

    Cain's line builds a city and produces herdsmen, musicians, and metalworkers. Lamech takes two wives and boasts of killing a man, claiming far greater vengeance than Cain.

    Sin and the fall
  4. Seth is born; people call on the Lord
    Genesis 4:25–26 · narrative

    Adam and Eve have another son, Seth, in place of Abel. Seth has a son Enosh, and at that time people begin to call on the name of the Lord.

    Worship and praise
  5. The book of Adam's generations
    Genesis 5:1–5 · genealogy

    This section begins with a reminder that God made humankind in his likeness, then records Adam's age at Seth's birth, his later years, and his death at 930.

    Image of God
  6. From Seth to Jared
    Genesis 5:6–20 · genealogy

    A repeated pattern lists each man's age at his son's birth, the years he lived afterward, and his death: Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, and Jared.

    Death and mortality
  7. From Methuselah to Noah's sons
    Genesis 5:25–32 · genealogy

    The list continues through Methuselah and Lamech. Lamech names his son Noah, hoping for comfort from the cursed ground's toil, and Noah later has Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

    Hope and endurance
  8. The sons of God and the daughters of men
    Genesis 6:1–4 · narrative

    As people multiply, the 'sons of God' take wives from the daughters of men. God limits human days to 120 years, and the Nephilim and mighty men of old are mentioned.

    Sin and the fall
  9. Blessing and rules for life after the flood
    Genesis 9:1–7 · speech

    God blesses Noah's family to be fruitful and fills the earth. He gives animals as food but forbids eating meat with its lifeblood and declares that human life is precious, made in God's image.

    CreationLaw and commandments
  10. Noah's vineyard and Ham's act
    Genesis 9:18–23 · narrative

    Noah plants a vineyard, becomes drunk, and lies uncovered. Ham tells his brothers, while Shem and Japheth cover their father without looking.

    Sin and the fall
  11. From Shem to Terah
    Genesis 11:10–26 · genealogy

    The line of Shem is traced from Arphaxad through Eber, Peleg, and others down to Terah, who fathers Abram, Nahor, and Haran.

  12. Terah's family leaves Ur for Haran
    Genesis 11:27–32 · narrative · 2100 BC – 2091 BC

    Haran dies in Ur, and Abram marries Sarai, who is barren. Terah takes Abram, Lot, and Sarai toward Canaan but settles in Haran, where he dies.

    Promise
  13. Sarai gives Hagar to Abram
    Genesis 16:1–6 · narrative · 2081 BC – 2080 BC

    Childless Sarai gives her Egyptian servant Hagar to Abram. When Hagar conceives and looks down on Sarai, Sarai blames Abram, and then treats Hagar so harshly she flees.

    Faith
  14. Ishmael is born
    Genesis 16:15–16 · narrative · 2080 BC

    Hagar bears Abram a son, whom Abram names Ishmael.

    Promise
  15. Sarah will bear Isaac
    Genesis 17:15–22 · dialogue · 2068 BC – 2066 BC

    God renames Sarai as Sarah and promises she will bear a son, Isaac. Abraham laughs and pleads for Ishmael, and God agrees to bless Ishmael but says the covenant will be with Isaac.

    PromiseFaith
  16. Lot's daughters and the origin of Moab and Ammon
    Genesis 19:30–38 · narrative · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Living in a cave, Lot's daughters fear they will have no children. They get their father drunk and each conceive a son, Moab and Ben-ammi, ancestors of the Moabites and Ammonites.

    Sin and the fallThe nations
  17. Isaac is born
    Genesis 21:1–7 · narrative · 2066 BC

    The Lord does for Sarah what he promised, and she bears Isaac. Abraham circumcises him at eight days, and Sarah says God has made her laugh.

    PromiseFaithfulness
  18. Sarah asks to send Hagar and Ishmael away
    Genesis 21:8–13 · narrative · 2064 BC – 2062 BC

    At a feast for Isaac, Sarah sees Ishmael mocking and demands that Hagar and her son be expelled. Distressed, Abraham is told by God to listen to Sarah, promising to make Ishmael a nation too.

    Promise
  19. Nahor's family
    Genesis 22:20–24 · genealogy · 2055 BC – 2035 BC

    Abraham hears that his brother Nahor has twelve sons from his wife and concubine, including Bethuel, who is the father of Rebekah.

  20. Sarah dies at Hebron
    Genesis 23:1–2 · narrative · 2030 BC – 2028 BC

    Sarah dies at 127 in Hebron, and Abraham mourns and weeps for her.

    Death and mortality
  21. Abraham makes his servant swear an oath
    Genesis 24:1–9 · dialogue · 2027 BC – 2026 BC

    Aging Abraham has his chief servant swear not to choose a Canaanite wife for Isaac but to find one among Abraham's own relatives. Isaac must not be taken back to the old homeland, and God's promise of the land is the reason.

    Obedience
  22. Laban welcomes the visitor
    Genesis 24:28–33 · narrative · 2026 BC

    Rebekah runs home and tells her family, and her brother Laban, having seen the jewelry, hurries out to invite the man in. The servant refuses to eat until he has explained why he came.

  23. The family agrees and Rebekah decides
    Genesis 24:50–60 · narrative · 2026 BC

    Laban and Bethuel say this is from the Lord and agree. After the servant gives gifts and worships, the family asks for delay, but Rebekah herself agrees to go at once, and they bless her.

    Providence
  24. Rebekah meets Isaac and marries him
    Genesis 24:61–67 · narrative · 2026 BC

    Rebekah and her maids travel to Canaan, where she first sees Isaac walking in the field and veils herself. Isaac takes her as his wife and is comforted after his mother Sarah's death.

    Romantic love
  25. Abraham's later children and death
    Genesis 25:1–11 · narrative · 2029 BC – 1991 BC

    Abraham has more sons through Keturah and other concubines, gives them gifts and sends them east, and leaves everything to Isaac. He dies at 175 and Isaac and Ishmael bury him beside Sarah.

    PromiseDeath and mortality
  26. The descendants of Ishmael
    Genesis 25:12–18 · genealogy · 2080 BC – 1943 BC

    This section lists Ishmael's twelve sons, who became tribal leaders, and gives his age at death. His descendants settled in the region between Havilah and Shur.

  27. Esau sells his birthright
    Genesis 25:27–34 · narrative · 1995 BC – 1970 BC

    The boys grow up very different, and each parent favors one. Famished, Esau trades his birthright to Jacob for a meal of lentil stew, and the text says he despised it.

    Pride and humility
  28. Isaac says Rebekah is his sister
    Genesis 26:7–11 · narrative · 1990 BC – 1970 BC

    Afraid he will be killed because of Rebekah's beauty, Isaac claims she is his sister. King Abimelech sees them together, confronts him, and orders that no one harm the couple.

    Sin and the fall
  29. Esau marries two Hittite women
    Genesis 26:34–35 · narrative · 1966 BC

    Esau, at forty, marries two Hittite wives, and they cause grief to Isaac and Rebekah.

  30. Isaac plans to bless Esau; Rebekah overhears
    Genesis 27:1–10 · narrative · 1930 BC – 1929 BC

    Old and nearly blind, Isaac asks Esau to hunt and prepare a meal so he can bless him. Rebekah overhears and tells Jacob to bring goats so that he can get the blessing instead.

    Sin and the fall
