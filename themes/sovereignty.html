  1. In the beginning: formless earth
    Genesis 1:1–2 · narrative

    The Bible opens with God creating the heavens and the earth. The earth is at first formless and dark, with God's Spirit hovering over the waters.

    Creation
  2. God warns Abimelech in a dream
    Genesis 20:1–7 · narrative · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Abraham again calls Sarah his sister, and Abimelech takes her. God warns him in a dream that she is married, and Abimelech pleads his innocence, so God tells him to return her.

    Dreams, visions and revelation
  3. Jacob's flocks grow
    Genesis 30:37–43 · narrative · 1915 BC – 1909 BC

    Jacob uses peeled branches at the troughs so that the flocks bear streaked and spotted young, and he takes the stronger animals for himself. He becomes very wealthy.

    Wealth and poverty
  4. Laban pursues and searches for his idols
    Genesis 31:22–35 · narrative · 1909 BC

    Laban chases Jacob for a week, but God warns him in a dream not to harm him. Laban confronts Jacob about leaving secretly and the missing gods, and Rachel hides them so they are not found.

    Idolatry
  5. God's plan for the elders and Pharaoh
    Exodus 3:16–22 · speech · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    God tells Moses to gather the elders and ask Pharaoh for a three-day journey to sacrifice. He warns that Pharaoh will refuse until compelled by wonders, after which the Israelites will leave with Egyptian valuables.

    Exodus and deliverance
  6. Moses heads back to Egypt
    Exodus 4:18–23 · narrative · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Moses gets Jethro's blessing and sets out with his family and the staff of God. The LORD warns that Pharaoh's heart will be hardened and gives the message to deliver: let my firstborn son Israel go.

    Obedience
  7. God makes Moses like God to Pharaoh
    Exodus 7:1–7 · speech · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The LORD says Moses will speak to Pharaoh with Aaron as his prophet. God will harden Pharaoh's heart and bring judgments so that Egypt will know he is the LORD, and Moses and Aaron obey.

    Prophecy
  8. The LORD announces the plague of blood
    Exodus 7:14–18 · speech · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to meet Pharaoh at the Nile and again demand that Israel be released. If he refuses, the Nile will turn to blood.

    Justice and judgment
  9. Flies come on Egypt but not Goshen
    Exodus 8:20–24 · miracle · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The LORD warns that swarms of flies will fill Egypt but not Goshen, so that the difference between his people and Pharaoh's will be clear. It happens as he said.

    Miracles and signs
  10. The plague on Egypt's livestock
    Exodus 9:1–7 · miracle · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The LORD warns of a severe plague on livestock and sets a time. Egypt's animals die while none of Israel's do, yet Pharaoh remains unmoved.

    Miracles and signs
  11. Warning of the coming hail
    Exodus 9:13–21 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to warn Pharaoh again that he could have destroyed Egypt but has raised him up to show his power. Hail is coming, and those who fear the LORD's word bring their servants and animals inside.

    Pride and humility
  12. Locusts threatened and Pharaoh's bargaining
    Exodus 10:1–11 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The LORD says he has hardened Pharaoh's heart so his signs may be told to future generations. Moses warns of locusts, Pharaoh's officials plead with him, and Pharaoh will allow only the men to go.

    Pride and humility
  13. Moses warns of the death of the firstborn
    Exodus 11:4–10 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses declares that at midnight every firstborn in Egypt will die and a great cry will rise, but Israel will be unharmed. He leaves in anger, and the text notes that Pharaoh's heart remains hardened.

    Justice and judgment
  14. Pharaoh changes his mind and pursues
    Exodus 14:1–9 · narrative · 1446 BC

    God tells Israel to camp by the sea, saying he will harden Pharaoh's heart so he pursues. Pharaoh regrets releasing them and sets out with 600 chariots and overtakes them.

  15. The angel and the talking donkey
    Numbers 22:22–35 · miracle · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    The angel of the LORD blocks Balaam's path three times, seen only by his donkey, who turns aside and is beaten. God opens her mouth to protest, then opens Balaam's eyes. He confesses and is told to go on but to speak only God's words.

    Angels and spiritual beingsMiracles and signs
  16. Balak welcomes Balaam and offers sacrifices
    Numbers 22:36–41 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balak meets Balaam at the border and Balaam warns that he can only speak what God puts in his mouth. Balak sacrifices and next morning takes Balaam up to Bamoth-baal, from where Israel's camp is visible.

    Words and speech
  17. Seven altars and a message from God
    Numbers 23:1–6 · ritual instruction · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balaam has Balak build seven altars and offer a bull and a ram on each. Balaam goes off alone to a barren height, where God meets him and gives him a message to bring back.

    Worship and praise
  18. A second place, a second altar
    Numbers 23:13–17 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balak moves Balaam to the top of Pisgah, hoping a different view will help, and again builds seven altars. Balaam meets the LORD and returns with a message.

  19. A third attempt at Peor
    Numbers 23:27–30 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balak takes Balaam to the top of Peor, and Balaam again has seven altars built with a bull and ram on each. Balak follows his instructions.

    Worship and praise
  20. Balak's anger and dismissal
    Numbers 24:10–14 · dialogue · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balak strikes his hands together in anger and tells Balaam to go home, saying the LORD has denied his reward. Balaam repeats that he can only speak the LORD's words, and offers to warn Balak what Israel will do in future.

    Words and speech
  21. Passing by Edom
    Deuteronomy 2:1–8 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Moses recalls that after years wandering near Mount Seir, God told Israel to pass peacefully through the land of their relatives, Esau's descendants, and to pay for food and water.

    The promised landThe nations
  22. Passing by Moab; the old generation dies
    Deuteronomy 2:9–15 · narrative · 1407 BC

    God tells Israel not to provoke Moab, the descendants of Lot. A note explains earlier peoples of the area, and the crossing of the Zered marks thirty-eight years since Kadesh, when the old generation had died.

    Death and mortalityThe nations
  23. Passing by Ammon and the order to fight Sihon
    Deuteronomy 2:16–25 · narrative · 1407 BC

    With the fighting men dead, God tells Israel to respect Ammon's land, then to cross the Arnon and begin taking the land of Sihon, the Amorite king, promising that the nations will fear Israel.

    The nationsWar and conflict
  24. The defeat of Sihon king of Heshbon
    Deuteronomy 2:26–37 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Moses offered Sihon peaceful passage, but Sihon refused. God gave him and his army to Israel at Jahaz, and Israel captured all his cities while keeping away from Ammon's land.

    War and conflictThe nations
  25. The defeat of Og king of Bashan
    Deuteronomy 3:1–11 · narrative · 1407 BC

    Israel defeated Og, king of Bashan, and captured his sixty fortified cities after God told Moses not to fear him. A note mentions Og's huge iron bed as evidence he was among the last of the Rephaim.

    War and conflictThe nations
  26. Moses is not allowed to enter the land
    Deuteronomy 3:23–29 · prayer · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses recalls asking God to let him cross the Jordan. God refused, telling him to view the land from Pisgah and to strengthen Joshua, who would lead the people in.

    PrayerMercy and compassion
  27. Moses hands over leadership
    Deuteronomy 31:1–6 · speech · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses, at 120, says he can no longer lead and will not cross the Jordan. He assures Israel that the LORD himself will go ahead of them with Joshua and tells them to be strong and courageous.

    LeadershipFaithfulness
  28. Israel lacks wisdom; vengeance belongs to God
    Deuteronomy 32:28–35 · poetry · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses says Israel lacks understanding and their defeat happens because the LORD gave them up. Their enemies' rock is not like Israel's Rock, and the LORD will repay in his time.

    Justice and judgmentWisdom
  29. The LORD vindicates his people
    Deuteronomy 32:36–43 · praise · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The song promises that the LORD will have compassion on his servants when their strength is gone. He mocks the useless idols, declares there is no God besides him, and promises vengeance on his enemies, so the nations rejoice.

    Mercy and compassionJustice and judgmentSalvation
  30. Victory and the sun standing still
    Joshua 10:8–15 · miracle · 1405 BC – 1404 BC

    The LORD promises victory, and Joshua attacks after a night march. God confuses the enemy and sends hailstones, and Joshua asks the sun and moon to stand still until the battle is won.

    Miracles and signsWar and conflictPrayer
