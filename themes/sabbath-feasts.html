Sabbath and feasts

90 passages · Kingdom

  1. God rests on the seventh day
    Genesis 2:1–3 · narrative

    Creation is complete, and God rests on the seventh day, blessing it and setting it apart as holy.

    Creation
  2. Instructions for the Passover lamb
    Exodus 12:1–13 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    The LORD makes this month the first of the year. Each household is to keep a year-old lamb, slaughter it at twilight, put its blood on the doorframes, and eat it roasted in haste, so that judgment will pass over them.

    Atonement and sacrificeSalvation
  3. The Feast of Unleavened Bread
    Exodus 12:14–20 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    The day is to be a memorial and a lasting statute. For seven days no leaven is to be eaten, and there are to be sacred assemblies on the first and last days.

    Obedience
  4. Moses passes on the instructions
    Exodus 12:21–28 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses tells the elders to kill the lamb and brush its blood on the doorframes with hyssop. He says the ritual must be kept in the promised land and explained to children, and the people bow and obey.

    Faith
  5. Who may share the Passover
    Exodus 12:43–51 · law · 1446 BC

    The LORD sets rules for the Passover meal. It is eaten in one house, with no bones broken, and the foreigner may join only if his household is circumcised, and the same law applies to native and foreigner.

    Covenant
  6. Remember the day with unleavened bread
    Exodus 13:3–10 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses tells the people to remember the day of their release and to eat no leavened bread. In the promised land they are to keep the feast each year and explain it to their children.

    Exodus and deliverance
  7. The Sabbath and the double portion
    Exodus 16:22–30 · narrative · 1446 BC

    On the sixth day the people gather twice as much, and Moses explains that the next day is a holy Sabbath. What is kept keeps fresh, some go out looking on the seventh day and find nothing, and the people rest.

    Obedience
  8. God's name and the Sabbath
    Exodus 20:7–11 · law · 1446 BC

    Israel is not to misuse the LORD's name. They are to keep the seventh day as a Sabbath, with no work for family, servants, animals, or foreigners, because God made everything in six days and rested on the seventh.

    Law and commandmentsWords and speech
  9. Sabbath years and days
    Exodus 23:10–13 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The land is to be worked six years and left fallow in the seventh so the poor can eat. The seventh day is a rest for people and animals, and Israel must not invoke other gods.

    Law and commandments
  10. Three annual feasts
    Exodus 23:14–19 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Israel is to keep the Feast of Unleavened Bread, the Feast of Harvest, and the Feast of Ingathering, appearing before the LORD three times a year. It also gives sacrifice rules and forbids boiling a young goat in its mother's milk.

  11. The Sabbath as a covenant sign
    Exodus 31:12–17 · law · 1446 BC

    God tells the Israelites to keep the Sabbath as a sign between him and them across generations. Work is for six days, the seventh is holy rest, mirroring God's rest after creating the heavens and earth.

    Covenant
  12. Festivals, firstborn and Sabbath
    Exodus 34:18–26 · law · 1446 BC

    God repeats laws about the Feast of Unleavened Bread, the firstborn, weekly rest, the Feasts of Weeks and Ingathering, appearing before him three times a year, and offerings. It ends with the command not to cook a young goat in its mother's milk.

    Law and commandments
  13. Sabbath rest before the work
    Exodus 35:1–3 · law · 1446 BC

    Moses gathers the congregation and reminds them of the Sabbath: six days of work, then complete rest. No work or fire-lighting on the seventh day.

    Work and rest
  14. Grain offering of firstfruits
    Leviticus 2:14–16 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    An offering of the first new grain is to be crushed, roasted heads of grain with oil and frankincense. The priest burns a memorial portion of it to the LORD.

    Worship and praise
  15. A permanent yearly day of rest
    Leviticus 16:29–34 · law · 1445 BC

    This is to be kept yearly on the tenth day of the seventh month, as a day of rest and self-denial when atonement is made for the people. The high priest and his successors make atonement once a year.

    Atonement and sacrificeRepentance
  16. Be holy: honor parents and avoid idols
    Leviticus 19:1–4 · exhortation · 1445 BC

    The LORD tells the whole community to be holy because he is holy. They are to respect their parents, keep his Sabbaths, and not turn to idols.

    HolinessFamily and marriageIdolatry
  17. The Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread
    Leviticus 23:1–8 · law · 1445 BC

    God introduces his appointed feasts as sacred assemblies, beginning with the weekly Sabbath of rest. He then sets the dates for Passover and the seven-day Feast of Unleavened Bread.

    Work and rest
  18. Firstfruits of the harvest
    Leviticus 23:9–14 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Once in the land, Israelites must bring a sheaf of the first grain to the priest, who waves it before the LORD with a lamb and other offerings. No new grain may be eaten until this is done.

    Worship and praiseThe promised land
  19. The Feast of Weeks and gleaning
    Leviticus 23:15–22 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Seven weeks, fifty days, after the firstfruits sheaf, Israel is to offer two loaves of leavened bread with animal offerings as a sacred assembly. The passage ends by telling farmers to leave the edges of their fields for the poor and foreigners.

    Generosity and givingLoving others
  20. Day of trumpet blasts
    Leviticus 23:23–25 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    On the first day of the seventh month, Israel is to rest from regular work, hold a sacred assembly announced with trumpet blasts, and present a food offering.

    Worship and praise
  21. The Day of Atonement
    Leviticus 23:26–32 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    The tenth day of the seventh month is a day of complete rest and self-humbling, when atonement is made for the people. Anyone who ignores it will be cut off from the people.

    Atonement and sacrificeRepentance
  22. The Feast of Tabernacles
    Leviticus 23:33–44 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    For seven days beginning on the fifteenth of the seventh month, Israel is to celebrate the harvest by rejoicing and living in booths, remembering the exodus from Egypt. The chapter closes by summarizing the feasts and noting that Moses announced them.

    Joy and thanksgivingExodus and deliverance
  23. The Sabbath year for the land
    Leviticus 25:1–7 · law · 1445 BC – 1406 BC

    Once in the land, Israel is to farm for six years and let the land rest completely in the seventh. What grows on its own may be eaten by everyone, including servants and animals.

    The promised landFaith
  24. Consecrated fields and the Jubilee
    Leviticus 27:16–25 · law · 1445 BC

    A field given to God is valued by the seed it needs and by the years left until Jubilee, and the owner may redeem it by adding a fifth. If not redeemed, it becomes the priests' property at Jubilee.

    The promised landWorship and praise
  25. Israel keeps the Passover at Sinai
    Numbers 9:1–5 · narrative · 1445 BC

    In the first month of the second year, the LORD tells Moses to keep the Passover at twilight on the fourteenth day, and Israel does so.

    Exodus and deliveranceObedience
  26. Unclean men ask about Passover
    Numbers 9:6–8 · dialogue · 1445 BC

    Some men are unclean from a dead body and cannot keep the Passover. They ask why they should be excluded, and Moses says he will seek the LORD's answer.

    Holiness
  27. A second-month Passover, open to foreigners
    Numbers 9:9–14 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD allows those unclean or traveling to keep the Passover a month later. Those who neglect it while clean are cut off, and foreigners may join under the same rules.

    The nationsMercy and compassion
  28. The two silver trumpets
    Numbers 10:1–10 · law · 1445 BC

    God tells Moses to make two silver trumpets. Signals with them will summon the people, tell the camps to set out, and remind God in battle and at feasts.

    Worship and praise
  29. The Sabbath wood gatherer
    Numbers 15:32–36 · narrative · 1444 BC – 1406 BC

    A man found gathering wood on the Sabbath is held, and God says he is to be stoned outside the camp, which the congregation carries out.

    Law and commandmentsJustice and judgment
  30. Sabbath and monthly offerings
    Numbers 28:9–15 · ritual instruction · 1406 BC

    Each Sabbath adds two more lambs with flour, oil and a drink offering. At each new moon, a larger offering of bulls, a ram, lambs and a sin-offering goat is added.

    Worship and praiseAtonement and sacrifice
