Resurrection

75 passages · Salvation

  1. Elijah raises the widow's son
    1 Kings 17:17–24 · miracle · 870 BC – 866 BC

    The widow's son dies, and Elijah prays over him until his life returns. The woman says she now knows Elijah is a man of God.

    Miracles and signsPrayer
  2. Elisha restores the boy to life
    2 Kings 4:29–37 · miracle · 852 BC – 841 BC

    Gehazi's staff produces no response, so Elisha goes in, prays, and stretches himself over the boy, who warms and then sneezes and opens his eyes. Elisha returns the living son to his mother.

    Miracles and signsPrayer
  3. Elisha's bones, God's compassion, and Israel's recovery
    2 Kings 13:20–25 · narrative · 798 BC – 782 BC

    After Elisha dies, a dead man thrown into his tomb revives on touching his bones. God is gracious to Israel because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and Jehoash defeats Ben-hadad three times.

    Mercy and compassionCovenant
  4. A tree may sprout again, but a man lies down
    Job 14:7–12 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job compares a felled tree, which can sprout again, with a person who dies and does not rise. Death is like water disappearing from a dried river.

    Death and mortalityHope and endurance
  5. I know that my Redeemer lives
    Job 19:23–29 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job wishes his words were carved in stone forever and states his confidence that his Redeemer lives and that he will see God for himself. He warns his friends to fear judgment.

    RedemptionHope and enduranceFaith
  6. Labor without birth, then the dead live
    Isaiah 26:16–19 · lament

    In distress the people cried out to God and were like a woman giving birth to wind, achieving no rescue. Yet God promises that His dead will live and their bodies will rise, so those in the dust can awake and sing.

    SufferingHope and endurance
  7. The LORD's will and the Servant's reward
    Isaiah 53:10–12 · poetry · 700 BC – 538 BC

    It was the LORD's will to crush him and make his life a guilt offering, yet he will see offspring, prolong his days and justify many. God says he will give him a portion with the great because he poured out his life and bore the sin of many.

    Atonement and sacrificeJustification and righteousness
  8. The valley of dry bones
    Ezekiel 37:1–14 · vision · 585 BC – 573 BC

    Ezekiel is brought by the Spirit to a valley of dry bones and told to prophesy to them. The bones come together, gain flesh and receive breath, and God says they represent Israel, whom he will bring out of their graves.

    The Holy SpiritHope and endurance
  9. Deliverance, resurrection and sealed words
    Daniel 12:1–4 · prophecy · 536 BC – 535 BC

    Michael will arise in a time of unparalleled distress, and those whose names are written in the book will be delivered. Many who sleep in the dust will awake, some to everlasting life and some to shame, and the wise will shine. Daniel is told to seal the book.

    Eternal lifeFinal judgmentAngels and spiritual beings
  10. Go on your way until the end
    Daniel 12:8–13 · dialogue · 536 BC – 535 BC

    Daniel hears but does not understand and asks what the outcome will be. He is told the words are sealed until the end, the wicked will not understand while the wise will, and he will rest and rise to his inheritance.

    Hope and endurance
  11. Jairus' daughter and the bleeding woman
    Matthew 9:18–26 · miracle · AD 28 – AD 30

    A synagogue leader asks Jesus to come and raise his daughter who has just died. On the way, a woman with twelve years of bleeding touches his cloak and is healed, and then Jesus takes the girl's hand and she gets up.

    Miracles and signsFaith
  12. The sign of Jonah
    Matthew 12:38–45 · dialogue · AD 29

    Scribes and Pharisees ask Jesus for a sign, and he says only the sign of Jonah will be given, three days in the earth. He says Nineveh and the Queen of the South will condemn this generation, and adds a story of an empty house refilled with worse spirits.

    The Messiah
  13. Elijah has already come
    Matthew 17:9–13 · dialogue · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus tells the three to keep the vision secret until he is raised from the dead. He answers their question about Elijah by saying Elijah has already come and was mistreated, and they understand he means John the Baptist.

    Prophecy
  14. Second prediction of his death
    Matthew 17:22–23 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus tells the disciples again that the Son of Man will be handed over to people and killed, and will be raised on the third day. The disciples are deeply grieved.

    The cross
  15. Third prediction of the passion
    Matthew 20:17–19 · teaching · AD 30 – AD 33

    Taking the Twelve aside, Jesus says that in Jerusalem the Son of Man will be handed over, condemned, mocked, flogged and crucified by the Gentiles, and raised on the third day.

    The crossDeath and mortality
  16. Sadducees ask about the resurrection
    Matthew 22:23–33 · dialogue · AD 30 – AD 33

    The Sadducees, who deny resurrection, pose a puzzle about a woman married to seven brothers in turn. Jesus replies that they do not know the Scriptures or God's power, that people in the resurrection are like angels, and that God is God of the living.

    Opposition and persecution
  17. The empty tomb and the risen Jesus
    Matthew 28:1–10 · narrative · AD 30 – AD 33

    At dawn on Sunday, an angel rolls back the stone and tells the women Jesus has risen and to tell the disciples to meet him in Galilee. Leaving in fear and joy, they meet Jesus, worship him, and he repeats the message.

    Hope and enduranceWorship and praise
  18. The guards' report and the cover story
    Matthew 28:11–15 · narrative · AD 30 – AD 33

    Some guards tell the chief priests what happened. The priests and elders pay them to say the disciples stole the body and promise to protect them, and the story spreads among the Jews.

    Opposition and persecution
  19. Jairus' daughter raised
    Mark 5:35–43 · miracle · AD 29 – AD 31

    Messengers say Jairus' daughter has died, but Jesus tells him to believe. Inside the house he takes the girl by the hand and she gets up and walks. He gives strict orders to tell no one.

    Faith
  20. Coming down and the question of Elijah
    Mark 9:9–13 · dialogue · AD 29 – AD 33

    On the way down, Jesus tells the three to keep this secret until he rises from the dead. They ask about Elijah coming first, and he says Elijah has come and that the Son of Man must suffer.

    Prophecy
  21. The Sadducees and the resurrection
    Mark 12:18–27 · dialogue · AD 30 – AD 33

    Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, pose a puzzle about a woman who married seven brothers. Jesus answers that those raised are like angels and that God is the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, of the living.

  22. The empty tomb
    Mark 16:1–8 · narrative · AD 30 – AD 33

    After the Sabbath, three women go to the tomb with spices and find the huge stone already rolled away. A young man in white tells them Jesus has risen and that his disciples should meet him in Galilee, but they flee in fear and say nothing.

  23. Jesus appears; disciples doubt
    Mark 16:9–14 · narrative · AD 30 – AD 33

    Jesus appears first to Mary Magdalene, then to two travelers, and the other disciples do not believe their reports. Finally he appears to the Eleven at a meal and rebukes their unbelief.

    Faith
  24. Jairus' daughter is raised
    Luke 8:49–56 · miracle · AD 29 – AD 30

    Word comes that Jairus' daughter has died, and Jesus tells him to believe. He takes her by the hand and calls her to get up, and she rises.

    FaithMiracles and signs
  25. Third prediction of the Passion
    Luke 18:31–34 · prophecy · AD 30

    Jesus takes the Twelve aside and says that in Jerusalem the Son of Man will be handed over, mocked, killed, and rise on the third day. The disciples do not understand.

    The cross
  26. The Sadducees ask about resurrection
    Luke 20:27–40 · dialogue · AD 30

    Sadducees, who deny resurrection, pose a puzzle about a woman who married seven brothers. Jesus answers that those in the age to come do not marry, and that Moses calls God the God of the living.

    Eternal lifeLaw and commandments
  27. The empty tomb
    Luke 24:1–12 · narrative · AD 30

    On the first day of the week, the women find the stone rolled away and the body gone. Two men in radiant clothes tell them Jesus has risen; the apostles think their story is nonsense, though Peter runs to see for himself.

    Angels and spiritual beingsFaith
  28. Two travelers on the Emmaus road
    Luke 24:13–24 · narrative · AD 30

    Two followers walk from Jerusalem to Emmaus, sad about Jesus' death, when Jesus joins them unrecognized. They tell him what happened, including the empty tomb reports they cannot yet believe.

    FaithHope and endurance
  29. Jesus opens the Scriptures and is recognized
    Luke 24:25–35 · narrative · AD 30

    Jesus explains from Moses and the Prophets that the Christ had to suffer and enter glory. At the meal in Emmaus he breaks bread and they recognize him, then rush back to Jerusalem to tell the others.

    The MessiahThe cross
  30. Jesus appears to the disciples
    Luke 24:36–49 · narrative · AD 30

    Jesus stands among the disciples, who fear he is a spirit. He shows his hands and feet, eats fish, opens their minds to the Scriptures, tells them to preach repentance to all nations, and to wait for power from on high.

    Gospel and missionRepentanceForgiveness
