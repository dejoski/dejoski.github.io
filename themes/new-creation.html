New creation

35 passages · Salvation

Genesis · 1Isaiah · 8Ezekiel · 7Amos · 1Zechariah · 1Luke · 1John · 1Romans · 31 Corinthians · 22 Corinthians · 1Galatians · 1Ephesians · 1Colossians · 11 Peter · 22 Peter · 1Revelation · 3
  1. God tells Noah to leave the ark
    Genesis 8:15–19 · narrative

    God tells Noah to come out with his family and the animals so they can spread over the earth and multiply. They all leave the ark.

    Obedience
  2. Peace across creation
    Isaiah 11:6–9 · poetry · 740 BC – 701 BC

    The prophet pictures wolf with lamb and lion with ox, and a little child leading them, so that nothing harms on God's holy mountain. The earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD.

    Peace and reconciliation
  3. The Spirit renews the land
    Isaiah 32:15–20 · oracle of hope · 715 BC – 701 BC

    The desolation lasts until the Spirit is poured out from on high, when the desert becomes fruitful. Justice and righteousness will dwell in the land, bringing peace and quiet confidence.

    The Holy SpiritPeace and reconciliation
  4. Healing and water in the wilderness
    Isaiah 35:5–7 · oracle of hope · 712 BC – 690 BC

    Blind eyes open, deaf ears hear, the lame leap and the mute shout for joy. Water gushes in the desert, turning dry land into pools and springs.

    Miracles and signsSalvation
  5. Water for the poor and needy
    Isaiah 41:17–20 · oracle of hope · 700 BC – 539 BC

    God promises to answer the thirsty poor by opening rivers and fountains and turning the desert into pools and planted trees. The purpose is that all will know God has done it.

    Mercy and compassionJustice for the poor and oppressed
  6. Something new after the exodus pattern
    Isaiah 43:14–21 · oracle of hope · 700 BC – 539 BC

    God promises to bring Babylon down and recalls how he once opened a path through the sea. He tells Israel not to dwell on the past because he is doing something new, making a way in the wilderness.

    Exodus and deliveranceExile and return
  7. The afflicted city rebuilt and secure
    Isaiah 54:11–17 · oracle of hope · 700 BC – 538 BC

    God promises to rebuild the storm-tossed city with precious stones and teach her children himself. She will be secure from oppression, and no weapon formed against her will prosper.

    Hope and endurancePromise
  8. Everlasting light and righteousness
    Isaiah 60:15–22 · oracle of hope · 700 BC – 500 BC

    God promises to turn Zion's shame into pride, with peace as her governor and violence gone. He will be her everlasting light, replacing sun and moon, and her righteous people will possess the land forever.

    GloryJustice and judgmentHope and endurance
  9. New heavens and a new earth
    Isaiah 65:17–25 · oracle of hope · 700 BC – 500 BC

    God promises to create new heavens and a new earth and a joyful Jerusalem without weeping or early death. People will enjoy their houses and vineyards, God will answer before they call, and the wolf and lamb will feed together.

    Heaven and the new earthJoy and thanksgivingDeath and mortality
  10. Hope for the exiles: a new heart
    Ezekiel 11:14–21 · oracle of hope · 592 BC

    God tells Ezekiel that those in Jerusalem say the exiles are far from him, but he has been a sanctuary for them. He will gather them back, give them one heart and a new spirit in place of stone, and they will be his people.

    Exile and returnPromiseCovenant
  11. Repent and get a new heart
    Ezekiel 18:30–32 · exhortation · 592 BC – 591 BC

    God calls Israel to repent and cast off their transgressions, make themselves a new heart and spirit, and live, since he takes no pleasure in anyone's death.

    RepentanceMercy and compassion
  12. A covenant of peace
    Ezekiel 34:25–31 · oracle of hope · 585 BC – 573 BC

    God promises a covenant of peace, safety from wild beasts, seasonal showers and fruitful land. His people will know he is with them, and he calls them his flock and himself their God.

    Peace and reconciliationCovenant
  13. A new heart and a new spirit
    Ezekiel 36:24–32 · oracle of hope · 585 BC – 573 BC

    God promises to bring Israel home, cleanse them, and give them a new heart and his Spirit so they will follow his laws. They will be his people and remember their past ways with shame.

    The Holy SpiritGraceForgiveness
  14. Ruins rebuilt like Eden
    Ezekiel 36:33–38 · oracle of hope · 585 BC – 573 BC

    When God cleanses Israel, the ruined cities will be rebuilt and the desolate land tilled until it is like the garden of Eden. The nations will see this, and towns will fill with people like flocks.

    The promised land
  15. The river flowing from the temple
    Ezekiel 47:1–12 · vision · 573 BC

    Ezekiel sees water flow from beneath the temple threshold, growing deeper as he is led along it. It turns the Dead Sea fresh and brings life, fish and evergreen trees with healing leaves.

    Temple and tabernacleSalvation
  16. The city's gates and its new name
    Ezekiel 48:30–35 · vision · 573 BC

    The city's perimeter is described with twelve gates named for the tribes, three on each side. From then on the city's name will be THE LORD IS THERE.

    Names of GodThe promised land
  17. The fallen tent of David restored
    Amos 9:11–15 · oracle of hope · 760 BC – 753 BC

    God promises to restore David's fallen tent and to make crops overflow. He will bring Israel back from captivity to rebuild ruined cities and plant them in the land, never to be uprooted again.

    PromiseKings and kingship
  18. Living water and the LORD as king
    Zechariah 14:6–11 · prophecy · 518 BC – 480 BC

    On that day there is a unique light at evening, and living water flows out of Jerusalem east and west all year. The LORD will be king over all the earth, and Jerusalem will be raised up and live in safety.

    Kingdom of God
  19. Questions about fasting and new wine
    Luke 5:33–39 · parable · AD 28 – AD 29

    Asked why His disciples do not fast, Jesus compares Himself to a bridegroom whose guests do not fast while He is with them. He adds sayings about patches and wineskins showing the new does not fit the old.

    Kingdom of GodLament and fasting
  20. Nicodemus and the new birth
    John 3:1–8 · dialogue · AD 27 – AD 28

    Nicodemus, a Pharisee and Jewish leader, visits Jesus by night. Jesus says one must be born again, or born of water and Spirit, and compares the Spirit's working to the wind.

    The Holy SpiritSalvation
  21. United with Christ in death and life
    Romans 6:5–11 · teaching · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul says believers who share Christ's death will also share his resurrection. Their old self was crucified so they are no longer slaves to sin, and they should consider themselves dead to sin and alive to God.

    ResurrectionThe cross
  22. Creation groans and we wait in hope
    Romans 8:18–25 · teaching · AD 56 – AD 57

    Paul says present sufferings are small compared with coming glory. Creation itself waits to be freed from decay, and believers, having the Spirit as a first installment, wait for the redemption of their bodies with hope.

    Hope and enduranceCreationSuffering
  23. Offer yourselves as living sacrifices
    Romans 12:1–2 · exhortation · AD 56 – AD 57

    In view of God's mercies, Paul urges believers to offer their bodies as a living sacrifice and to be transformed by the renewing of their minds instead of conforming to the world.

    ObedienceWorship and praise
  24. What kind of body is raised
    1 Corinthians 15:35–49 · teaching · AD 54 – AD 55

    Answering how the dead are raised, Paul uses seeds, different kinds of bodies, and the splendor of sun, moon, and stars. The body is sown perishable and raised imperishable, bearing the likeness of the heavenly man.

    ResurrectionCreation
  25. We will all be changed; victory over death
    1 Corinthians 15:50–58 · teaching · AD 54 – AD 55

    Paul reveals a mystery: at the last trumpet the dead will be raised imperishable and the living changed, so death is swallowed up in victory. He thanks God for victory through Jesus and urges steadfast work.

    ResurrectionDeath and mortalityThe end times
  26. New creation and the ministry of reconciliation
    2 Corinthians 5:16–21 · teaching · AD 55 – AD 56

    Paul says that in Christ a person is a new creation. God reconciled the world to himself in Christ, and Paul and his coworkers are ambassadors urging people to be reconciled.

    Peace and reconciliationGospel and mission
  27. Paul's closing in his own hand
    Galatians 6:11–18 · letter · AD 48 – AD 55

    Writing in large letters, Paul says those pressing circumcision want to boast in the flesh. He boasts only in the cross of Christ, since what matters is a new creation. He closes with peace and mercy and a blessing.

    The crossOpposition and persecution
  28. Put off the old self, put on the new
    Ephesians 4:17–24 · exhortation · AD 60 – AD 62

    Paul tells them not to live like the Gentiles in futile thinking and hardened hearts. They are to put off their old self and be renewed in mind and put on the new self created in righteousness and holiness.

    Sin and the fall
  29. Put off the old self
    Colossians 3:5–11 · exhortation · AD 60 – AD 62

    Paul tells them to put to death sexual immorality, greed, anger, slander and lying, since they have taken off the old self and put on the new, renewed in God's image. In this new humanity, ethnic, social and religious divisions no longer matter, for Christ is all.

    Purity and sexual ethicsSin and the fallObedience
  30. Love one another; the word endures
    1 Peter 1:22–25 · exhortation · AD 62 – AD 65

    Since they have been purified by obedience to the truth, Peter urges deep love for one another. They have been born again through God's living word, which lasts forever while all flesh fades like grass.

    Loving othersThe church
