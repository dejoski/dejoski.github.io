  1. Moses' objection
    Numbers 32:6–15 · speech · 1406 BC

    Moses asks if they will let their brothers fight while they stay behind. He warns of the earlier generation at Kadesh-barnea who discouraged Israel and were kept from the land, and says they risk the same.

    Sin and the fallLeadership
  2. The tribes' pledge and Moses' conditions
    Numbers 32:16–27 · dialogue · 1406 BC

    The tribes promise to build folds and cities for their families but to arm themselves and go ahead of Israel until everyone has their land. Moses agrees on condition they keep this promise.

    War and conflictObedience
  3. The people pledge loyalty
    Joshua 1:16–18 · speech · 1406 BC

    The eastern tribes answer that they will obey Joshua as they obeyed Moses. They add a warning that anyone who rebels will be put to death, and they echo the call to be strong.

    ObedienceLeadership
  4. Joshua sends the eastern tribes home
    Joshua 22:1–9 · speech · 1396 BC – 1394 BC

    Joshua praises Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh for keeping their promises, blesses them and sends them home. He urges them to love the LORD and keep His law.

    ObedienceLeadership
  5. A dispute over the altar
    Joshua 22:10–20 · narrative · 1395 BC – 1392 BC

    The returning tribes build a large altar by the Jordan, and the other Israelites gather at Shiloh to go to war. They first send Phinehas and ten chiefs to accuse them of rebelling, recalling Peor and Achan.

    IdolatryWar and conflict
  6. The tribes explain the altar
    Joshua 22:21–29 · speech · 1395 BC – 1392 BC

    The eastern tribes swear that they did not build the altar in rebellion. They say it is a copy meant as a witness that they too worship the LORD, so their descendants will not be shut out.

    Worship and praise
  7. Peace is restored
    Joshua 22:30–34 · narrative · 1395 BC – 1392 BC

    Phinehas and the chiefs accept the explanation, and the Israelites give up the idea of war. The eastern tribes name the altar Witness.

    Peace and reconciliationWorship and praise
  8. The tribes who joined and those who stayed
    Judges 5:12–18 · poetry · 1220 BC – 1125 BC

    Deborah and Barak are summoned to rise, and the tribes are named: Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir, Zebulun, Issachar, and Naphtali answered the call. Reuben, Gilead, Dan, and Asher are rebuked for staying behind.

    War and conflict
  9. Ephraim captures Oreb and Zeeb
    Judges 7:23–25 · narrative · 1175 BC – 1160 BC

    Israelites from several tribes join the pursuit, and Gideon calls Ephraim to seize the Jordan fords. They capture and kill the Midianite princes Oreb and Zeeb and bring their heads to Gideon.

    War and conflict
  10. Gideon calms the men of Ephraim
    Judges 8:1–3 · dialogue · 1175 BC – 1160 BC

    Ephraim complains that Gideon did not call them to the fight against Midian. Gideon answers modestly that their capture of Oreb and Zeeb outdid his own efforts, and their anger subsides.

    Pride and humility
  11. Judah hands Samson over to the Philistines
    Judges 15:9–13 · dialogue · 1070 BC – 1058 BC

    The Philistines camp in Judah at Lehi to arrest Samson, and 3,000 men of Judah go to Samson's cave to bind him. He lets them tie him with two new ropes after they promise not to kill him.

    Opposition and persecution
  12. Israel gathers at Mizpah and hears the Levite
    Judges 20:1–7 · narrative · 1380 BC – 1300 BC

    All Israel assembles at Mizpah, 400,000 men strong, and the Levite tells his account of what happened at Gibeah and asks for their verdict. He says the men of Gibeah meant to kill him and abused his concubine so that she died.

    Justice and judgmentWar and conflict
  13. Israel mourns the loss of a tribe
    Judges 21:1–7 · narrative · 1380 BC – 1300 BC

    Israel had sworn at Mizpah not to give daughters in marriage to Benjamin. Gathered at Bethel, they weep that one tribe is now missing and ask how the survivors can get wives without breaking the oath.

    Sin and the fall
  14. David is invited back as king
    2 Samuel 19:9–15 · narrative · 972 BC – 971 BC

    The tribes debate restoring David, and David asks Judah's elders not to be last. He offers Amasa Joab's place, and Judah calls him back.

    Kings and kingshipLeadership
  15. Judah and Israel argue over the king
    2 Samuel 19:41–43 · dialogue · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Israel complains Judah took the king without them. Judah replies that David is their relative, and Israel claims ten shares in him.

    Kings and kingship
  16. Sheba's revolt
    2 Samuel 20:1–3 · narrative · 971 BC – 970 BC

    A Benjamite named Sheba calls the tribes to abandon David, and Israel follows him while Judah stays loyal. David places the ten concubines he left behind under guard.

    Kings and kingshipWar and conflict
  17. Benjamite archers join David at Ziklag
    1 Chronicles 12:1–7 · list · 1012 BC – 1010 BC

    The chapter lists warriors from Saul's own tribe of Benjamin who joined David at Ziklag while he was banished. They were skilled with both hands at slinging stones and shooting arrows.

    War and conflict
  18. David tests loyalty, Amasai's oath
    1 Chronicles 12:16–18 · dialogue · 1012 BC – 1010 BC

    More Benjamites and Judahites come to David, who asks whether they come in peace or to betray him. Moved by the Spirit, Amasai declares their loyalty, and David receives them as leaders of his troops.

    FaithfulnessLeadership
  19. Manasseh joins David
    1 Chronicles 12:19–22 · narrative · 1011 BC – 1010 BC

    Some men of Manasseh join David when the Philistine rulers send him away from their war against Saul. They help him against raiders as more men come each day, until his army is great.

    War and conflict
  20. Armies gather at Hebron
    1 Chronicles 12:23–37 · list · 1003 BC

    The numbers of armed men from each tribe who came to Hebron to make David king are listed. They range from Judah to the tribes east of the Jordan.

    Kings and kingship
  21. Three days of feasting at Hebron
    1 Chronicles 12:38–40 · narrative · 1003 BC

    All the men came determined to make David king over all Israel and shared a three-day feast. Neighbors brought abundant food, and there was joy in Israel.

    Joy and thanksgivingKings and kingship
  22. Hezekiah invites all Israel to Passover
    2 Chronicles 30:1–5 · narrative · 716 BC – 714 BC

    King Hezekiah and his assembly decide to keep the Passover in the second month, since the priests and people were not ready in the first. He sends word to both Judah and the northern tribes inviting everyone to Jerusalem.

    RepentanceSabbath and feasts
  23. Repairs at the Old Gate and Broad Wall
    Nehemiah 3:6–12 · list · 444 BC

    The Jeshanah Gate is repaired, and craftsmen such as goldsmiths and perfumers join in. Two district rulers also work, one with the help of his daughters, continuing along the wall to the Tower of the Ovens.

    Work and rest
  24. The southwest gates are repaired
    Nehemiah 3:13–15 · list · 444 BC

    The Valley Gate, the Dung Gate, and the Fountain Gate are rebuilt by local residents and district rulers, each fitted with doors, bolts, and bars.

    Work and rest
  25. Work along the south and east near David's city
    Nehemiah 3:16–27 · list · 444 BC

    Levites, district rulers, and priests repair sections toward the tombs of David and the house of the high priest. Others work on the eastern side near Ophel, with the Tekoites doing a second section.

    Work and rest
  26. The wall is completed back to the Sheep Gate
    Nehemiah 3:28–32 · list · 444 BC

    Priests, guards, goldsmiths, and merchants repair their sections near the Horse Gate and East Gate, closing the circuit at the Sheep Gate where the chapter began.

    Work and rest
  27. The procession into the sanctuary
    Psalms 68:24–31 · poetry

    A procession of God into the sanctuary is described, with singers, musicians and tribal leaders. The psalm prays that God show his strength and that kings and nations, including Egypt and Cush, bring gifts to Jerusalem.

    Worship and praiseTemple and tabernacle
  28. Jerusalem, where the tribes go up
    Psalms 122:3–5 · praise · 1003 BC – 970 BC

    Jerusalem is described as a compact, unified city to which the tribes of the LORD go up to give thanks. It is also the seat of judgment, with thrones of David's house.

    Worship and praiseKings and kingship
  29. Pray for the peace of Jerusalem
    Psalms 122:6–9 · exhortation · 1003 BC – 970 BC

    The psalmist urges people to pray for Jerusalem's peace and security, and he pledges to seek its good for the sake of his people and the house of the LORD.

    Peace and reconciliationPrayer
  30. The blessing of unity
    Psalms 133:1–3 · wisdom · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    David says how good and pleasant it is when God's people live together in unity, comparing it to fragrant oil on Aaron's head and to dew from Hermon falling on Zion, where the LORD commands blessing.

    Community and friendship
