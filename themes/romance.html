Romantic love

32 passages · Humanity

Genesis · 4Ruth · 1Song of Solomon · 27
  1. The woman is made
    Genesis 2:21–25 · narrative

    God makes a woman from the man's rib and brings her to him. The man celebrates her as his own kind, and the narrator says a man and wife become one flesh, unashamed.

    Family and marriage
  2. Rebekah meets Isaac and marries him
    Genesis 24:61–67 · narrative · 2026 BC

    Rebekah and her maids travel to Canaan, where she first sees Isaac walking in the field and veils herself. Isaac takes her as his wife and is comforted after his mother Sarah's death.

    Family and marriage
  3. Jacob meets Rachel at the well
    Genesis 29:1–14 · narrative · 1929 BC

    Jacob arrives near Haran, learns from shepherds that Laban's daughter Rachel is coming, and rolls the stone from the well for her flock. He kisses her, weeps, and is welcomed by Laban.

    Family and marriage
  4. Jacob serves for Rachel and is tricked
    Genesis 29:15–30 · narrative · 1929 BC – 1922 BC

    Jacob agrees to work seven years for Rachel, but Laban gives him Leah on the wedding night. Laban explains local custom and Jacob agrees to work seven more years for Rachel.

    Family and marriageSin and the fall
  5. Ruth at Boaz's feet at midnight
    Ruth 3:6–9 · narrative · 1123 BC – 1100 BC

    Ruth carries out the plan. Boaz wakes startled at midnight and finds a woman at his feet, and Ruth asks him to spread his garment over her because he is a kinsman-redeemer.

    RedemptionFaithfulness
  6. Longing for the beloved's kisses
    Song of Solomon 1:1–4 · poetry · 970 BC – 931 BC

    The book opens with its title and the woman's yearning for her beloved, whose love and name she finds sweeter than wine. Others join in to praise him.

  7. Dark yet lovely, seeking the shepherd
    Song of Solomon 1:5–8 · poetry

    The woman tells the daughters of Jerusalem not to stare at her sun-darkened skin, explaining she was made to tend her family's vineyards. She asks her beloved where he pastures his flock and is told to follow the shepherds' tracks.

  8. The lover praises her beauty
    Song of Solomon 1:9–11 · poetry

    The man compares his beloved to a mare among Pharaoh's chariots and admires her adornments, promising gold and silver ornaments.

  9. She treasures him like perfume
    Song of Solomon 1:12–14 · poetry

    The woman describes her beloved as fragrant myrrh and henna blossom, delighting in his closeness.

  10. Mutual delight in the countryside
    Song of Solomon 1:15–17 · poetry

    The two exchange short praises of one another's beauty and picture the green outdoors as their bed and cedar and fir as their house.

    Creation
  11. Rose among thorns, apple tree among trees
    Song of Solomon 2:1–3 · poetry

    The woman calls herself a simple flower of the countryside, and the man says she stands out among other women like a lily among thorns. She answers that he is like an apple tree whose shade and fruit she enjoys.

  12. Faint with love in his embrace
    Song of Solomon 2:4–7 · poetry

    The woman describes being brought to a banquet under love's banner, feeling faint with love and held in his arms. She charges the daughters of Jerusalem not to stir up love before its proper time.

  13. He comes leaping over the hills
    Song of Solomon 2:8–9 · poetry

    The woman hears her beloved approaching, bounding like a gazelle across the hills, and finds him standing outside her wall looking in.

  14. Come away, for spring has come
    Song of Solomon 2:10–14 · poetry

    The beloved calls her to come away with him because winter is over and spring has arrived, with flowers, birds and ripening figs. He asks to see her face and hear her voice.

    Creation
  15. Little foxes and the lover's promise
    Song of Solomon 2:15–17 · poetry

    A refrain asks for the little foxes that spoil blooming vineyards to be caught, a picture of small threats to love. The woman affirms that she and her beloved belong to each other and asks him to return before daybreak.

  16. Searching the city for the beloved
    Song of Solomon 3:1–5 · poetry

    The woman tells of seeking her beloved at night through the city streets, asking the watchmen, and finally finding him and bringing him to her mother's house. She repeats her charge not to awaken love too early.

  17. Solomon's carriage and wedding day
    Song of Solomon 3:6–11 · poetry · 970 BC – 931 BC

    Onlookers describe a procession coming from the wilderness: a fragrant, luxurious carriage of King Solomon guarded by sixty warriors. The daughters of Zion are invited to see the king wearing his wedding crown.

    Kings and kingshipFamily and marriage
  18. He praises her from head to breast
    Song of Solomon 4:1–7 · poetry

    The man lists her features one by one, using images of doves, goats, sheep, pomegranates and towers, and concludes that she is beautiful without flaw. He says he will go to the mountain of myrrh.

    Family and marriage
  19. My bride, a locked garden
    Song of Solomon 4:8–15 · poetry

    He calls her to come away, says she has captured his heart, and praises her love as better than wine. He describes her as an enclosed garden and sealed spring filled with the finest spices and fresh water.

    Family and marriage
  20. Invitation into the garden
    Song of Solomon 4:16 · poetry

    The bride calls the winds to spread the garden's fragrance and invites her beloved to come into his garden and taste its fruits.

  21. He enjoys the garden; friends celebrate
    Song of Solomon 5:1 · poetry

    The man says he has come into his garden and enjoyed its spice, honey and wine, and voices urge the lovers to eat and drink freely.

    Joy and thanksgiving
  22. The knock at the door and his departure
    Song of Solomon 5:2–8 · poetry

    The woman tells of a dream-like night when her beloved knocked and she hesitated. When she opened he was gone, and the city watchmen beat her while she searched. She asks the daughters of Jerusalem to tell him she is sick with love.

  23. What makes her beloved special
    Song of Solomon 5:9–16 · poetry

    The daughters ask what sets her beloved apart, and she answers with a head-to-toe description of him in images of gold, jewels and cedars, ending by calling him her beloved and friend.

  24. Where has your beloved gone?
    Song of Solomon 6:1–3 · poetry

    The daughters of Jerusalem offer to help search for him, and the woman replies that he has gone to his garden. She affirms that they belong to each other.

  25. He praises her as unique
    Song of Solomon 6:4–10 · poetry

    The man again praises her beauty with familiar images and calls her his one perfect love, unmatched by queens and concubines. Others praise her as radiant like the dawn.

  26. Into the walnut grove; call to return
    Song of Solomon 6:11–13 · poetry

    The woman describes going to a walnut grove and being swept unexpectedly among royal chariots. Voices call the Shulammite to come back so they can gaze at her.

  27. Admiring her from feet to mouth
    Song of Solomon 7:1–9 · poetry

    The man describes her beauty from her feet upward with images of jewels, wheat, towers and a palm tree. He says he will climb the palm tree and take hold of its fruit.

  28. She invites him to the countryside
    Song of Solomon 7:10–13 · poetry

    The woman says she belongs to her beloved and invites him to spend the night in the fields and vineyards among the blossoms, where she will give him her love.

    Creation
  29. Wishing love could be open and public
    Song of Solomon 8:1–4 · poetry

    The woman wishes her beloved were like a brother so she could show affection freely. She pictures leading him to her mother's house and repeats her charge not to awaken love before its time.

    Family and marriage
  30. Love as strong as death
    Song of Solomon 8:5–7 · poetry

    Onlookers see the couple coming up from the wilderness, and the woman asks to be set as a seal on his heart. She says love is as strong as death and cannot be quenched by floods or bought with wealth.

    Death and mortality
