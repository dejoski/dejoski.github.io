  1. The garden of Eden and its rivers
    Genesis 2:8–14 · narrative

    God plants a garden in Eden and puts the man there. Trees grow, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and a river divides into four branches.

    Creation
  2. God's command about the trees
    Genesis 2:15–17 · narrative

    God places the man in the garden to work and care for it. He allows eating from every tree but forbids the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, warning of death.

    ObedienceDeath and mortality
  3. Jacob asks to leave; Laban bargains
    Genesis 30:25–36 · dialogue · 1915 BC – 1909 BC

    After Joseph's birth, Jacob asks permission to go home. Laban wants him to stay, and Jacob proposes that his wages be the speckled and dark animals. Laban agrees, then removes those animals and moves them away.

    Wealth and poverty
  4. Jacob decides to leave Laban
    Genesis 31:1–16 · speech · 1909 BC

    Jacob senses Laban's changed attitude, and God tells him to return home. He explains to Rachel and Leah how Laban cheated him and how God has protected him, and they agree to go.

    Faithfulness
  5. Jacob's angry defense
    Genesis 31:36–42 · speech · 1909 BC

    Jacob protests his innocence and lists his twenty years of faithful, hard service for Laban, including changed wages. He says God's help alone kept him from leaving empty-handed.

    Faithfulness
  6. Joseph prospers in Potiphar's house
    Genesis 39:1–6 · narrative · 1898 BC – 1890 BC

    In Egypt, the Lord is with Joseph, and Potiphar puts him in charge of his whole household. God blesses Potiphar's house on Joseph's account.

    FaithfulnessProvidence
  7. Bezalel and Oholiab called as craftsmen
    Exodus 31:1–11 · other · 1446 BC

    God announces that he has chosen Bezalel of Judah and Oholiab of Dan, filled with his Spirit and skill, to make the tent, furnishings, priestly garments, oil and incense.

    The Holy SpiritElection and calling
  8. Sabbath rest before the work
    Exodus 35:1–3 · law · 1446 BC

    Moses gathers the congregation and reminds them of the Sabbath: six days of work, then complete rest. No work or fire-lighting on the seventh day.

    Sabbath and feasts
  9. Call for offerings and skilled workers
    Exodus 35:4–19 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses tells the people what the LORD commands: bring willing offerings of materials and let skilled craftsmen come make everything on the list, from the tabernacle to the priestly garments.

    Generosity and givingTemple and tabernacle
  10. The people give freely
    Exodus 35:20–29 · narrative · 1446 BC

    The people go and bring gold jewelry, yarn, linen, skins, wood, metals, gems, spices and oil. Skilled women spin yarn, and all whose hearts are willing, men and women, give.

    Generosity and giving
  11. Bezalel and Oholiab named
    Exodus 35:30–35 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses tells the people that the LORD has called Bezalel and Oholiab, filled them with skill and given them the ability to teach others, so they can do every kind of craft.

    The Holy SpiritElection and calling
  12. Building the curtains and coverings
    Exodus 36:8–19 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The craftsmen make the ten linen curtains with cherubim, the eleven goat-hair curtains, and the coverings of ram skins and leather. The description repeats the commands given earlier almost word for word.

    Temple and tabernacle
  13. Building the frames and crossbars
    Exodus 36:20–34 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The workers make the upright acacia frames, silver bases, crossbars and gold overlay, forming the walls of the tabernacle exactly as commanded.

    Temple and tabernacle
  14. Making the ark and mercy seat
    Exodus 37:1–9 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Bezalel makes the gold-covered ark with its rings and poles, and the gold mercy seat with two hammered cherubim spreading their wings over it.

    Temple and tabernacle
  15. Making the table
    Exodus 37:10–16 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Bezalel makes the gold-covered table with its rim, rings and poles, and its gold plates, dishes, bowls and pitchers.

    Temple and tabernacle
  16. Making the lampstand
    Exodus 37:17–24 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Bezalel makes the lampstand of one piece of hammered gold with six branches, almond-blossom cups, seven lamps and its tools, using one talent of gold.

    Temple and tabernacle
  17. Incense altar, oil and incense
    Exodus 37:25–29 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Bezalel makes the gold-covered incense altar with its rings and poles, and also the sacred anointing oil and pure incense.

    Temple and tabernacle
  18. Work finished and inspected
    Exodus 39:32–43 · narrative · 1445 BC

    The Israelites complete all the work and bring every part of the tabernacle and garments to Moses. He inspects it, sees it was done as the LORD commanded, and blesses them.

    ObedienceTemple and tabernacle
  19. The Sabbath, Passover, and Unleavened Bread
    Leviticus 23:1–8 · law · 1445 BC

    God introduces his appointed feasts as sacred assemblies, beginning with the weekly Sabbath of rest. He then sets the dates for Passover and the seven-day Feast of Unleavened Bread.

    Sabbath and feasts
  20. Age limits for Levite service
    Numbers 8:23–26 · law · 1445 BC

    Levite men serve at twenty-five and retire from heavy work at fifty, though they may still assist.

    Priesthood
  21. Beware of pride and forgetting God
    Deuteronomy 8:11–20 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses warns that when Israel is prosperous they may grow proud and credit their own strength, forgetting God. He reminds them God gives the power to gain wealth and that forgetting Him will bring destruction.

    Pride and humilityIdolatry
  22. Victory, prosperity, and a name before the nations
    Deuteronomy 28:7–14 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The LORD will defeat Israel's enemies, bless their barns and work, and make them his holy people. Other nations will see and stand in awe, provided Israel does not turn to other gods.

    PromiseThe nationsElection and calling
  23. Blessings on Zebulun, Gad, Dan, Naphtali, Asher
    Deuteronomy 33:18–25 · blessing · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses blesses Zebulun and Issachar in their work, Gad's fierce strength, Dan as a lion's cub, Naphtali with favor, and Asher with abundance and security.

    The promised landElection and calling
  24. Ruth gleans in Boaz's field
    Ruth 2:1–7 · narrative · 1124 BC – 1100 BC

    Ruth, with Naomi's permission, goes to glean behind the harvesters and happens on the field of Boaz, a relative of Elimelech. Boaz arrives, greets his workers, and asks his foreman who she is.

    ProvidenceJustice for the poor and oppressed
  25. Solomon's labor force and quarrying
    1 Kings 5:13–18 · narrative · 970 BC – 966 BC

    Solomon conscripts thousands of workers who rotate in shifts to Lebanon. Workers and craftsmen quarry stone and prepare timber for the temple foundation.

    Temple and tabernacle
  26. Huram and the bronze pillars
    1 Kings 7:13–22 · narrative · 966 BC – 959 BC

    Solomon brings the bronze craftsman Huram from Tyre, who casts two decorated pillars for the temple porch and names them Jachin and Boaz.

    Temple and tabernacle
  27. More Judahite families and craftsmen
    1 Chronicles 4:11–23 · genealogy

    Further families of Judah are listed, including Othniel, Caleb son of Jephunneh and the descendants of Shelah. Notes mention craftsmen, linen workers and royal potters.

    Family and marriage
  28. Duties of the gatekeepers and Levites
    1 Chronicles 9:19–34 · history · 1010 BC – 444 BC

    The gatekeepers' work is described: guarding the entrance to the Tent as their fathers did, standing watch through the night and handling the utensils. Other Levites baked bread, prepared spices and served as musicians.

    Temple and tabernaclePriesthood
  29. Solomon plans the temple and labor force
    2 Chronicles 2:1–2 · narrative · 967 BC – 966 BC

    Solomon decides to build a house for God's Name and a palace for himself. He conscripts tens of thousands of workers to carry loads, cut stone, and supervise.

    Temple and tabernacle
  30. Solomon counts foreign workers
    2 Chronicles 2:17–18 · narrative · 967 BC – 966 BC

    Solomon counts the foreign residents of Israel, finding over 153,000, and assigns them as porters, stonecutters, and supervisors.

    The nations
