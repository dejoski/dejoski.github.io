  1. God remembers Noah; the waters recede
    Genesis 8:1–5 · narrative

    God sends a wind and closes the springs of the deep and the heavens' floodgates. The waters recede, the ark rests on the mountains of Ararat, and mountaintops become visible later.

    Mercy and compassion
  2. The covenant and the rainbow
    Genesis 9:8–17 · speech

    God makes a covenant never again to destroy all life by flood and sets the rainbow in the clouds as a sign of it.

    CovenantPromise
  3. Abram and Sarai in Egypt
    Genesis 12:10–20 · narrative · 2090 BC – 2086 BC

    Because of famine, Abram goes to Egypt and asks Sarai to say she is his sister. Pharaoh takes her, but the Lord afflicts his household, and Pharaoh sends Abram away with his wife and goods.

    Sin and the fall
  4. Isaac is born
    Genesis 21:1–7 · narrative · 2066 BC

    The Lord does for Sarah what he promised, and she bears Isaac. Abraham circumcises him at eight days, and Sarah says God has made her laugh.

    PromiseFamily and marriage
  5. God saves Hagar and Ishmael
    Genesis 21:14–21 · narrative · 2064 BC – 2062 BC

    Sent away with bread and water, Hagar despairs when the water runs out. God hears the boy, opens her eyes to a well, and promises to make Ishmael a great nation.

    Mercy and compassion
  6. The promise renewed by oath
    Genesis 22:15–19 · speech · 2056 BC – 2040 BC

    The angel calls again, saying God swears by himself to bless Abraham and multiply his descendants like the stars because he obeyed. All nations will be blessed through his offspring.

    PromiseCovenant
  7. The servant tells his story
    Genesis 24:34–49 · speech · 2026 BC

    The servant recounts to Rebekah's family his master's wealth, Abraham's oath, his own prayer, and how Rebekah met the sign. He then asks for a plain answer whether they will agree.

    Providence
  8. God renews the promise to Isaac
    Genesis 26:1–6 · speech · 1990 BC – 1970 BC

    During a famine Isaac goes to Gerar. God tells him not to go to Egypt but to stay in the land, and repeats to him the promises made to Abraham of land, many descendants, and blessing for all nations.

    PromiseCovenant
  9. Isaac prospers and digs wells
    Genesis 26:12–22 · narrative · 1985 BC – 1965 BC

    Isaac farms, grows very rich, and the Philistines become envious and block his father's wells. After moving and reopening the wells, he moves on until he digs one that is not disputed.

    Wealth and poverty
  10. Jacob decides to leave Laban
    Genesis 31:1–16 · speech · 1909 BC

    Jacob senses Laban's changed attitude, and God tells him to return home. He explains to Rachel and Leah how Laban cheated him and how God has protected him, and they agree to go.

    Work and rest
  11. Jacob's angry defense
    Genesis 31:36–42 · speech · 1909 BC

    Jacob protests his innocence and lists his twenty years of faithful, hard service for Laban, including changed wages. He says God's help alone kept him from leaving empty-handed.

    Work and rest
  12. Joseph prospers in Potiphar's house
    Genesis 39:1–6 · narrative · 1898 BC – 1890 BC

    In Egypt, the Lord is with Joseph, and Potiphar puts him in charge of his whole household. God blesses Potiphar's house on Joseph's account.

    ProvidenceWork and rest
  13. The Lord is with Joseph in prison
    Genesis 39:21–23 · narrative · 1889 BC – 1886 BC

    Even in prison the Lord shows Joseph kindness, and the warden puts him in charge of the other prisoners. Everything under his care succeeds.

    Providence
  14. Seven abundant years and two sons
    Genesis 41:46–52 · narrative · 1885 BC – 1878 BC

    At age thirty Joseph tours Egypt and stores grain in vast quantities. Before the famine he has two sons, whose names express how God eased his past hardship and made him fruitful.

    Family and marriage
  15. Jacob's family grows in Egypt
    Exodus 1:1–7 · history · 1876 BC – 1700 BC

    The book opens by listing the sons of Israel who came to Egypt with Jacob. After Joseph's generation dies, their descendants multiply until the land is full of them.

    Promise
  16. God hears Israel's groaning
    Exodus 2:23–25 · narrative · 1450 BC – 1446 BC

    Years later the king of Egypt dies, but Israel still groans under slavery. God hears their cry and remembers his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

    CovenantSuffering
  17. Israel leaves Egypt
    Exodus 12:37–42 · narrative · 1446 BC

    About 600,000 men on foot, plus women, children, a mixed crowd, and livestock, set out from Rameses. The text notes their unleavened bread and the 430 years of the stay, and calls it a night of vigil.

    Exodus and deliverance
  18. Manna described and a jar kept
    Exodus 16:31–36 · narrative · 1446 BC

    The manna is described as white like coriander seed and tasting like honey wafers. An omer is kept in a jar before the Testimony as a reminder, and Israel ate manna for forty years.

  19. The glory of the LORD fills the tabernacle
    Exodus 40:34–38 · narrative · 1445 BC

    A cloud covers the tent and the glory of the LORD fills it, so Moses cannot enter. The cloud's lifting or staying guides the Israelites' travels, and fire is in it by night.

    GloryTemple and tabernacle
  20. Pardon, but the generation will not enter
    Numbers 14:20–25 · speech · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    God pardons them but says none of that generation who tested Him will see the land, except Caleb, who followed Him wholeheartedly. They are to turn back to the wilderness.

    ForgivenessJustice and judgment
  21. Victory over the king of Arad
    Numbers 21:1–3 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    A Canaanite king attacks Israel and takes prisoners. Israel vows to the LORD to devote the enemy's cities to destruction, and God grants victory; the place is named Hormah.

    War and conflict
  22. Second oracle: God does not change His mind
    Numbers 23:18–26 · oracle of hope · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balaam says God is not a man who lies or changes His mind, and that God has commanded a blessing he cannot reverse. No spell can stand against Israel, whom God brought out of Egypt. Balak then says to neither bless nor curse.

    Promise
  23. Introducing the record of the journeys
    Numbers 33:1–4 · history · 1446 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses writes down each stage of Israel's journey out of Egypt at the LORD's command. It begins with leaving Rameses the day after Passover as the Egyptians buried their firstborn.

    Exodus and deliverance
  24. The confession over the firstfruits
    Deuteronomy 26:5–11 · prayer · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The worshiper recites Israel's story: a wandering forefather, slavery in Egypt, God's rescue, and the gift of the land. He presents the firstfruits, bows down, and rejoices with the Levite and the foreigner.

    Worship and praiseExodus and deliveranceRedemption
  25. Moses recalls what Israel has seen
    Deuteronomy 29:1–8 · speech · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses gathers all Israel in Moab for a covenant beyond the one at Horeb. He reminds them of the wonders in Egypt, forty years of care in the wilderness, and victories over Sihon and Og, yet says they still lack understanding.

    CovenantExodus and deliverance
  26. Moses hands over leadership
    Deuteronomy 31:1–6 · speech · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses, at 120, says he can no longer lead and will not cross the Jordan. He assures Israel that the LORD himself will go ahead of them with Joshua and tells them to be strong and courageous.

    LeadershipGod's sovereignty
  27. Song opens: God is faithful, Israel is not
    Deuteronomy 32:1–6 · poetry · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses' song calls heaven and earth to listen and praises the LORD as the Rock, perfect and just. It contrasts his faithfulness with Israel's corrupt behavior and asks whether this is how they repay their Father.

    Who God isSin and the fall
  28. God's care for Jacob
    Deuteronomy 32:7–14 · poetry · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The song recalls God finding Israel in the wilderness and guarding them like the apple of his eye, like an eagle carrying its young. He led them alone and fed them richly in the land.

    LoveMercy and compassion
  29. There is none like the God of Israel
    Deuteronomy 33:26–29 · praise · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses closes by praising the God who rides the heavens to help Israel, whose everlasting arms carry them. He declares Israel blessed as a people saved by the LORD, who will drive out their enemies and live in security.

    Who God isSalvationElection and calling

    Deuteronomy 34:1–4 · narrative · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses climbs Mount Nebo, and the LORD shows him the whole land from Gilead to the Western Sea. The LORD says this is the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, but Moses will not cross into it.
