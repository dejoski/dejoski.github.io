  1. The serpent questions God's word
    Genesis 3:1–5 · dialogue

    A crafty serpent asks the woman whether God really forbade eating from any tree. She restates the command, and the serpent contradicts it, saying God is withholding godlike knowledge.

    Temptation and spiritual warfareWords and speech
  2. The fruit is eaten
    Genesis 3:6–8 · narrative

    The woman eats the fruit and gives some to her husband, who eats. Their eyes are opened, they realize they are naked, sew fig leaves, and hide from God.

    Temptation and spiritual warfare
  3. God questions the man and woman
    Genesis 3:9–13 · dialogue

    God calls out to the man, who admits fear and hiding. When asked if he ate, the man blames the woman, and she blames the serpent.

    Justice and judgment
  4. Consequences spoken over serpent, woman, man
    Genesis 3:14–19 · oracle of judgment

    God pronounces consequences: the serpent is cursed with a coming conflict, the woman will face pain and strained relations, and the man will toil against a cursed ground until he returns to dust.

    Justice and judgmentDeath and mortality
  5. Clothing and expulsion from Eden
    Genesis 3:20–24 · narrative

    Adam names his wife Eve, and God clothes them in skins. He then sends them out of the garden and posts cherubim and a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree of life.

    Grace
  6. Cain and Abel's offerings
    Genesis 4:1–7 · narrative

    Eve bears Cain and Abel. Both bring offerings, and God favors Abel's but not Cain's. God warns the angry Cain that sin is crouching at his door but that he can master it.

    Worship and praise
  7. Cain kills Abel
    Genesis 4:8–12 · narrative

    Cain lures Abel to the field and kills him. When God asks where Abel is, Cain answers evasively, and God declares that Abel's blood cries out and Cain will be a wanderer.

    Justice and judgment
  8. Cain's descendants and Lamech's boast
    Genesis 4:17–24 · genealogy

    Cain's line builds a city and produces herdsmen, musicians, and metalworkers. Lamech takes two wives and boasts of killing a man, claiming far greater vengeance than Cain.

    Family and marriage
  9. The sons of God and the daughters of men
    Genesis 6:1–4 · narrative

    As people multiply, the 'sons of God' take wives from the daughters of men. God limits human days to 120 years, and the Nephilim and mighty men of old are mentioned.

    Family and marriage
  10. God grieves over human wickedness
    Genesis 6:5–8 · narrative

    God sees that human hearts are wholly evil and regrets making man, grieved. He resolves to blot out life from the earth, but Noah finds favor in his eyes.

    Justice and judgmentWrath
  11. Noah's vineyard and Ham's act
    Genesis 9:18–23 · narrative

    Noah plants a vineyard, becomes drunk, and lies uncovered. Ham tells his brothers, while Shem and Japheth cover their father without looking.

    Family and marriage
  12. Noah's words about his sons
    Genesis 9:24–27 · prophecy

    Waking, Noah curses Canaan, Ham's son, as a servant to his brothers, and blesses the Lord, the God of Shem. He asks that Japheth's territory expand and that Canaan serve them.

    The nations
  13. One language and the plan at Shinar
    Genesis 11:1–4 · narrative

    The whole world speaks one language. People settle in Shinar, make bricks, and decide to build a city and tower to make a name for themselves and avoid being scattered.

    Pride and humility
  14. Abram and Sarai in Egypt
    Genesis 12:10–20 · narrative · 2090 BC – 2086 BC

    Because of famine, Abram goes to Egypt and asks Sarai to say she is his sister. Pharaoh takes her, but the Lord afflicts his household, and Pharaoh sends Abram away with his wife and goods.

    Faithfulness
  15. Angels at Lot's house and the mob
    Genesis 19:1–11 · narrative · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Two angels arrive at Sodom and Lot insists they stay with him. The men of the city surround the house demanding the visitors, and Lot offers his daughters instead. The angels pull him in and strike the crowd with blindness.

    Angels and spiritual beingsPurity and sexual ethics
  16. Lot's daughters and the origin of Moab and Ammon
    Genesis 19:30–38 · narrative · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Living in a cave, Lot's daughters fear they will have no children. They get their father drunk and each conceive a son, Moab and Ben-ammi, ancestors of the Moabites and Ammonites.

    Family and marriageThe nations
  17. Abimelech confronts Abraham
    Genesis 20:8–13 · dialogue · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Abimelech, alarmed, asks why Abraham brought guilt on his kingdom. Abraham explains he feared no one there feared God and says Sarah is his half-sister.

    Words and speech
  18. Isaac says Rebekah is his sister
    Genesis 26:7–11 · narrative · 1990 BC – 1970 BC

    Afraid he will be killed because of Rebekah's beauty, Isaac claims she is his sister. King Abimelech sees them together, confronts him, and orders that no one harm the couple.

    Family and marriage
  19. Isaac plans to bless Esau; Rebekah overhears
    Genesis 27:1–10 · narrative · 1930 BC – 1929 BC

    Old and nearly blind, Isaac asks Esau to hunt and prepare a meal so he can bless him. Rebekah overhears and tells Jacob to bring goats so that he can get the blessing instead.

    Family and marriage
  20. Jacob's fear and Rebekah's disguise
    Genesis 27:11–17 · dialogue · 1930 BC – 1929 BC

    Jacob worries he will be found out and cursed, but Rebekah says she will take the curse. She cooks the meal and dresses Jacob in Esau's clothes with goatskins on his hands and neck.

    Family and marriage
  21. Jacob deceives Isaac and receives the blessing
    Genesis 27:18–29 · narrative · 1930 BC – 1929 BC

    Jacob claims to be Esau. Isaac is suspicious but touches him, smells his clothes, and gives the blessing promising rich land, rule over his brothers, and blessing for those who bless him.

    Family and marriagePromise
  22. Esau arrives too late
    Genesis 27:30–40 · dialogue · 1930 BC – 1929 BC

    Esau returns with his meal and Isaac realizes he has been tricked, trembling. Esau cries out for a blessing too, and Isaac gives a lesser one predicting a life of hardship and eventual rebellion.

    Family and marriage
  23. Esau's threat and Jacob sent away
    Genesis 27:41–46 · narrative · 1930 BC – 1929 BC

    Esau resolves to kill Jacob after Isaac dies. Rebekah warns Jacob to flee to her brother Laban in Haran, and she tells Isaac she wants Jacob to marry from her relatives, not from Hittite women.

    Family and marriage
  24. Jacob serves for Rachel and is tricked
    Genesis 29:15–30 · narrative · 1929 BC – 1922 BC

    Jacob agrees to work seven years for Rachel, but Laban gives him Leah on the wedding night. Laban explains local custom and Jacob agrees to work seven more years for Rachel.

    Romantic loveFamily and marriage
  25. Shechem seizes Dinah and asks to marry her
    Genesis 34:1–12 · narrative · 1907 BC – 1900 BC

    Shechem assaults Dinah, then wants her as his wife. His father Hamor asks Jacob's family to intermarry and settle among them, and Shechem offers any price.

    Purity and sexual ethicsFamily and marriage
  26. The brothers' deceitful condition
    Genesis 34:13–24 · narrative · 1907 BC – 1900 BC

    Jacob's sons say they can agree only if every Shechemite male is circumcised. Hamor and Shechem persuade the men of the city, pointing to the benefit of gaining Jacob's wealth, and all males are circumcised.

    Family and marriage
  27. Simeon and Levi's massacre
    Genesis 34:25–31 · narrative · 1907 BC – 1900 BC

    While the men are in pain, Simeon and Levi kill every male in the city and take Dinah back, and the other brothers loot it. Jacob fears retaliation, but they say their sister should not be treated like a prostitute.

    Justice and judgment
  28. The brothers plot; Joseph is thrown in a pit
    Genesis 37:12–24 · narrative · 1898 BC – 1897 BC

    Jacob sends Joseph to check on his brothers, who are grazing flocks, and he finds them at Dothan. They plan to kill him, but Reuben persuades them to throw him in an empty pit.

    Family and marriage
  29. Joseph is sold and Jacob is deceived
    Genesis 37:25–36 · narrative · 1898 BC – 1897 BC

    At Judah's suggestion, the brothers sell Joseph to traders bound for Egypt. They dip his robe in goat's blood and show it to Jacob, who believes Joseph is dead and mourns deeply, while Joseph is sold to Potiphar in Egypt.

    Family and marriageProvidence
  30. Judah's sons and Tamar
    Genesis 38:1–11 · narrative · 1897 BC – 1885 BC

    Judah leaves his brothers, marries a Canaanite woman, and has three sons. Tamar marries the eldest, Er, and after Er dies she is given to Onan, who also dies, and Judah sends Tamar home to wait for Shelah.

    Family and marriageDeath and mortality
