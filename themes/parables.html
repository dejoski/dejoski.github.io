Parables

44 passages · Jesus and the church

  1. Two builders and the crowd's reaction
    Matthew 7:24–29 · parable · AD 28 – AD 29

    Jesus compares those who hear and obey his words to a wise man building on rock, and those who hear and do not obey to a fool building on sand. When he finishes, the crowds are amazed because he teaches with authority, unlike their scribes.

    ObedienceDiscipleship
  2. The parable of the sower
    Matthew 13:1–9 · parable · AD 29

    Sitting in a boat, Jesus tells of a farmer whose seed falls on the path, rocky ground, thorns and good soil, with only the good soil producing a large crop. He ends by saying whoever has ears should hear.

    Kingdom of GodFaith
  3. Why Jesus speaks in parables
    Matthew 13:10–17 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus says the secrets of the kingdom are given to the disciples but not to others, and quotes Isaiah about people who see and hear but do not understand. He calls his disciples blessed for seeing what many prophets longed to see.

    Kingdom of God
  4. Jesus explains the sower
    Matthew 13:18–23 · teaching · AD 29

    Jesus explains that the seed is the message of the kingdom and the soils are different responses to it. Some hearers lose it, some fall away under trouble, some are choked by worry and wealth, and some bear fruit.

    Kingdom of God
  5. The parable of the weeds
    Matthew 13:24–30 · parable · AD 29

    A man sows good seed but an enemy sows weeds among the wheat. The owner refuses to pull the weeds early, since that would uproot the wheat, and says both will grow together until harvest when they will be separated.

    Final judgmentKingdom of God
  6. Mustard seed and yeast
    Matthew 13:31–35 · parable · AD 29

    Jesus says the kingdom is like a tiny mustard seed that grows into a large plant, and like yeast a woman mixes into flour until it all rises. Matthew notes Jesus spoke only in parables to the crowds, fulfilling a psalm.

    Kingdom of God
  7. Treasure, pearl, net and the storeroom
    Matthew 13:44–52 · parable · AD 29

    Jesus compares the kingdom to a hidden treasure and a fine pearl, each worth giving up everything to have, and to a net that catches good and bad fish to be sorted at the end. He closes by likening a trained scribe to a homeowner bringing out new and old treasures.

    Kingdom of God
  8. Landowner hires workers through the day
    Matthew 20:1–7 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    Jesus describes a landowner who hires workers for his vineyard at dawn for a denarius, then keeps hiring more at the third, sixth, ninth and eleventh hours, promising to pay whatever is right.

    GraceKingdom of God
  9. Equal wages and the grumbling workers
    Matthew 20:8–16 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    At evening everyone receives the same denarius, and the all-day workers complain. The owner answers that he has been fair and is free to be generous, so the last will be first and the first last.

    GracePride and humility
  10. Parable of the two sons
    Matthew 21:28–32 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    A father asks two sons to work in the vineyard. One refuses but later goes, the other agrees but does not. Jesus applies it: tax collectors and prostitutes believed John and enter God's kingdom ahead of the leaders.

    RepentanceObedience
  11. Parable of the wicked tenants
    Matthew 21:33–46 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    A landowner's servants and then his son are killed by tenants of his vineyard. Jesus cites the rejected stone psalm and warns that the kingdom will be given to others; the leaders realize it is about them and want to arrest him but fear the crowds.

    Opposition and persecutionKingdom of GodFinal judgment
  12. Parable of the wedding banquet
    Matthew 22:1–14 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    A king's invited guests refuse and mistreat his servants, so he destroys them and invites everyone from the streets. A guest without wedding clothes is thrown out, and Jesus closes by saying many are called but few chosen.

    Kingdom of GodElection and calling
  13. Parable of the ten virgins
    Matthew 25:1–13 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    Ten young women wait for a bridegroom; five wise ones bring extra oil and five foolish ones do not. When he arrives at midnight the unready are shut out. Jesus ends with the call to keep watch.

    The end timesDiscipleship
  14. Parable of the talents
    Matthew 25:14–30 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    A master entrusts money to three servants before traveling. Two double it and are praised, but the third buries his and is condemned as lazy and afraid. The master takes it away and casts him out.

    DiscipleshipFinal judgment
  15. Parable of the sower
    Mark 4:1–9 · parable · AD 29 – AD 31

    Teaching a crowd from a boat, Jesus tells of a farmer whose seed falls on the path, rocky ground, thorns and good soil, with only the good soil producing a large crop.

  16. Why Jesus teaches in parables
    Mark 4:10–13 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 31

    Alone with the Twelve, Jesus says the mystery of God's kingdom is given to them while outsiders get parables, echoing Isaiah about seeing without perceiving. He asks why they do not grasp this parable.

    Kingdom of God
  17. The sower explained
    Mark 4:14–20 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 31

    Jesus explains that the seed is the word and the soils are different responses: some lose it at once, some fall away under trouble, some are choked by worries and wealth, and some bear much fruit.

    Faith
  18. Lamp on a stand and the measure you use
    Mark 4:21–25 · wisdom · AD 29 – AD 31

    Jesus uses the image of a lamp to say that what is hidden will come to light, and urges careful listening because the measure people use will be measured back to them.

  19. Parable of the growing seed
    Mark 4:26–29 · parable · AD 29 – AD 31

    The kingdom of God is like a man who scatters seed and then sleeps and rises while the seed grows by itself, until the harvest comes.

    Kingdom of God
  20. Parable of the mustard seed
    Mark 4:30–34 · parable · AD 29 – AD 31

    Jesus compares the kingdom to a tiny mustard seed that grows into the largest garden plant and shelters birds. Mark adds that Jesus spoke in parables to the crowd but explained everything privately to his disciples.

    Kingdom of God
  21. Parable of the vineyard tenants
    Mark 12:1–12 · parable · AD 30 – AD 33

    Jesus tells of tenants who beat and kill the owner's servants and finally his beloved son. He cites the rejected stone that became the cornerstone, and the leaders realize the parable is aimed at them.

    Opposition and persecutionThe cross
  22. The parable of the sower
    Luke 8:4–8 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus tells of a farmer whose seed falls on the path, on rock, among thorns and on good soil, where it yields a hundredfold. He closes by calling those with ears to hear.

    Gospel and missionKingdom of God
  23. Jesus explains the sower
    Luke 8:9–15 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    The disciples ask about the parable, and Jesus explains that the seed is God's word and the soils are different responses to it. Only some hearers keep it and bear fruit with patience.

    FaithObedience
  24. A lamp on a stand
    Luke 8:16–18 · teaching · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus says no one hides a lamp, hidden things will be made known, and he urges care in how they listen.

    Words and speech
  25. The good Samaritan
    Luke 10:25–37 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    An expert in the law asks how to inherit eternal life, and they agree on loving God and neighbor. Asked who is his neighbor, Jesus tells of a Samaritan who cared for a wounded man ignored by a priest and a Levite, and says to go and do likewise.

    Loving othersMercy and compassionEternal life
  26. Persistence and the Father's gifts
    Luke 11:5–13 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus tells of a friend who gets bread at midnight through persistence, then says to ask, seek and knock. He adds that a father gives good gifts, so the heavenly Father will give the Holy Spirit to those who ask.

    PrayerThe Holy Spirit
  27. The rich fool
    Luke 12:13–21 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    A man asks Jesus to make his brother share an inheritance, and Jesus warns against greed. He tells of a rich man who stores up crops for himself and dies that night, unprepared.

    Wealth and povertyDeath and mortality
  28. Be ready and faithful
    Luke 12:35–48 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus tells the disciples to stay alert like servants awaiting their master and like a homeowner watching for a thief. When Peter asks whom this is for, Jesus speaks of the faithful and unfaithful manager and greater responsibility for those given more.

    The end timesFaithfulnessFinal judgment
  29. Parable of the barren fig tree
    Luke 13:6–9 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    A vineyard owner wants to cut down a fig tree that has borne no fruit in three years. The keeper asks for one more year of care before deciding.

    RepentanceMercy and compassion
  30. Parables of mustard seed and leaven
    Luke 13:18–21 · parable · AD 29 – AD 30

    Jesus compares the kingdom of God to a tiny mustard seed that grows into a large tree and to leaven that works through a whole batch of flour. Both picture small beginnings with great results.

    Kingdom of God
