  1. Moses meets God at the burning bush
    Exodus 3:1–6 · narrative · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    While shepherding, Moses sees a bush burning without being consumed. God calls to him from it, tells him to remove his sandals on holy ground, and identifies himself as the God of Moses' ancestors.

    Dreams, visions and revelationElection and calling
  2. Preparing for God's coming
    Exodus 19:9–15 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD says he will come in a dense cloud so the people will hear. Moses is to consecrate them, set a boundary around the mountain, and have them ready by the third day.

  3. God comes down on Sinai
    Exodus 19:16–19 · narrative · 1446 BC

    On the third day thunder, lightning, a thick cloud, and a loud ram's horn make the camp tremble. Moses leads the people to the foot of the mountain, which is wrapped in smoke and shakes, and God answers Moses in the thunder.

    Dreams, visions and revelationGlory
  4. Warnings about approaching the mountain
    Exodus 19:20–25 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The LORD calls Moses up and tells him to warn the people and even the priests not to break through to see him. Moses reminds him of the boundary, and God tells him to bring Aaron up.

  5. The people are afraid and stand at a distance
    Exodus 20:18–21 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The people tremble at the thunder and smoke and ask Moses to speak instead of God. Moses says God has come to test them and keep them from sinning, and he approaches the thick darkness.

    Obedience
  6. Reverence and holiness
    Exodus 22:28–31 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Israel must not curse God or its ruler and must give firstfruits and firstborn to the LORD. As a holy people, they must not eat meat torn by wild animals.

    Law and commandments
  7. The ark and mercy seat
    Exodus 25:10–22 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God gives dimensions for a gold-covered wooden ark with carrying poles, to hold the Testimony, and a gold mercy seat with two cherubim. God says he will meet and speak with Moses there.

    Temple and tabernacleAtonement and sacrifice
  8. The veil and entrance curtain
    Exodus 26:31–37 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God describes a veil that will divide the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place, where the ark sits, and where to place the table and lampstand. A curtain will also cover the tent entrance.

    Temple and tabernacle
  9. Priests and their holy garments
    Exodus 28:1–5 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God directs Moses to bring Aaron and his four sons for priestly service. Skilled craftsmen are to make sacred garments to give them dignity, using gold, colored yarn and linen.

    Priesthood
  10. Robe, bells and the gold plate
    Exodus 28:31–38 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God describes a blue robe with gold bells and pomegranates on the hem, and a gold plate reading HOLY TO THE LORD fixed to the turban. The bells sound as Aaron enters the sanctuary, and the plate helps make the people's gifts acceptable.

    Priesthood
  11. Clothing for Aaron's sons
    Exodus 28:39–43 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God directs that the remaining tunics, turban and sash be made, along with garments for Aaron's sons and linen undergarments. Priests must wear them when serving so they do not incur guilt and die.

    Priesthood
  12. Preparing and clothing the priests
    Exodus 29:1–9 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God outlines how to consecrate Aaron and his sons: gather a bull, rams and bread, wash them, dress them in the holy garments, and anoint Aaron with oil. The priesthood is to be theirs by a lasting statute.

    Priesthood
  13. The bronze washing basin
    Exodus 30:17–21 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God tells Moses to make a bronze basin between the tent and altar. Aaron and his sons must wash their hands and feet before serving so that they will not die.

    Priesthood
  14. The sacred anointing oil
    Exodus 30:22–33 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God gives a recipe of myrrh, cinnamon, cane, cassia and olive oil for oil to anoint the tent, its furnishings and the priests. It may not be used on ordinary people or copied.

    Priesthood
  15. The holy incense
    Exodus 30:34–38 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God gives a formula for fragrant incense of four spices with salt, to be placed before the Testimony. It is holy and must not be copied for personal enjoyment.

    Worship and praise
  16. God will not go with them
    Exodus 33:1–6 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to leave for the promised land with an angel, but says he will not go among such a stiff-necked people lest he destroy them. The people mourn and remove their jewelry.

    WrathSin and the fall
  17. Robe, tunics and gold plate
    Exodus 39:22–31 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The workers make the blue robe with bells and pomegranates, the linen tunics, turbans, headbands, undergarments and sashes, and the gold plate engraved HOLY TO THE LORD. Each step is done as the LORD commanded.

    Priesthood
  18. Guilt offering for harming holy things
    Leviticus 5:14–16 · law · 1445 BC

    When someone unintentionally wrongs the LORD's holy things, he brings a ram as a guilt offering. He must also repay the value plus a fifth to the priest.

    Atonement and sacrificeSin and the fall
  19. Law of the grain offering for priests
    Leviticus 6:14–18 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    After the memorial portion is burned, Aaron's sons eat the rest of the grain offering as unleavened bread in the courtyard. It is most holy and a permanent share for the priests.

    Priesthood
  20. Law of the sin offering
    Leviticus 6:24–30 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    The sin offering is slaughtered where the burnt offering is, and the priest who offers it eats it in the holy courtyard. If its blood was brought into the tent, the meat must instead be burned.

    Atonement and sacrificePriesthood
  21. Law of the peace offering
    Leviticus 7:11–21 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A peace offering of thanksgiving comes with cakes and must be eaten the same day. Vow or freewill offerings can be eaten for two days, and anyone unclean who eats the meat is cut off from the people.

    Worship and praiseJoy and thanksgiving
  22. Aaron washed, dressed, and anointed
    Leviticus 8:6–13 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Moses washes Aaron and his sons and dresses Aaron in the priestly garments, including the breastpiece and turban. He anoints the tabernacle, its furnishings, and Aaron with oil, then dresses Aaron's sons.

    PriesthoodThe Holy Spirit
  23. The ordination ram and its blood
    Leviticus 8:22–30 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Moses slaughters the second ram and puts its blood on Aaron's and his sons' right ear, thumb, and big toe. Portions of the ram and bread are waved before the LORD, and Moses sprinkles oil and blood on them and their garments.

    PriesthoodAtonement and sacrifice
  24. Blessing and the LORD's fire
    Leviticus 9:22–24 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Aaron blesses the people, and Moses and Aaron then come out of the Tent and bless them again. The glory of the LORD appears, and fire consumes the offering, so the people shout for joy and fall on their faces.

    GloryWorship and praise
  25. Nadab and Abihu offer unauthorized fire
    Leviticus 10:1–7 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu offer fire the LORD had not commanded, and fire from the LORD kills them. Moses has their cousins carry the bodies out of the camp and tells Aaron and his other sons not to mourn openly.

    ObedienceWrath
  26. Priests not to drink wine
    Leviticus 10:8–11 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD tells Aaron that he and his sons must not drink wine or strong drink when they enter the Tent of Meeting. They must tell holy from common and clean from unclean, so they can teach the Israelites.

    Priesthood
  27. The burned sin offering goat
    Leviticus 10:16–20 · dialogue · 1445 BC

    Moses is angry that the sin offering goat was burned, not eaten in the holy place. Aaron explains that after what happened to his sons that day, eating it seemed wrong, and Moses accepts this.

    LeadershipSuffering
  28. Clean and unclean land animals
    Leviticus 11:1–8 · law · 1445 BC

    Israelites may eat animals that have a split hoof and chew the cud. The camel, rock badger, rabbit, and pig fail one test and are unclean, so their meat and carcasses are not to be touched.

    Law and commandments
  29. Clean and unclean water creatures
    Leviticus 11:9–12 · law · 1445 BC

    Anything in the seas or streams with fins and scales may be eaten. Sea creatures without both are detestable, and their meat is not to be eaten.

    Law and commandments
  30. Unclean birds and insects
    Leviticus 11:13–23 · list · 1445 BC

    This section lists birds that are detestable, such as eagles, vultures, ravens, and owls. It also bans most winged insects that walk on four legs, but allows locusts, crickets, and grasshoppers.

    Law and commandments
