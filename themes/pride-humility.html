Pride and humility

  1. One language and the plan at Shinar
    Genesis 11:1–4 · narrative

    The whole world speaks one language. People settle in Shinar, make bricks, and decide to build a city and tower to make a name for themselves and avoid being scattered.

    Sin and the fall
  2. The Lord confuses the language at Babel
    Genesis 11:5–9 · narrative

    The Lord comes down, sees the project, and confuses their language. The people are scattered across the earth and stop building, and the place is named Babel.

    Justice and judgmentThe nations
  3. Esau sells his birthright
    Genesis 25:27–34 · narrative · 1995 BC – 1970 BC

    The boys grow up very different, and each parent favors one. Famished, Esau trades his birthright to Jacob for a meal of lentil stew, and the text says he despised it.

    Family and marriage
  4. Jacob prays for rescue
    Genesis 32:9–12 · prayer · 1909 BC – 1908 BC

    Jacob prays, reminding God of His promises. He admits he is unworthy of God's kindness and asks to be delivered from Esau.

    PrayerPromise
  5. Joseph, the favored son
    Genesis 37:1–4 · narrative · 1898 BC

    The story of Jacob's family now centers on Joseph, seventeen, who reports on his brothers. Jacob shows him favor with a special robe, and the brothers hate him.

    Family and marriage
  6. Joseph is brought in; Pharaoh tells his dreams
    Genesis 41:14–24 · dialogue · 1885 BC

    Joseph is hurriedly cleaned up and brought to Pharaoh. He credits God, not himself, with any answer, and Pharaoh recounts both dreams.

    Dreams, visions and revelation
  7. Jacob blesses the boys, crossing his hands
    Genesis 48:8–16 · narrative · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    Jacob kisses the boys and blesses them, crossing his hands so his right hand rests on the younger Ephraim. He prays that God, who has shepherded him, will bless them.

    Election and calling
  8. Pharaoh refuses to let Israel go
    Exodus 5:1–5 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    Moses and Aaron relay the LORD's demand that Israel be allowed to hold a feast in the wilderness. Pharaoh answers that he does not know the LORD and will not let them go.

    Opposition and persecution
  9. Pharaoh begs for relief then relents
    Exodus 8:8–15 · narrative · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Pharaoh asks Moses to pray to remove the frogs and promises to let Israel go. Moses prays and the frogs die, but when Pharaoh sees relief he hardens his heart.

    Prayer
  10. Pharaoh bargains, then hardens again
    Exodus 8:25–32 · dialogue · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Pharaoh offers to let Israel sacrifice within Egypt, then within a short distance in the wilderness. Moses insists on a three-day journey, prays, and the flies leave, but Pharaoh again refuses.

  11. Warning of the coming hail
    Exodus 9:13–21 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to warn Pharaoh again that he could have destroyed Egypt but has raised him up to show his power. Hail is coming, and those who fear the LORD's word bring their servants and animals inside.

    God's sovereignty
  12. Locusts threatened and Pharaoh's bargaining
    Exodus 10:1–11 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The LORD says he has hardened Pharaoh's heart so his signs may be told to future generations. Moses warns of locusts, Pharaoh's officials plead with him, and Pharaoh will allow only the men to go.

    God's sovereignty
  13. Miriam and Aaron criticize Moses
    Numbers 12:1–3 · narrative · 1445 BC

    Miriam and Aaron criticize Moses over his Cushite wife and ask if the LORD speaks only through him. The narrator notes that Moses was very humble.

    Leadership
  14. Korah's rebellion and Moses' response
    Numbers 16:1–11 · narrative · 1444 BC – 1406 BC

    Korah, Dathan, Abiram, On and 250 leaders challenge Moses and Aaron, saying all the people are holy. Moses says the LORD will show whom He chooses and rebukes the Levites for seeking the priesthood.

    LeadershipOpposition and persecution
  15. Do not forget God when you prosper
    Deuteronomy 6:10–19 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses warns that in a land full of blessings they did not build, Israel must not forget the God who freed them. They should serve Him alone, avoid other gods, and not test Him as at Massah.

    IdolatryExodus and deliverance
  16. Remember the wilderness lessons
    Deuteronomy 8:1–10 · exhortation · 1446 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses tells Israel to remember how God led them forty years, humbling and testing them, feeding them manna so they would learn that people live by God's word. The good land ahead is described, and they are to bless God when they eat.

    ProvidenceObedience
  17. Beware of pride and forgetting God
    Deuteronomy 8:11–20 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses warns that when Israel is prosperous they may grow proud and credit their own strength, forgetting God. He reminds them God gives the power to gain wealth and that forgetting Him will bring destruction.

    IdolatryWork and rest
  18. Not because of your righteousness
    Deuteronomy 9:1–6 · speech · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses says Israel will soon cross the Jordan and face strong nations, but God will go ahead of them. He stresses that the land is given because of the nations' wickedness, not Israel's righteousness, since Israel is stubborn.

    Grace
  19. Rules for a future king
    Deuteronomy 17:14–20 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    When Israel later wants a king, he must be one God chooses from among his fellow Israelites, and must not gather many horses, wives, or wealth. He is to copy the law and read it daily so he stays humble.

    Kings and kingshipLaw and commandments
  20. Israel grows fat and forgets the Rock
    Deuteronomy 32:15–18 · lament · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Jeshurun, another name for Israel, becomes prosperous and abandons God, turning to foreign gods and demons. The song accuses the people of forgetting the God who gave them birth.

    IdolatryDemons and deliverance
  21. The LORD reduces Gideon's army to 300
    Judges 7:1–8 · narrative · 1175 BC – 1160 BC

    The LORD tells Gideon he has too many men, so that Israel will not boast that its own hand saved it. Twenty-two thousand fearful men go home, and a test at the water leaves only three hundred.

    FaithWar and conflict
  22. Gideon calms the men of Ephraim
    Judges 8:1–3 · dialogue · 1175 BC – 1160 BC

    Ephraim complains that Gideon did not call them to the fight against Midian. Gideon answers modestly that their capture of Oreb and Zeeb outdid his own efforts, and their anger subsides.

    Unity
  23. Jephthah and the Ephraimites at the Jordan
    Judges 12:1–7 · narrative · 1100 BC – 1094 BC

    Ephraim complains that Jephthah did not call them to fight Ammon and threatens to burn his house. Jephthah says they did not answer his call, and fighting breaks out; the Gileadites seize the Jordan fords and identify Ephraimites by their pronunciation of 'Shibboleth', killing 42,000.

    War and conflict
  24. God reverses fortunes of strong and weak
    1 Samuel 2:4–8 · poetry · 1105 BC – 1090 BC

    The song describes God overturning human situations: the mighty are broken, the hungry are fed, the barren bear children, and the poor are raised to honor. He rules life, death, poverty and wealth.

    God's sovereigntyJustice for the poor and oppressed
  25. David volunteers and tells Saul of lions and bears
    1 Samuel 17:28–37 · dialogue · 1022 BC – 1015 BC

    David's brother Eliab angrily accuses him of pride, but David keeps asking. Brought to Saul, David offers to fight Goliath, saying that the LORD who delivered him from a lion and a bear will deliver him from this Philistine.

    Faith
  26. The women's song makes Saul jealous
    1 Samuel 18:6–9 · narrative · 1020 BC – 1015 BC

    Returning from battle, women sing that Saul has slain thousands and David tens of thousands. Saul is furious and begins to watch David with suspicion.

    Kings and kingship
  27. Nabal insults David; David arms his men
    1 Samuel 25:9–13 · narrative · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Nabal scornfully refuses, saying he does not know David and calling him a runaway servant. David tells his men to strap on swords, and about four hundred set out.

    War and conflictWords and speech
  28. Michal rebukes David's dancing
    2 Samuel 6:20–23 · dialogue · 1002 BC – 1000 BC

    Saul's daughter Michal, who had despised David as she watched him, criticizes his behavior. David answers that he celebrated before the LORD who chose him over her father, and Michal has no children.

    Worship and praiseFamily and marriage
  29. David's prayer of thanks and request
    2 Samuel 7:18–29 · prayer · 1000 BC – 995 BC

    David responds humbly, praising God's greatness and his choice of Israel. He asks God to confirm the promise about his house so that God's name is honored forever.

    PrayerPromise
  30. David's envoys humiliated by the Ammonites
    2 Samuel 10:1–5 · narrative · 995 BC – 990 BC

    David sends servants to console Hanun on his father's death, but Ammonite princes suspect spies. Hanun shaves half of each man's beard and cuts their garments, and David tells them to wait in Jericho.

    War and conflictThe nations
