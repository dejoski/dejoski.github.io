Exile and return

  1. Exile and desolation of the land
    Leviticus 26:27–39 · oracle of judgment · 1445 BC

    If Israel still refuses, God warns of fury, ruined cities and sanctuaries, and scattering among the nations. The land will then enjoy the Sabbaths it missed, and the survivors will live in fear.

    Justice and judgmentWrath
  2. Confession and God's remembered covenant
    Leviticus 26:40–46 · oracle of hope · 1445 BC

    If the exiles confess their sins and humble themselves, God promises to remember his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and not to destroy them. The chapter closes by noting these are the laws given at Sinai.

    CovenantRepentanceMercy and compassion
  3. Warning of exile and promise of return
    Deuteronomy 4:25–31 · prophecy · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses warns that if Israel later makes idols, they will be scattered among the nations. If they seek God with all their heart from there, they will find Him, because He is merciful and will not forget His covenant.

    IdolatryRepentanceMercy and compassion
  4. Exile, failed harvests, and reversal
    Deuteronomy 28:36–44 · oracle of judgment · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Israel and its king will be driven to a foreign land and worship other gods. Crops will be eaten by locusts and worms, children will go into captivity, and foreigners will rise above Israel.

    IdolatryJustice and judgmentKings and kingship
  5. Scattering and a life of fear
    Deuteronomy 28:64–68 · oracle of judgment · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    The LORD will scatter Israel among the nations, where they will find no rest and live in constant dread. The chapter ends with a return to Egypt in ships to be sold as slaves.

    The nationsSuffering
  6. A ruined land and the reason why
    Deuteronomy 29:22–28 · prophecy · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses foresees future generations seeing the devastated land, like Sodom and Gomorrah. When nations ask why, the answer will be that Israel abandoned the covenant and served other gods.

    The promised landIdolatryJustice and judgment
  7. Return, restoration, and a changed heart
    Deuteronomy 30:1–10 · oracle of hope · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses says that when Israel, scattered after curses, returns to the LORD with all their heart, he will bring them back, have compassion, and prosper them. God will circumcise their hearts so they love him.

    RepentanceMercy and compassionPromise
  8. David flees Jerusalem; Ittai stays loyal
    2 Samuel 15:13–23 · narrative · 972 BC – 971 BC

    David hears that Israel follows Absalom and leaves the city with his household, leaving ten concubines behind. He urges Ittai the Gittite to turn back, but Ittai vows to stay with the king.

    FaithfulnessKings and kingship
  9. Prayer for foreigners, war and exile
    1 Kings 8:41–53 · prayer · 959 BC – 958 BC

    Solomon asks God to hear foreigners who pray toward the temple, to uphold Israel in battle, and to forgive and restore them if they are exiled and repent.

    PrayerThe nations
  10. Hoshea and the fall of Samaria
    2 Kings 17:1–6 · history · 732 BC – 722 BC

    Hoshea becomes king of Israel and pays tribute to Assyria, but a plot with Egypt leads to his arrest. Assyria besieges Samaria for three years and deports the Israelites.

    Kings and kingshipThe nations
  11. Why Israel fell
    2 Kings 17:7–18 · teaching · 722 BC

    The narrator explains that Israel fell because it worshiped other gods, copied surrounding nations, and rejected repeated prophetic warnings. As a result the LORD removed them from his presence, leaving only Judah.

    IdolatrySin and the fallProphecy
  12. Judah too and Israel's exile
    2 Kings 17:19–23 · teaching · 722 BC

    Judah also fails to keep the commands and copies Israel's ways, and the LORD rejects all of Israel's descendants. The cause is traced to Jeroboam's sin, and Israel is exiled to Assyria as the prophets said.

    Sin and the fallJustice and judgment
  13. Samaria falls; Sennacherib takes Judah's cities
    2 Kings 18:9–16 · history · 722 BC – 701 BC

    Assyria finishes its siege of Samaria and exiles Israel because of its disobedience. Years later Sennacherib captures Judah's fortified cities, and Hezekiah pays heavy tribute, stripping the temple's silver and gold.

    War and conflictSin and the fall
  14. Envoys from Babylon and Isaiah's warning
    2 Kings 20:12–19 · narrative · 703 BC – 701 BC

    The king of Babylon sends envoys, and Hezekiah shows them all his treasures. Isaiah tells him that everything will be carried off to Babylon and some descendants taken away, and Hezekiah accepts this, thinking of peace in his own time.

    Pride and humilityWealth and povertyProphecy
  15. The LORD's judgment on Jerusalem
    2 Kings 21:10–16 · oracle of judgment · 697 BC – 642 BC

    Through the prophets, the LORD declares that Jerusalem and Judah will suffer calamity for Manasseh's sins. Jerusalem will be wiped out like a bowl turned upside down, and Manasseh also fills the city with innocent blood.

    Justice and judgmentProphecy
  16. Babylon invades; Judah's judgment explained
    2 Kings 24:1–4 · narrative · 605 BC – 601 BC

    Nebuchadnezzar invades and Jehoiakim serves him three years before rebelling. The LORD sends raiders against Judah, and the writer ties this to Manasseh's sins and his shedding of innocent blood.

    Justice and judgmentSin and the fallThe nations
  17. Jehoiachin surrenders to Babylon
    2 Kings 24:8–12 · narrative · 597 BC

    Jehoiachin becomes king at eighteen and does evil. Babylon besieges Jerusalem, and after three months he surrenders with his mother and officials and is taken captive.

    Kings and kingshipThe nations
  18. First deportation and Zedekiah installed
    2 Kings 24:13–17 · narrative · 597 BC

    Nebuchadnezzar strips the temple and palace treasures and deports thousands of leaders and skilled workers, leaving only the poorest. He makes Jehoiachin's uncle Mattaniah king and renames him Zedekiah.

    Temple and tabernacleThe nations
  19. Zedekiah rebels against Babylon
    2 Kings 24:18–20 · narrative · 589 BC – 588 BC

    Zedekiah becomes king at twenty-one and does evil, as Jehoiakim had. The writer says all this came from the LORD's anger, and Zedekiah then rebels against Babylon.

    Kings and kingshipSin and the fall
  20. Siege of Jerusalem and Zedekiah's capture
    2 Kings 25:1–7 · narrative · 588 BC – 586 BC

    Babylon besieges Jerusalem for about two years until famine is severe and the walls are breached. Zedekiah flees but is captured, his sons are killed before him, and he is blinded and taken to Babylon.

    War and conflictKings and kingship
  21. Jerusalem burned and its people exiled
    2 Kings 25:8–12 · narrative · 586 BC

    Nebuzaradan burns the temple, the palace and the city's houses, and the army tears down the walls. Most of the remaining people are exiled, with only some poor left to tend the land.

    Temple and tabernacleJustice and judgment
  22. Temple bronze and treasures carried away
    2 Kings 25:13–17 · narrative · 586 BC

    The Babylonians break up the bronze pillars, stands and Sea and take the temple's bronze, gold and silver articles. The writer notes the great weight of bronze and describes the pillars.

    Temple and tabernacle
  23. Leaders executed at Riblah
    2 Kings 25:18–21 · narrative · 586 BC

    Nebuzaradan takes the chief priest, other priests, officials and sixty men to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where they are put to death. The writer concludes that Judah was taken into exile from its land.

    PriesthoodJustice and judgment
  24. Gedaliah governs, is murdered; people flee
    2 Kings 25:22–26 · narrative · 586 BC – 582 BC

    Babylon appoints Gedaliah as governor, and he urges the remaining leaders to serve Babylon. Ishmael kills him, and the people and army commanders flee to Egypt in fear.

    LeadershipSin and the fall
  25. Jehoiachin released from prison in Babylon
    2 Kings 25:27–30 · narrative · 561 BC

    In the thirty-seventh year of his exile, Jehoiachin is freed by Evil-merodach. He is treated kindly, given a place of honor, and provided for at the king's table for the rest of his life.

    Hope and enduranceMercy and compassionKings and kingship
  26. The kings of Judah after Solomon
    1 Chronicles 3:10–16 · genealogy · 930 BC – 586 BC

    The line of kings is traced father to son from Solomon's son Rehoboam down to Josiah. His sons are listed, ending with the last kings before the exile.

    Kings and kingship
  27. Jeconiah's line after the exile
    1 Chronicles 3:17–24 · genealogy · 597 BC – 450 BC

    The descendants of Jeconiah, the exiled king, are listed, including Shealtiel and Pedaiah's son Zerubbabel. The record continues several generations beyond, bringing David's family line into the chronicler's own period.

    PromiseKings and kingship
  28. Manasseh's unfaithfulness and exile
    1 Chronicles 5:23–26 · history · 734 BC – 722 BC

    The half-tribe of Manasseh is described, with its famous family heads. They were unfaithful to God and worshipped the gods of the land, so God brought the Assyrians to take the eastern tribes into exile.

    IdolatrySin and the fall
  29. The line of Levi and the high priests
    1 Chronicles 6:1–15 · genealogy · 1700 BC – 586 BC

    The sons of Levi are named, and the priestly line is traced from Aaron through Eleazar and Phinehas down to Jehozadak, who went into exile under Nebuchadnezzar. Moses and Miriam are noted as Amram's children.

    Priesthood
  30. Exile and the first to resettle
    1 Chronicles 9:1–9 · history · 538 BC – 444 BC

    The chapter says all Israel was recorded in the genealogies, while Judah was exiled to Babylon for unfaithfulness. It lists the first settlers who returned to Jerusalem from Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim and Manasseh.

    The promised land
