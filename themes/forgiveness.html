  2. Jacob and Esau reconcile
    Genesis 33:1–11 · narrative · 1909 BC – 1907 BC

    Jacob bows before Esau seven times, but Esau runs, embraces him, and they weep. Jacob introduces his family and urges Esau to accept his gift, saying that seeing Esau's face is like seeing God's face.

  3. Joseph reveals himself to his brothers
    Genesis 45:1–15 · speech · 1876 BC – 1875 BC

    Joseph clears the room, weeps, and tells his brothers who he is. He says God sent him ahead to preserve life, urges them to bring Jacob to Goshen, and embraces them.

  4. The brothers fear; Joseph forgives
    Genesis 50:15–21 · dialogue · 1859 BC – 1850 BC

    Fearing Joseph will now take revenge, the brothers plead for forgiveness. Joseph weeps and says what they meant for evil, God meant for good, and promises to provide for them.

    ProvidenceMercy and compassion
  5. Moses intercedes for forgiveness
    Exodus 32:30–35 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The next day Moses returns to the LORD and asks him to forgive the people, or else blot Moses out of his book. God says he will blot out those who sinned, tells Moses to lead on with his angel, and sends a plague.

  6. Sin offering from a commoner: a goat
    Leviticus 4:27–31 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    An ordinary person who sins unintentionally brings an unblemished female goat. The priest applies its blood to the altar and burns the fat, and the person is forgiven.

  7. Sin offering from a commoner: a lamb
    Leviticus 4:32–35 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A commoner may instead bring an unblemished female lamb, handled the same way, and the priest makes atonement so the person is forgiven.

    Atonement and sacrifice
  8. Sins of silence, uncleanness, and rash oaths
    Leviticus 5:1–6 · law · 1445 BC

    Several offenses, such as failing to testify, touching something unclean, or swearing thoughtlessly, bring guilt even if unnoticed at first. The person must confess and bring a female lamb or goat as a sin offering.

  9. Guilt offering for unknowing wrongs
    Leviticus 5:17–19 · law · 1445 BC

    A person who breaks a command without realizing it is still guilty and brings a ram as a guilt offering. The priest makes atonement and he is forgiven.

    Leviticus 16:20–22 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    Numbers 14:20–25 · speech · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    Unintentional sins by the whole community or one person can be forgiven with offerings. Defiant sin is despising the LORD's word and brings being cut off.
