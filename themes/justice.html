  1. God questions the man and woman
    Genesis 3:9–13 · dialogue

    God calls out to the man, who admits fear and hiding. When asked if he ate, the man blames the woman, and she blames the serpent.

    Sin and the fall
  2. Consequences spoken over serpent, woman, man
    Genesis 3:14–19 · oracle of judgment

    God pronounces consequences: the serpent is cursed with a coming conflict, the woman will face pain and strained relations, and the man will toil against a cursed ground until he returns to dust.

    Sin and the fallDeath and mortality
  3. Cain kills Abel
    Genesis 4:8–12 · narrative

    Cain lures Abel to the field and kills him. When God asks where Abel is, Cain answers evasively, and God declares that Abel's blood cries out and Cain will be a wanderer.

    Sin and the fall
  4. Cain's protection and departure
    Genesis 4:13–16 · narrative

    Cain protests his punishment is too great. God places a mark on him so no one will kill him, and Cain settles in Nod, east of Eden.

    Mercy and compassion
  5. God grieves over human wickedness
    Genesis 6:5–8 · narrative

    God sees that human hearts are wholly evil and regrets making man, grieved. He resolves to blot out life from the earth, but Noah finds favor in his eyes.

    Sin and the fallWrath
  6. The flood begins
    Genesis 7:11–16 · narrative

    The deep's fountains burst open and the heavens' floodgates open, with rain for forty days. All those on the ark enter, and the Lord shuts Noah in.

    Creation
  7. The waters rise and cover the earth
    Genesis 7:17–24 · narrative

    The waters swell, lift the ark, and cover the mountains. Every living thing on dry land dies, and only Noah and those with him survive.

    Death and mortality
  8. The Lord confuses the language at Babel
    Genesis 11:5–9 · narrative

    The Lord comes down, sees the project, and confuses their language. The people are scattered across the earth and stop building, and the place is named Babel.

    The nationsPride and humility
  9. The Lord decides to look into Sodom
    Genesis 18:16–21 · speech · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    As the men leave, the Lord reflects that he has chosen Abraham to teach his household justice. He says the outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah is great and he will go see for himself.

    Election and callingCovenant
  10. Abraham pleads for Sodom
    Genesis 18:22–33 · dialogue · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Abraham asks whether God will sweep away the righteous with the wicked and bargains down from fifty righteous to ten. The Lord agrees each time to spare the city, and then leaves.

    PrayerMercy and compassion
  11. Sodom and Gomorrah destroyed
    Genesis 19:23–29 · narrative · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    The Lord rains sulfur and fire on Sodom, Gomorrah, and the plain. Lot's wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt, and Abraham sees smoke rising the next morning.

    Wrath
  12. Simeon and Levi's massacre
    Genesis 34:25–31 · narrative · 1907 BC – 1900 BC

    While the men are in pain, Simeon and Levi kill every male in the city and take Dinah back, and the other brothers loot it. Jacob fears retaliation, but they say their sister should not be treated like a prostitute.

    Sin and the fall
  13. Joseph accuses his brothers of spying
    Genesis 42:6–17 · dialogue · 1877 BC – 1876 BC

    The brothers bow to Joseph without recognizing him. He accuses them of being spies, and they describe their family; he demands they prove it by bringing their youngest brother, and jails them three days.

    Sin and the fall
  14. Joseph's terms; the brothers' guilt
    Genesis 42:18–24 · dialogue · 1877 BC – 1876 BC

    Joseph softens the demand: one brother stays while the rest take grain home. The brothers, unaware he understands, admit guilt over Joseph; Joseph weeps and has Simeon bound.

    RepentanceSin and the fall
  15. Joseph declares Benjamin will be his slave
    Genesis 44:14–17 · dialogue · 1876 BC – 1875 BC

    The brothers return and fall before Joseph. Judah admits God has exposed their guilt and offers all of them as slaves, but Joseph insists only Benjamin will stay.

    Family and marriage
  16. Sayings on Reuben, Simeon and Levi
    Genesis 49:3–7 · poetry · 1860 BC – 1859 BC

    Jacob rebukes Reuben for defiling his bed and condemns Simeon and Levi for their violent anger, saying they will be scattered in Israel.

    Sin and the fall
  17. The LORD announces the plague of blood
    Exodus 7:14–18 · speech · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The LORD tells Moses to meet Pharaoh at the Nile and again demand that Israel be released. If he refuses, the Nile will turn to blood.

    God's sovereignty
  18. The Nile turns to blood
    Exodus 7:19–25 · miracle · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Aaron strikes the Nile and all Egypt's water turns to blood, killing the fish and making the water undrinkable. The magicians imitate it, Pharaoh is unmoved, and seven days pass.

    Miracles and signs
  19. The plague of frogs begins
    Exodus 8:1–7 · miracle · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    The LORD warns of frogs in every part of Egypt, and Aaron stretches out his staff. Frogs cover the land, though Pharaoh's magicians can also produce them.

    Miracles and signs
  20. The plague of boils
    Exodus 9:8–12 · miracle · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Moses tosses soot from a furnace into the air and festering boils break out on people and animals. Even the magicians cannot stand before Moses, and the LORD hardens Pharaoh's heart.

    Miracles and signs
  21. Hail strikes Egypt but not Goshen
    Exodus 9:22–26 · miracle · 1446 BC

    Moses stretches his staff toward heaven and thunder, lightning, and the worst hail in Egypt's history destroy people, animals, and plants in the fields. Goshen alone is untouched.

    Miracles and signs
  22. The plague of locusts
    Exodus 10:12–20 · miracle · 1446 BC

    An east wind brings locusts that cover the land and eat everything left by the hail. Pharaoh confesses his sin and asks for forgiveness, and a west wind blows the locusts into the Red Sea, but the LORD hardens his heart.

    Miracles and signs
  23. Three days of darkness
    Exodus 10:21–29 · miracle · 1446 BC

    A darkness thick enough to feel covers Egypt for three days while Israelites have light. Pharaoh offers to let all but the livestock go, Moses refuses, and Pharaoh orders Moses out and says he will die if he returns.

    Miracles and signs
  24. The LORD announces one more plague
    Exodus 11:1–3 · speech · 1446 BC

    The LORD says one more plague will make Pharaoh drive Israel out. He tells the people to ask their neighbors for silver and gold, and gives them favor with the Egyptians.

    Exodus and deliverance
  25. Moses warns of the death of the firstborn
    Exodus 11:4–10 · speech · 1446 BC

    Moses declares that at midnight every firstborn in Egypt will die and a great cry will rise, but Israel will be unharmed. He leaves in anger, and the text notes that Pharaoh's heart remains hardened.

    God's sovereignty
  26. The firstborn die and Pharaoh releases Israel
    Exodus 12:29–36 · narrative · 1446 BC

    At midnight the LORD strikes down every firstborn in Egypt, and Pharaoh summons Moses and Aaron by night and tells Israel to go. The Egyptians urge them to hurry and give them silver, gold, and clothing.

    Exodus and deliverance
  27. The sea closes over Pharaoh's army
    Exodus 14:26–31 · miracle · 1446 BC

    At daybreak Moses stretches out his hand again and the waters cover the whole Egyptian army. Israel sees the Egyptians dead on the shore, and the people fear the LORD and believe in him and Moses.

    SalvationFaith
  28. Laws on violence and offenses against parents
    Exodus 21:12–17 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The text sets the death penalty for deliberate murder, but provides a refuge for accidental killing. It also covers striking or cursing parents and kidnapping.

    Law and commandmentsFamily and marriage
  29. Injuries and compensation
    Exodus 21:18–27 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The laws set compensation for injuries in a fight and limits on harming servants. Harm to a pregnant woman is addressed, and eye for eye, tooth for tooth sets a limit on the penalty.

    Law and commandments
  30. Liability for animals and pits
    Exodus 21:28–36 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The laws cover an ox that kills a person and owner responsibility if the animal was known to be dangerous. They also address a pit left uncovered and one ox killing another.

    Law and commandments
