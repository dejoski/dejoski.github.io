Law and commandments

  1. Blessing and rules for life after the flood
    Genesis 9:1–7 · speech

    God blesses Noah's family to be fruitful and fills the earth. He gives animals as food but forbids eating meat with its lifeblood and declares that human life is precious, made in God's image.

    CreationFamily and marriage
  2. The first two commandments
    Exodus 20:1–6 · law · 1446 BC

    God says he is the LORD who brought Israel out of slavery. Israel is to have no other gods and make no idols, and God describes himself as jealous but showing love to those who love him.

    IdolatryWho God is
  3. God's name and the Sabbath
    Exodus 20:7–11 · law · 1446 BC

    Israel is not to misuse the LORD's name. They are to keep the seventh day as a Sabbath, with no work for family, servants, animals, or foreigners, because God made everything in six days and rested on the seventh.

    Sabbath and feastsWords and speech
  4. Commands about family and neighbors
    Exodus 20:12–17 · law · 1446 BC

    God says to honor father and mother and forbids murder, adultery, theft, false witness, and coveting a neighbor's house, spouse, or belongings.

    Family and marriage
  5. Laws about servants
    Exodus 21:1–11 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    God begins the ordinances with rules for Hebrew servants, who go free in the seventh year. Special protections cover daughters sold as servants, including care and freedom if their rights are denied.

    Justice for the poor and oppressed
  6. Laws on violence and offenses against parents
    Exodus 21:12–17 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The text sets the death penalty for deliberate murder, but provides a refuge for accidental killing. It also covers striking or cursing parents and kidnapping.

    Justice and judgmentFamily and marriage
  7. Injuries and compensation
    Exodus 21:18–27 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The laws set compensation for injuries in a fight and limits on harming servants. Harm to a pregnant woman is addressed, and eye for eye, tooth for tooth sets a limit on the penalty.

    Justice and judgment
  8. Liability for animals and pits
    Exodus 21:28–36 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The laws cover an ox that kills a person and owner responsibility if the animal was known to be dangerous. They also address a pit left uncovered and one ox killing another.

    Justice and judgment
  9. Theft and self-defense
    Exodus 22:1–4 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    A thief must repay several times what he stole. If he cannot pay he can be sold, and rules limit force against a night-time intruder.

    Justice and judgment
  10. Damage, safekeeping, and borrowing
    Exodus 22:5–15 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The laws require restitution for livestock that stray into another field and for fires that spread. They also set rules for goods and animals left in a neighbor's care or borrowed.

    Justice and judgment
  11. Seduction and serious offenses
    Exodus 22:16–20 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    A man who seduces an unengaged virgin must pay the bridal price. The passage also names sorcery, bestiality, and sacrificing to other gods as capital offenses.

    Purity and sexual ethics
  12. Protecting the vulnerable
    Exodus 22:21–27 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Israel must not oppress foreigners, widows, or orphans, since God hears their cry. Loans to the poor carry no interest and a cloak taken as pledge must be returned by sunset.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedLoving others
  13. Reverence and holiness
    Exodus 22:28–31 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Israel must not curse God or its ruler and must give firstfruits and firstborn to the LORD. As a holy people, they must not eat meat torn by wild animals.

    Holiness
  14. Sabbath years and days
    Exodus 23:10–13 · law · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The land is to be worked six years and left fallow in the seventh so the poor can eat. The seventh day is a rest for people and animals, and Israel must not invoke other gods.

    Sabbath and feasts
  15. Moses ascends into the cloud
    Exodus 24:12–18 · narrative · 1446 BC

    The LORD calls Moses higher to receive stone tablets, so Moses leaves Aaron and Hur in charge and goes up with Joshua. The glory of God covers the mountain like fire, and Moses stays forty days and nights.

    Glory
  16. The two tablets given
    Exodus 31:18 · narrative · 1446 BC

    When God finishes speaking with Moses on Sinai, he gives him two stone tablets of the Testimony, written by the finger of God.

    Covenant
  17. Festivals, firstborn and Sabbath
    Exodus 34:18–26 · law · 1446 BC

    God repeats laws about the Feast of Unleavened Bread, the firstborn, weekly rest, the Feasts of Weeks and Ingathering, appearing before him three times a year, and offerings. It ends with the command not to cook a young goat in its mother's milk.

    Sabbath and feasts
  18. Moses writes the covenant
    Exodus 34:27–28 · narrative · 1446 BC

    God tells Moses to write down these words. Moses stays forty days and nights without food or water, and writes the Ten Commandments on the tablets.

    Covenant
  19. Goat offering and ban on fat and blood
    Leviticus 3:12–17 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A goat is offered with the same fat portions burned, and all the fat is declared the LORD's. The chapter ends with a permanent rule that the Israelites must not eat fat or blood.

    Atonement and sacrifice
  20. Forbidden fat and blood
    Leviticus 7:22–27 · law · 1445 BC

    The Israelites must not eat the fat of oxen, sheep, or goats, nor any blood, wherever they live. The penalty for breaking these rules is being cut off from the people.

    Atonement and sacrifice
  21. Summary of the offering laws
    Leviticus 7:37–38 · list · 1445 BC

    These verses sum up the laws for the burnt, grain, sin, guilt, ordination, and peace offerings, given on Mount Sinai when the Israelites were told to bring their offerings.

    Worship and praise
  22. Clean and unclean land animals
    Leviticus 11:1–8 · law · 1445 BC

    Israelites may eat animals that have a split hoof and chew the cud. The camel, rock badger, rabbit, and pig fail one test and are unclean, so their meat and carcasses are not to be touched.

    Holiness
  23. Clean and unclean water creatures
    Leviticus 11:9–12 · law · 1445 BC

    Anything in the seas or streams with fins and scales may be eaten. Sea creatures without both are detestable, and their meat is not to be eaten.

    Holiness
  24. Unclean birds and insects
    Leviticus 11:13–23 · list · 1445 BC

    This section lists birds that are detestable, such as eagles, vultures, ravens, and owls. It also bans most winged insects that walk on four legs, but allows locusts, crickets, and grasshoppers.

    Holiness
  25. Touching carcasses and crawling creatures
    Leviticus 11:24–31 · law · 1445 BC

    Touching or carrying the carcass of an unclean animal makes a person unclean until evening, and carrying one requires washing clothes. Small creatures such as the mole, mouse, and lizards are also unclean.

    Holiness
  26. Objects and food contaminated by carcasses
    Leviticus 11:32–40 · law · 1445 BC

    Items such as wood, cloth, and pots become unclean if a dead creature falls on them, with clay pots to be broken. Springs and dry seed stay clean, and even a clean animal that dies of itself makes people unclean.

    Holiness
  27. Summary of skin disease and mildew laws
    Leviticus 14:54–57 · list · 1445 BC

    This summary lists the laws for skin diseases, scaly outbreaks, mildew in clothing or houses, and swellings, rashes, and spots. Their purpose is to teach when something is clean and when unclean.

    Holiness
  28. Sacrifices only at the Tent of Meeting
    Leviticus 17:1–9 · law · 1445 BC

    Any Israelite who slaughters an ox, lamb, or goat must bring it to the Tent of Meeting as an offering. This ends sacrifices in open fields and to goat demons, and it applies to foreigners living among them too.

    Worship and praiseIdolatry
  29. Blood makes atonement
    Leviticus 17:10–12 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD forbids Israelites and foreigners to eat blood, since the life of the flesh is in the blood. He has given it on the altar to make atonement for their lives.

    Atonement and sacrifice
  30. Draining the blood of game
    Leviticus 17:13–16 · law · 1445 BC

    An animal or bird hunted for food must have its blood poured out and covered with earth. Eating an animal found dead requires washing, and anyone who does not wash bears the consequences.

    Death and mortalityHoliness
