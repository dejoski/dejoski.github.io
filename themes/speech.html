  1. The serpent questions God's word
    Genesis 3:1–5 · dialogue

    A crafty serpent asks the woman whether God really forbade eating from any tree. She restates the command, and the serpent contradicts it, saying God is withholding godlike knowledge.

    Temptation and spiritual warfareSin and the fall
  2. Abimelech confronts Abraham
    Genesis 20:8–13 · dialogue · 2067 BC – 2066 BC

    Abimelech, alarmed, asks why Abraham brought guilt on his kingdom. Abraham explains he feared no one there feared God and says Sarah is his half-sister.

    Sin and the fall
  3. Moses objects and Aaron is appointed
    Exodus 4:10–17 · dialogue · 1447 BC – 1446 BC

    Moses says he is slow of speech, and God replies that he made the mouth and will help him. When Moses asks him to send someone else, God grows angry and appoints his brother Aaron as spokesman.

    Election and callingLeadership
  4. God's name and the Sabbath
    Exodus 20:7–11 · law · 1446 BC

    Israel is not to misuse the LORD's name. They are to keep the seventh day as a Sabbath, with no work for family, servants, animals, or foreigners, because God made everything in six days and rested on the seventh.

    Law and commandmentsSabbath and feasts
  5. A man blasphemes the Name
    Leviticus 24:10–12 · narrative · 1445 BC – 1444 BC

    A man with an Israelite mother and an Egyptian father fights with another Israelite in the camp and curses God's name. He is taken to Moses and held until the LORD's will is known.

    HolinessNames of God
  6. Balak welcomes Balaam and offers sacrifices
    Numbers 22:36–41 · narrative · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balak meets Balaam at the border and Balaam warns that he can only speak what God puts in his mouth. Balak sacrifices and next morning takes Balaam up to Bamoth-baal, from where Israel's camp is visible.

    God's sovereignty
  7. Balak's anger and dismissal
    Numbers 24:10–14 · dialogue · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Balak strikes his hands together in anger and tells Balaam to go home, saying the LORD has denied his reward. Balaam repeats that he can only speak the LORD's words, and offers to warn Balak what Israel will do in future.

    God's sovereignty
  8. Keeping vows, and a father's role
    Numbers 30:1–5 · law · 1406 BC

    Moses tells the tribal heads that anyone who makes a vow to the LORD must keep it. A young woman's vow in her father's house stands unless her father forbids it the day he hears of it.

    ObedienceFamily and marriage
  9. Vows of a married or widowed woman
    Numbers 30:6–9 · law · 1406 BC

    A married woman's vow stands if her husband hears and says nothing that day, but he may nullify it. The vows of a widow or divorced woman are binding on her.

    Family and marriage
  10. A husband's power to confirm or cancel vows
    Numbers 30:10–16 · law · 1406 BC

    If a wife's vow is heard by her husband, it stands if he says nothing, and is cancelled if he nullifies it the same day. If he cancels it later, he bears responsibility for it. The chapter closes by summarizing these rules.

    Family and marriage
  11. Boundary markers and honest witnesses
    Deuteronomy 19:14–21 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses forbids moving a neighbor's boundary marker and requires more than one witness for any charge. A false witness must receive the punishment he intended for the accused.

    Justice and judgment
  12. Runaway slaves, interest, vows, and neighbors' crops
    Deuteronomy 23:15–25 · law · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses gives rules against returning a fugitive slave, shrine prostitution, and charging interest to a fellow Israelite. He also says vows must be kept and neighbors' fields may be eaten from but not carried away.

    Justice for the poor and oppressedWealth and poverty
  13. The Gibeonites' deception
    Joshua 9:3–13 · dialogue · 1405 BC – 1404 BC

    The people of Gibeon disguise themselves with worn clothing and moldy bread. They come to Gilgal and claim to be from a far country, asking for a treaty.

    The nations
  14. Jephthah's vow and victory
    Judges 11:29–33 · narrative · 1100 BC – 1095 BC

    The Spirit of the LORD comes on Jephthah, who vows that whatever first comes out of his house on his return will be the LORD's and offered as a burnt offering. The LORD gives him victory and he devastates twenty Ammonite cities.

    Sin and the fallFamily and marriage
  15. Samson's riddle at the wedding feast
    Judges 14:10–14 · dialogue · 1072 BC – 1060 BC

    At the seven-day wedding feast, Samson bets thirty companions thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothes that they cannot solve his riddle. They fail to answer for three days.

    Wealth and poverty
  16. Eli mistakes her for drunk, then blesses her
    1 Samuel 1:12–18 · dialogue · 1110 BC – 1095 BC

    Eli sees her lips moving and wrongly thinks she is drunk. Hannah explains she is pouring out her sorrow to the LORD, and Eli sends her off with a blessing, after which she is no longer downcast.

    Prayer
  17. David asks Nabal for provisions
    1 Samuel 25:4–8 · speech · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Hearing that Nabal is shearing sheep, David sends ten young men to greet him. They are to say David's men protected Nabal's shepherds and to ask for whatever he can spare at feast time.

    Wealth and poverty
  18. Nabal insults David; David arms his men
    1 Samuel 25:9–13 · narrative · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Nabal scornfully refuses, saying he does not know David and calling him a runaway servant. David tells his men to strap on swords, and about four hundred set out.

    Pride and humilityWar and conflict
  19. Abigail pleads with David
    1 Samuel 25:23–31 · speech · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Abigail bows, takes the blame on herself, and calls Nabal a fool. She offers her gift, credits the LORD with restraining David, and says that a future without needless bloodshed will spare him guilt.

    Peace and reconciliationWisdom
  20. David calls out to Abner
    1 Samuel 26:13–16 · speech · 1015 BC – 1012 BC

    From a distant hilltop David shouts to Abner, accusing him of failing to guard the king and pointing to the missing spear and jug as proof.

    Leadership
  21. Shimei curses David
    2 Samuel 16:5–14 · narrative · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Shimei of Saul's family throws stones and curses David as a man of bloodshed. David refuses Abishai's offer to kill him, saying the LORD may have told him to curse and might repay good for it.

    Pride and humilityGod's sovereignty
  22. Two runners bring the news
    2 Samuel 18:19–32 · narrative · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Ahimaaz and a Cushite run to David with the news. The Cushite tells him Absalom is dead in indirect words.

    Death and mortality
  23. Prophets promise victory; Micaiah hints otherwise
    1 Kings 22:10–18 · dialogue · 854 BC – 853 BC

    Four hundred prophets, led by Zedekiah, predict victory. Micaiah at first says the same, but Ahab presses him, and he describes Israel scattered like sheep without a shepherd.

    False teaching
  24. The Rabshakeh speaks to the people
    2 Kings 18:26–37 · speech · 701 BC

    Hezekiah's officials ask him to use Aramaic, but he shouts in Hebrew to the people on the wall. He tells them not to trust Hezekiah or the LORD and offers peace on Assyrian terms, and the people stay silent.

    FaithThe nations
  25. Memucan urges a decree against Vashti
    Esther 1:16–20 · speech · 483 BC – 482 BC

    Memucan argues that Vashti's conduct wronged everyone and will make wives across the empire despise their husbands. He proposes an irrevocable decree removing her as queen and replacing her with a better woman.

    Family and marriageLaw and commandments
  26. Faithless friends like dried-up streams
    Job 6:14–23 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says his friends are unreliable like seasonal streams that vanish in summer and disappoint thirsty caravans. He notes he never asked them for money or rescue, only for kindness.

    Loving others
  27. Show me my error, and be fair
    Job 6:24–30 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job invites his friends to show him where he is wrong. He says their arguments prove nothing and asks them to look at him honestly, insisting his integrity is at stake.

    Justice and judgment
  28. Zophar accuses Job of empty talk
    Job 11:1–6 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Zophar says Job's stream of words should not go unanswered and that Job claims to be pure. He wishes God would speak and says God is exacting less than Job's sin deserves.

    Justice and judgment
  29. Job answers with sarcasm
    Job 12:1–6 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job sarcastically says his friends must be the only wise people and insists he understands as much as they do. He says the righteous are mocked while those who provoke God are secure.

    Wisdom
  30. Job rebukes his friends' false defense of God
    Job 13:1–12 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says he knows all that they know and wants to argue his case with God. He calls his friends worthless physicians and warns that defending God with lies will not please Him.

    Justice and judgment
