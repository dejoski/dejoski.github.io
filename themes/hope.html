  1. From Methuselah to Noah's sons
    Genesis 5:25–32 · genealogy

    The list continues through Methuselah and Lamech. Lamech names his son Noah, hoping for comfort from the cursed ground's toil, and Noah later has Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

    Family and marriage
  2. Raven, dove, and dry ground
    Genesis 8:6–14 · narrative

    Noah sends out a raven and then a dove three times. The dove returns with an olive leaf and then does not return. Noah sees the ground dry and the earth fully dry by the second month.

    Salvation
  3. Jehoiachin released from prison in Babylon
    2 Kings 25:27–30 · narrative · 561 BC

    In the thirty-seventh year of his exile, Jehoiachin is freed by Evil-merodach. He is treated kindly, given a place of honor, and provided for at the king's table for the rest of his life.

    Mercy and compassionKings and kingshipExile and return
  4. God does not reject the blameless
    Job 8:20–22 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Bildad concludes that God does not reject the blameless or support evildoers. He says God will yet fill Job's mouth with laughter while the wicked are shamed.

    Justice and judgment
  5. Return to God and find security
    Job 11:13–20 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Zophar tells Job to direct his heart to God and remove injustice, and then he will feel secure and forget his misery. The wicked, he says, will find no escape.

    Repentance
  6. Though He slay me, I will hope in Him
    Job 13:13–19 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says he will speak whatever the risk and will still hope in God and defend his ways to God's face. He believes he will be vindicated.

    FaithSuffering
  7. A tree may sprout again, but a man lies down
    Job 14:7–12 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job compares a felled tree, which can sprout again, with a person who dies and does not rise. Death is like water disappearing from a dried river.

    Death and mortalityResurrection
  8. If only You would hide me and remember me
    Job 14:13–17 · prayer · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job wishes God would hide him in Sheol until His anger passes and then call him back. He wonders whether a dead man will live again and imagines God covering his sin.

    Death and mortalityWrath
  9. Hope wears away like stone
    Job 14:18–22 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job says God erodes human hope as water wears away stone. The dead are unaware of their family and feel only their own pain.

    Death and mortalitySuffering
  10. Where then is my hope?
    Job 17:10–16 · lament · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job challenges his friends to try again but says he will find no wise person among them. He describes the grave as his home and asks where his hope lies.

    Death and mortality
  11. I know that my Redeemer lives
    Job 19:23–29 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job wishes his words were carved in stone forever and states his confidence that his Redeemer lives and that he will see God for himself. He warns his friends to fear judgment.

    RedemptionResurrectionFaith
  12. The godless have no hope
    Job 27:7–12 · speech · 2100 BC – 1700 BC

    Job wishes his enemies to be like the wicked and asks what hope the godless have when God takes their life. He says he will teach them about God's power and asks why they are still speaking emptily.

    Justice and judgment
  13. The LORD has heard my weeping
    Psalms 6:8–10 · prayer · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The mood changes as the psalmist tells his enemies to leave, because the LORD has heard his cry. He is confident that his enemies will be ashamed and turn back.

    PrayerFaith
  14. Arise, LORD; I will see your face
    Psalms 17:13–15 · prayer · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He asks God to confront the wicked and rescue him from people who live only for this life. He ends by saying he will see God's face and be satisfied when he wakes.

    PrayerJustice and judgment
  15. Even in the darkest valley
    Psalms 23:4 · praise · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    Even walking through the darkest valley, he will fear no evil because God is with him. The shepherd's rod and staff comfort him.

    Faith
  16. Wait for the LORD
    Psalms 27:11–14 · prayer · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He asks God to teach him and protect him from false witnesses. He says he is sure to see God's goodness and encourages himself and others to wait patiently for the LORD.

    FaithPrayer
  17. How great is your goodness
    Psalms 31:19–24 · praise · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He praises God's goodness stored for those who fear Him, who hides them from human schemes. He blesses the LORD for help in a besieged city and urges all the faithful to love the LORD and be strong.

    Mercy and compassionFaith
  18. Our soul waits for the LORD
    Psalms 33:20–22 · prayer

    The people say they wait for the LORD, their help and shield. They rejoice in Him and ask that His loving devotion rest on them.

    Joy and thanksgiving
  19. Be still; the meek will inherit the land
    Psalms 37:7–11 · wisdom · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    The reader is told to wait patiently, avoid anger, and not fret, because evildoers will be cut off. The meek will inherit the land and enjoy peace.

    The promised landPride and humility
  20. I saw the wicked vanish
    Psalms 37:35–40 · wisdom · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He recalls seeing a powerful wicked man flourish and then disappear. He concludes that the blameless have a future while the wicked will be destroyed, and the LORD rescues those who take refuge in Him.

    Justice and judgment
  21. I wait for you; come quickly
    Psalms 38:15–22 · prayer · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He waits for God to answer and asks that his enemies not gloat when he slips. He confesses his sin, and asks God not to forsake him and to come quickly.

    PrayerRepentance
  22. My hope is in you
    Psalms 39:7–11 · prayer · 1010 BC – 970 BC

    He says his hope is in God and asks to be saved from his sins. He accepts that God's discipline is behind his suffering and that people are as fragile as something a moth consumes, merely a vapor.

    SufferingSin and the fall
  23. Why are you downcast, my soul?
    Psalms 42:5 · exhortation

    He speaks to himself, asking why he is downcast, and tells himself to hope in God, whom he will praise again.

  24. Hope in God
    Psalms 42:11 · exhortation

    He repeats the refrain to himself, telling his soul to hope in God, his Savior, and he will yet praise Him.

    Salvation
  25. Why are you downcast, my soul?
    Psalms 43:5 · exhortation

    The refrain returns a third time: he tells himself to stop being downcast and to hope in God.

  26. God is our refuge amid chaos
    Psalms 46:1–3 · praise

    The psalm opens by declaring God a refuge and strength. Even if the earth gives way and the seas roar, the people will not fear.

    Who God isFaith
  27. All die, but God will redeem me
    Psalms 49:10–15 · wisdom

    Wise and foolish alike die and leave their wealth behind, and their graves become their homes. The psalmist contrasts this fate with his own hope that God will redeem him from Sheol and take him to himself.

    Death and mortalityRedemption
  28. God scatters attackers; longing for salvation
    Psalms 53:5–6 · prayer

    The psalm says the enemies were overwhelmed by dread and God put them to shame. It ends with a wish that God's salvation would come from Zion and restore his people.

    SalvationJustice and judgment
  29. Cast your burden on the LORD
    Psalms 55:22–23 · exhortation

    The psalmist encourages people to cast their burdens on the LORD, who sustains the righteous. He says God will bring down the violent and deceitful, and ends with his own trust.

    FaithPrayer
  30. The righteous see justice done
    Psalms 58:10–11 · praise

    The righteous will rejoice when they see the wicked judged. People will then recognize that the righteous are rewarded and that there is a God who judges the earth.

    Justice and judgmentGod's sovereignty
