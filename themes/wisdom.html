  1. Pharaoh's troubling dreams of cows and grain
    Genesis 41:1–8 · narrative · 1885 BC

    Pharaoh dreams of seven thin cows eating seven fat ones, then of thin heads of grain swallowing plump ones. His court's wise men cannot explain either dream.

    Dreams, visions and revelation
  2. The cupbearer remembers Joseph
    Genesis 41:9–13 · speech · 1885 BC

    The cupbearer finally confesses his earlier failure and tells how a young Hebrew in prison accurately interpreted his dream and the baker's.

    Providence
  3. Joseph explains: seven good years, seven famine
    Genesis 41:25–32 · speech · 1885 BC

    Joseph says the two dreams are one message from God: seven years of abundance will be followed by seven years of severe famine. The repeated dream shows God has settled the matter.

    Dreams, visions and revelationProvidence
  4. Joseph's plan to store grain
    Genesis 41:33–36 · speech · 1885 BC

    Joseph advises Pharaoh to appoint a wise overseer and officials to collect a fifth of the harvest during the good years and store it against the coming famine.

    Leadership
  5. Pharaoh makes Joseph ruler of Egypt
    Genesis 41:37–45 · narrative · 1885 BC – 1884 BC

    Pharaoh and his officials approve the plan and recognize God's Spirit in Joseph. Pharaoh appoints him second only to himself, gives him a ring, robes, an Egyptian name and a wife.

    ProvidenceLeadership
  6. The famine begins and Joseph sells grain
    Genesis 41:53–57 · narrative · 1878 BC – 1877 BC

    The seven lean years begin as Joseph foretold. Egyptians turn to Pharaoh, who sends them to Joseph, and people from every nation come to Egypt to buy grain.

    Leadership
  7. Jethro advises Moses to share the load
    Exodus 18:13–23 · dialogue · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Seeing Moses judge the people from morning to evening, Jethro says the work is too heavy. He advises Moses to teach the laws and appoint capable, God-fearing men to judge minor cases.

    Leadership
  8. Obey the law and show wisdom to the nations
    Deuteronomy 4:1–8 · exhortation · 1407 BC – 1406 BC

    Moses calls Israel to listen and keep the law without adding or subtracting, recalling how those who followed Baal of Peor died. Keeping the law will show other nations Israel's wisdom and the nearness of God.

    Law and commandmentsObedienceThe nations
  9. Israel lacks wisdom; vengeance belongs to God
    Deuteronomy 32:28–35 · poetry · 1406 BC – 1210 BC

    Moses says Israel lacks understanding and their defeat happens because the LORD gave them up. Their enemies' rock is not like Israel's Rock, and the LORD will repay in his time.

    Justice and judgmentGod's sovereignty
  10. Israel makes a treaty without asking God
    Joshua 9:14–15 · narrative · 1405 BC – 1404 BC

    The men of Israel examine the food but do not ask the LORD. Joshua makes peace with the Gibeonites, and the leaders swear an oath.

    CovenantObedience
  11. Jotham's fable of the trees
    Judges 9:7–21 · parable · 1130 BC – 1125 BC

    From Mount Gerizim, Jotham tells of trees seeking a king who are refused by olive, fig, and vine, until a thornbush accepts. He applies it: if Shechem acted honestly toward his father's house, may they rejoice, but if not, may fire consume both Abimelech and Shechem.

    Kings and kingshipLeadership
  12. David pretends madness before Achish
    1 Samuel 21:10–15 · narrative · 1016 BC – 1012 BC

    David flees to Gath, and Achish's servants recall the song about him. Afraid, David acts insane, and Achish sends him away.

    Opposition and persecution
  13. A servant warns Abigail
    1 Samuel 25:14–17 · speech · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    One of Nabal's young men tells Abigail that David's men had treated the shepherds well, that Nabal insulted them, and that disaster now threatens the household. He urges her to act.

    Leadership
  14. Abigail sets out with gifts
    1 Samuel 25:18–22 · narrative · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Abigail quickly loads donkeys with bread, wine, meat, grain, raisins and figs and sends them ahead without telling Nabal. Meanwhile David, riding toward her, has just vowed to kill every male of Nabal's household by morning.

    Peace and reconciliationGenerosity and giving
  15. Abigail pleads with David
    1 Samuel 25:23–31 · speech · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    Abigail bows, takes the blame on herself, and calls Nabal a fool. She offers her gift, credits the LORD with restraining David, and says that a future without needless bloodshed will spare him guilt.

    Peace and reconciliationWords and speech
  16. David blesses God and accepts her gift
    1 Samuel 25:32–35 · speech · 1016 BC – 1013 BC

    David thanks the LORD for sending Abigail, praises her discernment, and admits he would have killed Nabal's men. He accepts her gift and sends her home in peace.

    Providence
  17. The woman of Tekoa's story
    2 Samuel 14:1–11 · dialogue · 978 BC – 975 BC

    Joab sends a wise woman to pose as a mourning widow whose surviving son is threatened with death for killing his brother. David promises that no harm will come to her son.

    Family and marriageJustice and judgment
  18. She applies the story to Absalom
    2 Samuel 14:12–20 · dialogue · 978 BC – 975 BC

    The woman says the king's decision condemns his own refusal to bring back his banished son. David realizes Joab is behind it, and she praises David's wisdom.

    Kings and kingshipMercy and compassion
  19. David on the Mount of Olives; Hushai sent back
    2 Samuel 15:30–37 · narrative · 972 BC – 971 BC

    David climbs weeping, barefoot, and prays that Ahithophel's counsel will fail. He sends Hushai back to feign loyalty to Absalom and counter Ahithophel.

    PrayerSuffering
  20. Hushai declares loyalty to Absalom
    2 Samuel 16:15–19 · dialogue · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Absalom arrives with Ahithophel, and Hushai greets him as king. Questioned about leaving David, he says he will serve the one chosen by the LORD and the people.

    Opposition and persecutionLeadership
  21. Ahithophel's advice to Absalom
    2 Samuel 16:20–23 · dialogue · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Ahithophel urges Absalom to sleep with David's concubines so all Israel sees a permanent break with his father. Absalom does so on the palace roof, and the narrator says Ahithophel's counsel was highly regarded.

    Sin and the fallKings and kingship
  22. Ahithophel and Hushai give rival advice
    2 Samuel 17:1–14 · dialogue · 972 BC – 971 BC

    Ahithophel proposes pursuing David that night with 12,000 men. Hushai counters that David and his men are fierce and advises gathering all Israel first, and Absalom prefers Hushai because the LORD meant to thwart Ahithophel.

    ProvidenceLeadership
  23. Ahithophel's death; David at Mahanaim
    2 Samuel 17:23–29 · narrative · 972 BC – 971 BC

    When Ahithophel sees his advice ignored, he puts his affairs in order and hangs himself. David reaches Mahanaim, where several supporters bring supplies, while Absalom camps in Gilead.

    Death and mortalityKings and kingship
  24. The wise woman of Abel ends the siege
    2 Samuel 20:14–22 · narrative · 971 BC – 970 BC

    Joab besieges the city where Sheba took refuge, and a wise woman persuades him to spare it. The people give up Sheba's head, and Joab returns to Jerusalem.

    Peace and reconciliationWar and conflict
  25. God offers Solomon anything at Gibeon
    1 Kings 3:5–15 · dialogue · 970 BC – 967 BC

    In a dream God tells Solomon to ask for anything. Solomon asks for an understanding heart to govern, and God grants wisdom and adds riches and honor.

    Dreams, visions and revelationPrayer
  26. Solomon judges the two mothers
    1 Kings 3:16–28 · narrative · 970 BC – 966 BC

    Two women dispute over a living baby. Solomon orders it to be cut in two, and the true mother's plea shows who she is. All Israel sees God's wisdom in him.

    Justice and judgment
  27. Solomon's God-given wisdom and fame
    1 Kings 4:29–34 · praise · 970 BC – 931 BC

    God gives Solomon wisdom greater than anyone's; he composes proverbs and songs and teaches about plants and animals. People from all nations come to hear him.

    The nations
  28. The queen of Sheba visits Solomon
    1 Kings 10:1–13 · narrative · 946 BC – 935 BC

    The queen of Sheba tests Solomon with hard questions and sees his wisdom and wealth. She praises the LORD, gives gifts and returns home.

    The nationsWealth and poverty
  29. Solomon surpasses all kings
    1 Kings 10:23–29 · history · 946 BC – 931 BC

    Solomon surpasses all other kings in riches and wisdom, and visitors bring tribute. He gathers chariots and horses and trades them with Egypt and other kings.

    Wealth and povertyKings and kingship
  30. Rehoboam rejects the elders' advice
    1 Kings 12:1–15 · narrative · 931 BC

    Israel comes to Shechem and asks Rehoboam to lighten Solomon's heavy burden. He rejects his elders' advice and threatens to make it heavier, which the narrator says was part of God's plan.

    LeadershipPride and humility
