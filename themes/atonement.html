  1. Noah's offering and God's promise
    Genesis 8:20–22 · narrative

    Noah builds an altar and offers burnt offerings. The Lord is pleased and resolves never again to curse the ground or destroy all life, as long as the seasons of the earth continue.

    Worship and praisePromise
  2. God tests Abraham
    Genesis 22:1–8 · narrative · 2056 BC – 2040 BC

    God tells Abraham to offer Isaac as a burnt offering on Moriah. Abraham sets out early. Isaac asks where the lamb is, and Abraham answers that God will provide.

    FaithObedience
  3. The angel stops Abraham; the ram
    Genesis 22:9–14 · narrative · 2056 BC – 2040 BC

    Abraham binds Isaac and raises the knife, but the angel of the Lord stops him. Abraham sees a ram caught in a thicket, offers it instead, and names the place 'The Lord Will Provide'.

    FaithSalvation
  4. Instructions for the Passover lamb
    Exodus 12:1–13 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    The LORD makes this month the first of the year. Each household is to keep a year-old lamb, slaughter it at twilight, put its blood on the doorframes, and eat it roasted in haste, so that judgment will pass over them.

    Sabbath and feastsSalvation
  5. Covenant sealed with blood and vow
    Exodus 24:1–8 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    The LORD summons Moses and leaders to worship from a distance. Moses relays the laws, the people promise to obey, and Moses writes them down, builds an altar, offers sacrifices, reads the Book of the Covenant and sprinkles blood on the people.

    CovenantObedience
  6. The ark and mercy seat
    Exodus 25:10–22 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God gives dimensions for a gold-covered wooden ark with carrying poles, to hold the Testimony, and a gold mercy seat with two cherubim. God says he will meet and speak with Moses there.

    Temple and tabernacleHoliness
  7. The bronze altar
    Exodus 27:1–8 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God gives dimensions for a square hollow altar of acacia wood covered in bronze, with horns on the corners, bronze utensils, a grate and carrying poles.

    Temple and tabernacle
  8. Sacrifices for ordination
    Exodus 29:10–28 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God describes the bull as a sin offering, one ram as a burnt offering, and a second ram of ordination whose blood is put on the priests' ear, thumb and toe. Portions are waved before the LORD and become the priests' share.

    Priesthood
  9. Seven days of ordination
    Exodus 29:29–37 · ritual instruction · 1446 BC

    God says Aaron's garments pass to his successors, the ordination meal must be eaten at the tent entrance and leftovers burned. For seven days a bull is offered daily to cleanse and consecrate the altar.

    Priesthood
  10. The half-shekel atonement payment
    Exodus 30:11–16 · law · 1446 BC

    When a census is taken, each man twenty or older must pay a half shekel, rich and poor alike, as a ransom for his life. The money goes to the service of the Tent of Meeting.

    Redemption
  11. Moses intercedes for forgiveness
    Exodus 32:30–35 · dialogue · 1446 BC

    The next day Moses returns to the LORD and asks him to forgive the people, or else blot Moses out of his book. God says he will blot out those who sinned, tells Moses to lead on with his angel, and sends a plague.

    ForgivenessPrayer
  12. Making the bronze altar and basin
    Exodus 38:1–8 · narrative · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    Bezalel makes the bronze-covered altar of burnt offering with its horns, utensils, grate and poles. He also makes the bronze basin and stand from the mirrors of the women who served at the tent entrance.

    Temple and tabernacle
  13. Totals of gold, silver and bronze
    Exodus 38:24–31 · list · 1446 BC – 1445 BC

    The passage records the amounts of gold, silver and bronze used, including silver from the half-shekel payment of 603,550 men. It says what the metals were used for, such as bases, hooks and the bronze altar.

    Temple and tabernacle
  14. The LORD introduces offerings to Israel
    Leviticus 1:1–2 · law · 1445 BC

    The LORD calls to Moses from the Tent of Meeting and tells him to instruct the Israelites about bringing animal offerings from the herd or flock.

    Worship and praiseTemple and tabernacle
  15. Burnt offering from the herd
    Leviticus 1:3–9 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    An unblemished bull is brought to the entrance, the worshiper lays a hand on it, and it is slaughtered. The priests splash the blood on the altar and burn the whole animal as a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

    Worship and praise
  16. Burnt offering from the flock
    Leviticus 1:10–13 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    If the worshiper brings a sheep or goat instead, it is also an unblemished male, killed on the north side of the altar and burned whole in much the same way.

    Worship and praise
  17. Burnt offering of birds
    Leviticus 1:14–17 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A poorer worshiper may bring a turtledove or young pigeon. The priest prepares it at the altar and burns it, and the text again calls it a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

    Wealth and povertyWorship and praise
  18. Peace offering from the herd
    Leviticus 3:1–5 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A male or female animal from the herd, without blemish, is presented as a peace offering. The worshiper lays a hand on it, and the priests burn its fat, kidneys, and liver lobe on the altar.

    Worship and praise
  19. Peace offering from the flock
    Leviticus 3:6–11 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A lamb or sheep from the flock is offered in the same way, with its fatty tail included in the portions burned on the altar as food for the LORD.

    Worship and praise
  20. Goat offering and ban on fat and blood
    Leviticus 3:12–17 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A goat is offered with the same fat portions burned, and all the fat is declared the LORD's. The chapter ends with a permanent rule that the Israelites must not eat fat or blood.

    Law and commandments
  21. Sin offering for the anointed priest
    Leviticus 4:1–12 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    When the anointed priest sins unintentionally and brings guilt on the people, he brings a young bull. Its blood is sprinkled before the veil and put on the incense altar's horns, and the carcass is burned outside the camp.

    Sin and the fallPriesthood
  22. Sin offering for the whole community
    Leviticus 4:13–21 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    If the whole congregation sins unintentionally, the elders lay hands on a young bull, and the priest handles its blood as for the priest's own offering. The bull is burned outside the camp and the people are forgiven.

    Sin and the fall
  23. Sin offering for a leader
    Leviticus 4:22–26 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A leader who sins unintentionally brings an unblemished male goat. Its blood goes on the altar of burnt offering, its fat is burned, and the priest makes atonement.

    Sin and the fallLeadership
  24. Sin offering from a commoner: a goat
    Leviticus 4:27–31 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    An ordinary person who sins unintentionally brings an unblemished female goat. The priest applies its blood to the altar and burns the fat, and the person is forgiven.

    ForgivenessSin and the fall
  25. Sin offering from a commoner: a lamb
    Leviticus 4:32–35 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    A commoner may instead bring an unblemished female lamb, handled the same way, and the priest makes atonement so the person is forgiven.

    Forgiveness
  26. Offerings for those who cannot afford a lamb
    Leviticus 5:7–13 · law · 1445 BC

    A person too poor for a lamb may bring two birds, one for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering. If even that is too much, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour, without oil or frankincense, is accepted.

    Wealth and povertyMercy and compassion
  27. Guilt offering for harming holy things
    Leviticus 5:14–16 · law · 1445 BC

    When someone unintentionally wrongs the LORD's holy things, he brings a ram as a guilt offering. He must also repay the value plus a fifth to the priest.

    HolinessSin and the fall
  28. Guilt offering for unknowing wrongs
    Leviticus 5:17–19 · law · 1445 BC

    A person who breaks a command without realizing it is still guilty and brings a ram as a guilt offering. The priest makes atonement and he is forgiven.

    Sin and the fallForgiveness
  29. Law of the sin offering
    Leviticus 6:24–30 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    The sin offering is slaughtered where the burnt offering is, and the priest who offers it eats it in the holy courtyard. If its blood was brought into the tent, the meat must instead be burned.

    HolinessPriesthood
  30. Law of the guilt offering
    Leviticus 7:1–10 · ritual instruction · 1445 BC

    The guilt offering is handled like the sin offering: fat burned, blood splashed on the altar, and the meat eaten by male priests in a holy place. Priests also receive the hide of burnt offerings and most grain offerings.

    Priesthood
