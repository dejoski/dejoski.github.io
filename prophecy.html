The promised deliverer

From Eden and Abraham to David: the promises that everything later builds on.

The offspring who will crush the serpent

Traditional reading

After the fall, God tells the serpent that the woman's offspring will strike its head while it strikes his heel. Christian tradition has long read this as the first hint of a coming deliverer (sometimes called the protoevangelium). The New Testament alludes to it (Romans 16:20) but does not quote it as a prediction of Jesus.

The promise / pattern

15 And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed. He will crush your head, and you will strike his heel.”

Genesis 3:15
→
Echoed in

20 The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.

Romans 16:20 — "The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet."

When: Set at the beginning of human history; written down within the Torah.

Ways people read this (2)
  • Traditional Christian reading: a promise of Christ's victory over evil.
  • Many Jewish and some modern Christian readers see a general statement about the ongoing conflict between humanity and evil.

A virgin will conceive and bear a son

Debated

Isaiah gives King Ahaz a sign: a young woman will conceive, bear a son, and call him Immanuel ('God with us'). Matthew says Jesus' virginal conception fulfilled this. Readers differ about how the sign worked in Isaiah's own day and how it points to Jesus.

The promise / pattern

Where the New Testament applies it

Matthew 1:22–23

When: Spoken c. 735–732 BC during the Syro-Ephraimite crisis; applied by Matthew, c. AD 60–85.

Ways people read this (4)

  • Hebrew 'almah' means a young woman; the Greek translation (Septuagint) used 'parthenos', which Matthew follows.

Jesus and the Scriptures

Matthew’s fulfillment formulas and the texts Jesus and the gospel writers quote, from his temptation to his last words.

He will be called a Nazarene

Debated

Matthew says that by settling in Nazareth, Jesus fulfilled what was spoken 'by the prophets', yet no Old Testament verse says these words. Matthew uses the plural 'prophets', so he may be summarizing a theme rather than quoting one text. Interpreters propose several links, none certain.

The promise / pattern

1 Then a shoot will spring up from the stump of Jesse, and a Branch from his roots will bear fruit.

Isaiah 11:1

3 He was despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows, acquainted with grief. Like one from whom men hide their faces, He was despised, and we esteemed Him not.

Isaiah 53:3

5 For behold, you will conceive and give birth to a son. And no razor shall touch his head, because the boy will be a Nazirite to God from the womb, and he will begin the deliverance of Israel from the hand of the Philistines.”

Judges 13:5
→
Where the New Testament applies it

23 and he went and lived in a town called Nazareth. So was fulfilled what was spoken through the prophets: “He will be called a Nazarene.”

Matthew 2:23

When: Matthew's account of Jesus' childhood; written c. AD 60–85.

Ways people read this (4)
  • A wordplay on the Hebrew 'netzer' (branch, shoot) in Isaiah 11:1, so 'Nazarene' recalls the Davidic Branch.
  • An allusion to the despised status of Nazareth and of the suffering servant (compare John 1:46 and Isaiah 53:3).
  • A reference to Nazirite-like holiness (Judges 13:5), though Jesus is not described as a Nazirite.
  • Matthew cites a prophetic theme (contempt and lowliness) rather than a specific verse, or a source we no longer have.
